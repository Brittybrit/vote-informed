Hernando Beach is a coastal community located along canals and channels directly connected to the Gulf of Mexico. The 
area is highly vulnerable to storm surge and coastal flooding due to its low elevation and proximity to sea level. The entire 
community lies within FEMA-designated Coastal Flood Zones, making the area particularly susceptible to flooding and its 
associated environmental impacts. 
The project aims to enhance the wastewater infrastructure's resiliency against these climate-related threats, reducing the 
potential for system failures, ensuring proper wastewater management during extreme weather events, and preventing the 
environmental contamination that could result from system overflow or failure.

 The Hernando Beach Wastewater Resiliency Project – Inflow and Infiltration Reduction seeks funding to strategically 
upgrade and repair the conventional gravity sanitary sewer system in Hernando Beach. The proposed improvements aim 
to mitigate the impacts of stormwater infiltration and enhance the resilience of the community’s wastewater infrastructure. 
Sewer slip lining, manhole coatings, lift station upgrades, and installing manhole inflow protectors.
