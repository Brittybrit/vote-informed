 1. Project Title   Enhancements to Hernando County Sheriff's Office Animal Shelter and

 2. Senate Sponsor   Blaise Ingoglia 
 3. Date of Request   3/4/2025 
The Hernando County Sheriff's Office Animal Shelter faces challenges that limit its ability to effectively serve both the 
animals and the community. This proposal seeks to enhance the facility by addressing critical infrastructure issues, 
expanding space, and upgrading key areas to create a safe, comfortable environment for animals in its care. Some key 
improvements include renovating the dog kennels and cat rooms; a new adoption center for meet-and-greets and adoption 
processing; a dedicated grooming area to ensure proper hygiene and care; expanding intake, quarantine, and storage 
space; and adding a vet clinic and pet food pantry for low-income families. These upgrades will enhance animal welfare, 
encourage responsible pet ownership, and promote healthier pets, leading to stronger community engagement and benefit 
public health.

 This proposal aims to create a safe, comfortable, and efficient environment for all animals in the shelter's care by 
addressing critical infrastructure challenges and enhance its capacity to serve both the animals and the public. We also 
seek to educate and support the community by making veterinary services and adoption more accessible, while 
promoting responsible pet ownership and reducing pet abandonment. Ultimately, the goal is to modernize and improve 
the shelter, so it can better serve both the animals and the community, ensuring long term benefits for animal welfare and 
public health. 
Our goal is to renovate and upgrade key infrastructure, including dog kennels, drainage, ventilation, pole barn, and cat 
rooms. We plan to add a new adoption center to better accommodate meet-and-greets and adoption processing, along 
with a dedicated grooming area to ensure proper hygiene and care. We will also increase space for intakes, quarantine, 
and storage, a veterinary clinic and pet food pantry for low-income individuals/families, and implement a Trap-Neuter- 
Vaccinate-Return (TNVR) program for stray cats in the community. 
The new adoption center will enhance meet-and-greet sessions and streamline the adoption process, improving the 
overall experience for the public and reducing pet returns. The addition of a veterinary clinic and pet food pantry will 
support low-income pet owners who may not otherwise have access to essential veterinary care, promoting responsible 
pet ownership and reducing pet abandonment. Implementing a Trap-Neuter-Vaccinate-Return (TNVR) program will help 
reduce the stray animal population, promoting healthier, more sustainable communities. 
The target population includes the animals as well as the staff and volunteers at the animal shelter, along with the 
218,000 residents of Hernando County. 
Improved well being of the animals, measured by observations or feedback from staff and volunteers. Higher adoption 
rates, measured by shelter data. Less adoption returns, measured by shelter data. Increased public engagement, 
measured by increased adoption rates, pet retention rates, and customer satisfaction surveys. Increased workplace 
engagement, performance, and mental well being for the employees and volunteers, measured by surveys and feedback. 
We are committed to meeting all deliverables and performance measures outlined in the contract, and if we're unable to 
do so, we believe the standard contract penalties will be appropriate.
