 4 PARKLAND, FLORIDA, AMENDING CHAPTER 22 ENTITLED "ZONING";

 6 SECTION 22-242 ENTITLED "SUPPLEMENTAL REGULATIONS TO

 8 PHARMACIES AND OTHER USES NOT ASSOCIATED WITH HOSPITALS
 9 WHICH DISPENSE PRESCRIPTION PAIN MEDICATIONS; PROVIDING

 12 CLAUSE; AND PROVIDING FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE.
 13
 14 WHEREAS, the public health, safety, and welfare is a legitimate public purpose
 15 recognized by the courts of Florida; and
 16
 17 WHEREAS, there has been a recent proliferation of pain management clinics in
 18 South Florida; and
 19
 20 WHEREAS, the City of Parkland does not currently regulate pharmacies and other
 21 uses that are not associated with hospitals which dispense prescription pain medications;
 22 and
 23
 24 WHEREAS, on November 19, 2009, the State Attorney's Office for the 17th Judicial
 25 Circuit in Broward County, Florida, issued an interim report of the Broward County Grand
 26 Jury titled "The Proliferation of Pain Clinics in South Florida," which established the
 27 following facts: (i) from August 2008 to November 2009, a new pain clinic opened in
 28 Broward and Palm Beach counties on average every three (3) days; (ii) during the first six
 29 (6) months on 2008, eighteen (18) of the top twenty-five (25) dispensing doctors of
 30 Oxycodone in the nation were located in Broward County, Florida; and (iii) in 2008,
 31 prescription drugs were attributed to an average of nearly 13 IA deaths per day in Florida;
 32 and
 33
 34 WHEREAS, the Broward County Grand Jury found that in the areas where pain
 35 management clinics are located, burglaries, robberies, drug trafficking in prescription
 36 drugs, street level sales of prescription drugs, identify theft, and organized criminal
 37 activities have all increased; and
 38
 39 WHEREAS, on February 2, 2010, the Broward County Board of County
 40 Commissioners created a Pain Management Clinic Task Force to review, research, and
 41 make recommendations to the Broward County Board of County Commissioners
 42 regarding the issues involved with regulating pain management clinics; and
 43
 44 WHEREAS, on January 21, 2011, the Pain Management Clinic Task Force
 45 submitted its final report to the Broward County Board of County Commissioners; and

 18 Sec. 22-242. Supplemental regulations to master business list.
 19
 20 The following are the supplemental regulations to the master business list which are
 21 designated by number on the list:
 22
 23 (1) Must be completely enclosed in a soundproof, air-conditioned building.
 24
 25 (2) The following regulations shall apply where a plot in a nonresidential district
 26 is utilized for a permitted residential use:
 27
 28 a. Such residential use shall not exceed fifty (50) percent of the gross
 29 floor area of any structure.
 30
 31 b. Such residential use shall conform to the RM-16 district regulations.
 32
 33 c. No residential use shall be located on the first floor or ground floor
 34 other than those uses specified below:
 35
 36 1. Lobby or foyer serving a residential use.
 37
 38 2. Accessory uses, such as storage areas, garages and other
 39 similar facilities.
 40
 41 d. No building permit shall be issued for any uses involving residential
 42 uses until such time as the city commission determines that the residential
 43 use is consistent with the city land use plan and the allocation of the
 44 necessary reserve units has been approved by the city commission.
 45
 46 (3) Dry-cleaning establishments for direct service to customers subject to the
 47 following limitations and requirements:

 24 (8) Pain management clinics and pharmacies are deemed special exception
 25 uses. In addition to all other requirements, such facilities shall comply with
 26 additional requirements. To provide adequate protection to the City and
 27 establish the legitimacy of the facility, the Special exception application
 28 submission for pharmacies, pain management clinics or any other uses that are
 29 not associated with hospitals which dispense prescription pain medications must, in
 30 addition to the criteria set forth in Chapter 22, Article XXIII, address and comply
 31 with the following:

 33 a. No business approved as a special exception under this section shall
 34 limit the form of payment for services or prescriptions to cash only.

 38 policy in its application and the failure of any business to accept
 39 insurance, Medicare or Medicaid reimbursements shall be considered by
 40 the Planning and Zoning Board and City Commission in making its
 41 decision as to the appropriateness of granting a special exception
 42 application.

 17 medications shall include an affidavit by the owner or the physician of

 20 independent contractor or volunteer having regular contact with customers
 21 of the business, has been convicted of a drug-related felony within the five
 22 (5) year period prior to the date of the application, and that the business
 23 shall not employ or allow any such convicted employee, independent
 24 contractor, or volunteer on the premises thereafter.

 26 f. The business shall not be owned in whole or in part by any person who
 27 has been convicted of or who has pled guilty or nolo contendere to any
 28 felony in this state or in any other state within the five-year period prior to
 29 the date of application for a special exception use. However, in no event
 30 shall the business be owned in whole or in part by any person who has
 31 been convicted of or who has pled guilty or nolo contendrere at any time
 32 to an offense constituting a felony in this state or in any other state
 33 involving the prescribing, dispensing, supplying or selling of any controlled
 34 substance.
 35

 38 limited to the hours of operation between 9:00 a.m. and 9:00 p.m.,
 39 Monday through Saturday.
