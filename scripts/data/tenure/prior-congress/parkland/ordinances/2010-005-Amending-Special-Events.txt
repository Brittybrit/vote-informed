 4 (b) Special events definition. Special events shall mean any meeting, activity,
 5 parade or gathering of a group of persons, animals or vehicles or combination
 6 thereof, having a common purpose on any publicly or privately owned property,
 7 sidewalk, alley, park, lake or publicly or privately owned place or building, which
 8 special event substantially inhibits the usual flow of pedestrian or vehicular travel
 9 or which occupies any public place or building so as to preempt normal public or
 10 private use of space or which deviates from the established, legally permitted use
 11 of a space or building. Special events are activities that are of a temporary or
 12 seasonal nature. Usage of the city park pavilions through an executed rental
 13 agreement and activities located on private or public schools which are contained
 14 within the school site and will not require the assistance form from the City of
 15 Parkland shall not require a special events permit. Any activity that is not so
 16 determined, shall comply with all applicable city requirements including, but not
 17 limited to, the city's land development regulations. All temporary and seasonal
 18 sales on publicly or privately owned property or parks shall be considered special
 19 events.

 7 Sec. 22-372. Permit requirements.
 8 The applicant shall provide all information solicited on the special event permit
 9 application form, unless waived by the planning-director Planning and Zoning Director
 10 and/or Parks and Recreation Director, upon finding that such information is not
 11 necessary.
 12
 13 (1) Authority. Notarized evidence that the property owner/managing agent
 14 authorizes the use of the site for the special event reflected on the special event
 15 permit application.
 16
 17 (2) Site plan. A detailed site plan for the event, including but not limited to,
 18 property boundaries; road access; location of trash receptacles, sanitary
 19 facilities, tents or other structures; location of rides if applicable; location of
 20 parking; location of temporary dwellings, offices, and equipment; and proposed
 21 setbacks of activities, fences, tents, booths, etc. from adjacent properties.

 23 (3) Statement of use. A detailed statement of use, including but not limited to
 24 sponsor(s), vendors(s), band(s) and/or other musical operations, planned
 25 activities, duration of event, hours of operation, anticipated attendance,
 26 temporary lighting to be provided on-site, security, utilities, and use of
 27 generators.

 29 (4) Signage. Signs are permitted only if related to a special event.

 38 proposed location, size, type, copy area, graphics, and color, including the
 39 dates of installation and removal of the sign for with the event.

 42 may be approved per the discretion of the planning director. No signs shall
 43 be permitted in the safe site visibility triangles.

 1 (1) Location. Special events may occur only on properties possessing the
 2 following nonresidential zoning district designations: B-1, B-2, B-3, PCD, OP, OS,
 3 CR, and CF. In residential portions of PUDs, PCDs, or PRDs, a special events
 4 permit must be obtained for special events occurring on public or private schools,
 5 churches and houses of worship, and public parks. For private property in
 6 residential zoning districts, a special events permit is not required as long as the
 7 property owner can provide documentation that the event will not interfere with
 8 the public's safety and welfare which includes, at a minimum, parking will be
 9 provided on site and not spill to adjacent properties.
 10
 11 (2) Duration. A special event shall not exceed seven (7) consecutive days. The
 12 planning director may authorize one administrative time extension of up to seven
 13 (7) days for good cause and for the public interest of the city. Pumpkin sales and
 14 Christmas tree sales shall not exceed thirty (30) days.
 15
 16 (3) Number per year.
 17
 18 a. No more than three (3) special events every calendar year shall be
 19 held by any applicant at one (1) location, unless otherwise approved by
 20 the city commission following a recommendation by the planning director.
 21
 22 b. No events shall occur consecutively, except for those with city
 23 commission approval, and if approved shall require a separate permit and
 24 fee for each event.
 25
 26 c. Tenants located in commercial/office plazas, are permitted three (3)
 27 special events per tenant each calendar year; however, this number of
 Z8 permitted special events shall exclusively apply to each tenant, and
 29 cannot be transferred to another tenant or party.
 30
 31 d. Sub-tenants within commercial/office plazas are not permitted any
 32 special events.
 33
 34 e. Commercial/office plazas shall be limited to a maximum of ten (10)
 35 special events per calendar year. However, the city commission may
 36 authorize additional special events per year for good cause and for the
 37 public interest of the city.
 38
 39 f. Anchor tenants are each permitted three (3) events per calendar year
 40 exclusive of the limitation in subsection e. above.
 41
 42 g. No more than two (2) special events at any given time may occur
 43 simultaneously at any commercial/office plaza. Special event applications
 44 will be reviewed and approved by the city on a first-come, first-serve basis.

 20 Sec. 22-374. Denial of special event permit.
 21 A special event permit is a special privilege granted by the city and may be denied for
 22 reasons such as, but not limited to the following:
 23
 24 (1) The application is not complete in all material. The planning director
 25 Planning and Zoning Director and/or Parks and Recreation Director may allow for
 26 any additional materials to be submitted within seven (7) days of notification;
 27
 28 (2) All of the conditions set forth in this article and all contractual requirements
 29 imposed by the city, if any, have not been met;
 30
 31 (3) The special event will interfere with or unduly burden municipal services
 32 including but not limited to police, fire, and emergency medical protection, water
 33 and sanitary sewer service and solid waste removal;
 34
 35 (4) The public benefit has not been demonstrated;
 36
 37 (5) The permit fee has not been paid; and
 38
 39 (6) Any other valid reason including but not limited to noise, traffic, harm to the
 40 health, safety or public welfare, as so determined by the city.
 41

 14 Sec. 22-376. Exemption of public right-of-way closures.
 15 Any block party that is temporarily blocking off, fencing, or in any way appropriating any
 16 publicly dedicated street, sidewalk, or alley within the city for a block party is hereby
 17 exempt from a filing fee; however, such permit application shall be subject to review by
 18 the planning- director Planning and Zoning Director and/or Parks and Recreation
 19 Director only. Closure of any public right-of-way not within the city's jurisdiction shall
 20 require approval from the appropriate authority. A "block party" requires approval from
 21 the board of directors of a property owners association, if one exists, or a majority of
 22 residents living on the block to be closed to consent to closure, and the event must be
 23 sponsored solely by such residents and not be for any commercial or for-profit purpose.

 27 Sec. 22-379. Fire rescue services/emergency medical services.
 28 The city's fire rescue department shall review the permit application and assess the
 29 need for fire rescue services, fire watch and/or emergency medical services. If such
 30 services are required, the applicant will be informed of such and will take responsibility
 31 for contacting the fire rescue department directly. The applicant is responsible for the
 32 cost of such services. The fire chief or his/her designee is the final authority on the need
 33 for fire rescue and/or emergency medical services.
 34
 35 Sec. 22-380. Appeal of permit application.
 36 The applicant may appeal, by written request, the denial of a special event permit to the
 37 city manager's office or designee within five (5) business days of the planning director's
 38 Planning and Zoning Director and/or Parks and Recreation Director's decision. The
 39 written request shall specifically state what portion of the denial is being appealed. The
 40 city manager's office shall have five (5) business days to make a decision and to notify
 41 the applicant. The city shall reserve the right to charge a nonrefundable filing fee to
 42 offset all, or a portion of, the costs involved in handling the appeal.
