 15 Section 1. Section 2-142 of the City Code is hereby amended to read as follows:
 16
 17 Sec. 2-142. Competitive bids.
 18 All purchases and contractual services except as otherwise provided herein, when the
 19 estimated cost shall exceed twenty-five thousand dollars ($25,000.00) shall be purchased
 20 by a formal written contract from the lowest responsive bidder after due notice inviting bids.
 21 Purchases or contractual services whose estimated or actual value exceeds twenty-five
 22 thousand dollars ($25,000.00) must be presented to the city commission for acceptance or
 23 rejections. The City Manager has authority to execute contracts for under $25,000.00
 24 where the funds have been budgeted by the City Commission for the general purpose of
 25 the contract. Formal bidding may be waived in lieu of piggybacking opportunities as
 26 defined in section 2-144.9C or as otherwise excepted in this Code.
 27 Section 2. Section 2-143.1 of the City Code is hereby amended to read as
 28 follows:
 29 Sec. 2-143.1. Bid specifications.
 30 The preparation of specifications is the responsibility of the user department. Specifications
 31 are important and are intended to promote competition among vendors. Specifications
 32 should be written in terms which define the performance required or the design of the
 33 goods/services, rather than using specific brand names. Specifications should be clear and
 34 concise, and they should provide complete and detailed descriptions of the item(s) and/or
 35 services(s) being solicited, including quantity of items or dates of contractual services.
 36 If the goods or services desired is outside the expertise of the department personnel and
 37 specifications cannot be prepared by the department, the department head may contact
 38 prospective bidders with a written request for information and request recommended
 39 solutions to provide city staff with an adequate understanding to prepare the specifications.
 40 Prospective bidders and city personnel are not permitted to discuss pricing during this

<<PG>>
 41 process.
 42 In addition to the specifications, the bid package must contain the specific instructions and
 43 information to potential bidders. The bid package must:
 44 (a) State the time, date and place where bids will be received.
 45 (b) State statement of work, instructions for preparing proposals and evaluation criteria
 46 to determine the best bidder.
 47 (c) Provide standard bid language to address Public Entity Crimes Act; Compliance
 48 with Laws; Drug Free Work Place; and EEO and M/WBE Participation.
 49 The bid package must inform potential bidders of the following requirements. Bid must:
 50 (a) Be sealed.
 51 (b) Have the bid number plainly marked on the outside of the envelope with proposer's
 52 return address.
 53 (c) Have the response due date and/or the time and date of the bid opening plainly
 54 marked on the outside of the envelope.
 55 (d) State the date by which goods and services must be delivered.
 56 The bid package may contain such other requirements as deemed appropriate and is at
 57 the sole discretion of the city.
 58 Section 3. Section 2-143.2 of the City Code is hereby amended to read as
 59 follows:
 60 Sec. 2-143.2. Bonding.
 61 (1) Bid bond. The purpose of a bid bond is to protect the city against the failure or
 62 refusal of the low bidder to supply the necessary performance and payment bonds, as
 63 required, to proceed with performance under the contract. Bid bond requirements shall
 64 be at the discretion of the department director, however all construction bids over one
 65 hundred thousand dollars ($100,000.00) must have a bid bond.
 66 Procedure
 67 (a) A bid bond shall be provided by the bidder at the time of the bid, and is submitted
 68 with the bid.
 69 (b) The amount of the bid bond is generally expressed as a percentage of the total
 70 amount of the bid. Typically, this is five (5) percent unless otherwise stated in the
 71 solicitation to bid.
 72 (c) If a contractor fails to submit a bid bond with their bid, the bid will be rejected.
 73 (d) The City of Parkland reserves the right to accept a certified check in lieu of a bid
 74 bond.
 75 (2) Performance bond, payment bond and warranty bond. The purpose of bonds is to
 76 protect the City of Parkland against any losses resulting from the contractor's failure to
 77 meet the plans and/or specifications, providing labor and materials and faulty
 2

<<PG>>
 78 workmanship or defective materials of a contract.
 79 Payment and performance bonds shall be required for projects in excess of two
 80 hundred thousand dollars ($200,000.00) or as otherwise provided by law.
 81 Section 4. Section 2-143.5 of the City Code is hereby amended to read as
 82 follows:
 83 Sec. 2-143.5. Award of bid.
 84 Bids shall be awarded by the city commission to the "lowest responsible and most
 85 qualified responsive bidder" submitting a responsive bid. In determining the lowest
 86 responsible and most qualified responsive bidder, in addition to the price, the following
 87 may be considered:
 88 (a) The ability, capacity, and skill of the bidder to perform the contract.
 89 (b) Whether the bidder can perform the contract within the time specified,without delay
 90 or interference.
 91 (c) The character, integrity, reputation, judgment, experience and efficiency of the
 92 bidder.
 93 (d) The quality of performance on previous public or private contracts of a similar type.
 94 (e) The previous and existing compliance by the bidder with laws and ordinances
 95 relating to the contract.
 96 (f) The ability of the bidder to provide future maintenance and service.
 97 (g) Tie bids. The tie may be broken and the successful vendor selected by the following
 98 criteria presented in order of importance and consideration:
 99 1. Quality of the items or services if it is ascertainable
 100 2. Time of Delivery if provided in the bid
 101 3. If the above criteria are impossible to determine with any reasonableness or do not
 102 resolve the issue, the award will be given to that vendor whose bid was received
 103 first as indicated by the time stamp on the envelope containing the bid.
 104 Bids shall be unconditionally accepted without alteration or correction, except as authorized
 105 in this policy. Bids shall be evaluated based on the requirements set forth in the invitation
 106 for bids, which may include additional criteria to determine suitability for a particular
 107 purpose.
 108 Section 5. Section 2-143.7 of the City Code is hereby amended to read as
 109 follows:
 110 Sec. 2-143.7. Miscellaneous requirements.
 111 Bids shall include the following additional provisions:
 112 (1) Public entity crimes—In accordance with the applicable portions of Section 287.133,
 113 Florida Statutes, which reads as follows "A person or affiliate who has been placed on
 114 the convicted vendor list following a conviction for a public entity crime may not submit
 3

<<PG>>
 115 bids on leases of real property to a public entity may not be awarded or perform work
 116 as a contractor, supplier, subcontractor, or consultant under a contract with any public
 117 entity, and may not transact business with any public entity in excess of the threshold
 118 amount provided in Section 287.017 for CATEGORY TWO for a period of 36 months
 119 from the date of being placed on the convicted vendor list."
 120 (2) Non-collusive affidavit—Each proposer shall complete the non-collusive form and
 121 shall submit the form with the proposal. The failure to submit this document could result
 122 in the rejection of the proposal.
 123 (3) Compliance with laws—The selected firm, its officers, agents, employees, and
 124 contractors, shall abide by and comply with all federal, state, and local laws. It is agreed
 125 and understood that if city calls the attention of contractor to any such violations on the
 126 part of the contractor, its officers, agents, employees, contractors, then contractor shall
 127 immediately desist from and correct such violation. If contractor is in violation of any
 128 law, contractor shall be solely responsible for coming into compliance with such law and
 129 shall be solely responsible for the payment of any fine charged for such violation.
 130 (4) Drug-free workplace—In accordance with Florida Statute 287.087, preference shall
 131 be given to businesses with drug-free workplace programs. Whenever two or more
 132 proposals which are equal with respect to price, quality, and service are received by the
 133 city for the procurement of commodities or contractual services, a proposal received
 134 from a business that completes the attached DFW form certifying that it is a DFW shall
 135 be given preference in the award process.
 136 (5) EEO and M/WBE participation—Proposers are advised that the city promotes equal
 137 employment opportunity (EEO) and encourages the participation of minority and
 138 women business enterprises (M/WBE) as well as small business enterprises in all
 139 aspects of contracting. Joint venturing at the prime and sub-consultant levels is
 140 encouraged where the joint venture results in a more qualified and/or more diverse
 141 proposer. Proposers shall make measurable efforts to maintain no less than fifteen (15)
 142 percent M/WBE participation for contracts over one hundred thousand dollars
 143 ($100,000.00).
 144 (6) The use of multiple contracts may not be employed in order to avoid compliance
 145 with these bid/quotes procedures.

 147 Section 6. Section 2-143.9 of the City Code is hereby amended to read as
 148 follows:
 149 Sec. 2-143.9. Exempt purchases.
 150 The following purchases are exempted from this article and do not require sealed
 151 bidding, or requests for proposals, due to the nature of the goods or service. These
 152 include, but are not limited to the following:
 153 (a) Payment of utility bills for normal monthly service such as Florida Power & Light
 154 (electric), A T & T, Solid Waste Authority (solid waste disposal fees), and any other
 155 utility service.
 4

<<PG>>
 156 (b) Payment of payroll related deductions or liabilities, such as FICA/Medicare, Federal
 157 Withholding Taxes, Pension or (457) Contributions, Credit Union, US Savings
 158 Bonds, IRS and Child Support Garnishment.
 159 (c) Piggy-back bids—If the goods to be purchased have been approved by the city
 160 manager and are included in the annual budget, the user department or city may
 161 "piggyback" any currently authorized public agency bid of the federal government,
 162 the State of Florida, or any local government within the State of Florida. The
 163 purchase order for these goods must refer to the current public agency bid number
 164 and the goods must have the same specifications as those in the bid documents. A
 165 copy of the contract must be provided to the finance department before the
 166 purchase order can be approved.
 167 (d) Payment to service providers needed to maintain the city operations such as
 168 plumbers, electricians, temporary employment services, computer consultants, or
 169 air conditioning service. (This should only pertain to emergencies, not routine
 170 maintenance and does not include the replacement of major capital equipment or
 171 components).
 172 (e) Payment of subscriptions, memberships, dues, education, and travel expenses.
 173 (f) Payment of insurance billings, postage, and postage meter permits.
 174 (g) Payment of authorized debt service.
 175 (h) Payment of authorized donations, or scholarships.
 176 (i) Sole source—A contract within the award authority of the city manager may be
 177 awarded for supplies, services or construction without competition when determined
 178 that there is only one (1) source or one (1) reasonable source for the required
 179 supply, service or construction.
 180 (j) Professional services or a-s contracts otherwise required by law.
 181 (k) Contracts with other governmental entities or agencies for public services.
 182 (I) Franchises to provide municipal or other public services.

 184 Section 7. Section 2-144 of the City Code is hereby amended to read as follows:
 185 Sec. 2-144. Emergency purchases/special circumstances.
 186 The procedures set forth in sections 2-141 through 2 1111 2-143.8 shall not apply for
 187 emergency purchases or where there are special circumstances as set forth herein. An
 188 emergency purchase shall be a purchase during an emergency declared by the
 189 Governor, by the Mayor of Broward County, or the Mayor of the City of Parkland.
 190 Additionally, an emergency purchase shall be a purchase made to avert an imminent
 191 danger to life, safety, health and/or property or breakdowns of equipment that may
 192 cause serious determent of services to residents and where following the procedures
 193 set forth in sections 2-141 through 2 11111 2-143.8 would cause harm to persons or
 194 property. A special circumstance shall include a purchase where there is a lack of
 195 available bidders or vendors, sole source contracts, or other circumstances (not caused
 196 by the city) which make it contrary to the public interests to comply with the
 5

<<PG>>
 197 requirements of sections 2-141 through 2 111 2-143.8. Where the city commission
 198 awards such a contract, the basis for the exception shall be set forth in a resolution.
 199 Where the city manager finds that there is an emergency or that there are special
 200 circumstances, the city manager may approve a contract to purchase materials or
 201 services up to an amount of fifty thousand dollars ($50,000.00). For purchases over
 202 twenty-five thousand dollars ($25,000.00), the city manager shall provide notice to the
 203 city commission of the purchase, state the basis for the emergency or the special
 204 circumstances, and place the notice on record at the next regular city commission
 205 meeting. No contract lasting more than thirty (30) ninety (90) days may be executed
 206 pursuant to the authority granted by this section 2-145 unless ratified by the city
 207 commission prior to the conclusion of the thirty (30) ninety (90) days.
 208 Section 8. Section 2-145 of the City Code is hereby amended to read as follows:
 209 Sec. 2-145. Bid protests.
 210
 211 Where a bidder who submits a bid pursuant to sections 2-141 through2 111 2-143.8
 212 believes that the city has violated any provision of sections 2-141 through 2 114 2-
 213 143.8 , said bidder shall submit written notice to the city manager within three (3)
 214 business days of the subject incident. Said notice shall be labeled as a bid protest
 215 and shall contain the basis for the protest, including, but not limited to, a description
 216 of the sections of the Code violated, and the circumstances which constitute the
 217 violation. Upon receipt of a timely filed notice, the city manager shall schedule a
 218 hearing before the city commission for a determination on the bid protest. The city
 219 manager shall provide notice of the hearing to the person filing the protest and to
 220 any other bidder who has submitted a bid. The city commission determination on the
 221 bid protest shall be final. No bid protest may be filed subsequent to the award of a
 222 bid by the city commission. As a condition of any bid protest, the protestor shall
 223 present the City with a nonrefundable filing fee payable in accordance with the
 224 schedule provided below:

 228 the protestor. If no contract amount was submitted, the estimated contract amount shall be
