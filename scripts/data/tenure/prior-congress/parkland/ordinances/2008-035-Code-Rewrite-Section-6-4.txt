 36 Sec. 6-4. Definitions.
 37 Development permit means any building permit, zoning permit, subdivision or
 38 plat approval, rezoning, special exception, variance or other official action of the city
 39 having the effect of permitting development, but does not include any variance or other
 40 official action necessary solely for the purpose of issuing a permit, other than a building
 41 permit, pursuant to the Florida Building Code, 1992, Broward Edition, as may be
 42 amended from time to time.
 43

 2 (1) Residential construction. New construction or substantial improvement of
 3 any residential structure shall have the lowest floor, including basement, sunken
 4 living room, etc., elevated no lower than the base flood elevation (100-year storm
 5 event), or eighteen (18) inches above the crown of the public access road,
 6 whichever is greater. Should solid foundation perimeter walls be used to elevate
 7 a structure, openings sufficient to facilitate the unimpeded movements of
 8 floodwaters shall be provided in accordance with standards of subsection (3) of
 9 this section.
 10
 11 (2) Nonresidential construction. New construction or substantial improvement
 12 of any commercial, industrial, or nonresidential structure shall have the lowest
 13 floor, including basement, elevated no lower than the base flood elevation (100-
 14 year storm event), or eighteen (18) inches above the crown of the public access
 1s road, whichever is greater. Noninhabitable structures located in all A Zones are
 16 exempt from this chapter. A registered professional land surveyor shall certify
 17 that the standards of this subsection are satisfied. Such certification shall be
 18 provided to the local administrator as set forth in Section 6-25(2).
 19
 20 (3) Elevated buildings. New construction or substantial improvements of
 21 elevated buildings that include fully enclosed areas formed by foundation and
 22 other exterior walls below the base flood elevation shall be designed to preclude
 23 finished living space and designed to allow for the entry and exit of floodwaters to
 24 automatically equalize hydrostatic flood forces on exterior walls.
 25
 26 a. Designs for complying with this requirement must either be certified by
 27 a professional engineer or architect or meet the following minimum
 28 criteria:
 29
 30 1. Provide a minimum of two (2) openings having a total net area
 31 of not less than one square inch for every square foot of enclosed
 32 area subject to flooding;
 33
 34 2. The bottom of all openings shall be no higher than one (1) foot
 35 above grade; and
 36
 37 3. Openings may be equipped with screens, louvers, valves or
 38 other coverings or devices provided they permit the automatic flow
 39 of floodwaters in both directions.
 40
 41 b. Access to the enclosed area shall be the minimum necessary to allow
 42 for parking of vehicles (garage door) or limited storage of maintenance
 43 equipment used in connection with the premises (standard exterior door)
 44 or entry to the living area (stairway or elevator);

 14 (5) Floodways. Located within the areas of special flood hazard established in
 1s Section 6-6 are areas designated as floodways. Since the floodway is an
