REGULAR AGENDA

Add-on item regarding the Broward County School Board update.

City Attorney Andrew Maurodis explained that the City Commission decided that there would be

no annexation unless there was a provision for additional school sites in the Wedge area.

Developers have moved forward and the commitments in the annexation agreements have been

honored. Presented to the City Commission are two agreements for the dedication of two (2)

sites as committed to by Triple H, Inc. and Debuys in conjunction with Standard Pacific. He

explained that the agreements have been executed by the Developer, they will go to the School

Board and then to Broward County for approval.

Commissioner Moskowitz clarified for the record that the School Board has sole authority to
decide whether or not to build a school, however, it is either a school or nothing.

City Attorney Maurodis explained that there is no deed restriction, but the sites will be dedicated
on the plat for schools and the agreement indicates that the land will be used for school purposes.
Commissioner Rosenof questioned if the agreement can be changed.

City Attorney Maurodis explained that the agreement is much easier to enforce if it is part of a
deed restriction, but the School Board has committed to using the site for a school purpose
although they have indicated that it can be used for passive educational purposes in the interim.
The plats also indicate the sites are dedicated for schools.

Commissioner Moskowitz clarified that the School Board can make no changes.

City Attorney Maurodis explained that the School Board can make changes, but the City has its
agreement with the Developers.

Commissioner Willis clarified that the land in question is located at the intersection of Nob Hill
and County Line now called Hillsboro and this land is dedicated for the building of an
elementary and middle school in the Wedge.
City Attorney Maurodis replied in the affirmative and explained that when the Hendrix property
owners get their land use plan amendment, the 40-acres must be committed for a high school.
Commissioner Willis clarified that there is no ability for the land to come back to the City and be
used for a Charter School - that this was only a contingency if the School Board did not want the
land.
City Attorney Maurodis replied in the affirmative.

1. BUDGET
1A. BUDGET PRESENTATION
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young explained that staff is not seeking approval of the budget
rather it is a presentation to show that the City of Parkland can meet a balanced budget based
upon the proposal for the millage rate, fire assessment and the solid waste assessment. Ms.
Nancy Morando then gave a brief presentation. She announced that a Budget workshop will take
place on August 15, 2012 and there will be two (2) Public Hearings on September 12, 2012 and
September 19, 2012 to finalize the budget.
1B. RESOLUTION NO. 2012-69: PROPOSED MILLAGE RATE FOR
PROPERTY APPRAISER (TRIM)
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY
OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, AUTHORIZING THE CITY
MANAGER TO SEND A PROPOSED MILLAGE RATE OF
4.0198 MILLS TO THE PROPERTY APPRAISER FOR FISCAL
YEAR 2013 AND SETTING SEPTEMBER 12, 2012 AT 7:00
P.M. AT THE PARKLAND COMMISSION CHAMBERS, 6600
UNIVERSITY DRIVE, PARKLAND, FLORIDA AS THE DATE,
TIME AND PLACE FOR THE FIRST PUBLIC BUDGET
HEARING; SETTING SEPTEMBER 19, 2012 AT 7:00 P.M. AT
PARKLAND COMMISSION CHAMBERS, 6600 UNIVERSITY
DRIVE, PARKLAND, FLORIDA AS THE DATE, TIME, AND
PLACE FOR THE SECOND PUBLIC HEARING; PROVIDING
FOR CONFLICTS; PROVIDING FOR SEVERABILITY; AND
PROVIDING FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE.
Ms. Nancy Morando read the title of Resolution No. 2012-69: Proposed Millage Rate for Property
Appraiser (TRIM). She explained that the proposed millage rate is 4.0198 and the City is not
requesting an increase or decrease. The current gross taxable value is $3 billion compared to last year
at 2.9 billion. She explained that new construction value added $42 million in the City’s taxable
value. The total increase in the City’s taxable value was $103 million. She explained that from a
financial perspective, the City received $300,000 ad valorem revenue this year which is about a 2%
- increase in revenue. The roll back rate is set at 3.9372 which equates to the resident’s charge to
obtain the current level of taxes. She also explained that there is no change in the fire assessment rate
of $210 per household and .4965 to commercial properties. The fire rescue services contract
increased 3.67% which equates to approximately $200,000. The solid waste services is a franchise
agreement with Waste Management which is a mandated agreement increased by the CPL. The CPI
reading in December was 3.1% which equates to the new rate of $338.52. She explained that the
General Fund budget increased by $1.6 million. The current year general fund budget was $21.1
million and the proposed general fund budget is $22.7 million. Some major highlights of the general
fund is an addition of $1.1 million for building related revenues attributed to the development of the
Wedge. Two (2) additional police officers will be added to the budget. Human Resources and

VII COMMENTS BY THE CITY MANAGER
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young reported that she met with the property owner for Misty
Meadows and in the attempt to purchase the property, some environmental issues came up. She
explained that the property owner feels the site can be cleaned at different levels — residential,
park, or industrial. Based on conversations with the City Commission, the City would want to go
with the more strict level of clean-up which is residential. The property owner has indicated that
they are only willing to clean at the park level. She gave several options. Option 1 is to reduce
the purchase price of the 53 acres in order to be able to clean the site at the residential level
which equates to approximately $1 million. Option 2 is to purchase 51 acres, leaving out the
parcels with the environmental issues reducing the cities liability and enter into an agreement
with the property owner for clean-up of the site or Option 3 is to walk away from deal.
Commissioner Moskowitz threw out a 4% Option suggesting to purchasing all 53 acres and have
the property owner to clean the site and build on only 51 acres — simultaneously set up liquidated
damages that after the property owner has cleaned the property to the park standards, if the
ground water becomes contaminated, the property owner will be responsible for remediation.
Discussion ensued.
