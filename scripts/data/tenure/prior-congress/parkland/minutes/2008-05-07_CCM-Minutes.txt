INSURANCE TRUST FOR PROPERTY INSURANCE
FOR THE CITY OF PARKLAND.
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Resolution No. 2008 -38. She explained that
the City had previously been under the SERMA program; however, Lake Worth had last year
announced they were terminating their association with SERMA. Lake Worth's withdrawal had
no longer made that pool viable.
Finance and Administrative Services Director Barbara Hastings reviewed that the City had
provided a notice to SERMA last year of the City's possible withdrawal. The City joined
SERMA in 1994, when the pool was comprised of nine members, and only four members
remained: the City, Lake Worth, Lake Clarke Shores, and Coconut Creek. She noted that the
City's general property insurance will expire May 15 it had been a seven and one -half month
policy with a premium of $350,097. An RFP had been published and a risk manager /consultant
hired to review the RFP and help with the process. Separate policies with SERMA for workers'
compensation, general liability, and other policies would come due September 30 and these
would be handled under a separate RFP.
Ms. Hastings noted that she had provided the Commission with three proposals, which she briefly
reviewed; the consultant had also provided a side -by -side analysis. Staff recommended award to
the lowest bidder, Wells Fargo.
Commissioner Weissman questioned whether the City could have realized any savings by having a
comprehensive policy, including all of the liability that would be replaced in October. Ms.
Hastings stated that, unfortunately, all of the policies could not be placed together as the property
had to be addressed immediately. The Commissioner asked whether a quote could have been
requested to include the additional liability coverages for the last 12 months of the 16 months,
grouping general liability and property coverage together using one carrier, as opposed to bidding
the coverages separately in four and -a -half months. Ms. Hastings responded that the policies had
always expired at different times and a possible savings was not known. She anticipated that the
same parties would bid on the general liability.
The Commissioner asked if it would be the intention to bid the entire package in one year from
October in 16 months. Ms. Hastings commented that the PGIT program was not a contract;
the City would not be tied and it could be bid out at any time when the policy expires. She
confirmed that one package would be published for bid in October 2009.
Commissioner Smith believed that the City would be better off with the lowest bid, and self
insuring for the difference.
Mayor Udine commented that he also favored the PGIT proposal.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked whether the $160,000 savings would be available for the
2008/2009 budget year, and Ms. Hastings responded affirmatively and hoped it to be even more.

City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Resolution No. 2008 -35.
For the record, Dodie Keith Lazowick, representing the applicant, waived quasi-judicial.
Planning and Zoning Director Roberta Moore gave a brief presentation about the property,
located at the southwest corner of Trail's End and University Drive and currently vacant. The
variances requested reductions in the landscape buffer: 12 feet along Trail's End and 17'/2 feet
along University Drive, totaling 6,070 square feet. She noted that the landscape plan included in

the back -up material provided the Code required quality and quantity of landscape materials; they
had requested only the reduction in area.
As to the Master Plan Amendment, Ms. Moore stated that a detailed Master Plan had been
approved by the Commission in 2005, which included a 10.41 -acre school site, two banks with
drive thrus, and a 15,120- square -foot pharmacy. The petitioner was proposing a "bubble" plan,
indicating where the access to the properties would be and the general areas /square footage of the
parcels. She noted that each particular parcel would require a site plan, including how they would
relate, and each would be brought to the Commission. The lake area would not be modified from
its original approval.
Ms. Moore stated that the Community Appearance Board petition was specifically for the
transition pavilion, the overlook feature, benches, and trash receptacles.
Ms. Moore indicated that staff recommended approval of all three petitions, and she noted that
the Planning and Zoning Board had done the same. She displayed slides of the previously
approved Master Plan and the proposed Master Plan and explained some of the detail.
Commissioner Rosenof asked regarding the reduction in the landscaping.
Donaldson Hearing of Cotleur Hearing indicated that the 6,070- square -foot reduction
represented about 11 percent of the buffer. He recalled that the original plan for four outparcels
had been modified to three to provide more drainage area and to have the lake abut the public
areas of Trail's End and University Drive; the effective buffer then became 124,000 square feet,
which would be substantially greater than required by the Code.
Commissioner Rosenof also questioned that the "tightening" of the landscaping would have no
damaging effects on the plant material. Ms. Moore offered that the project had been within the
DRC process for some time. Mr. Archer had reviewed the project, and he had no objections.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked whether any landscaping material had been lost, and Mr. Hearing
offered that there had in fact been a slight addition of material. There was more than adequate
room for all of the trees to do well.
Mayor Udine opened to the public.
Andrew Anderson, 7661 Old Thyme Court and President of the Caseras association, stated that
he had not heard in the proposal the rationale for the reduction, as well as any assessment of the
impact to aesthetics or acoustics.
Mr. Hearing gave a brief history of the site and the original inclusion of the elementary school,
which would have required turn lanes. Aesthetically, they had spent considerable time in the
design of the buffer and the internal site. He believed that any noise would be mitigated by the
opacity of the landscaping.
