City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young recognized the members of the Chinese Drywall Task Force
who were present (John Willis, Murray Zweig and Building Official Steve Pizzillo) and then
introduced Mr. Willis, who gave a presentation.

Mr. Willis provided an update on the following topics:

o Status of remediation of Parkland Estates homes by Lennar.

¢ Unofficial remediation standards being used by Lennar and others.

e Status of remediation of WCI homes in Heron Bay and Parkland Golf & Country Club

o Current effects on the City of Parkland — many vacant homes, tax base, no insurance,
short sales, foreclosures, and HOA dues.

o Status of ongoing CPSC investigation, correlation of Chinese drywall to corrosion and
health effects, remediation standards, and cost of remediation at approximately $86 per
square foot.

® 50% assessed value of home (not land) for Chinese drywall homes in the City.

o Residents’ complaints about WCI continuing to do business in the City.

o Status of block grants for Parkland.

. o Status of FEMA declaration request by Parkland and other cities and counties to
Governor.

e $2500 grant for Parkland permit fees for remediation of Chinese drywall.

e Status of IRS tax deduction for Chinese drywall.

o Status of litigation affecting Parkland Chinese drywall victims:

o Multi-district litigation — WCI Chinese Drywall Trust product liability action

against Knauf, the German manufacturer.
o Multi-district litigation — WCI Chinese Drywall Trust declaratory action against
WCI and subcontractors’ liability insurers.

o Broward/Delaware — WCI HOA class action for failure to warn.
Regarding the complaint about WCI’s ability to continue to do business in the City, Mayor Udine ’
said attempts were made to stop WCI from doing certain things, but because of the bankruptcy
stay, the City’s hands are tied. He stated that through email communication with Senator
Nelson’s office and with Mike Ryan, the FEMA declaration was discussed and Governor Crist
asked for it knowing it was going to get rejected. He said that the block grants are a problem for
the City and for everybody because of income requirements.
Mayor Udine added that from the beginning, the Commission tried to do everything they could to
help and while there is still bad news, he feels that the City is starting to see some level of
remediation. He then spoke about what he sees as the three groups involved: the Lennar people,
the WCI owned homes, and the original homebuyers, who he believes are among the 115 people
who have applied to the property appraiser for reduction of assessed values. Mayor Udine added
that the good news for the 115 homes is that, although slow and not the resolution that is desired,
the situation is moving forward; the City needs to continue being a facilitator of information.
Commissioner Moskowitz spoke about minimum standards and uniform remediation, and noted
that the City does not want to enter that arena because of liability concerns. He said that he feels

2. DISCUSSION: PARKLAND GOLF & COUNTRY CLUB &

HERON BAY COMMUNITY FEATURES

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young gave a brief synopsis of this item, noted that representatives
of WCI and NSID were present, and introduced Planning & Zoning Director Roberta Moore.
Planning & Zoning Director Moore explained that CH2M Hill, who is the consultant for NSID,
has indicated there are bond monies available for the construction of water features that were
approved by the Commission within Parkland Golf & Country Club, specifically on the north
side of Trails End, along County Line Road and University Drive. She said that there is a
question of whether or not the previous approvals are really what the Commission is looking for
at this time; in particular, there are some issues with the lights that are currently in the existing
water features.
Planning & Zoning Director Moore noted that the previous approvals for the water features have
expired and any future construction would have to go back through the site plan process. She
questioned if the same type of features are desired and whether the lighting in the features should
be changed due to maintenance and functioning issues. She then spoke about the previous
approvals for the water features.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young clarified the history of the NSID bond money.
Jane Early, P.E., CH2M Hill, representing NSID, explained that the reasons NSID came to the
City are to perhaps reduce some of the maintenance issues, utilize the funding that is remaining
in the bonds while pricing is good in order to have money left over to pay down some of the
bonds, and to meet its obligations under the bonds.
Mayor Udine said that he feels the two options are doing what has been approved or tweaking
what has been built.
Ms. Early spoke about maintenance calculations of the fountains being approximately $16 per
month per household, and said the lighting is an issue because it is underwater and is an ongoing
problem.

Commissioner Moskowitz stated that, the fountains aside, the biggest part of this is the need for
the other entrance to be built. He then spoke about the assessments in PGCC and noted that
defective lights should not be replaced with the same product. He said that the critical questions
needing to be answered include what the new maintenance costs will be if the new fountains
were to be built. Commissioner Moskowitz added that the fountains to be built could possibly be
scaled back to save additional maintenance fees, especially since they would benefit only a select
few residents. He acknowledged that there would be some fees associated with maintenance of
the new entrance, but the whole community would see a benefit from it.

Danville R. Leadbetter, Director of Land Development, WCI Communities, LLC, stated that
although he does not have the answers today as to what the association costs are, these numbers
are easily obtainable. He said that WCI will work to ensure that the Commission and the
residents understand what the costs currently being paid are versus costs associated with
additional features.

Commissioner Moskowitz reiterated that the numbers are extremely important in PGCC due to
how high the fees are right now and added that he does not believe the homeowners are currently
paying for the new maintenance costs of these features.

Mayor Udine requested that Ms. Early work with Mr. Leadbetter to determine the makeup and
future amount of the association fees. He added that if the work can be done cheaper now and
the bond is paid down with leftover funds, this could lower the actual assessment.

Commissioner Weissman asked about the areas for some of the amenities and about existing
contracts with homebuyers, and said that he wants the fountains to look something like what the
buyers were shown when they purchased their property.

Commissioner Moskowitz emphasized that because he lives in the community and attends HOA
meetings, he is aware of the debate regarding association fees; the fountains will be built because
they have to be built, but ways to figure out how to lower the maintenance costs should be looked
at when they are constructed.

Mr. Leadbetter suggested that perhaps there are some common area features that do not need to
be water features and said there are probably areas where some of the overall scheme could be
reduced.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young summarized that the Commission has said that the features
need to be looked at yet again, they may have expired so WCI/NSID may have to start at the
beginning of the process, and WCI/NSID should come back and take into consideration the cost
that may be incurred. She clarified that there are multiple assessments at play: the NSID
assessment, which, if less money from the bond is used, that assessment will be reduced; and the
association assessment for maintenance costs, which could be reduced if those costs are already
included in the amount currently being paid.

3. DISCUSSION: COMMUNITY GARDEN
Landscape Architect Planner Brian Archer gave a presentation and his comments included the
following:

e The location that is being proposed by the Community Garden Committee is at the
northwest corner of the library.

e The community garden will be organically grown which gives rise to issues such as
watering and certain requirements to make it an organic garden.

e The initial raised bed will be 4’x12°x11” high; future expansion is listed in the
Commission backup.

e There will be a planting day in November 2010 to kick off the garden.

e Maintenance is an issue because this is an organic garden and will require much more
labor intensive maintenance.

e There will need to be a detailed maintenance schedule, which will be coordinated by
volunteers.

e Maintenance will be undertaken on a weekly basis and each group would be assigned a
specific week.

e Volunteer groups include the local schools, Girl Scouts, Boy Scouts, and Stoneman
Douglas AP Environmental Science class, with Broward County Master Gardener
volunteers helping with the scheduling.

e Harvested produce will initially be donated to a local food bank; if the project takes off,
produce will be taken to the Farmers Market to sell and perhaps generate funds for the
community garden.

eo The garden committee’s desire is to have everything funded through donations or
Sponsors.

e One bed will cost approximately $1,890; there is a company involved that will possibly
come in and construct the bed.
