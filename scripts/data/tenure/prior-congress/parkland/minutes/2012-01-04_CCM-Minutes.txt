1. ORDINANCE NO. 2011-36: DEBUYS REZONING &
MASTER PLAN AMENDMENT
COMMENTS: SECOND READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND, FT.ORIDA, REZONING
APPROXIMATELY 368.999 ACRES FROM PALM BEACH COUNTY
SPECIAL AGRICULTURAL (“SA”) TO CITY OF PARKLAND
PLANNED RESIDENTIAL DEVELOPMENT DISTRICT (“PRD”) AND
APPROXIMATELY 10.678 ACRES FROM PALM BEACH COUNTY
SPECIAL AGRICULTURAL (“SA”) TO CITY OF PARKLAND
PLANNED COMMERCIAL DEVELOPMENT DISTRICT (“PCD”)
(DEBUYS); APPROVING A MASTER PLAN WITH DEVELOPMENT
CONDITIONS TO GOVERN DEVELOPMENT ON THE REZONED
LAND; CONTAINING A SEVERABILITY CLAUSE; AND
CONTAINING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; CASE NUMBERS 03-Z-11 AND
02-MP-11. (first reading 11/16/11) (tabled 12/7/11) (quasi-judicial)
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Y oung explained that Item number 1: Ordinance 2011-36 and Item number
2: Resolution No. 2011-87 coincide and asked that one presentation be presented for both items. She
then read the title of the second reading of Ordinance No. 2011-36: Debuys Rezoning & Master Plan
Amendment. She explained that this item is a quasi-judicial hearing and asked the applicant if he wanted
to exercise or waive the quasi-judicial procedure. Mr. Dennis Mele, Applicant Representative expressed
that he wished to waive the quasi-judicial procedure.
Ms. Roberta Moore, Planning & Zoning Director then gave a brief presentation. She cxplained that this
property was annexed into the City with the removal of University Drive several years ago. See attached
presentation for additional details. She requested that all documentation presented be part of the public
record as well as the presentation.
Mr. Mele pointed out a condition affecting agenda items number 1 and 2 specifically located in the
second paragraph of Condition No. 6 regarding the construction of University Drive.
Commissioner Rosenof questioned the condition of the commercial property. Mr. Mele replied that the
landscape would be maintained by the applicant.
Commissioner Rosenof questioned the number of entrances to this community during construction for
emergency vehicle purposes. Mr. Mele explained that the construction entrance and the main entrance
could both be used for emergency vehicles. It was discussed that City staff would determine what is best
for ingress and egress at all times.
