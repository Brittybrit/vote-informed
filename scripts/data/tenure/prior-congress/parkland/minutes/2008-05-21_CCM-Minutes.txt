1. RESOLUTION NO. 2008 GLUCK VARIANCE
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, DENYING A
VARIANCE REQUEST FOR PROPERTY LOCATED AT
6061 NW 69 WAY; CASE NUMBER 01 -V -08.
(quasi-judicial)
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Resolution No. 2008 -46.
Mr. and Mrs. Richard Gluck, 6061 N.W. 69 Way, were present and waived quasi-judicial.
Planning and Zoning Director Roberta Moore gave a brief presentation about the background of
this item. Staff recommended denial of the wood shed, the concrete basketball court, the light
pole, and the seven -foot fence, and she explained the Code and appropriate setbacks. Staff
recommended that the carport be approved since it was less than one foot from the property line.
Ms. Moore recalled a previous request for variance on a wood shed and the fact that the
Commission had provided an alternative process for that petitioner. She stated that properties
within a 500 -foot radius were notified for both the Planning and Zoning Board and Commission
hearings, and she noted that the Planning and Zoning Board had recommended approval 5 -2: for
the shed and carport to remain; to allow the basketball court to remain, but require the petitioner
to obtain a building permit; to limit the fence to five feet in height; and to required a building
permit for the light pole, which should be reduced to 10 feet in height.
Commissioner Rosenof asked which of the five items would have required a permit and whether
permits been pulled on any of them, even before the Gluck purchase of the property. Ms. Moore
responded that all of the items would have required permits. According to the Building
Department, they were no records that any of the five structures were permitted or built
according to the Florida Building Code.
Commissioner Rosenof questioned whether there had been any responses to property owner
notifications. Ms. Moore indicated that one individual, who she indicated was present, had come
in for more information. It was also brought to light that a rear fence had been installed without
permit, which could become a code enforcement issue.

Commissioner Rosenof asked how the original complaint came about. Ms. Moore stated she was

Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked the resident how much had been spent on the project. Mr. Gluck

dollars, and the safer route may be to take the shed down.
Ms. Moore clarified that staff's only recommended approval was for the carport.
Commissioner Rosenof felt that each issue should be separately considered. He believed the shed
the more egregious because it could not be determined that it was constructed per the Florida
Building Code and could be a hazardous in a hurricane. He felt that the basketball court was
acceptable and that the light could be adjusted.
Commissioner Smith questioned why the construction of the basketball court had not been shut
down if it had been observed by Code Enforcement during construction. Ms. Moore indicated
that Code Enforcement could not shut down a project; only the Building Department had the

separately. He commented that he also did not have a problem with the basketball court.

to the public.
Mayor Udine felt that the fence must be five foot in accordance with Code and that the light pole

Commissioner Rosenof reiterated his feeling that the shed was the most egregious. He was not

violation, he did not believe it would undermine the neighboring property values.
Commissioner Weissman commented that he did not agree. The court had been built well into the
setbacks, without permits, and he believed it set a bad precedent.

A. RESOLUTION NO. 2008-45: OLD CITY HALL PUBLIC WORKS
FIRE STATION VARIANCE
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, GRANTING THE
VARIANCE PETITION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND
FROM SECTION 15 -5 OF THE CODE OF ORDINANCES
TO PERMIT A REVOLVING OR ROTATING SIGN, AS
MORE PARTICULARLY DESCRIBED HEREIN; AND
PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; CASE NUMBER
02 -V -08. (quasi-judicial)
Ms. Gardner -Young read the title of Resolution No. 2008 -45.
Diagrams of the signage were displayed, and Ms. Gardner -Young explained sign placement and
the mechanism.
Planning and Zoning Manager Roberta Moore explained that the Code did not permit electronic
signs, and staff had requested a variance. The Site Plan and Community Appearance Board
applications were for the construction of a permanent Fire Station and Public Works Building and
associated signage. She offered that: the building was proposed at 7,229 square feet, with a
conference room that may be utilized by residents; thirty -nine standard and two handicapped
parking spaces would be provided; overall height of the structure would not exceed 24 feet; and
ingress and egress to the public would be provided from Parkside Drive, while emergency access
only had been obtained for Holmberg Road. The site met Code requirements for setbacks,
parking, landscape materials, and lighting. Residents within 500 feet had been notified for both
the Planning and Zoning Board and Commission hearings. The Board had recommended
approval of all three petitions based on star recommendations, with suggestions that the
monument sign not be located at the corner of Holmberg Road and Parkside Drive and that
landscape material on the site be increased.
At additional explanation from Ms. Moore, Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked regarding a building
elevation with the landscaping shown. Ms. Gardner -Young noted that landscape plans were
"hefty," as landscaping planned for the cell tower enclosure, located on the interior of the
property, would be added to that of the exterior.

Ms. Moore offered that the Planning and Zoning Board had wanted two separate, double -sided
electronic monument signs one on Parkside Drive and one on Holmberg Road. Due to the cost,
staff recommended that one monument sign be provided.
Ms. Gardner -Young commented that she and the City Attorney were working on Code language
for future signage issues. She felt that, since it was a variance for a community facility, it would

announcements would continue to come through the City Manager's Office. Ms. Gardner -Young
noted that the sign would be solar powered with back -up.
Mayor Udine acknowledged the Planning and Zoning Board's desire for two signs, but he was
personally not in favor. He felt that the aesthetics of two signs, as well as their cost, was not
appropriate.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz agreed with the Mayor for one sign. He voiced concern, however, that it
may be distraction at that location.
Ms. Gardner -Young noted that the purpose of the traffic circle was to slow down traffic, and
traffic often stopped when yielding to traffic in the circle. She did not believe that it would be a
distraction.

Mayor Udine suggested that it must be a high -end sign that would satisfy everyone and yet be
readable and not distracting to traffic.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked that the actual display unit be brought to the Commission. Ms.
Gardner -Young indicated that staff would provide a memo showing what it would look like. If
