3. CONSIDERATION: PRE-ANNEXATION AGREEMENTS
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis suggested that discussion begin with the Hendrix pre-
annexation agreement. He explained that as a condition of the rezoning and prior to recordation
of any plat, Hendrix will dedicate 40 continuous acres of land for a school; if at the end of seven
years from the first building permit for residential, a school has not been started, then the City
will return the 40 acres less five acres.
Commissioner Weissman said when the Wedge piece is master planned out and if it is found that
the 40-acre school site on this property is not in the best interest of the City or the school district
he would like the ability to swap this land out, if necessary, in order to accomplish what is
needed.
Dick Coker, representing the Hendrix parcel, said he does not see how that works.
Commissioner Weissman stated that there are an estimated 8,000 kids who may be attending K-
12 in the Wedge and the City needs this flexibility to find the best school site.
Commissioner Rosenof commented that he feels the City is doing this backwards; if there is the
opportunity to master plan the entire Wedge, then that should be done before the City enters into
pre-annexation agreements.
Mayor Udine said that he feels it is not possible to do master planning because all of the land is
not yet in the City and he noted that what the Commission is doing with these annexation
agreements is they are guaranteeing and teeing up land for the schools.
Commissioner Rosenof stated that he is talking about a general master plan to determine what
will be needed in this area and added that the Commission needs to formalize what will be
required in the Wedge.
Mayor Udine said that it is a mistake to tie everyone’s hands in master planning now; he will
support this concept presented and will vote to have City Attorney Maurodis bring back a more
formal agreement to the Commission.
Commissioner Moskowitz stated that he would also support it, and noted that he asked Mr.
Coker for the 40 acres for a high school and Mr. Coker came through with the 40 acres.
Bob Marks, member of the EAB, said school board member Bartleman was not aware that there
was resistance from the school board and she asked that the letter received from school board
staff be forwarded to her. Mr. Marks noted that the EAB’s concern is donation versus dedication
and questioned the status of the five acres should the land not be utilized for a school. He added
that the EAB was hoping for donations to the City so if the school board rejected any given site,
the City would receive either a donation of cash or land to be used for education purposes.

4. CONSIDERATION: NSID LOCAL BILL REQUEST

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young reviewed the discussion held at the previous Commission
meeting about NSID agreeing to pull their local bill within 90 days, said bill requesting that the
Wedge piece be included as part of their district, if they did not meet the requests of the
Commission. She then listed what the requests were and results of the follow-up. City Manager
Caryn Gardner-Young noted that the NSID board entered into a median maintenance agreement
for the area in front of Westglades, NSID hopes to open County Line Road and University Drive
within a short period of time, and Rod Colgne, NSID Director of Operations, would address the
concern regarding a wastewater treatment plant.
Commissioner Weissman said there were some concerns with future maintenance and ownership
of some roads by NSID.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young responded that generally, NSID constructs the roadways
and then transfers them. She explained her response when NSID came to her regarding transfer
to the City and noted that Mr. Colgne is waiting to hear from FDOT as to whether they want the
roads.
Commissioner Weissman stated that as part of the Commission’s support for the local bill, NSID
needs to commit to maintaining these roads and the medians, and that the City will have someone
locally to go to if there is a problem, as opposed to dealing with FDOT.

<<PG>>
[OCR] PARKLAND CITY COMMISSION MINUTES

PAGE 16

Mr. Colgne commented that most government entities have a public works department that deals
with streets and highways; NSID’s specialty is drainage, wastewater collection and water
treatment and does not have the personnel to maintain the roads.

Commissioner Weissman said that he would like to see long-term replacement and maintenance
of these roads remain the responsibility of NSID.

Mayor Udine spoke about the possibility of NSID working with the City insofar as the Public
Works Department would maintain the medians and there could perhaps be cost recovery from
NSID. He added that no matter who owns the roads, the City gets the calls.

There was then discussion about an interlocal agreement between the City and NSID.

Vice Mayor Smith questioned whether a wastewater treatment facility would be required.

Mr. Colgne responded that NSID is not required under their water supply plan to build a
treatment facility, however, under the City Comprehensive Plan, a reuse facility in the area is
required. He added that NSID is looking at an area as far north and west as possible and then he
spoke about residents’ concerns about their property values.

Commissioner Rosenof said he feels more comfortable about it now knowing where it is going to
be located and questioned whether support of the bill could be conditioned on the facility being
in the extreme northwest corner of the property with a certain amount of wetlands mitigation
around it.

Mr. Colgne added that NSID is looking at either the Saber Land Trust or Bishops Pit, but is only
in the beginning stages of the process.

MOTION made by Vice Mayor Smith to pull the NSID local bill.

MOTION died for lack of a second.

Mayor Udine commented that the Commission will continue its support of the bill.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young said staff is recommending that Items 11 and 12 be tabled
indefinitely.

13. DISCUSSION: FIRE CONTRACT

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young provided a brief overview of recent events in this process

and stated that Coral Springs indicated they would have what amounts to a counter proposal sent

to her by the beginning of next week.

Commissioner Weissman said this means the Commission will have yet another document to

review, perhaps by the next Commission meeting, the terms of which may or may not be

acceptable, and then it would be sent back to Coral Springs. He added that the clock would keep

ticking and the City would be beyond the point where it can go elsewhere for fire services.

 This form is for use by any person serving at the county,city,or other local level of government on an appointed or elected board, council,

 Your responsibilities under the law when faced with voting on a measure in which you have a conflict of interest will vary greatly depending
 on whether you hold an elective or appointive position. For this reason, please pay close attention to the instructions on this form before
 completing the reverse side and filing the form.
 INSTRUCTIONS FOR COMPLIANCE WITH SECTION 112.3143, FLORIDA STATUTES
 A person holding elective or appointive county, municipal, or other local public office MUST ABSTAIN from voting on a measure which
 inures to his or her special private gain or loss. Each elected or appointed local officer also is prohibited from knowingly voting on a mea-
 sure which inures to the special gain or loss of a principal (other than a government agency) by whom he or she is retained (including the
 parent organization or subsidiary of a corporate principal by which he or she is retained);to the special private gain or loss of a relative; or
 to the special private gain or loss of a business associate. Commissioners of community redevelopment agencies under Sec. 163.356 or
 163.357, F.S., and officers of independent special tax districts elected on a one-acre, one-vote basis are not prohibited from voting in that
 capacity.
 For purposes of this law, a "relative" includes only the officer's father, mother, son, daughter, husband, wife, brother, sister, father-in-law,
 mother-in-law, son-in-law, and daughter-in-law. A"business associate" means any person or entity engaged in or carrying on a business
 enterprise with the officer as a partner,joint venturer, coowner of property, or corporate shareholder(where the shares of the corporation
 are not listed on any national or regional stock exchange).
