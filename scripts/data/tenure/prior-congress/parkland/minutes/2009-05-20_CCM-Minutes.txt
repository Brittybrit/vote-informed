1. PRESENTATION: CHINESE DRYWALL
John Willis spoke on behalf of the Chinese Drywall Task Force on the following items:
• Noted the Parkland Chamber of Commerce will host a round table meeting May 21
2009 at the Heron Bay Golf Club at 7:30 a.m. on the topic of Chinese drywall.
• Commented on the topic of relocation and related issues:
1. Homestead exemption status questions should be directed to the Broward County
Property Appraiser's office at (954) 357 -6830. This office will request some type
of proof of Chinese drywall in your home, i.e. a response from the Florida
Department of Health stating your home met the Chinese Drywall criteria, in
order to maintain your homestead exemption status.
2. School assignment questions; per Stephanie Kraft at the Broward County School
Board, automatic reassignment procedure in place for persons displaced due to
Chinese drywall. Parents need to apply for reassignment by May 1 and June 15
2009 and more information can be found on their web site at:
<a href="http://www.browardschools.com">www.browardschools.com</a>

3. Commented on the first report issued by the Environmental Protection Agency
(EPA) released on May 19 2009 which noted some contents found in the
Chinese drywall boards, such as; sulfur, acrylic paint compounds and high levels
of strontium.
4. Spoke on families who are in dire need to relocate due to health concerns; one
family had built their home in Heron Bay specifically for the needs of their
handicapped child and were having a difficult time finding another handicapped
accessible home to relocate to.
5. Advised residents to be careful when relocating that the rental home does not also
contain Chinese drywall.
6. Addressed the WCI bankruptcy case; Attorney Mike Ryan had obtained an
extended deadline for his clients to file a claim against WCI and clarified the City
of Parkland cannot do this on the residents behalf; the resident must contact an
Attorney directly if they wish to file.
Commissioner Moskowitz requested the City Manager, Caryn Gardner -Young and the City
Attorney, Andrew Maurodis, address potential liabilities on behalf the City of Parkland with the
Bankruptcy Attorney, Mr. Abrams.
Mayor Udine reiterated residents must procure their own Attorney in order to file against WCI.
Commissioner Smith advised there were communities built prior to the use of Chinese drywall
that could still contain Chinese drywall if there had been any post hurricane repairs done.
The Commission discussed the Task Force drafting a letter which would endeavor to further
educate residents regarding Chinese drywall issues and it was suggested adding a note pertaining
to residents obtaining independent legal council on the matter.
Mayor Udine added additional comments:
• Advised residents who have relocated are able to apply for the vacation credit extension
from Waste Management for unused collection service while they are not living in their
residence and the applicable form is available at City Hall.
• Noted he and Representative Martin Kiar had sent letters to WCI regarding the security
gate issue on behalf of residents.
Commissioner Moskowitz commented on how the City of Parkland had taken the lead on behalf
of its residents to assist in moving this issue to the forefront and gaining the attention of higher
authorities. He suggested the Task Force draft an additional letter requesting Governor Crist to
appoint a State operated task force; then moving forward, the City's Task Force main purpose
will be to continue to monitor new developments and to add useful information to their web site.
Mr. Willis continued with his presentation; he noted Parkland resident Holy Krulik along with
Attorney Mike Ryan will be attending the 10:30 a.m. meeting of United States Senate Committee
on Commerce in Washington, D.C. which will include a hearing regarding Chinese drywall and
can be watched on the C -SPAN cable channel.

He discussed the Chinese drywall survey and reviewed a summary of the results to date; they
received a total of (189) respondents including (103) responses from Heron Bay, (47) from
Parkland Golf & Country Club (PGCC) and the remainder from various communities that may or
may not have Chinese drywall. He added that 49% of respondents answered that they felt they
had experienced some type of adverse health reaction (mostly related to respiratory ailments) to
the Chinese drywall, so far no insurance claims have been paid out to those residents who had
contacted their homeowners insurance companies, only (3) respondents answered that their
developer would repair the problem and to his knowledge the only developer taking this step is
Lennar Homes, and lastly almost 50% of respondents answered they were planning to relocate.
Mr. Willis concluded that without more detailed research and analysis, the survey results were of
limited reliability and not scientifically conclusive.
Mr. Willis presented a letter the City Commission requested be sent out to the PGCC
homeowners, concentrating on unoccupied homes, to ensure their awareness of the potential
problems of Chinese drywall.
The Commission requested the City Manager, Caryn Gardner -Young to review the language of
the letter with the City Attorney, Andrew Maurodis for final approval.
Commissioner Rosenof requested clarification if WCI had paid for a bonded warranty on the
PGCC development.
Mr. Willis responded that the task force had not been able to clarify this with WCI as of yet,
however this information should be made available during the bankruptcy process.
Commission discussion ensued regarding the future responsibilities for the task force, such as;
continue monitoring the web site, add updates as needed and wait for official protocol from the
appropriate State or Federal Agency.
Mr. Murray Zweig spoke briefly on the few remediation efforts that are in the beginning stages
by Lennar Homes and noted the Board of Appeals board members had recently toured homes
with Chinese drywall to see the issues first hand. Mr. Zweig introduced guest speaker, Dr. Tim
Townsend, Professor at the University of Florida - College of Engineering, Environmental
Engineering Sciences department.
Dr. Townsend advised mainly his research has been related to Construction and Demolition
Debris Management, and has found some issues that have come up when disposing of dry wall
boards in a land fill, then found it can produce odor causing gasses, particularly in Florida, which
can be dangerous in high concentration and a nuisance at lower concentrations. He described his
research will entail collecting various samples of dry wall for comparison and research with the
assistance of students from the University.

Commissioner Moskowitz requested Dr. Townsend's opinion, after seeing what the affect these
chemicals have on metals, what affect they may have on the human body?
Dr. Townsend responded the definitive answer was still unknown and further research would be
required, possibly by the Department of Health or other such agency, and based on past research
it might have the potential to be a health issue. He advised he would present his research results
as soon as available to help determine solutions.
Mayor Udine advised Dr. Townsend will attend the roundtable meeting hosted by the Chamber
of Commerce tomorrow, May 21 2009 at the Heron Bay Golf Club at 7:30 a.m. on the topic of
Chinese drywall.
Lastly, John Willis noted that the City of Norfolk, Virginia City Commission banned Chinese
drywall use in their City and has required that all builders must certify that that the drywall being
used is not Chinese drywall.
Commissioner Moskowitz added that he had discussed previously a similar course of action with
the City Attorney, Andrew Maurodis, who advised the Florida building code in Broward County
prohibits municipalities from passing laws regarding specific construction materials.
