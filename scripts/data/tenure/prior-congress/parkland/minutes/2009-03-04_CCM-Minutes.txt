III PUBLIC INPUT
Mayor Udine opened to the Public.
Mr. Ira Goldman, Whittier Oaks resident and publisher of the `Parkland Patriot', spoke on
Trotter's Lane situation and presented some questions he had. He questioned where the water
from the soil in the retaining pond will drain to, how was it decided to place the soil on the
Liberty Park area and what was the outcome of samples taken?
Engineering Projects Coordinator, Horaeio Danovich responded that the soil will remain on the
property only until it dried while a plastic material was used to secure sediments from
contaminating the property and once dry the soil will be hauled away by Waste Management. He
advised that the soil was placed on the Liberty Park area for cost effectiveness.
Mr. Jeff Schwartz, Parkland resident and President of the Parkland Historical Society, stated he
came "bearing gifts" and referenced the mock up of historical markers representing "Portals of
Parldand's Past ". The Historical Society is presenting the markers as a gift to the community and
residents of Parkland. He reviewed potential locations, topics for the portals and its overall
design.
Mayor Udine thanked Mr. Schwartz for his presentation and suggested he work through the City
Manager to finalize the logistics of the project. He advised his work and that of the Historical
Society on behalf of the City is very much appreciated.
Commissioner Moskowitz added this is not the first time the Schwartz family and the Parkland
Historical Society had done something to benefit the community.

6. DISCUSSION: BUILDING DEPARTMENT STATUS
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young opened the discussion regarding the status of the building
department. Ms. Gardner -Young advised the Commission this was a difficult topic for all to
discuss, unfortunately other local municipalities as well as Broward County are also currently
dealing with this issue. She advised that she and city staff had begun an analysis of revenue and
expenditures for the building department several months ago and currently results have shown
revenues are coming in far below the estimated projections for this fiscal year, at approximately
$500,000 under budget.
Ms. Gardner -Young presented two options for review: Option #1 would entail outsourcing the
building department responsibilities to an outside private firm or a neighboring municipality,
while Option #2 would involve maintaining the building department work in house with a
reduction of operating expenses by reducing staff costs by removing 4 full time positions and
reducing the remaining staff hours.
Staff recommended Option #2 - maintaining the building department in house.
Ms. Gardner -Young reviewed pros and cons of both scenarios for the Commission.
Mayor Udine commented on similar situations that had been written about in the local
newspapers, Broward County had to let go 27 people from their building department staff
recently and unfortunately the City of Parkland cannot continue losing money in this manner.
Commissioner Moskowitz noted this was a very personal vote for him from the perspective of a
City Commissioner and as an employee himself. He commented that originally he had the idea of
furlough versus staff lay offs. Commissioner Moskowitz requested further information regarding
outsourcing, such as, would the outsource company be able to retain building department
employees as part of their staff and how would outsourcing affect the level of service the
residents would receive? He felt this decision was more difficult without this information.

<<PG>>

Vice Mayor Weissman spoke on Option #1 — outsourcing. He stated he had been a proponent of
outsourcing in the past regarding the City's fire /rescue and police services as he felt it offered a
higher level of service for residents. He noted he was not certain it would' be so in the building
department's case and thought the City might be able to lessen the deficit of Option #2 through
cost recovery.
Mayor Udine clarified that with Option #2 the building department would still operate at a loss.
Commissioner Rosenof stated he has also been a supporter of outsourcing and felt a private
construction firm might be able to offer a higher level of service and expertise.
Commissioner Smith requested staff clarify outsourcing options.
Commissioner Moskowitz questioned if further details regarding outsourcing options could be
compiled for further review by the next City Commission meeting on March 18 2009 and if the
final decision on this matter could be put on hold until that time?
Vice Mayor Weissman noted there was no guarantee outsourcing bids for the building
department would come in to meet all of the City's needs and had concern for the moral of the
employees during the bid out process.
Mayor Udine stated if they decided on the outsourcing option, it would most likelytake until the
end of the budget year to put together proposals through the bid process and he agreed it was a
long shot that a private company would be able accommodate their conditions. He said based on
the information available he would be in favor of Option #2.
Ms. Gardner -Young advised that she had researched potential outsourcing companies last
October (2008) and representatives at that time appeared resistant to absorb the City's building
department staff or negotiating a short term contract.
Mayor Udine opened to the public.
David Gray, former Construction Department Manager for WCI Communities from 2000 -2007,
dealt with the City of Parkland's building department for over seven years and feels the staff take
a personal interest in every house. He appreciated the Commissioners concern to be fiscally
responsible to the tax payers but suggested the City wait until they have done further research on
the options.
Steve Mrachek, of Mrachek Brothers Building Company, noted he had been building in Parkland
since 1979 and had dealt with the City of Parkland's building department staff since that time
and feels they are quality personnel. He advised he has the same situation and decisions currently
with his own employees. He believes the question is how do you do the right thing and try to
work to keep the employees?
There were no further comments or questions and the Mayor closed to the public.

ADDED AGENDA ITEMS
1. DISCUSSION: SQUILLACE VS. CITY OF PARKLAND
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis spoke on the City Commission Executive Session held just prior
to the Regular City Commission meeting tonight and the settlement agreement for Mr.
Squillace's bridge case that was discussed. He stated that both party's attorneys recommended
approval of a settlement amount of $84,000 payable to .Mr. Squillace. This money would be
funded by receiving $40,000 from the insurance money and $44,000 from the City of Parkland.
Mr. Maurodis also advised the City would recoup their portion of the settlement payment from
the law suit the City has filed against the company that caused the oil spill in The Ranches. Mr.
Maurodis requested authority, along with the City Manager Caiyn Gardner -Young and Special
Counsel Mr, Michaelson, to execute the settlement to Mr. Squillace.
MOTION made by Vice Mayor Weissman to approve settlement of the Squillace bridge case in the
amount of $84,000. Seconded by Commissioner Moskowitz.
