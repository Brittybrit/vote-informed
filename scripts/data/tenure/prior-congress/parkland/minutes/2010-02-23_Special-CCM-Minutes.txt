City Attorney Andrew Maurodis gave a presentation and his comments included the following:

e At the last Commission discussion of this item, two issues were raised: the interaction
between the density requested and the 40-acre school parcel, and the early dedication of
University Road and Lox Road.

¢ Hendrix is requesting what is called a dashed line, which entails a dashed line around the
entire parcel of property and they are asking for two units per acre and 40 acres of
commercial.

e Normally without the dashed line, that entire area would be taken minus 40 acres of
commercial to arrive at a residential acreage figure.

e With the dashed line configuration, the entire acreage for the residential is counted,
including the 40 acres of commercial, so in essence, there are 80 additional units gained
by the dashed line.

e Although the Commission expressed that there is not necessarily an issue with this, there
is concern because the developer is saying that if he does not get this, the 40-acre school
parcel is off the table.

e It is unknown whether County staff may have an issue with the dashed line.

City Attorney Maurodis said that he negotiated with Richard Coker, the Hendrix family attorney,
and he then read the compromise reached on page 11, section 7 of the pre-annexation agreement.
He explained that Hendrix is going to apply for the maximum, i.e., the dashed line and if he does
not get it and if the City wants to get the 40-acre school site, the City has to then apply flex units.
Planning & Zoning Director Roberta Moore stated that the County Planning Council staff
indicated this morning that there are no flexibility units available for any of the property in the
Wedge.

City Attorney Maurodis said that if the City is unable to apply flex units, then this would default
into the pro rata reduction of the school site. He added that in light of the fact that the County
said there are no flex units, there is now the question of whether the Commission wants to go
through this exercise the way it presently is.

Commissioner Weissman calculated that if there was a reduction of 80 units out of the overall
acreage, it would reduce the school site from 40 acres to approximately 37.

Commissioner Rosenof asked for an explanation of how the pro rata works and City Attorney
Maurodis read from page 12 of the pre-annexation agreement. He noted that, at this point, there
are no flexibility units so that particular language in the agreement may need to be revisited.
Mayor Udine spoke about County action taken on the application, and City Attorney Maurodis
clarified that today’s decision by the County allows the process to start and the application will
go to various agencies including the Planning Council, County Commission, and DCA.

Mayor Udine commented that the down side is if the land use plan amendment does not get
approved by another governmental agency and the 40-acre school site is off the table. He then

spoke about the significance of the 80 units and City Attorney Maurodis said as long as the City
makes every effort possible legally to get the applicant those 80 units through flex, then the deal
is still on the table but less than the 40 acres.

City Attorney Maurodis then read from page 10 of the pre-annexation agreement regarding
circumstances that could trigger the pro rata reduction. He noted that this is not the preferred
way, but it is the deal that is before the Commission and Mr. Coker has indicated that this is the
only deal they will make.

Mayor Udine asked what the risk would be to the City in getting the school site teed up and City
Attorney Maurodis explained that the pro rata does not apply to the 80 units attributable to the
commercial parcel; if through the land use process the applicant would get less than 80 units, the
entire 40-acre school site is off the table.

Commissioner Weissman said he is not comfortable with this as he believed that the pro rata
applied to any lost units and added that he would not vote for this.

Mayor Udine agreed and stated that the Commission needs to figure out a way to tee up the
school site as he feels they are in a risky position.

City Attorney Maurodis commented that the Commission could approve the agreement with a
straight pro rata reduction although this likely would not be agreed to by Hendrix.

Commissioner Weissman stated that the density is based on gross acreage only because the City
was getting the school site; otherwise, it should have been based on net acreage with roadways
and drainage taken out. He added that this gives Hendrix a huge windfall and in addition to that,
they now want to double up on the 40 acres of commercial. He said that the Commission should
approve the agreement subject to the full pro rata.

Commissioner Rosenof said the Commission could either agree to the full pro rata share or just
reject the agreement, and questioned the advantage of one over the other.

Discussion was held regarding the options before the Commission.

Commissioner Moskowitz commented that he feels the Hendrix people are not thinking that they
are going to get away with zero acres for a school and reiterated that the 40-acre school parcel
was a condition of the County’s approval.

Mayor Udine spoke about the Legislature bringing the Hendrix property into the City prior to any
of the land use happening, which is why the Commission has been working out this agreement.
Commissioner Moskowitz questioned flex units and City Attorney Maurodis provided an
explanation, and stated that the language in the agreement regarding flex units is good. He
reiterated that the question is how comfortable is the Commission with an all or nothing gain on

the two units per acre because once it drops below the pure two units per acre, the school site is

There was discussion about pro rata over the entire acreage and the differing amounts of school
site acreage.
Commissioner Moskowitz asked if there is a way to safeguard a certain amount of acreage for a
school regardless of action taken by another entity that decreases the units per acre or denies flex

City Attorney Maurodis said the Hendrix application will likely get the dashed line, or two units

¢ Going with pro rata on any units Hendrix loses.

e Agreeing to an arbitrary number of acres for a school site.

e Setting a minimum number of acres for a school site.
City Attorney Maurodis suggested a straight pro rata, but with the provision that if Hendrix’s
density goes below 1.5 units per acre, the City’s deal is off the table and Hendrix just contends
with the school board.
After additional discussion, Commissioner Moskowitz noted that Mr. Coker has already said
they will not agree to pro rata.
City Attorney Maurodis added that Mr. Coker would be more adverse to a minimum of 32 acres
than the pro rata.
Commissioner Rosenof said he does not want the number to go to zero.
Commissioner Weissman commented that he is willing to accept the pro rata share and take the
chance that Hendrix will not be knocked down to one unit per acre.
Vice Mayor Smith stated that he would like to put a floor on the school site acreage.
There was discussion regarding the best way for the Commission to proceed.
Mayor Udine said that he feels the City will not lose the deal as Hendrix needs to get this done
before Hometown Democracy; he wants to ensure that the City does not lose all this land and he
is okay with it if a couple of acres has to be shaved off for one reason or another.
City Attorney Maurodis confirmed the consensus of the Commission up to this point in the
discussion.

Mayor Udine spoke about flex units and questioned the flex unit process, and City Attorney
Maurodis provided a detailed explanation. Planning & Zoning Director Moore added that
Broward County has not designated a flex zone yet for this area.
Commissioner Weissman stated that the City is not getting anything it would have liked to have
gotten in this agreement, other than the school site.
Mayor Udine agreed and said he can not risk the whole school site.
City Attorney Maurodis commented that Hendrix will likely not accept the revisions to the
agreement under discussion by the Commission.
Mayor Udine asked what the downside would be if the Commission does not approve the pre-
annexation agreement and pulls the bill in Tallahassee.
City Attorney Maurodis responded that Hendrix continues to farm, which has been his intent all
along, and when he is ready to develop, he has to come and deal with the Commission then.
Commissioner Moskowitz said that he would rather have Hendrix “inside the tent” while all the
other land use and land planning is going on in the Wedge rather than have him out there and not
included. He suggested the Commission push for the 40 acres and if they do everything they can
for the Hendrix property and some other entity takes it away by denying two units per acre, the
City still needs to get something. Commissioner Moskowitz added that the City needs to come
to an agreement with Hendrix on a number and the question becomes what number the
Commission is willing to go to before the Commission walks away from the deal.
Discussion was held regarding the viability of a provision stating that a specific amount of
acreage would come to the City even if another entity denies Hendrix the two units per acre.
MOTION made by Commissioner Weissman to approve Resolution No. 2010-17, Hendrix Pre-
Annexation Agreement, as amended, providing for the pro rata share to be extended to the two
units per acre and any units below the two units per acre on the remaining property, assuming
Hendrix does not get the dashed line units, there would be a further pro rata reduction in the
amount of acreage that would be provided for the school site. Seconded by Commissioner
Moskowitz.

: Commissioner Weissman clarified that the pro rata would extend all the way through the
commercial acreage and down through the two units per acre.
There was a brief discussion on the motion.

MOTION passed unanimously.
Mayor Udine commented that the intent of this has been that the Commission wants to work with
Hendrix to get this done and the message that should go back to him is that, on behalf of the
residents, the Commission can not risk this whole school site.
Commissioner Weissman spoke about impact fees, in particular, the formula and methodology
for calculating fees and at what point they are calculated. He stated that he believes the
Commission has the ability to amend the method that is utilized to determine how the number of
residents who are going to come into the City are counted and when the counting is performed.
Commissioner Weissman noted that this fine tuning would allow the City to get what he feels is
needed in the future, especially in regards to the Wedge piece. He added that counting the
number of bedrooms that are being built would give the City the ability to generate more impact
fees for that area.
Mayor Udine said the Commission would look at this in the future.
Jui ADJOURNMENT
MOTION made by Commissioner Weissman to adjourn the February 23, 2010 Special City
Commission meeting at 8:01 p.m. Seconded by Vice Mayor Smith.
MOTION passed unanimously.
