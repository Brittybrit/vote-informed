REGULAR AGENDA
ADDED AGENDA ITEM: DISCUSSION ON STIMULUS PLAN
Commissioner Moskowitz noted cities around the Country and Broward County are beginning to
focus on the new federal stimulus bill. He felt that this is an important opportunity and the City
should do whatever necessary in order to receive a portion of the stimulus plan funding, even if it
required hiring outside assistance or a consultant. He suggested city staff begin to concentrate
efforts on researching how to apply for funding and prioritizing projects. He felt the city would
be doing a disservice to its residents if they did not attempt to receive stimulus funding.
Mayor Udine agreed with Commissioner Moskowitz. He used the Western Fire Station as an
example of spending a portion of City money now in order to qualify for additional stimulus
funding in the future. He advised that the money would need to be spent at some point for this
project and others. He suggested staff begin gathering information on which projects are most
likely to qualify for funding and would be willing to call an emergency City Commission
meeting if required for further discussion.
Commissioner Moskowitz added that the City may not receive any funding but still felt the
possibility was worth the City's best efforts to serve the residents.
Commissioner Rosenof suggested prioritizing projects that would be most likely to gain approval
for funding.
The City Commission agreed unanimously for staff to pursue stimulus funding.

City Manager, Caryn Gardner -Young gave a brief history of the Education Advisory Board
(EAB) Safety Sub - Committee. She reviewed the Safety Committee had been created two years
ago and under the Ordinance the committee would sunset unless the City Commission adopted a
Resolution to continue the committee.
Ms. Suzi Hayes, Library Director and staff liaison for the EAB, gave a short presentation
referring to the agenda item memo included in the Commission meeting packet. She advised the
sub committee had found the topics for discussion were either the purview of the Broward
County School Board or broader than just education related issues and they recommended
evolving into an overall City Safety Advisory Committee. The EAB would continue to monitor
issues concerning safety in the schools as part of their strategic plan.
Mayor Udine recognized Christine Hunschofsky, EAB Chair and Rebecca Eikevik, EAB Board
member were also present.
Vice Mayor Weissman commented that the safety of the City's children should remain their main
concern all though he would like the City Commission discuss the option of creating a city -wide
safety committee which would consider safety issues of all the resident's such as bike lanes and
Aston Gardens security.
Commissioner Moskowitz suggested this topic be added to a future Commission Workshop for
further review and to extend the current Safety Committee until furhter determination could be
made.
Mayor Udine opened to the public, there were no comments or questions and he closed to the
public.
MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to continue the EAB Safety Advisory Board via a
future resolution, for a maximum period of six months or until a City -wide Safety Board is
established. Seconded by Vice Mayor Weissman,

City Manager, Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Ordinance No. 2009 -10, amending HR
Rules & Regulations and gave a brief explanation.
Ms. Gardner -Young advised currently there were two separate books that address employee
benefits, conduct and procedures. The `Employee Handbook' had been adopted in 1996 and the
`Rules & Regulations' had been adopted in 2003 and these two separate books do not always
agree. Staff recommended merging these into one complete `Employee Rules & Regulations'
hand book and presented a draft for the Commission to review.
Mayor Udine advised he would vote for approval on first reading of this item though he planned
on taking additional time to review the extensive document prior to second reading.
City Attorney, Andrew Maurodis noted the City saved approximately $75,000 by doing this
project in house rather than hiring an outside consultant.
Commissioner Moskowitz added comments referring back to the stimulus plan discussion and on
the future issue of the City's Building department. He questioned if staff could be utilized to
research how to acquire funding from President Obama's stimulus package plan by those with a
lighter work load. He advised he would like to see the City research alternative options in place
of employee lay offs such as furloughs or salary reductions.
Mayor Udine advised the City Manager will bring options back to the Commission in March.
Commissioner Moskowitz stated March may be too early to discuss the issue until they know
more in regards to the stimulus bill.

Mayor Udine opened to the public.
City Manager, Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Ordinance No. 2009 -02, Energy Incentives
Ordinance Amendment and introduced Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer.
Mr. Archer reviewed changes to the Ordinance from first reading of the item from the February
4, 2009 City Commission meeting.
Mayor Udine re- clarified that some changes had come from City of Parkland resident Mr. Weiss
via email and had been incorporated in part to the Ordinance. He also noted language had been
added to permit the City Manager or the designee to award incentives for energy saving ideas not
specifically listed in the program but consistent with the program categories.
Commissioner Moskowitz thanked Mr. Archer for his work on this item and reminded that
resident tax dollars are not utilized for the program awards.
Mr. Kevin Meyers, a resident of the City of Parkland, questioned how was the program funded if
resident tax dollars were not being utilized?
Mayor Udine explained how the funding comes from part of the revenue from a cell tower.
Mr. Meyers stated he felt city government should not play a role in resident's personal decisions
and had concerns about government placing restrictions on residents.
There were no further comments or questions and the Mayor closed to the public.
Commissioner Moskowitz responded that they could agree to disagree and reported many cities
across the country have some type of incentive program to encourage energy and environmental
conservation. He offered that the government had created restrictions on environmental issues for
years, such as fuel standards, mileage per gallon standards, and so forth.
MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to approve Ordinance No. 2009 -02, Energy
Incentives Ordinance Amendment. Seconded by Vice Mayor Weissman.
ROLL CALL VOTE:
MOTION passed on a 4 -1 vote.
