James White, Weiss Serota, special counsel, said some of the concerns from the dais on first reading of
this Ordinance were:

e if applicable regulations were in place to address some of the negative impacts that would be
associated with the ancillary or accessory tree trimming operations associated with a forestry
operation. He prepared a memorandum for Commission review and he reviewed this with City
staff and looked at those applicable regulations that the City could turn to with regard to
operations that could get out of hand.

o The City already has the following in place: parking and screening requirements for commercial
vehicles, regulations with regard to overnight parking of heavy commercial vehicles and limits
on those, screening of those “heavy commercial vehicles and commercial vehicles, weight
limitations and restrictions, and provisions pertaining to noise nuisances. There are also
provisions pertaining to a public nuisance and lastly, the issue that this an accessory or ancillary
use to the primary or principle use, which would be forestry, and if a tree trimming operation
became such that it went beyond the parameters of the definition of an ancillary use, then the
city could rely upon that to curtail that operation.

Mr. White said given that memo, both City staff and he feel comfortable that the City have safeguards
in place to address any sort of accessory uses that might be associated with this forestry operation.
Commissioner Moskowitz said one of the other concerns that he brought up at the last meeting was if
by doing this, would that open the City up to any litigation as the City was so threatened at the last
meeting.

Mr. White said he did look at that and he spoke with the City Manager and staff on that issue. He
stated this is a legislative matter, and unlike quasi-judicial matters, legislative matters are those matters
that the standard of review by a court gives great deference to a legislative body and the legislative
decision making process. He said it is a fairly debatable standard. Basically, as long as there is a
rational basis for the decision making, behind the ordinance, generally speaking, the courts are not
going to second guess the legislative decision making abilities for the general public. This is the type
of ordinance that is legislative in nature, it’s very specific to your definition of forestry based upon the
unique circumstances in the Ranches, based upon the fact that you’re making this consistent with the
other uses in the Ranches.

Commissioner Moskowitz said the next concern is there are two sides to this. There are people in the
ranches who just want to be able to do business and feed their family and the other side is the people
who have homes there who don’t run a business and they are concerned that this will lead to more
commercialization of the Ranches.
