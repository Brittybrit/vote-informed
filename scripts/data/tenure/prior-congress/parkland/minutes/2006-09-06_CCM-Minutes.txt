continuation of the "leave us alone" policy would bring deterioration to the neighborhood.

Cesar Perez commented that, in the past, the Ranches area had chosen to be left alone. He
resented changes for the benefit of specific individuals, which he believed would splinter the
neighborhood further, and believed the neighborhood should be brought together through
compromise. He suggested that the people already residing there should be left alone, but that
any new commercial businesses should be prevented.

Former Mayor Harold Bockhold asked whether public hearings had been held regarding the
November Charter changes. Additionally, he questioned the extension of terms to four years and
the imposition of term limits, which he believed would be opposed.

Mayor Udine commented that four-year terms were proposed to allow the City to move to the
November election cycle.

Dean Fulton of the Ranches felt the situation had been blown out of proportion. He had reread
the Ranches covenants and believed there was misunderstanding of the intent. He felt it clear
that many people did not want change, with which he agreed. However, he did not want
businesses operating in the Ranches and wanted the Commission to move forward and approve
the ordinances.

that all members of the Commission lived in gated communities. He asked regarding the
amendment to the 80/20 rule and who might vote on it, noting his understanding that only
Pinetree and Ranches residents would vote.

Mr. Maurodis explained that the 80/20 rule was adopted by referendum in 1974 and that the
separate Charter was adopted in 1982. The 80/20 rule was attached to the Charter as Article 13
and the Municipal Code Corporation indicated it was codified because they were asked to do so.
The Charter Review Board wanted to make sure there was no question that the 80/20 rule was
part of the Charter and wanted to provide extra protection; the proposed amendment would
formally place the 80/20 rule in the City Charter. Mr. Maurodis offered his opinion that the
80/20 rule continues to stand -- today and after November ih, requiring an affirmative vote of 80
percent, plus one, of Pinetree and Ranches residents.

City Manager Mark Lauzier added that a recent flyer distributed in the Ranches contained
misinformation as there was no fight to keep the 80/20 vote. It was believed that the Charter
amendment, if passed, would strengthen the 80/20 issue. Even should the question not pass, the
80/20 rule would still apply.

Michael Schiff of the Ranches asked further clarification about the 80/20 vote and noted that the
80/20 rule had not been challenged for more than 30 years. Mr. Maurodis provided further
explanation. He indicated that the Charter Review Board had struggled with the question, but
believed it would be made stronger with formal insertion into the Charter.

Mr. Schiff also commented about change and resident complaints, noting that he had not seen
anything proposed that would change what a small group of residents were fighting about. He
opposed the City's proposal about manure storage.

Ricky Gordon of Pinetree Estates believed it interesting that old ordinances referred to the
Ranches as the City of Parkland. He asked that the Commission preserve the reason why people
moved to the City and stop the commercialization of the Ranches.

Dodie Lazowick of the Ranches stated that she had in the past expressed concern about the non-
agricultural uses and had continually said that she did not understand how it would be addressed
unless the uses, both agricultural and commercial, were defined and enforced by the City. She
believed the proposed ordinances would affect Ranches residents but not address the commercial
uses. She opposed "grandfathering" unless it would go away when the property is sold. She
believed the problems in the Ranches stemmed from non-enforcement.

Kevin Bolling of the Ranches stated that he had invited Jason Curtis, an expert in agricultural
exemptions from the Property Appraiser's Office, to explain to the Commission his
interpretation. Mr. Bolling reiterated his disagreement with the City's position on the "forestry"
definition.

At the Mayor's invitation, Mr. Curtis indicated he would prefer to present his comments, based
on case law and as a representative of the Property Appraiser's Office, during the pertinent
agenda items.

COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, APPROVING AN
AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE CITY OF PARKLAND
AND TO PERFORM
LOBBYIST/STRATEGIST SERVICES.

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the title of Resolution No. 2006-85. He reviewed that the #1
Strategic Goal and Objective was the "Wedge" parcel and that funds had been budgeted for the
services of a lobbyist to strategize its annexation. He had not anticipated, however, needing to
make sure that people understood what the City hoped to accomplished and what the monies
were for. Referring to the budget, he noted that the $150,000 had been set aside for two
purposes: 1) professional assistance from a lobbyist and 2) conceptual master planning of the
"Wedge."

Mr. Lauzier recalled that, at the last meeting, he and the City Attorney had been instructed to
prepare an agreement, which had occurred; the solutions were the naming of the company and a
dollar amount. There had been conversations with the landowners, who recognize their role and
that of the City and its representation, as well as conversations with the individuals who attended
the last meeting and expressed interest in working together as suggested. He asked the
Commission's direction.

Mayor Udine advised that he had spoken with the landowners and their lobbyist recently. It was
his understanding of the timeline that there may be nothing to do until December at the earliest.
He voiced concern about the process that had taken place to obtain lobbyist services, as well as
the two responses received. He believed the City would lose nothing by publishing a Request for
Letters of Interest, tightening the requirements and, with a fairer bidding process, money would
be saved.

Vice Mayor McGilvray commented that the funds spent on lobbyist services would be trivial
compared to the tax revenues the City would receive for the "Wedge" parcel, and she believed
the money well spent. She did not agree that the services should be re-bid. She recalled
discussion about utilizing the developers' lobbyist to do some of the work, but she believed the
City needed someone to specifically represent the City. The Vice Mayor believed the time until
December could be well spent with the developers and determining like interests.

Commissioner Moskowitz stated he did not have a problem spending $150,000 and even more
for the lobbyist in order to obtain the "Wedge." He felt it absolutely necessary to annex the
property because, if not, University Drive would connect north to Palm Beach County, thereby
affecting the City and its lifestyle. He agreed that the City should obtain more proposals to make
sure that the dollars were spent wisely. The Commissioner believed the landowners would be the
driving force in the decision of whether or not the property was annexed to the City, and he
believed their efforts would start in November. He agreed that two weeks should be used to
publish and accept RLI proposals.

Planning Director Roberta Moore overviewed that these eight zoning text amendments had been
considered by the Planning and Zoning Board at three separate meetings: July 25th, August 10th,
and August 15th.

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2006-21.

Ms. Moore offered explanation regarding the revisions ofthe Ordinance through the July 25th and
August 10th Board meetings. Staff revised the Ordinance, placing separate limitations for
residential and commercial properties. Two-acre residential parcels would be allowed two
flagpoles and residential parcels under two acres would be permitted one. For Commercial and
Community Facility designated zoning districts, one flagpole would be permitted, and a site plan
approval process would be required prior to the building permit approval. Three flags were
permitted per pole.

Vice Mayor McGilvray commented that the word "building" in Section 1 (d) should be clarified,
and she questioned the elimination of the remainder of the paragraph. Ms. Moore indicated that
