2. PRESENTATION: CITY INFRASTRUCTURE AND FACIL11IF S
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young indicated Public Works Director Jim Berkman would make
a presentation regarding the City Infrastructure and Facilities.
Tim Berkman explained the topics for discussion today would be how the City approaches
infrastructure from a general point of view and from a capital point of view and what the City
will be looking at over the next five years. Today's presentation will mostly focus on Capital
Construction Maintenance and Repair, these items come from the capital improvement budget and
generally include large ticket items over $25,000. Examples would be one time construction or
irregular maintenance schedule. 85% of this type of work is preformed by private contractors.
Mr. Berkman reviewed the Roads education portion of the presentation. He advised under ideal
conditions the life cycle of a road is approximately twenty years. In the first fifteen years the road
deteriorates about 40 percent. After that you get rapid deterioration so in the next two and a half
years you loose another 40% of the road. In the presentation Pine Tree Road was used as an
example. He advised if a road was constructed in 1965 and resurfacing maintenance was done
every fifteen years since that year, the next resurfacing would be due around 2010 at the cost
$4,275,000.00. By not doing any of the proper maintenance on roads the costs overall would be
approximately $7,000,000.00 to reconstruct the roads. Mr. Berkman emphasized the point is to
show the importance of doing road maintenance on a regular schedule.
Mr. Berkman stated the City currently maintains 18 miles of city roads as listed in the power point
presentation and he anticipates another 19 miles being added in the near future, At 2008 prices the
City is paying about $350,000.00 per mile of road to accomplish resurfacing and milling if
necessary. The total miles of city wide roads in the future would be 37 miles not including
Loxahatchee Road. This would make the total cost over 15 years at $12,950,000.00. This
includes an ongoing, every 15 year road resurfacing program which would mean the annual
required budget would be $863,000 per year and the City would be resurfacing roughly 2.5 miles
of road per year.
Mr. Berkman advised that generally the city does not include sidewalks as a capital maintenance
issue as they are normally done out of the normal line item budget. However, there are some
sidewalks on Holmberg Road that he recommends be replaced if possible, specifically the section
between US 441 and Riverside Drive that has not yet been converted to concrete and the cost
would be $184,000.00 if the City Commission elects to have this done.
Mr. Berkman reviewed the HVAC systems which are the air conditioning and heating systems in
all the City buildings. He presented a list of all the city buildings for the Commission to review
and commented that it reflects a significant inventory of HVAC equipment to be maintained. Mr.
Berkman advised that the life span of a typical HVAC unit is 15 years and the current age of the
systems in the City are between 11 to 15 years old which means 10% of these units will require
replacement in the next 5 years with 34% between 6 to 10 years. Mr. Berkman stated the majority

Mr. Berkman reviewed the City's Roof systems and noted there are several different types of roof

basis but paint has a definite limit to its life span before fading or chipping occurs.

Commissioner Moskowitz questioned the financial choice to convert fields to synthetic turf. Mr.

ensure the safety of the citizens of Parkland by providing better field conditions year round.
Mr. Berkman reviewed Irrigation Pump stations. He advised currently the City has approximately
$170,000 worth of irrigation pump stations. The typical life span is 10 -15 years and 47% are over
the life span limit and some are at a point where the cost of repairs is more than the value of the
equipment. Mr. Berkman stated over the next (5) years this equipment will require replacement at
a cost of $83, 700.
Utilities and Stormwater these projects are usually a piece of a larger project and cost $80,000 a
year out of the general fund on storm water maintenance. This includes canals, lake maintenance,
catch basins and storm pipe cleaning on public right of ways through out the City.
In summary, Capital funding for infrastructure over the (5) year plan: $4 million for roads,
$38,000 for HVAC, $5,000 for roofs, $280,000 painting, $83,700 for irrigation, $184,000
remainder of sidewalk on the south side of Holmberg Road for a total of $4,907,050.00 to bring
the city infrastructure up to par.

1. DISCUSSION: GREEN INITIATIVES
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young introduced Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer
and Building Official Margaret Bertolami to discuss green initiatives and building codes.
Brian Archer explained the City of Parkland's Strategic Plan deals with five main topics: reducing
the carbon foot print, water conservation, green building, recycling and sustainable landscaping.
Mr. Archer reviewed one of the ways to embattle the carbon footprint is to purchase carbon
footprint offset credits. He stated many companies sell carbon credits to commercial entities and
individuals who are interested in voluntarily participating in this program. These credits range in
cost from $10.00 to $30.00 depending upon the company and the type of renewal energy source it
is affecting.

Mr. Archer described the City of Parkland's `Carbon Footprint Tracker', which is similar to a
carbon calculator. This Footprint Tracker will track everything that the City does that affects the
carbon foot print and green house gas emissions. Mr. Archer commented the purpose is to
promote what steps the City has taken to reduce the carbon footprint such as the plastic bag
recycling contest and `Ride your Bike to the Park' events.
Mr. Archer began the discussion on the next green initiative: water conservation. He referred to
the rain censor program discussed in the Energy Incentive program. Although it is a code
requirement to have a rain censor Mr. Archer feels that most people do not know about them or
how they work or the rain censors are not working properly.
Commissioner Moskowitz commented the City needs to work further with the Home Owner's
Associations and Golf Courses to promote proper use of rain censors as residents who live in
these communities do not have any control over the irrigation and rain censor systems.
Commissioner Smith questioned Mr. Archer regarding a state law that was put in place
approximately five years ago that stated new homes were required to have rain censors installed.
Mr. Archer confirmed that this is correct though the problem is in the ongoing maintenance of the
rain censors to ensure they are in good working order.
Mr. Archer noted that the local school recycling programs are progressing well on an individual
basis. He reviewed ways to reward the individual programs and schools for enhancing their
programs. The students are directly responsible for these programs and they vary in cost but some
options to look into would be sponsorships and grants that could help.
Mr. Archer reviewed the City's ongoing idea of holding a `Household Hazardous Waste
Collection Day'. He advised a few ways that the City could review in order to hold this event.
Broward County Recycling currently has a program that could come in to hold this event but it
would be a regional event for the City of Parkland and the neighboring cities. In order to have a
specific City of Parkland event for our residents we would need to hire the private recycling
contractor directly. Currently the City budgets $30,000 to $35,000 a year for Parkland residents
to go to the transfer station on their own to dispose of their hazardous waste items. Mr. Archer
noted that one possibility would be to use this money to offset the cost of the household
hazardous waste collection day event.
Commissioner Moskowitz questioned that if the recycling plastic bag event this weekend went
well possibly the City could do something similar for household hazardous waste?
Mr. Archer replied that they could arrange a similar event though the City would still need to hire
a licensed state approved recycling contractor to oversee the event. This would make it cost
prohibitive to hold more than a one time event.
Mayor Udine said that he would not want to eliminate the ability for residents to go on their own
to the transfer station to dispose of their hazardous waste. Mayor Udine said the City should try
to team up with neighboring cities.

Lastly Mr. Archer presented Green Building Coalition initiatives. In order to encourage green
building, the City could offer fast tracking permits or even to waive permit fees to encourage
green building. Mr. Archer invited Building Official Margaret Bertolami to begin her portion of
the presentation.
Commissioner Moskowitz thanked Brian Archer for his efforts on the environment and green
initiatives as well as his research on new initiatives.
Building Official Margaret Bertolami introduced an overview of the Florida residential green
building programs. Ms. Bertolami reviewed examples of green building programs: the `Built
Green' program developed in the city of Seattle, the `Earth Advantage' program in Oregon and
the Florida Green home designation certification program in Florida. These programs have in
common a point system which is a flexible allowance for green building components used. Each
project starts with green building plans designed by an architect who employs a third party
certified agent with inspections performed throughout the project. Florida green building homes
must achieve a minimum of 100 points out of 300 total credit points in eight categories with two
prerequisites.
Ms. Bertolami reviewed the Ordinance template prepared by Miami -Dade County and noted the
City of Gainesville adopted this ordinance which they have mandated only for city projects and
residential use is voluntary. Some of their residential incentives are fast track permitting, reduced
fees up to 50 monetary and cash incentives subject to available funding for remodeling and
passive solar water systems. Ms. Bertolami concluded by recommending a review of this model
ordinance for adoption by the City of Parkland
Mayor Udine thanked Ms. Bertolami for her presentation and noted that one of the issues that will
come up is cost versus benefits. The Mayor was not certain if this should become mandatory due
to the increase in green home building costs. He suggested he would like to see if we can
encourage some of these ideas voluntarily.
Ms. Bertolami responded that the ordinance template suggests separating mandatory items from
voluntary items which would be less restrictive but still informative for the residents.
Commissioner Moskowitz commented that based on the city's demographics we will find the
environment is an important issue to current and future residents.
City Manager Caryn Gardner Young said the Mayor brought up a good point earlier in suggesting
the City may want to offer incentives to encourage further green building initiatives. She stated
the city will need to decide what type of approach to take instead of mandating it.

3. DISCUSSION: SOLAR POWERED WATER HEATER
REQUIREMENTS
Environmental Resources Director, Mr. Brian Archer said that in keeping with the City of
Parkland's character, and encouraging green initiatives, the City Commission asked the City
Attorney, Andy Maraudis to draft a solar water heater ordinance for discussion.
Mr. Maraudis reviewed the revised ordinance with the City Commission. He advised that the
intent is to implement a requirement regarding residential solar powered water heaters and to
ensure consistency with the South Florida Building codes. He feels it does, as this ordinance deals
with how the unit is powered, not how it is built. After January 1s 2009 new home building
permits would require one solar powered unit per home. Mr. Maraudis also advised they wish to
add language for an out clause for hardship cases where residents can apply for relief.
Mr. Archer advised that based on today's direction this ordinance will appear for first reading on
the November 19 regular City Commission meeting.
Commissioner Moskowitz advised the reason he asked for this item to come before the
Commission is two fold. One, currently we have done similar things with our businesses as they
are required to go green in the future and in addition the City also follows these guidelines. He
feels it is time to move this to our residential sector. Initially the solar powered heater cost is more
expensive upfront but by the fifth year residents would begin to save money. The purpose is not
to just save money but to save energy as well.
Commissioner Smith asked if this would apply to existing homes if the water heater goes out or
just new construction.
