1. DISCUSSION: PRELIMINARY RANCHES DRAINAGE STUDY

City Manager, Mark Lauzier reported to the Commission about the preliminary Ranches
drainage study. He had requested that Keith & Associates review options to resolve the
drainage issues in the Ranches. Currently, there is no fonnal drainage plan existing in the
Ranches; what the City calls drainage ditches were originally intended to be irrigation
ditches, not drainage ditches. Some of these ditches feed into the Hillsboro Canal. The
property owners historically manage the drainage on their own property. Keith &

Associates analyzed the issues concerning the drainage in the Ranches and they outlined

six options in their report, in addition, as a seventh option, the City can maintain the

status quo.

Now the City needs to go to the next phase. For Keith & Associates to do calculations for
the City, it would cost approximately $25,000 to $50,000, not including any surveying
costs. Staff wants to: 1) Move forward with NRCS agreement to clean out canals and
ditches; 2) Determine and evaluate what the problem actually is; and 3) Plan for next
phase. At this point, the staff is still analyzing the options presented by Keith and
Associates and is not ready to make any recommendations. The City is currently
operating under the presumption that residents are managing their own water. The most
important issue is establishing a new method for dealing with emergencies in case there is
a big weather event. In order to appropriately respond, the water district needs to allow
the City of Parkland to open up the flood gates.

Patty Hitchcock from Keith & Associates stated that the irrigation system which is now
being used for drainage connects to the Hillsboro Canal via a pump which can be used to
regulate water levels. During a storm event, it would be desirous to allow a gravity outfall
to the Hillsboro Canal, but it is currently not permitted by the South Florida Water
Management District. In order to address ponding issues in the Ranches, existing ditches
need to be cleaned out, they must identify any crushed pipes and get approval from the
Water District for outfall. This is an immediate need.

Mr. Lauzier said they are trying to get approval from the Water District. They need to
submit a specific report as part of the approval process.

Mayor Udine opened to questions from the Commission.

Commissioner Moskowitz asked if there are any studies outlining the scope of work or
cost. Many of the ditches are filled in with debris and dirt rendering them completely
non- functional.

City Engineer, Charlie DaBrusco, said it will take approximately 180 days to clean out
the canals and there is no room to put in swales.

Mr. Keith of Keith and Associates said that he felt the City was being cooperative with
the Water District. He is also looking at the possibility of grant money.

The City Manager stated that the City should not spend money on a solution before
investigating the possibility that we may be able to fund through grant money.

Mayor Udine said that the piping is different elevations and sizes. He asked how it would
affect the project if the pipes were a uniform size.

Mr. Keith replied that the entire system needs to be analyzed.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky stated that she was concerned about the issues with the pavement
too close to the ditches; she wants to prioritize high risk areas. She feels they can rule out
some of the options due to cost restrictions. She felt the City needs to define the scope of
the project before it can focus on execution. She had a question about Mr. Keith being a

Mr. DaBrusco said the emergency discharge into Hillsboro Canal is the biggest issue. For

Commissioner Smith asked if the drainage issues are the responsibility of the City or the
homeowner.

City Manager Lauzier stated that there are two key issues: first, the permit from the
district for emergency water runoff from the roads. Second, since 1989 the county has
required everyone to manage their own water, he thinks that in this case the rule applies
and the property owners should manage the water on their own property.

Mr. DaBrusco clarified that the ditches are the City's responsibility because they need to
be maintained to accommodate runoff from the roadway.

Mr. Keith said that the City needs to demonstrate to the Water District that they looked at
all the options, even if they are cost prohibitive. We can explain our reasoning for not
choosing a specific option to the District.

Ms. Hitchcock stated that it is important that the City can show they are reqUIrIng
residents to participate in the program. In order to pick the seventh option (maintain
status quo) the City must demonstrate that they looked at all the other options first. It is
helpful to show that the City is requiring every individual property owner to comply with
requirements and that the City is complying with the drainage requirements in the right of
way.

Mayor Udine suggested that they complete the first phase as soon as possible; it may be
enough to resolve most of the issues.

Commissioner McGilvray stated that she thought they were headed in the right direction.
The City should keep up with code enforcement issues in the Ranches and regulate new
developments by requiring a drainage plan.

Mr. DaBrusco said that all newer developments have some type of lake system to store
water.

Mr. Lauzier stated that awarding of the bid to clean out the ditches will be on the agenda
at the May 24th meeting. There will also be further discussion about the permitting
process and review of some of the options, including pricing.

2. DISCUSSION : ENVIRONMENTAL WORK PLAN

City Manager, Mark Lauzier, stated that this was a strategic goal from a year ago. They
had a plan developed however, in response to Hurricane Wilma, they added a tree
replacement program and a hurricane recovery process to the work plan. The staff is now
ready to revisit the Environmental Work Plan.

Brian Archer, Environmental Resources Director, gave a power point presentation to the
Commission reviewing the phases of the Environmental Work Plan, please see copy
attached. In addition, he made the following comments.

Phase 1: There are five sites under a maintenance agreement with the city, county or state
government from 2005 through 2007. During the years 2007-08, the City will be
responsible for all maintenance. Up to now they have only been responsible for the non -
exotics.

Holmberg Road natural area is a 38 acres site. In the past, the City spent $6,000 for
maintenance on a 10 acre section, next year they will have to provide maintenance for the
entire 38 acre site, causing an increase in maintenance costs.

Ternbridge Preserve is a four acre tract designated as a conservation easement. Hurricane
Wilma changed how the City needs to maintain the area. Mr. Archer projects it would be
$1,200 for twice a year maintenance.

In Terramar Park Preserve, it will cost approximately $10,000 to deal with the exotics.

Six Acre Wood currently has no maintenance agreement, however the CIP calls for
exotic maintenance and removal, and landscaping and buffering in the future, costing
approximately $30,000. Maintenance will be approximately $4,500 per year.

The money budgeted for the CIP only takes the City through 2008. After that they would
require $60,000/year through 2010 to control exotics. Once exotics are under control, it
will cost approximately $49,000 to $50,000 a year to maintain all the properties. This
includes Pine Trails wetland mitigation.

Phase II includes Margate Blount Archeological Site in Trails End which is self funding
from grants and reimbursements. It also will include the Beaty property if it is purchased.
In addition, there are four acres at end of Parkside Drive which is all Australian Pines;
action is pending development, as with old city hall.

Possible environmental needs for Covered Bridge Park are to bring trees from Parkside
Drive, but the east and west ends of the park are inhabited solely by invasive exotics.

Ternbridge Trail is part private, part City owned.

Riverglades Elementary School had an issue with carrotwoods however the hurricane
took care of that. Future needs may be taken care of through the Community Wildlife
Habitat Program.

Phase III: The street and road rights of way were inventoried, listing where public works
maintains landscaping in the right of way.

Phase IV: The City has begun to promote enviromnental awareness by posting
information on the City's web site, it is also provided when a resident obtains a permit.

Phase V: The tree replacement program is beginning this week with very successful
results.

Phase VI: Hurricane loss prevention includes investigating species for a variety of
characteristics, including root and soil issues and proper growing space for trees.

Phase VI: Deals with recycling concerns, particularly at the parks. City staff is currently
investigating improving these services.

Attachment A explains the cost and scheduling of various projects.

Attachment B provides information regarding amending the Landscape Code to include
additional species oftrees.

Mr. Lauzier responded that they need to make a policy decision about removing invasives
from City property. Cost will be a factor. Once that decision is made, they can proceed

3. DISCUSSION: HOLMBERG ROAD IMPROVEMENT DESIGN

Mayor Udine said that concerns about Holmberg Road were brought back to the
Commission because of the EAR issue when it was submitted to Tallahassee. He thinks
the City should have another joint meeting between the Commission and the Planning
and Zoning Board to discuss Holmberg Road. In addition, they need to explain to the

public as to why the City is revisiting this issue. He then asked Caryn Gardner - Young to
explain the situation.

The Assistant City Manager, Ms. Gardner - Young, explained that every seven years the
City needs to reevaluate the comprehensive plan. Upon review, they decided it was
important to maintain the current lifestyle. The City is then required to submit a report to
the State which has oversight responsibility. The State commented that Holmburg Road
between University and 441 is over capacity and they want the City to address this issue.
If Parkland does not take action, all future comprehensive plan amendments will not pass
the State, therefore it is imperative that this issue be addressed.

Mr. Maurodis stated that it was important for the Commission to set policy for the right
of way.

Mayor Udine reiterated that he wants to hold a joint meeting with the Planning and
Zoning Board that is open to the public.

Mr. Lauzier said that the intent of the comprehensive plan was to preserve lifestyle
issues. He suggested that they can start the process with the State by narrowing down the
options but still leave the specifics for the Commission to discuss.

Mayor Udine asked for and received a consensus from the Commission.
