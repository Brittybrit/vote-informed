III PUBLIC INPUT

Hope Gramlich, a Coconut Creek resident, addressed the Commission regarding the intent of the
Federal Aeronautics Administration to remove low-altitude radar from the tower at Palm Beach
International Airport. She noted that Fort Lauderdale airport did not have radar, and that both
high and low altitude radar for the entire southeast Florida coast would be administered by
Miami International. There would be no air support should the Miami center go down due to a
disaster such as a hurricane. Mrs. Gramlich distributed a packet of informative materials.

STRATEGIC PLAN PROGRESS - YEAR 1

City Manager Mark Lauzier presented a one-year progress report of the goals and
accomplishments in preparation for the Strategic Planning Retreat, to be held Friday and
Saturday, May 1ih and May 13th:

Financially-sound City government:
. Adopted the City's first fully-funded, five-year Capital Improvement Program;
. Developed a multi-million dollar parks grant program;
. Refinanced the Terramar Park debt, thereby saving $800,000 and adding additional
revenues to complete Pine Trails Park Phase 2;
. Replenished the hurricane reserve, noting that FEMA dollars had been received in
advance of the expenditures;
. Implemented service fees for fire assessment, park impact, and development.

Public schools of excellence:

transaction;

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the second reading of Ordinance No. 2005-32.

Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer gave a brief presentation regarding the research
requested by the Commission. At his City-wide review of trees with power line issues, he found
that approximately 75 to 80 percent of the trees in conflict with overhead utilities were located on
private property, with the remaining 20 to 25 percent on public property. He estimated that 90
percent of all trees in potential conflict were the tall, mature slash pines, cypress, and Australian
pines which had, in most cases, been regenerated through self-seeding. The remaining 10
percent were common landscape trees, planted in most cases by residents or builders 15 to 20
years ago. In order to remove the trees on City property, a cost evaluation of each tree would be
required and would result in significant budget needs. Mr. Archer noted no issues west of
University Drive existed because utilities were located underground.

Mr. Archer noted that FPL had scheduled a contractor to clear vegetation in the Parkland area
within the next 60 days. The vegetation, where possible, would be removed down to or near
grade level, resulting in lesser pruning and removal costs for both residents and the City.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky thanked staff for the additional clarifications.

Mayor Udine opened to the public. There were no comments or questions, and the Mayor closed
to the public.

Mr. Lauzier noted that two minor changes had been made to the Ordinance, and Vice Mayor
Ribotsky read them into the record: Section 9-31.1, paragraph 1 - General Provisions, "All trees
within 30 feet of overhead utility lines installed..." and, within paragraph 6 - Requirements, "All
businesses, which perform tree pruning services in accordance with the requirements of Section
9-31.1.3 'Nithin the City of Parkland, are required... ". Mr. Lauzier offered that these changes
provide clarification and have no conflict with other landscaping ordinances.

Mayor Udine commented, for the record, that the Planning and Zoning Board had voted 7-0 in
favor of approval, and the Commission had approved the Ordinance 5-0 on first reading.

MOTION made by Vice Mayor Ribotsky to approve Ordinance No. 2005-32, Right Tree Right
Place. Seconded by Commissioner Smith.

STRATEGIC PLAN PROGRESS - YEAR 1

This item was moved ahead in the agenda.

Mayor Udine commented regarding the issue which would have been presented by Mr. Hart had
he arrived.

Mayor Udine offered that the MPO would have on their agenda next Thursday whether it would
support the County Commission's desire for a November ballot item regarding the imposition of
a one cent sales tax for transportation projects. The presentation has been made to the City of
Tamarac Commission, which supports it. He did not know whether the Coral Springs
Commission had met, but he had heard there was support there as well. Parkland's support may
be moot. Margate and Pembroke Pines have already passed resolutions of support.

Mayor Udine explained there was approximately $10 billion in transportation projects that were
not being addressed because the dollars were funneled elsewhere that provide matching funds
and a local dedicated revenue source. He gave as an example the east/west train proposal. The
studies he had seen indicated that the proposed tax would be a $45 annual burden on the average
family, but it was estimated that it would save the average family in gas and traffic delays
approximately $450 annually.
