6. ORDINANCE NO. 2007-06: SUNSHADE ZTA
COMMENTS: FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND,
FLORIDA, AMENDING SECTION 11-72 (A) OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND CODE OF ORDINANCES
ENTITLED "RECREATIONAL ACTIVITIES AND
FACILITIES" TO ALLOW TEMPORARY SUN-SHADE
CANOPIES IN CITY PARKS; PROVIDING FOR
CONFLICTS; PROVIDING FOR SEVERABILITY; .AND
PROVIDING FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE.
Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the first reading. of Ordinance No. 2007-06.
Leisure Services Director Scott Davidoff stated this amendment was as a result of requests from
residents, and particularly those involved in sports leagues, because the current Ordinance
prohibits all tents, sunshades, and canopies of any nature. This Ordinance was brought to the
Parks and Recreation Advisory Board and was unanimously approved.

<<PG>>
PARKLAND CITY COMMISSION MEETING MINUTES
MARCH 28, 2007
PAGE 14
Mayor Udine indicated that he strongly supported this Ordinance and that he would have himself
asked for the amendment due to the volume of comments and questions he had experienced at
the fields. He asked why sunshades had previously been prohibited.
Ms. Gardner-Young stated that neither she nor Mr. Davidoff had been able to discover the
Ordinance history.. Mr. Davidoff commented that there was now a proliferation of sunshades that
were both affordable and safe.
Mayor Udine believed that the current Ordinance should not be enforced at the parks as sun
exposure was recognized as a major health concern. Family life and sports involvement are high
priorities in Parkland. He asked that the Ordinance amendment be conveyed to residents and the
Park Rangers.
Commissioner Weissman commented concerning the provision that the sunshades not impede
the visibility of other spectators, and he asked how that might be enforced. Mr. Davidoff
responded that the Rangers would have the authority to make determinations as to their
placement and number.
Commissioner Weissman also expressed concern that sunshades may become projectiles in
unexpected high winds, and Mr. Davidoff acknowledged that possibility. Commissioner Smith
suggested an amendment that, should winds exceed a certain velocity, the sunshades must be
taken down. Commissioner Weissman noted there may not be time to do so. Discussion ensued.
Mayor Udine commented that it was not possible to legislate every possible issue, but he
believed the benefits of this Ordinance far outweighed its negatives, and he would support the
Ordinance as amended. He suggested that this issue might be revisited should it become a
problem. He acknowledged the potential liability issue with flying tents, but he believed that
sunshades offered a more important benefit to residents.
Mayor Udine opened to the public.
Natalie Bigio of Pinetree Estates believed the sunshades were a great idea. She noted that
canopies were up at the concert and that people held onto the poles as the showers came through.
She believed that responsible adults would hold onto the poles as winds began to gust. Ms. Bigio
suggested that all playground areas in the City's parks should have some type of sunshades.
There were no further comments or questions, and Mayor Udine closed to the public.
MOTION made by Commissioner Smith to approve the first reading of Ordinance No. 2007-06,

7. DISCUSSION: IT PLAN
Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young recalled that the Commission had asked that staff
do an overall examination of Information Technology needs when the Granicus program was
brought before the Commission. Finance and Administrative Services Director Barbara Hastings
had taken the lead in the study.
Ms. Hastings advised that IT needs had been discussed with department directors. She reviewed
that $354,000 was currently in the budget under capital programs, with $35,000 earmarked for an
IT evaluation by a consultant. She recommended, however, that these funds instead be used for
immediate needs. She stated that the Finance Department operates on a very old system, which
is no longer able to handle its functions; payroll had been outsourced to ADP, costing
approximately $50,000 per year, and fixed asset maintenance had been outsourced to the
auditors. Ms. Hastings noted that an RFQ for auditing could soon be published, and she believed
there may be issues should there be a transition to a new auditing firm. Additionally, with the
Leisure Services move to the new facility, software for their system would cost approximately
$80,000. She reviewed needs for GIS, CADD, a computer training center for the Library, and
Granicus; unfortunately, all of these could not be funded.
Ms. Hastings recommended funding financial system software, which would also help other
operating departments, as well as that for Leisure Services. She also recommended that afull-
time IT person be added to staff, an individual who would understand the City's needs, provide a
higher level of service, and assist with the conversion to the new financial system. She explained
that Argent personnel presently come to City Hall on Monday, Wednesday, and Friday at a cost
of $70 per hour per individual; Argent had recommended that more time be spent at City Hall
due to the volume of work. Ms. Hastings indicated that she had reorganized the Finance
Department, reduced the Human Resources Director to a part-time position, and readjusted some
salaries within the department, which would save approximately $60,000 in salaries. Money
would also be saved on the Argent contract with an in-house person.
Ms. Gardner-Young added there would be zero cost for the IT person by the actions already
taken and those proposed. She believed it a win-win situation. She was supportive of what had
been presented. Clearly, as even indicated by the independent auditor, the financial upgrade was
essential, and technology was needed for Leisure Services. She asked the Commission's support.
Mayor Udine asked clarification that the City Manager's Office recommended funding items
numbered 1 and 2, which Ms. Gardner-Young confirmed. She indicated that the other programs
would be reevaluated in the future.
