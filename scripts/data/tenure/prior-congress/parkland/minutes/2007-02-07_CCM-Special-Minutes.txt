MOTION made by Commissioner Weissman to add The Tamara Peacock Company to the short

MOTION passed unanimously.
City Manager Mark Lauzier explained the purpose of the meeting. He indicated that a committee
of six staff members had examined design and construction, with the charge to short-list the eight
submissions to five; the committee had not ranked the firms. He noted that the Commissioners
had reviewed the proposals; they would evaluate the team's presence and rank the six 15-minute
presentations.
ALAI ASSOCIATES
Adolpho Cotilla, President of ALAI Associates, introduced Cindy Baldwin, Director of
Operations, who made the presentation.
Ms. Baldwin indicated that ACAI, an Hispanic minority firm, was founded in 1985 and had been
repeatedly chosen for projects by both public and private agencies. They were multi-tasking in
infrastructure, with architects, engineers, and general contractors on staff, giving the firm the
opportunity to view projects from 360 degrees. They were a full service, including
architectural/engineering, programming, master planning, waterproofing, and security and
communications integration. The team was comprised of: Delta G Consulting Engineers, Inc.,
with extensive fire station experience; S & F Engineers, Inc., for structural; and Craven
Thompson & Associates, Inc., for civil engineering. Ms. Baldwin stated they were a large firm,
with over 28 members and support staff in place, and they could start immediately. Additionally,
there were sub-teams that could, as needed, step up and facilitate so that the project would be
kept on time and on budget.
Ms. Baldwin reviewed their past experiences of projects in the area and spoke regarding their
operations-based .design approach to the site plan and floor plan for the different user groups of
the facilities. She also addressed issues of concern in the community.
There were no Commission questions.
STEPHEN BORUFF ARCHITECTS & PLANNERS, INC.
Stephen Boruff, principal architect with Stephen Boruff Architects & Planners, Inc., first
apologized for the failure of their PowerPoint presentation. He introduced the team members
present and spoke regarding the organization of their team, which included: Miller Legg,. Inc.;
Grant Thornborough & Associates; O'Donnell, Narraroto, Mignogna; International Consulting
Engineers, Inc.; Tierra, Inc.; and CMS -Construction Management Services, Inc. Mr. Boruff
reviewed their Broward County .experience and their current projects ranging from $1.5 million
to over $40 million, and he noted that many clients were repeat clients.
Presentations were made by team members regarding .their municipal and governmental
experience; their research of the site; and apparatus access.

Mr. Boruff concluded the presentation, indicating that they would welcome input from
Fire/Rescue and Public Services. He briefly mentioned the AIA awards to the firm, and he
assured that the construction schedule would allow time for client review and that all insurance
requirements would be met.
Commissioner Weissman asked confirmation that staff would be based out of their West Palm
Beach office. Mr. Boruff indicated affirmatively, but noted that the construction administration
person resided in Coral Springs and would be assigned to this project.
Mayor Udine questioned whether there had been any communications with neighboring
homeowners' associations or other groups. Mr. Boruff responded in the negative. He stated that
the site had been surveyed, and he acknowledged the need for buffering. He believed that part of
the process would be to hold a workshop for neighborhood involvement and input.
C3TS
Jeff Crews, a principal associate with C3TS, commenced the presentation. He indicated that the
120-person full service architectural and engineering firm had been in business for 19 years in
South Florida. He introduced other team members representing TLC, Inc., and Rosenberg
Gardner Design. Mr. Crews briefly reviewed their organizational chart, the expertise that would
be brought to the project, and their past projects for public safety and fire.
Project Manager Mathilde Reyes spoke regarding her inspection of the existing site and the
relevant issues of access, response times, ease of maintenance, control of construction costs, and
long-term operating costs. She believed that the project must relate to the overall character. of the
community. She explained their process, which would commence with consultation with the key.
players in order to define the project's scope, budget, and schedule.
Javier Salman, principal in charge of architecture, apologized for his lateness. He believed his
team understood fire and emergency service facilities and noted their recent completion of a
facility in Coral Springs and their good relationship with the Fire Department.
CURRIE SOWARDS AGUILA ARCHITECTS
Jose Aguila introduced his partner, Jess Sowards, and indicated that their firm had been
established in 1969 in Delray Beach. Hundreds of buildings of all types had been designed,
garnering 58 awards from various organizations including the AIA and magazine features. In the
year 2000, they were the Firm of the Year from the American Institute of Architects, Florida
Chapter. He reviewed the organizational chart and information about members of the team. He
noted that their. firm had done 15 to 17 fire stations.
Mr. Sowards reviewed some of their municipal projects, which included recent new construction
and renovation of existing fire stations in Boca Raton. Fire stations were interacting more and
more with other municipal services. He noted the different character of various stations to fit
within the surrounding community.

Mr. Aguila noted that, in their 38-year. history, they had not been named a party in any litigation
on any project they had worked on, which he believed spoke to the value of their clients and the
communication with them. He believed management and communication were crucial to every
project, and he acted as the conduit through which all information flowed. Detailed minutes and
reports were prepared, distributed, and used to track activities. They were prepared to make
presentations to the public, officials, and communities and coordinate all activities throughout
the process.
WALTERS ZACKRIA ASSOCIATES and C3TS
Robert Walters made a PowerPoint presentation in behalf of his firm, formerly Robert Walters
Architects for 25 years, which he described as specializing in municipal architecture for Broward
~~ County cities. He commented that Parkland was the only city in Broward County that he had not
yet worked for. He introduced other team members from C3TS and TLC, as well as Mr. Zackria.
He reviewed some of their past and current projects throughout the County, as well as fire station
experience. with key design elements. He noted their expertise in "green" architecture, which tied
the buildings to the environment: protecting and restoring habitat and optimizing energy,
daylight, water usage, recycled materials, mechanical systems, and ventilation. They are a full
service firm.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz inquired about "green" architecture, a trend in some cities. Mr. Walters
indicated that it would be recommended for as many elements as possible. He and team
members briefly spoke regarding sustainable design and aspects of projects underway.
THE TAMARA PEACOCK COMPANY
Tamara Peacock recalled her former residency in Parkland, when she had served on the
Community Appearance Board serving with Michael Schiff, and she noted that she had come in
second in the design of City Hall.
Ms. Peacock stated that she had been in business for more than 20 years. It was a 100-percent
minority team, and she introduced the team members present. They were currently working in
eight Broward cities; they had worked in 16. Approximately one-third of their work was
municipal architecture, and she reviewed some of those projects. She believed they could design
a building that was memorable as well as a statement for the City.
There were presentations of a schematic design for the site, including the element of emergency
vehicle access, and the construction project schedule.
Mr. Lauzier outlined the procedures for the 7:00 p.m. meeting. The Commissioners were asked
to individually rank the firms, and Mr. Maurodis commented that only the top three were needed.
III ADJOURNMENT
MOTION made by Vice Mayor Moskowitz to adjourn the meeting at 6:46 p.m. Seconded by
Commissioner Weissman.
