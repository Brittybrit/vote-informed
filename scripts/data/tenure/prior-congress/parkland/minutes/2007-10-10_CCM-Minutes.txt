City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young and City Clerk .Sandra Couzzo made a brief PowerPoint
presentation. It was stated that, due to the Charter amendments, the date of Commission
elections had changed, as had the terms of the commissioners. In the past, it had been
Commission policy that advisory board positions run concurrently with the commissioner who
made the appointment. With the change in the Charter, there should also be changes made in the
Code, and staff was seeking Commission direction.
Commissioner Weissman believed that the individual appointments made by the Commissioners
should have terms of two years. Noting that it would allow more people to participate, it would
also give the Commission the opportunity to consider the effectiveness and attendance of their
appointees.
Mayor Udine agreed. He believed that appointments should run concurrent with the
commissioner and, since the City had gone to four year terms, he agreed that board appointments
should be for two years. There would be no prohibition about reappointing the same person, but
commissioners would also have the ability to give other people an opportunity.
Commissioner Weissman added that some terms would normally end in March, and Ms.
Gardner-Young noted that a number of Board terms expired last March but they would sit until
their successors are appointed. The Commissioner added that he did not foresee a problem if the
appointments were left until next November, when two Commission seats would be filled.
Mayor Udine agreed that it should start next November, so that it would not interfere with
whoever the new commissioner might be. He acknowledged that a process to update board
appointments should be formulated, and he believed this a good way to do it.
Commissioner Weissman suggested that the Commission should consider two-year terms for the
at-large positions as well -from whatever date those terms expire.
Both Mayor Udine and Vice Mayor Moskowitz agreed with two years across-the-board.
Ms. Couzzo noted that the both Education Advisory Board and the Planning and Zoning Board
had language that members' terms run concurrent with the commissioner appointing them.
Commissioner Weissman indicated that the Commission must then amend the enabling
ordinances, with which Mr. Maurodis agreed.
Mayor Udine commented that his personal opinion was that a board appointment should cease
when the appointing commissioner no longer served, with a new commissioner having the ability
to appoint Board members. Vice Mayor Moskowitz felt that, if board members' terms ran
concurrently with that of the appointing commissioner, board terms would cease when a
commissioner left office. There was consensus to "clean up" the language, and Mr. Maurodis
indicated that the Commission's intent would be translated.

Commissioner Weissman recalled the recent removal of a board member on which the entire
Commission had voted. He believed that in the future the Commission should not vote. If a
commissioner decides to remove or replace their appointee on a board, he believed it should be
the commissioner's decision and the Commission should not in any way vote on that as an issue.
Mayor Udine agreed. He clarified that, with a commission vacancy, a new commissioner should
have the right to make their own board appointments -whether appointing new people or
retaining the prior appointees.
Mr. Maurodis indicated that language should be included that the individuals appointed would
serve at the pleasure of the appointing commissioner; terms would end when the appointing
commissioner left office.
Commissioner Weissman noted that, with the Charter change, there had been a Commission
vacancy for many months and some of the previous commissioner's appointments were still
serving. He asked how the remaining Commission might address those appointments.
Mayor Udine believed that it would be addressed through Mr. Maurodis' efforts to draft language
that board appointments would end when a commissioner left office. Mr. Maurodis suggested
that the appointment could become at-large, limited to a specific period of time and until a new
commissioner was elected.
1. DISCUSSION: PINE TRAILS PARK CELLULAR TOWER
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young stated that, during the discussion about the installation of a
cell tower at Old City Hall, there had been several comments made about the quality of cell
service in the area of Heron Bay and the western portions of the City. As a result, she had
investigated the installation of a cell tower at Pine Trails Park, having spoken with two firms
already doing business with the City: Florida Towers LLC, the group doing the installation at
Old City Hall, and Verizon, which has the cell tower at Liberty Park. Both groups were present,
and Ms. Gardner-Young indicated that the City could also choose to go through the bid process.
She asked the Commission's direction.
Mayor Udine thought it interesting that Verizon was present when, in the past, there had been
difficulty getting in touch about problems with the flag.
A Verizon representative explained that they represented site development, and their response
had been to the City's inquiry regarding location. As to the operations, he indicated that they
should be dealing with the City 24/7 for maintenance requests, and he promised to investigate.
Mayor Udine assured that the City's past failings with Verizon would not be "smeared," but he
indicated that he would not vote for the cell tower without the provision that, should maintenance
issues not be addressed by the cell tower company, the City would do the repair and then bill the
company. He made it clear that an agreement with Verizon would include the Liberty Park cell
tower maintenance issues as well.

Ms. Gardner-Young commented that, by keeping a spare flag, the City's issues with Verizon
could be addressed.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz indicated that he would not vote for a flag tower, believing that the City
should have only one; additionally, a tree would be easier. He recalled that Florida Towers LLC
had spoken of several providers on the Old City Hal tower, and Ms. Gardner-Young stated that
two were certain but that four or five could be viable. At the Vice Mayor's question of
collocation on a Verizon site, Ms. Gardner-Young confirmed that occurred at the Liberty Park
tower. Vice Mayor Moskowitz stated that, while he would like to see proposals from both
companies, it might be best to put it out to bid. He questioned the results of other cities' efforts.
Ms. Gardner-Young stated that the City could not know for sure until the bid was published. She
noted that these two firms were clearly interested and timing was a consideration. The proposals
were structured very differently. She noted that the Pine Trails Park tower could include a
structure, such as a clock tower, to somehow incorporate it into the park theme.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz offered that he had no problem doing a structure; he did not believe the
City should have another flag. He asked if either firm had guaranteed the number of providers,
and Ms. Gardner-Young offered that the number of providers who would collocate would be
dependent upon the height of the structure and the coverage needed for their customers.
John Riff with Florida Towers LLC addressed the Commission. He briefly spoke regarding his
proposal with up to five carriers.
The Verizon representative was also given an opportunity to address the Commission.
Commissioner Weissman acknowledged the interest of the two companies, but felt it would be in
the City's best interest and its residents to go out to bid. He estimated that revenues could
exceed $2 million over the 25-year term of the lease and assuming that location was needed and
maximized. An "apples to apples" comparison could then be made.
City Attorney .Andrew Maurodis suggested that, as a property lease, it should be kept fairly
general.
Mayor Udine suggested that the bid could be done in the form of a Request for Letters of Interest
or a Request for Proposal.
Vice Mayor suggested that the tower's appearance should not be completely specified as he
would like to see some creativity.. Additionally,. he stated that he had a difficult time accepting
that a tower owner would willingly accept the competition to collocate. He suggested that a third
party owner, maximizing the leases, rents, and making the most money, made the most sense.
Mayor Udine agreed. He suggested that Verizon should be asked the collocation history of the
Liberty Park tower.

Planning Director Roberta Moore commented that Verizon was currently the only provider on
that tower, and offered that another firm had attempted a petition to collocate on the tower.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested that, for a decade, Verizon had been the only provider and, for
that reason, he would not support a tower sponsored by the cell phone company. Additional
information was provided by the company representatives.
With further discussion, there was Commission consensus that the tower should be bid.
Commissioner Weissman suggested that the bid require that companies furnish the location of all

frequency needs in specific geographic areas to enhance coverage.
Mayor Udine asked Ms. Gardner-Young to find out in the interim about Verizon's collocation
department and how to get service when the flag needs replaced.
2. DISCUSSION: ENVIRONMENTAL WORK PLAN UPDATE
RECYCLE & ENERGY ACTION PLAN
Environmental- Resources Director Brian Archer gave a brief presentation. He believed that
promotion of the plan was the key to its success. Staff would propose a resolution for the
October 16th Commission meeting to amend the Environmental Work Plan to include the
amended recycling and the new energy action plan.
Mayor Udine thanked Mr. Archer for his work.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that he and Mr. Archer had spoken at length. This update
would be part of the City's efforts with Waste Management in recycling. The plan would
eventually come to the Commission for vote, and he asked that changes be publicized,
particularly the solar heating grant program. In addition, the Vice Mayor stated that he would
present to the Commission at the first meeting in November an environmental plan which would
encourage resident participation through tax rebates, and he hoped for the Commission's support.
Mayor Udine stated that he liked the idea of incentives, but he believed the City should
understand incentives in the overall scheme of property tax reform. He suggested there should be
numbers more in line with what people were actually paying in property taxes.

Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that, in discussing this with the City Manager, he had hoped
to compile eight or ten proposals, arrive at a cost, and then estimate the number of residents who
might sign up in order to calculate the total amount of money that the program might leverage
out of the City. He was unaware of any other governmental entity in the State doing this type of
program and he hoped to lead on the issue. While he agreed it could not be so lucrative that
people would not pay taxes to the City or that the City would lose substantial revenues, he
believed the amount must be in that area considered an incentive. He hoped to bring a balanced
plan to the Commission.
Mayor Udine believed that the City must make a concerted effort with Waste Management,
assuming that the method of collection will eventually be used throughout the City. He had
spoken with a number of residents, and he suggested that a major educational push must be
made. He spoke of comparatives in the sizes of the 95-gallon trash bin and the 65-gallon
recyclable bin. While he believed that a majority of residents would be in favor, he emphasized
there must be considerable education to successfully roll out the program.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz stated that he had spoken with Waste Management, and he was aware of
their education programs elsewhere. Since the City would be at the forefront of single-stream
recycling, he believed Waste Management must pick up the cost for a serious educational
campaign -- well beyond their standard releases - and that the City should monitor their efforts.
Mayor Udine commented that he liked the idea of Commission recognition of residents who go
above and beyond in recycling efforts. He believed this "green" plan would be very positive for
the City and that it should be promoted to the fullest extent.
