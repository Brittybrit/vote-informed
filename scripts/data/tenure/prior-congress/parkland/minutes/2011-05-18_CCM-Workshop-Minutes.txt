1. DISCUSSION: CLEANUP & MAINTENANCE OF THE

PINETREE NORTH DRAINAGE DITCH

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young introduced Jim Berkman, Public Works Director, who
gave a status update of the project. Mr. Berkman explained that in the 1980s, the Broward
County Board of Health required that a drainage system be put in place to take care of flooding
issues. The City of Parkland passed an Ordinance that swales be dedicated to comply. The
Ordinance required the adjacent homeowners to maintain the ditches and the swales behind and
between their homes. He explained that due to the overgrowth of vegetation, a big rain event
would cause flooding in the area.
Commissioner Smith asked if the City owns the property in the swales. Mr. Berkman replied
the homeowner owns the property, but the easement is there for the purpose of drainage.
Commissioner Weissman asked City Attorney Maurodis to review Section 16.55 of the City
Code of Ordinance regarding water bodies, its maintenance and the responsibility of
maintenance of the drainage ditches.
City Attorney Maurodis stated the clear intent of the Ordinance places the responsibility on the
property owner.
Vice Mayor Rosenof asked who benefits from the maintenance of the ditches.
Jim Berkman replied all the properties in Pine Tree North (approximately 400 residents)
benefit from the drainage ditch.
Commissioner Moskowitz asked for clarification as to who has been maintaining the easement
over the past few years.
Mr. Berkman stated that the Pine Tree Water Control District did at one time maintain the
easement. He stated it has not been maintained by the City for the last 5-1/2 years.
Vice Mayor Rosenof gave a historical update on the maintenance of the ditches in Pine Tree
Estates. He spoke about conformance of swales and that the same standards are being applied
by Code Enforcement to the Pine Tree ditches. He stated it is not possible for the homeowners
to maintain the ditches using a lawn mower.
Commissioner Moskowitz said everyone is tightening their budgets and looking at projects for
cost savings. The City Commission talked about assigning the easement to the Pine Tree
Water Control District. Commissioner Moskowitz expressed that he would like the code cases
dropped against the residents until a determination is made by the City Commission with the
understanding that the cases can be re-filed if it is determined that the owners must maintain
the ditches. Residents will be given a timeframe to comply.

City Manager Gardner- Young expressed concern for the potential of flooding if the ditches are
not cleared.

Commissioner Weissman commented that taking on the additional responsibilities of
maintenance of the drainage ditches was not a part of the legislative language when Pine Tree
was created.

Mayor Udine asked what would be the cost to maintain the area in question.

Mr. Berkman replied the area in question is approximately a % - 1 mile of ditch with a one-time
clean-up fee of approximately $8,000 and a quarterly maintenance fee of $2,000-$2,500.

Vice Mayor Rosenof discussed the idea of spending money on an engineering study to see how
deep the drainage ditch has to be. Some residents have expressed that they would like to fill it
in and utilize it.

Mayor Udine commented that he preferred to spend the $8,000 initially for the cleanup rather
than do an engineering study.

Commissioner Smith inquired of the City Attomey if the City becomes responsible if it
performs the maintenance this one time. The consensus was that the City will take care of it
initially to reduce a health, life, and safety concern.

Vice Mayor Rosenof said he would like a permanent solution to be found.

Ms. Annette Friedman, a North Pine Tree resident said the residents merely want a solution.
They feel it is a safety issue for the residents to maintain the drainage ditch themselves. If the
Broward County Health Department says there is no need for the swale or ditch any longer,
then the residents would like to fill it in. She further expressed concern why only 19 residents
would be charged if all 400 residents benefit from the ditch.

Robert Palieri, NW 63% Way, stated that after reviewing Municode, he did not find anything
that says the residents are responsible for maintenance of the drainage ditch and expressed that
the ditch only needed to be cut twice a year.
Attorney Maurodis clarified the way special assessment works. If the ditches provide drainage
for the entire area, then the special benefit would accrue to all the homes in the area.
Staff will talk to the Health Department and the Pine Tree Water Control District to come up
with a long term solution.

3. DISCUSSION: SPORTS FEE CHARGES
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young turned it over to Scott Davidoff, Parks and Recreation
Director for a brief discussion of sports fee charges. He stated currently the City does not
receive any fees from any of the sports leagues. He spoke about city run programs vs.
programs run by non-profit organizations or separate entities. Staff made a recommendation to
charge a $20 per participant fee per sport to be used for field maintenance.
Mayor Udine was hesitant to charge but wants to wait until budget time.
Vice Mayor Rosenof asked if the Parks Board had discussed the sports fee charges. Mr.
Davidoff replied in the affirmative that it was the Parks Board who established the fee to
charge each participant.
