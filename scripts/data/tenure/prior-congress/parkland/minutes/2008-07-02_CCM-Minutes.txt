III PUBLIC INPUT
For those residents present to ask concerning the oil spill in the Ranches, Mayor Udine noted that
Public Works Director Jim Berkman would give an update prior to approval of the agenda.
Darlene Squillace, of 7250 N.W. 84 Avenue in the Ranches, spoke regarding the oil spill. While
she thanked the City for their efforts, tar balls continue to rise from the bottom, and she was
concerned with the length of time taken to clean up. She believed the canals should be dug out
with a backhc a to remove the tar at the bottom. She attended the meeting of June 18 and had
been upset by some of the comments made. An EPA employee had taken test samples from her
water system and the canal at Trotter's Lane on Monday, June 16 and she had been told that the
test results would take at least two weeks. She found it odd that the City could have had test
results in two days. Ms. Squillace noted that the repercussions of the contamination, if any, were
unknown. She felt the origin of the tar was important; as her neighbor had witnessed run -off
gullies being dug, she believed the road crew should be held responsible for the clean-up.
Jeff Schwartz of the Ranches recalled that, at the last meeting, Vice Mayor Moskowitz had
offered the Historical Society building mementos from Old City Hall. Since the Society was
preparing a time capsule as a gift to the City, he asked permission to remove the brass plaque
from the building so that it might be included.
Mayor Udine reviewed that he, the City Manager, and Mayor Bockhold had discussed the closure
of Old City Hall. He had no objection to the Society working with the City Manager for removal
of the plaque, and he asked whether there were Commission objections. Commissioner Weissman
suggested that the plaque should be mounted on the new building as a remembrance.
Mayor Udine asked whether a motion regarding the plaque was necessary, and City Attorney
Andrew Maurodis indicated that would not be necessary.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that the Society may want other things from Old City Hall,
such as the original dais, and that they should work through the City Manager.
There was Commission agreement that the plaque could be removed for placement into the time
capsule.
Former Mayor Harold Bockhold spoke regarding the energy incentives program; he felt that
people should voluntarily conserve and not be rewarded for doing so. As to the prohibition of
plastic bags, while it may be a very good idea, he believed the bags were of national concern and
that a local ordinance would be improper. He noted, however, that the bags were recyclable;
people should be urged to separate and recycle them through the grocery stores.
There were no further comments or questions, and Mayor Udine closed to the public.

At the Mayor's request, Public Works Director Jim Berkman gave an update on the oil spill in the
Ranches. Since the last meeting, City staff had been working with the State's contractor. On this
date, staff began creating a sandbag dam at each side of the spill area; the water would be pumped
out, and the State's contractor would then backhoe the empty canal area. It was expected to take
two weeks to complete.
Mayor Udine commented that the project would be completed during the Commission's summer
hiatus, and he promised that an update would be given to residents via e-mail.
From the audience, Mr. Schwartz asked for further details regarding the dam location, and Mr.
Berkman stated that the dam would be just outside the current location of the buoys.
Mayor Udine suggested that the residents meet with Mr. Berkman for their specific questions.

which provided language to further clarify the intent and applications. Sections 4 and 5 were

City Attorney Andrew Maurodis commented that he was in agreement with Section 4; however,

Mayor Udine recalled that Commissioner Weissman had brought up the issue of signage and that
the Commission had agreed not to "tackle" that in this Ordinance. The zoning -in- progress had

Vice Mayor Moskowitz recalled Commission discussion, which he believed recognized that it was
less feasible to accomplish at this moment in time rather than later when the technology may be
more advanced, cheaper, and readily available. He noted that the Ordinance specifically
addressed building permits subsequent to January 1, 2009, and he believed this too soon and

Parkland Commons, noting that the center had not been built to accommodate this technology and
that it would be unfair to hold them to these standards. The development had, however, that
recognized what the City was trying to accomplish and had LED signage under consideration.
