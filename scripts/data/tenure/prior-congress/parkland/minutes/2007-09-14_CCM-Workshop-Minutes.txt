1. DISCUSSION: REDISTRICTING
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young explained that, under the recently-adopted Charter
amendments, the. City was required to reevaluate Commission districts every three years by an
outside expert. She introduced redistricting consultant Maureen Tartaglione of the University of

Florida to discuss their redistricting documents and to answer Commission questions concerning
any potential alternatives they might wish to consider.
Ms. Tartaglione presented maps of the redistricting alternatives. She indicated that her partner,
Scott Richard, would be available by phone to address demographic information. She offered
that Mr. Richard was affiliated with UF's Bureau of Business and Economic research.
Ms. Tartaglione made a brief PowerPoint presentation, which will be attached to the Minutes.
She explained the necessity for redistricting and the standards and methodologies used in the
preparation of their proposals for the City's 23,329 residents, which had been divided into four
relatively equal districts. She also spoke regarding the formulation of the two proposals and their
respective benefits or advantages.
At the Mayor's questions of procedure, Ms. Gardner-Young indicated .that two public hearings
would be held on the redistricting. The workshop presentation was to offer the consultants' ideas
and receive Commission feedback. Mr. Maurodis recommended, however, that the Commission
allow public input.
Commissioner Weissman commented on the two proposed plans, indicating that he could accept
either one. He believed, however, that Plan 2 had an advantage in that the Terramar district
would then have two commissioners representing that area.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz questioned that Plan 2 divided the south Pine Tree in half. He asked if
all of Pine Tree -both north and south -- might be in one district, and he made some suggestions
as to other alternatives. He noted that growth would occur only in District 1 and District 2.
Ms. Tartaglione indicated that the Vice Mayor's questions could be answered .immediately by
Mr. Richards. She admitted that they had been aware of the neighborhood issues and their split
was a valid point; they had already examined some variations.
Commissioner Smith noted that his district would remain much the same, losing only a portion
of Heron Bay North. He hoped that some of the ideas of the Vice Mayor and Commissioner
Weissman could be implemented.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz envisioned the future movement westward of the districts, and he
believed that the City should plan for the future. He asked that some alternatives to Plan 1 and
Plan 2 be offered -such as trying to keep all of Pinetree in one district and trying to keep just
south Pinetree in one district..
Mayor Udine commented that he agreed with much of what was said. and would like to see some
alternatives. His issue was that somehow the Commission was "stacked" with the western part
of the City. While Plan 1 would continue that perception, Plan 2 would assure two
commissioners east of University and two commissioners west of University. He believed it
would help remove the perceived bias in the public.

As to Pinetree, Mayor Udine appreciated the position of the Vice Mayor, but felt that it could
also be of benefit to split Pinetree because the area could then potentially have more than one
representative on the Commission. Mayor Udine noted that the City was different in that some
districts were wholly comprised areas with homeowners' associations, which handled the
majority of their issues; other districts had areas without associations, such as Pinetree and the
Ranches, which theoretically required that issues be brought to the Commission. While he was
leaning toward that, he believed it presented an interesting problem since all constituent issues
would involve that Commissioner.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that both plans had either the Ranches or Pinetree in three
districts. He suggested that, with all of Pinetree placed within one district, there would then be
two districts.
Mayor Udine acknowledged both sides of the issue. He stated that he could accept either plan,
understanding that Plan 1 mirrors what currently exists and that Plan 2 was a little more radical.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz believed that Plan 2 would be the future. He suggested that his district
in four to eight years would no longer encompass CypressHead because all of the districts would
shift west. Ms. Tartaglione commented that the City would have the best "picture" in four years
with the incorporation of 2010 census data.
Mayor Udine opened to the public.
Natalie Bigio of Pinetree Estates believed that the Commission remarks reflected the thinking of
single member districts. She noted, however, that Commission elections were City-wide and,
theoretically, all Commissioners have been and should be called; all should deal with the issues.
Mayor Udine noted that Ms. Bigio's comments were correct: that the Commissioners were
elected at-large. He stated, however, that the Commission had always tried to defer to the
Commission member in whose district the issue was located.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz explained that, in the past, Pinetree issues had involved both himself and
former Commissioner McGilvray. He believed that areas should not be split.
Pinetree resident Dave Rosenof agreed with the Vice Mayor about keeping south Pinetree in one
district. He believed Pinetree had unique issues that he would like 'to see represented by a
commissioner who also resided there. He favored the implementation of Plan 1 or some
modification of that -keeping south Pinetree together at a minimum.
Former Mayor Robert Marks, Cypresshead, explained that, historically, the City had wanted to
keep the commissioners as the experts in their districts. Pinetree had uniqueness -- with wells,
septic tanks, soundwalls, and other things, and it was believed important to have a person who
was intimately involved and knowledgeable. He also favored keeping Pinetree together.
There were no further comments or questions, and Mayor Udine closed to the public.

2. DISCUSSION: WASTE MANAGEMENT CONTRACT
Public Services Director Jim Berkman indicated that the franchise agreement with Waste
Management would conclude at 12:01 a.m. on April 1, 2008. Staff had been in discussion with
Waste Management, exploring different possibilities and options which had been outlined in the
staff memo of the agenda packet. An option was also to offer it for bid in the competitive
market. He offered further explanation of the options, which included putting the fees back on
the tax roll or contracting for a new roll-out cart program.
Commissioner Weissman asked the City Attorney's opinion of the City's "safest ground" since
the Waste Management contract would come to an end and no extensions were possible.
Acknowledging that he had discussed this issue with Commissioner Weissman, City Attorney
Andrew Maurodis indicated that the legal issue was to bid or not to bid. In the past, during
modifications of the contract with Waste Management, the interpretation had been that the
franchise was not necessarily a purchase of service in the traditional way under the City's Code.
With regard to his comfort level and the law, he believed the bidding process would be the safer
route.
Mayor Udine suggested that, before any presentation, a decision should be made whether or not
to bid the contract. His thoughts were that he had no problem bidding it out, but he wanted to
make sure that the City's specifications were precise. He wanted to make certain that every
company would bid on the exact same thing.
Mr. Maurodis agreed that formulating the exact specifications would be difficult and that it must
have input from people knowledgeable with this service.

Ms. Gardner-Young offered that she and Mr. Berkman had discussed this, and she noted that Mr.

different interpretations, and she hoped for the Commission's guidance with the services. At

Commissioner Weissman recalled that, when the contract had been renewed with Waste
Management in 1996, they had rolled back the rates.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz questioned whether there had been interest shown from other companies,
and Mr. Berkman responded in the affirmative. Commissioner Weissman asked whether any of
those other companies were now present, and there was an affirmative response.
Mayor Udine suggested there would be at least three bidders.

that the City would go out for bid,'he felt that there was no regard for their past service, their
presence, what they had offered, who they were, and what they had done. He felt it a casual
move, despite the long history between his firm and the City, and their efforts in putting the
package together.
Mayor Udine acknowledged Mr. Spadaccia's comments and felt his points should be taken into
account. He believed the City had received quality service from Waste Management, but the

work on the recycling program but, from a financial standpoint, he felt that the City must explore
whether the City's residents were getting the "best bang for the buck." He did not believe Waste
Management should be discouraged; 'the City wanted to find out about the competition in the
marketplace. It did not automatically mean that the City would go in a different direction. He
again stated that he would like to see the Waste Management presentation in order to educate the
Commission.
John Albert, representing Waste Management, stated that they did not have a problem educating

3. DISCUSSION: PINE TRAILS PARK PHASE IV
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young indicated that one phase remained in Pine Trails Park.
Phase I was completed; Phase II, consisting of the Community Center and Amphitheater was
nearing completion; and the design and construction of three additional softball fields in Phase III
had been authorized. Phase IV included items determined at the time of the project's inception
five years ago, and staff wanted the Commission's input whether the original designs should
stand or be changed.
Leisure Services Director Scott Davidoff confirmed that Pine Trails Park Phase IV had been in
development for some tune. Using a PowerPoint presentation, he reviewed the overall phases,
the master plan, and the elements of Phase IV.
Mr. Davidoff spoke regarding growth in league participants. He indicated the greatest need for
field space was additional multi-purpose fields to accommodate football, soccer, and Lacrosse
and, secondly, for Little League. He noted that baseball participation had declined by 20 percent
over the past three years, which he believed was primarily attributable to the growth of the
Lacrosse program in the City. The only "big boy" field in the City was now located at Terramar
Park; there would be room in Phase IV to include one "big boy" and two Little League fields.
Mr. Davidoff stated that the other major area in Phase IV was an accommodation of tennis, with
plans for eight courts and a pro shop. There were now approximately 30 adults and 25 youths of
"hard-core" players playing three or more times per week. A contingent of female players
continued to request clay courts at Terramar Park, and it was felt by them that four additional
clay courts could accommodate their needs. The tennis pros felt that four additional courts
would provide them with adequate teaching space as well as for public play; the USTA also
believed that four courts would be more than adequate for tournaments. He explained a proposal
to move basketball courts from Terramar to Pine Trails Park and other renovations needed at
Terramar Parlc.
Based on the original plan, Mr. Davidoff indicated. that approximately $5.4 million would be
required.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz stated that Mr. Davidoffs recommendation to consolidate tennis and
basketball made sense, but he questioned the placement of baseball fields. He commented that
