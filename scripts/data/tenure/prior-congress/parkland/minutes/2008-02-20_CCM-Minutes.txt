3. RESOLUTION NO. 2008-18: GIRL SCOUT CAMP TELOGIA
SITE PLAN AMENDMENT
CAB APPROVAL
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, FOR SITE PLAN
AMENDMENT AND COMMUNITY APPEARANCE
BOARD APPROVAL FOR THE GIRL SCOUTS CAMP,
CAMP TELOGIA, LOCATED AT 6501 HOLMBERG
ROAD, CASE NUMBERS 13- SPA -07 AND 18- CAB -07.
(quasi-judicial)
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Resolution No. 2008 -18.
Planning Director Roberta Moore explained the request for the revitalization of the Girl Scouts'
Camp Telogia.
For the record, Mayor Udine noted that Dodie Keith, of Keith Associates and representing the
Girl Scouts, had waived the quasi-judicial.
After describing the Camp's location, Ms. Moore reviewed the additions to the site, as well as
operational changes and landscaping. She displayed a colored rendering and color board. She
noted that the Holmberg Road right -of -way had been provided as part of the project. Staff
recommended approval of the Site Plan Amendment and Community Appearance Board
applications. Ms. Moore offered that a revised Resolution had been placed on the dais which
included one additional condition of approval regarding the electronic entry gate.

Ms. Moore noted that staff had requested tabling of the application at the December 13 Planning
and Zoning Board meeting due to a number of issues, which she explained.
Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer spoke regarding irrigation in the landscape areas
of native plantings, Code requirements, and the existing buffer in conjunction with the Holmberg
Road Landscape Theme. He stated that some modifications in design would have to take place if
a berm were to be installed in lieu of existing vegetation.
At the January 10 meeting of the Planning and Zoning Board, Ms. Moore stated that the Board
had recommended approval of the applications based on staff recommendations; they requested
that the Commission work with the Girl Scouts to prioritize the requirements within their budget
constraints. She asked that the staff report and plans become part of the public record.
Representing the Girl Scouts, Ms. Keith believed that Ms. Moore's explanation had been very
clear. The Girl Scouts had been proud to be part of the Parkland community; the facility would
remain a camp and the improvements would help bring the facility up to Code. They hoped to
continue their rustic ambiance and keep their natural vegetation; a berm would be a definite
problem in that it would be a financial hardship and change the look of their facility.
Commissioner Weissman offered that he and Ms. Keith had spent some time discussing this issue.
He understood that the project would take considerably longer than the 18 months of their
original permit and could possibly become a four- to five -year project at a minimum. He stated
that he liked the overall plan. Relating to Mr. Archer's comments, he recommended that
irrigation not be required, and he indicated that he did not have a problem with the already
permitted PVC fence, which would be buffered from the view of Holmberg Road. As to the
stucco wall, the Commissioner suggested this aspect be tabled until something a little more
decorative could be achieved. As to the berm or the existing buffer, he acknowledged that the
Girl Scouts stand for environmental issues, and he would like to see the invasives removed during
the time of the overall project without requiring the berm. He believed it would give ample time
for an opaque buffer to grow before the invasives were taken down.
Commissioner Rosenof indicated that he had been present at both Planning and Zoning Board
meetings. He believed staff had done a great job "boiling it down" to the four contentious issues.
While he agreed with Commissioner Weissman's remarks, his only issue would be with the
invasives, which he believed should be removed and for which he would "swap" the berm.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked that the fence visibility from the road be significantly reduced. He
favored tabling the issue of the stone wall; he understood the request had come from a neighbor,
and he suggested that the neighbor should help. He agreed that the berm could be very expensive,
and he did not believe it should be imposed on the Girl Scouts. As to the invasives, while he
deferred to Commissioners Weissman and Rosenof he expressed concern that a policy would be
set by requiring the removal of the opaque buffer provided by the Brazilian pepper. He believed
that guidelines for Holmberg Road should first be discussed and firmly established. The Vice
Mayor also suggested that this might be the time to consider adding some turn lanes while the $3
million Holmberg Road improvement project was underway.

Mayor Udine commented that the Girl Scouts would gladly accept donations from the public
toward their multi -year project. He agreed with the comments about the stone wall and that it
should be tabled; irrigation would be far too expensive, and he believed it unnecessary. Since the
fence met Code requirements and had not been a problem, he felt it should be allowed to remain.
As to the buffer, the Mayor stated that he would favor removal of the invasives but still provide
an opaque buffer; he would defer to Commissioners Weissman and Rosenof. He believed another
important consideration was that of the safety of the Girl Scouts sleeping there at night so that
people could not see into the camp from the outside.
Mayor Udine noted that no neighbors of the Girl Scout camp were present to speak. He felt it
indicative that the Girl Scouts had been and wanted to be good neighbors.
Mayor Udine summarized that the Commission appeared to be in agreement that the stone wall
portion should be tabled to a later time and that items 2 and 3 were not necessary.
With comments about the approval made by Ms. Lazowick and Mr. Maurodis, Mayor Udine
reviewed that the application should be approved with the stone wall, but with the ability to give
the Girl Scouts relief in the event it could not be worked out.
Addressing some of the issues, Ms. Lazowick stated that, with respect to the white fencing and
landscaping, the fencing on the side street was set back 10 feet from the right -of -way line. One of
the first actions would be to plant landscaping in front of the fencing to soften its look as well as
to hide the camp. The right -of -way along Holmberg Road as well as the side street had been
dedicated. Ms. Lazowick noted they were also assisting with the placement of fire hydrants along
Holmberg Road. She described the project as a grassroots effort, and they would welcome
donations and the involvement of the community.
Commissioner Weissman asked that Mr. Archer work with the Girl Scouts to create an opaque
buffer with cost cognizant use of plant material needing minimum care. He noted that the Girl
Scouts would have approximately five years for the material to grow. Mr. Archer indicated that
the plant pallet chosen was comprised of native material, which would work nicely.
Mr. Maurodis suggested that the Resolution include a five -year phase out of the Brazilian pepper,
with the right of the Girl Scouts to petition for a longer period. At Ms. Lazowick's question, he
confirmed this would be without the berm requirement.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz reiterated his belief that the Commission should workshop Holmberg
Road landscape guidelines so that it would flow from one property to the next. He believed that
"opaque" should also be defined.
Mayor Udine opened to the public. There were no comments or questions, and the Mayor closed
to the public.

4. RESOLUTION NO. 2008-20: PINE TRAILS PARK CELL TOWER
LEASE
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, TO AUTHORIZE THE
APPROPRIATE CITY OFFICIALS TO EXECUTE THE
ATTACHED LEASE AGREEMENT FOR
INSTALLATION OF A STEALTH CELLULAR TOWER
AND ASSOCIATED EQUIPMENT CABINETS AT PINE
TRAILS PARK BY FLORIDA TOWERS LLC;
PROVIDING FOR CONFLICTS; PROVIDING FOR
SEVERABILITY; AND PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE
DATE.
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Resolution No. 2008 -20. She reviewed the
events which had led to the cell tower proposal, and she noted that it was the same lease
agreement as previously approved for the cell tower at Old City Hall, with only minor changes.
John Ruth, representing Florida Towers, LLC, was present. She stated that a flagpole had been
proposed; a spare flag, paid for by the petitioner, would be kept on hand for replacement by staff.
Replacement flags would be purchased by the petitioner. She noted that flag lighting would be
appropriate. Two companies AT &T and Verizon were proposed to collocate on the tower,
and staff recommended approval.

<<PG>>

Vice Mayor Moskowitz indicated that, while pleased that Florida Towers had been selected and
that the tower would be moving forward, he had not wanted another flagpole, and he questioned
its height of 135 feet. Mr. Ruth stated that the pole would allow up to five carriers, which would
be at 10 -foot intervals; even the carrier in the lowest position could operate. He noted that both
carriers had requested elevations of 120 feet.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz noted that the cell towers around town appeared to be growing shorter,
and he would rather keep the tower shorter so that it might better blend in. He noted huge light
poles in the park, and he asked if it might be possible to incorporate the tower with those. Mr.
Ruth stated that would be possible, using more than one light pole one carrier with flush
mounted antennas on each pole. Mr. Ruth stated his understanding that the actual design was not
being approved at this meeting, but indicated they were receptive to different proposals.
Mayor Udine commented about a lack of lights at the amphitheater, and he asked if it would be
possible to then have two light poles for that area.
Ms. Gardner -Young stated that the intention was that the future T -ball field lights would help
light the great lawn, and she had already spoken with Mr. Davidoff about having lights near the
restrooms. She agreed that the cell towers could be incorporated into the lighting.
Ms. Gardner -Young suggested that the lease agreement could be amended to require the proposal
of the exact tower method.
Mayor Udine suggested that the agreement could be approved with the understanding that it must
come back to the Commission for site plan and Community Appearance Board approval. He
offered that paragraph 6 would be removed, and Mr. Maurodis noted that the language could read
that the facility would be approved by the Commission.
Commissioner Weissman asked regarding the maximum number of carriers per light pole, and Mr.
Ruth indicated that two could possibly collocate. Mr. Ruth stated that he would like to have the
ability to erect the light poles based upon carrier readiness. The Commissioner noted that one
pole could be erected immediately and that two other locations should be scouted.
Mayor Udine noted that anything would have to come back to the Commission for the ultimate
approval of the design. He did not believe that the tower should be limited to light poles.
Commissioner Weissman noted that the base revenue to the City would be $45,000 plus the four
percent, and he questioned the escalator when $90,000 in revenue was exceeded. Mr. Ruth
responded that typically their escalators were three percent, but they could try for four. Ms.
Gardner -Young suggested that the City could choose the process to follow, and Mr. Ruth could
provide the collocate lease agreements so that the City would know exact amounts each year.
MOTION made by Vice Mayor Moskowitz to approve Resolution No. 2008 -20, Pine Trails Park
Cell Tower Lease, as amended. Seconded by Commissioner Weissman.

ADDED AGENDA ITEM:
LOCAL GOVERNMENT INVESTMENT POOL
Commissioner Weissman noted that the Commission had received a memorandum about Holland
Knight and Pasco County regarding the investment pool, and he had asked the City Manager to
investigate further. The understanding was that it was not intended as a class action filing; they
were looking for participants in the lawsuit to protect both the assets and the integrity of the
investment. The City has in the vicinity of $11 or $12 million invested there. He believed the
Commission should examine this issue and decide its position whether to participate or not
with the realization that the City may have to seek its own representation in the future.
Mayor Udine noted that this issue had been mentioned at the League of Cities, and competing
investment pools were under discussion. The funds have been frozen, and he agreed with
Commissioner Weissman that it should be considered. He would like the Finance Director to
participate in the discussion, and he asked that the item be agendized for the March 5 meeting.
Commissioner Weissman suggested that the City Manager find out the intentions of Holland
Knight and what their costs might be. He also asked that City Attorney Andrew Maurodis
analyze the most appropriate legal representation.
