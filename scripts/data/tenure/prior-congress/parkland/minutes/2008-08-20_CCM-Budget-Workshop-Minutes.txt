Mayor Udine asked that the record reflect no members of the public were present; the only 
people in attendance were City staff and BSO and CSFD agency employees. 
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young commented that this was the first workshop for the 
2008/2009 Budget, a copy of which was provided at the time of the millage determination. She 
noted that there had been some subsequent changes. 
Finance & Administrative Services Director Barbara Hastings began the budget presentation. 
Changes in the budget would be brought to the Commission’s attention and explained. A list of 
unfunded capital projects was provided, and she asked that their priority be considered. 
Ms. Hastings indicated that gross taxable property values had increased by .2 percent as new 
construction value had increased $215 million. The loss in taxable value due to Amendment 1 
was $167 million. She explained the current rollback rate of 3.6070, and staff had proposed a 
decrease in the operating millage to 3.4083 from rollback. The impact of Amendment 1 was a 
$570,000 loss of revenue, while new construction added $705,000 in revenue. 
Ms. Hastings compared the taxes paid last year versus the coming year of homesteaded 
properties valued at $350,000 and $500,000. 
Mayor Udine suggested that staff continue with the presentation and that Commission questions 
be held until the end. 
Budget and Strategic Planning Manager Nancy Morando presented budget highlights. The Fire 
Rescue Assessment would remain at $151, generating $1.23 million in revenue. The fire 
services contract had increased by $305,200, or eight percent; she noted that a 10 percent 
increase had been anticipated. The police services contract had also increased by $342,000 
versus the anticipated $238,200. The changes in this area were attributed to the addition of an 
SRD at a cost of $137,860; as the new school would be open only two months in next year’s 
budget, the remaining $114,000 could be used by Chief O’Connell for potential overtime. Ms. 
Morando stated that: energy costs had increased $180,000; property, general liability, and 
worker’s compensation insurance decreased by $337,120, or 35 percent; and the staffing level 
was decreased by eight full-time employees and two part-time positions. Additionally, cost-of- 
living for general employees was proposed at three percent, which had originally been set at two 
percent. Health insurance costs increased by 16 percent and dental insurance by 14 percent. 
Pay-for-performance would remain at zero to three percent, and the pension would remain the 
same with an additional four percent matching for exempt employees. 
Using a pie chart, Ms. Morando noted that 65 percent of the City’s revenues were property taxes, 
indicating the City counted more and more on property taxes to fund the City. There had been a 
$1.1 million loss in building permits and license revenue. Greatest expenditures (at 50 percent) 
were for police and fire services. Development Services was currently at nine percent versus at 
13 percent last year.

Ms. Hastings continued that a revenue shortage of $597,800 had been anticipated when the 
budget presentation was made in July; it now stood at $285,250. Revenues were down $1.1 
million from last year, and expenditures were down $700,000. She reviewed the five-year 
forecast and the assumptions made. Additionally, Ms. Hastings explained the expense 
assumptions and the projected fund balance. While she hoped that $3.8 million would remain in 
reserve, the projection was that the City could be “in the hole” by $9.6 million by 2014. 
Ms. Hastings reviewed the changes made with respect to department and non-departmental 
budgets, including unfunded capital projects. The total of funded projects for the years 2010 
through 2013 would be $2.8 million. 
Ms. Hastings addressed the Commission questions proffered at the last meeting. She explained 
that, as the revenue for licenses and permits stood at seven percent and the expenditures for 
Development Services stood at nine percent, it was costing the City more to run Development 
Services than it generated in fees. She provided a further detailed analysis. 
As to portability, Ms. Hastings explained that Amendment 1 was approved in January and, 
normally, all exemption applications must be made by March; it had been extended to May this 
year. For the next fiscal year, the cut-off will return to March; however, applications for 
homestead portability may be filed again next year retroactive to January 2007. 
Regarding the School Resource Deputies versus the Community Services Aides, Ms. Hastings 
stated that three SRDs had been included from 2009 to 2014. She noted that the School Board 
would increase their funding up to 50 percent, which would be reached in 2012. She reviewed 
the total costs of three elementary SRDs, after the School Board reimbursement, versus having 
three CSAs, for whom the School Board had confirmed they would not reimburse. She indicated 
there would be some savings. 
As to the four-day work week, Ms. Hastings offered that cities were seriously considering this 
option, with its positive environmental impacts. There would be longer operating hours during 
the days the City was open, yet there would be a decrease in the City’s electricity bill. Expected 
results would include an increase in employee productivity through improved morale, less 
absenteeism, and a reduction in an employee’s personal expenses. She noted that Margate would 
enact the four-day work week in the coming fiscal year and that other cities were considering it. 
Ms. Gardner-Young updated the Commission on the Building Department operations. Since the 
July meeting discussions, she had met with numerous experts in the field. After listening to the 
various opinions, it was her belief that the City did not need to outsource the department, but

Building Department involvement in City activities and improvements in the permit and 
inspection processes. Ms. Gardner-Young anticipated providing a status report in January. 
Mayor Udine opened the meeting to Commission comments. 
Commissioner Rosenof asked regarding differing widths of Pinetree streets and the inclusion of 
legal fees for additional easements, which Ms. Hastings confirmed was included. The 
Commissioner recalled that past discussions had included water and sewer, including lines 
through rear swales. Ms. Hastings stated that water and sewer were not included. Public Works 
Director Jim Berkman indicated that their certified cost estimate had included water and sewer; it 
was not, however, in the recommendation. He stated that lines through the rear drainage 
easements had not been investigated. Commissioner Rosenof suggested that, before going 
further with the process, a town hall meeting should be held to get the input of Pinetree residents. 
Mayor Udine noted that funds were budgeted for the study and preliminaries. However, the $9+ 
million was unfunded and somewhere in the future. 
Regarding the Building Department and being “revenue neutral,” Commissioner Rosenof 
questioned the inclusion of Environmental Services so that it may be evaluated yearly. Ms. 
Hastings explained that the entire division of Environmental Services was included. The 
Commissioner suggested that, in the future, Environmental Services not be included. Ms. 
Gardner-Young offered that the landscaping reviews were done by Environmental Services and 
were part of the development process. She indicated that the costs could be separately totaled 
for information purposes. Ms. Hastings cautioned that the State Auditor General required that 
the division be rolled into Development Services. 
Addressing the SRDs, Commissioner Rosenof asked regarding a guarantee from the School 
Board that they would pay the increased reimbursement. Ms. Gardner-Young responded in the 
negative; only the $12,000 reimbursement was “guaranteed.” While the commitment had been 
made, the funding had not yet been supplied. 
Commissioner Rosenof also commented regarding the four-day work week, and he asked who 
might work the four days. Ms. Hastings confirmed that Public Works would work five regular 
days and the Library would still have their operating hours; it would primarily be City Hall 
staffers. The Commissioner offered his personal belief that operating four days did not serve the 
public. 
Commissioner Smith questioned the proposed Pinetree road widening. He believed that, should 
the residents truly want that and the City decided to go forward, it should be handled via a 
special assessment on the tax rolls per lot per year. He noted that it would increase the City’s 
collection rate. Ms. Hastings agreed that, without doing so, residents would be without incentive 
to pay. 
Commissioner Smith also commented that constituents wanted deputies assigned to each school, 
without being shared, and he believed the City needed to do that.

Mayor Udine offered that, this year, each school would have a deputy, and he believed that at 
least SAC, SAF, and the principals should be notified of that fact. He recalled workshop 
discussion that they would have to revisit the issue for the next school year, when the new 
elementary is completed. He suggested that the extra dollars in this year’s budget could be used 
for overtime or additional patrol officers. 
Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented regarding about the Building Department. He stated that he 
would let the economy make the decision for him: should the Building Department go into the 
red and cost the City money, he believed the City should no longer have a Building Department. 
He spoke regarding the number of permits pulled for last year compared to this year and what 
was anticipated for the future; the Building Department would become a significant expense. He 
would like the numbers reviewed in December; if the City should not be on par with this year, he 
believed the decision should then be made. 
Mayor Udine recalled that the City Manager had stated that a status report would be provided in 
January, and he suggested that the Vice Mayor’s request coincide with that discussion. The Vice 
Mayor agreed. 
Vice Mayor Moskowitz reiterated that one school resource deputy would be in each school this 
year. He noted that no patrol officers had been added in perhaps a decade, and the City had 
grown by at least 10,000 residents since then. While the City could not add a new patrol zone 
due to the cost, he had asked that at least one officer be added to improve police presence on the 
street. He believed the idea for utilizing this year’s extra dollars toward overtime a good one, 
but he also would like $50,000 less placed in reserve, and specifically earmarked for this use, so 
that payment of overtime could continue. 
With additional discussion, Ms. Hastings interpreted the Vice Mayor’s request to take a formal 
action to designate the funds. It would prevent their use for any other purpose. She offered that, 
close to the end of each budget year, a “clean-up budget amendment” was brought to the 
Commission. She suggested that the designation could be brought forward at that point. The 
Vice Mayor agreed. 
As to the four-day work week, Vice Mayor Moskowitz agreed with Commissioner Rosenof’s 
comments, but he asked the total amount of savings. At Ms. Hasting’s response that $12,000 in 
energy costs would be saved, the Vice Mayor stated that he did not feel that a savings of only 
$12,000 warranted the removal of one day’s service from the City’s residents. 
Mayor Udine commented that the $12,000 may not be a “real” number since the air conditioning 
would remain on. 
Ms. Gardner-Young noted that the four-day work week would allow employees to make their 
doctor or dental appointments on their Friday day off without any loss of workweek efficiency.

Regarding the Pinetree roads, Vice Mayor Moskowitz believed the City should decide upon a 
plan, and he suggested that the Commission should establish a non-binding, end-of-2008 goal 
toward that end. He noted that the infrastructure of the east side of the City had been “glossed 
over” for many years. This Commission had addressed Holmberg Road, had improved Trotter’s 
Lane and repaved Ranch Road and, in the next years, there would be more repaving in the 
Ranches. Since this Commission had tackled the infrastructure, he felt there should be a plan in 
place by the end of 2008 for Pinetree Roads as well. 
Mayor Udine commented that the Vice Mayor’s comments about the Pinetree roads were 
consistent with the City’s Strategic Plan. 
Commissioner Weissman recalled the discussion about portability at the last meeting, noting that 
he had asked for the comparison numbers from January 2007 through May 2008. While it was 
noted that 154 purchasers had portability, he asked how many did not have portability in order to 
analyze the trends from year-to-year, perhaps making it easier for the City to budget in the 
future. Ms. Hastings indicated that she would provide that information. 
With respect to the four-day work week, Commissioner Weissman stated that he had spoken 
with Coconut Creek, where it was working quite well, and he suggested that it might be 
reexamined in future years. He noted, however, that this proposal had been for four days, 
continuing a 40-hour work week. He believed that a change in hours to a 32-hour work week, 
with continuing employee benefits, should also be examined for savings – as opposed to cutting 
staff. 
Mayor Udine commented regarding the four-day work week. While he felt it a good concept, he 
did not believe the City had the employee base to realize significant savings. He would not be in 
favor of cutting employee hours. 
The Mayor thanked staff for their presentation and the assistance they had provided in the past 
month. 
Mayor Udine asked that the School Resource Deputy information be relayed to SAC and SAF 
immediately. He agreed that the City should outline a plan for Pinetree roads sometime in the 
Fall, including engaging the public. He noticed that three more Holmberg Road crossings from 
Pinetree had been included in the budget, and he questioned which streets they were. Mr. 
Berkman indicated this information would be provided. Mayor Udine agreed this was an 
important issue, especially with the Pinetree bus situation. 
Mayor Udine noted that the capital plan included $6+ million for the work underway this year, 
and he asked the accounting in next year’s budget. Ms. Hastings stated that the dollars would be 
carried over through budget amendment with the assumption that capital projects were not 
always completed at the fiscal year’s end. 
With respect to the Holmberg Road north sidewalk, Mayor Udine questioned the budget year. 
Ms. Hastings indicated that the project had been moved to the unfunded list for 2008/2009. The
