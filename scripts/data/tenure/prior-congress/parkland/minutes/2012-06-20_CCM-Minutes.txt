3 PRESENTATION: CITYWIDE TRAFFIC STUDY

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young explained that the Citywide Traffic Study was required by

the Florida Department of Transportation when staff brought forward the Comprehensive Plan

Amendments. She explained that FDOT was going to object to the Comp Plans unless the City

did a Citywide Traffic Study. She introduced Mr. James Sphinx, P.E. with Calvin Giordano and

Associates who presented the results of the traffic study.

Mr. Sphinx explained that FDOT reviewed the Comprehensive Plan and made several

recommendations which included revisions to the Comp Plan, Capital Improvement Elements,

and identified the need to achieve and maintain the adopted transportation level of service.

The boundaries included Sample Road on the South, Glades Road on the North, Florida

Turnpike on the East, and the Everglades on the West. After discussions with FDOT on behalf

of the City of Parkland, Calvin Giordano & Associates were able to negotiate a reduced study

limit of Wiles Road on the South, Loxahatchee Road on the North, State Road 7 on the East and

the Everglades on the West.

A review of the Southeast Regional Planning model was completed and an arterial link analysis

for the base year 2005 and future year 2035 daily link volume was conducted. Growth rates were
h reviewed and year to year traffic volume comparisons were analyzed to assure that the models

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
The purpose of this report is to document the results of a level of service analysis of the City of
Parkland’s major roadways and surrounding roadways to account for residential and commercial
development in "The Wedge" utilizing the daily link volumes forecasted within both the 2005
and 2035 Southeast Regional Planning Model (SERPM). This study's primary focus is to address
concerns raised by FDOT with "The Wedge" and if needed will identify transportation
improvements needed to support development of "The Wedge" area and mitigate for impacts to
the wider transportation network created by removal of the cross-county University Drive
connection.
House Bill 1315 approved the transfer of a 1,949 acre wedge shaped property located between
County Line Road and Loxahatchee Road from Palm Beach County to Broward County. In
addition, House Bill 1315 approved the annexation of two parcels located within “The Wedge” in
the City of Parkland at the same time. House Bill 1315 specifies that the Palm Beach County
land use plan and zoning designations in “The Wedge” shall continue until the applicable local
government change those designations. The majority of the parcels within “The Wedge” are
designated Rural Residential - 10 on the Palm Beach County Future Land Use Map, which allows
one (1) dwelling unit per 10 acres.
A Transportation Network Needs Assessment was completed in November 2007 and approved
by the Broward and Palm Beach Metropolitan Planning Organizations (MPOs) in January 2008
and specified a cross-county area utilized as a study area. The cross-county area covered by the
Transportation Network Needs Assessment was bounded by Sample Road on the south, Glades
Road on the north, Florida's Turnpike on the east, and the Everglades on the west.
As part of the FDOT review of the proposed II-IAR comprehensive plan amendment package for
the City of Parkland which consisted of the annual update to the Capital Improvements Element
(CIE), amendments to the comprehensive plan map series to reflect annexation of a portion of
the wedge area south of the Hillsboro Canal into the City, and amendments to the Community
Facilities Element; FDOT recommended that the City of Parkland perform a number of revisions
to the Comprehensive Plan, Capital Improvement Element and identify any other improvements
needed to achieve and maintain adopted transportation LOS standards.
After discussion with FDOT staff, the proposed study area defined by the Transportation
Network Needs Assessment and the cross-county area was agreed upon to be reduced for this
study. The study area to be utilized for this study is bounded by Wiles Road on the south,
Loxahatchee Road on the north, State Road 7 on the east, and the Everglades on the west.
The daily link volumes were obtained utilizing the FDOT/MPO approved 2005 and 2035 SERPM
model. Future traffic projections were developed for the study area using the 2005 and 2035
Southeast Regional Planning Model (SERPM).
In general the same roadway links that fail in 2005 and or 2009 are the same roadway links
that fail in 2035 which demonstrates that development of “The Wedge” does not have a
significant impact on the roadway network. Comparison of the actual 2009 daily volumes to the
2005 SERPM output volumes reveals that many of the links within the study area are both

It is anticipated that failures shown on Loxahatchee Road may not occur as indicated in the

Particular deficiencies of concern are those which show unsatisfactory levels of service for the
2009 counts and/or both the 2005 and 2035 model output volumes, such as SR 7, Holmberg

SR 7 has several studies on-going to address these concerns by the Palm Beach County MPO,
Broward County MPO and FDOT. These studies include transit-supportive land use planning and
urban design studies by both the Palm Beach and Broward County MPO’s and the SR 7 Project
Development and Environmental (PD&E) study which started in 2011 by FDOT.
Existing deficiencies along Holmberg Road are evident that will likely worsen with development
of “The Wedge”. Although model volumes appear to be over-forecasting volumes on segments
of Holmberg Road for both the 2005 and 2035 model outputs, existing (2009) traffic data
counts show segments of concern that may need to be addressed by the City of Parkland in the
future.
Wiles Road 2005 model volumes east of Rock Island Road appear to extremely over-estimate
AADT, hence the 2035 volumes are unrealistic. However, even considering the over-forecasting,
the existing four lane divided typical section will not be sufficient in the future. Therefore,
capacity improvements along Wiles Road east of University Drive should be investigated by
Broward County in coordination with the City of Coral Springs.
In conclusion, this study demonstrates that “The Wedge” does not have a significant impact on
the roadway network.
Next steps for the City of Parkland shall include:

e Continued coordination with FDOT on the SR 7 PD&E study

o Implement “developer related” roadway recommendations of The Master Plan for
“The Wedge”

e Coordination with both the Broward County MPO and the Palm Beach County MPO
regarding development within “The Wedge” and cross-county impacts.

e Create a concurrency management system which can be used to monitor
development in “The Wedge” and trigger any recommended improvements within
the City of Parkland to alleviate congestion.

1.1 Project Description
House Bill 1315 approved the transfer of a 1,949 acre wedge shaped property located
between County Line Road and Loxahatchee Road from Palm Beach County to Broward
County. In addition, House Bill 1315 approved the annexation of two parcels located
within “The Wedge” in the City of Parkland at the same time. House Bill 1315 specifies

District (NSID), whom will be providing services to these future residents. Therefore, a
need was identified to develop a comprehensive Master Plan for “The Wedge” property
and adjacent vacant lands that will guide all future development to assure consistency
and compatibility with the existing community.

2. Level of Service Analysis
Analysis of existing traffic conditions was performed for the daily conditions on the
roadway segments within the study area. The Florida Department of Transportation
(FDOT) Generalized Level of Service (LOS) Tables were used to identify the vehicular
capacity on the roadway segments. The levels of service thresholds used for the
analysis are based on the “Generalized” tables for Urbanized Areas within the FDOT
Level of Service Handbook for the Broward County adopted threshold, which is Level of
Service "D”. Generalized level of service tables included in Appendix C.
The Level of Service calculation analyzes the geometry of the road, peak hour traffic
volumes and environmental conditions, including the lane and shoulder widths, amount
of available passing, and the mix of vehicle types in order to calculate the performance
rating. Six (6) LOS are defined for each type of facility that have HCM analysis
procedures available. Letters designate each level, from A to F, with LOS A representing
the best operating conditions and LOS F the worst. Each level of service represents a
range of operating conditions and the driver's perception of those conditions. Safety is
not included in the measures that establish service levels.
The number of signalized intersections per roadway facility is a factor used in FDOT
Generalized Tables analyses. For the LOS Report, the number of signalized intersections
is determined by averaging the number of intersections (both signalized and ones
requiring the through movement to stop) in the peak directions, not counting the
starting one, with the number of intersections, not counting the starting one, in the off-
peak direction. When using the FDOT Generalized Tables, deviation from the input value
assumptions for: traffic characteristics, including the planning analysis hour (K100)
factor, directional (D) factor, peak hour factor (PHF), and adjusted saturation flow rate;
roadway characteristics; and signal characteristics is not permitted.
The following provides general definitions for each level of service:
LOS A - “describes primarily free-flow operation. Vehicles are completely unimpeded in
their ability to maneuver within the traffic stream. Control delay at the boundary
intersections is minimal. The travel speed exceeds 85% of the base free-flow speed.” ’

LOS B - “describes a reasonably unimpeded operation. The ability to maneuver within
the traffic stream is only slightly restricted and control delay at the boundary
intersections is not significant. The travel speed is between 67% and 85% of the base
free-flow speed.”
LOS C - “describes stable operations. The ability to maneuver and change lanes in mid-
segment locations may be more restricted than at LOS B. Longer queues at the
boundary intersections may contribute to lower than average travel speeds. The travel
speed is between 50% and 67% of the base free-flow speed.”
LOS D - “indicates a less stable condition in which small increases in flow may cause
substantial increases in delay and decreases in travel speed. This operation may be due
to adverse signal progression, high volume, and inappropriate signal timing at the
boundary intersections. The travel speed is between 40% and 50% of the base free-
flow speed.”
LOS E - “is characterized by unstable operation and significant delay. Such operations
may be due to some combination of adverse progression, high volume, extensive delays
at critical intersections and inappropriate signal timing, and inappropriate signal timing
at the boundary intersections. The travel speed is between 30% and 40% of the base
free-flow speed.”
LOS F - “is characterized by flow at extremely low speed. Congestion is likely occurring
at the boundary intersections, as indicated by high delay and extensive queuing. The
travel speed is 30% or less of the base free-flow speed. Also, LOS F is assigned to the
subject direction of travel if the trough movement at one or more boundary intersections
has a volume-to-capacity ratio greater than 1.0.
In general, it is desirable to maintain traffic conditions at a LOS D or better, the Broward
County adopted level of service threshold.
