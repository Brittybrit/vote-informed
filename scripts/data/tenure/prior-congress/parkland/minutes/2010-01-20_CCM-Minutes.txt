m PUBLIC INPUT
Mayor Udine opened to the Public.
Jeff Schwartz, Parkland Historical Society, spoke about the numerous things the Historical Society
has done in the City, including the Portals of Parkland, and he thanked the City for their support and
the office space. He also spoke about the fire truck, which is a piece of the City’s history, and said
he is the only one with keys to that truck. Mr. Schwartz stated that they are working with their
budget to get the brake system fixed and the Coral Springs fire chief has offered some funds to equip
the truck. He added that he has also contacted a gentleman who owns a body shop about cleaning up
the body and said, in the near future, he will come to the City with a budget and request money to
help in the restoration of the fire engine.
Marie Gosser, Pine Tree Estates, spoke about the disaster in Haiti and said a letter should be sent to
Israel thanking them for being the first to build a hospital in Haiti.
There were no further comments or questions and the Mayor closed to the public.
Iv COMMENTS BY MAYOR AND COMMISSION
Comments made by Mayor Udine:

e Asked for a moment of silence for the people in Haiti who died in the earthquake.

e Said the City has set up an account to collect monetary donations for Haiti.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young said the City has three options to assist:

1. The City has partnered with Food for the Poor, which is based in Coconut Creek; there are
drop off boxes in City Hall and the needs include water, condensed milk, canned goods and
baby food.

2. Those wanting to make a monetary donation are encouraged to donate to the American Red
Cross, who needs the money to get the personnel to Haiti.

3. The City has 13 employees who have family and/or friends in Haiti; the City has created a
bank account with Suntrust called the Haiti Relief Fund and funds from this account will be
distributed by a committee to the employees to get it to their family members or for
transportation to Haiti.

2. DISCUSSION: THE WEDGE PARCELS — PRE-ANNEXATION
AGREEMENTS

A. TRIPLE H & DUBUYS
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis said that the Triple H and Dubuys owners are not present because
everything has been resolved with them.

B. HENDRIX
City Attorney Maurodis stated that the Hendrix agreement is in good shape and he hopes a finalized
agreement will be ready for approval at the next Commission meeting.

C. BRUSCHI
City Attorney Maurodis said that at the last meeting, the Commission agreed to allow gross density,
but with language that gave the Commission authority to not grant the maximum amount of density,
and he then read aloud the clause in the agreement setting this forth. City Attorney Maurodis
explained how the size of the required donation for the school site will be determined based upon the
total number of units and said that they will donate four acres to the City for any municipal purpose
if the school board determines that a school is not needed. He noted that this donation will not be a
credit against any type of fees.
Commissioner Moskowitz questioned whether it might be better for the City to receive a straight
donation of five to six acres versus four acres since the City already has land in place for an
elementary, middle, and high school and it is unlikely the school board would build four schools in
the City.
Commissioner Weissman spoke about the possibility of only one elementary school being inadequate
for this area and the benefit of having enough sites to allow the school board to have a choice.
Commissioner Moskowitz commented that land has not yet been secured for a fire or police station.
Mayor Udine said that he would like to keep the agreement as is and spoke about the plan for fire
stations in the City. He commented that he feels the property owners would be receptive if the City’s
needs change later.

3. DISCUSSION: FIRE CONTRACT

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young brought the Commission up-to-date on the fire contract and

said that, although some progress was made, there are two big items requiring guidance from the

Commission. She noted that, at the Commission’s direction to continue exploring alternatives, she

contacted BSO and asked them to provide some information.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young’s comments regarding the two items included the following:

e The $4.8 million contract price provided was in 2010 dollars, which means that as of October
2010, it would be $4.8 million plus whatever Coral Springs determines would be the
increases.

o She asked Coral Springs to give her a definitive figure and they gave her a flat $5.2 million;
this would be the baseline figure beginning October 2010 and the costs would increase each
year based on a percentage.

e The second item is the termination provision: the first option was a five-year contract with
the ability to terminate only for cause and to pay for excess overtime caused by the exodus of
firefighters during the last year of the contract.

e The new option for termination is a 16-month notice, but it would require the City to retain
between 0-33 firefighters, depending upon the staffing needs of the subsequent service
provider.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young said there are some other issues which she has not had the
opportunity to get back to Coral Springs about, and staff is looking for some guidance at this point.
She added that she will continue to negotiate if that is the Commission’s desire.

Commissioner Weissman asked if the amount not capped would be for personnel and City Manager
Caryn Gardner-Young responded in the affirmative. Finance Director Nancy Morando confirmed
that 85% of the fire budget would have no cap. Commissioner Weissman then spoke about a
spreadsheet, prepared by Finance Director Morando, which annualized increases for the term of the
existing contract.

Mayor Udine commented that the residents are satisfied with the fire services and are willing to pay
for those services. He said the City wanted some kind of audit provision on EMS recovery fees and
that was accomplished. Mayor Udine stated that the second issue was the City wanted some element
of caps on the contract, but Coral Springs said from the beginning they would not cap the labor costs,
rather it would be a direct pass through and the City will pay what Coral Springs will pay. He added
that no one will cap labor costs.

Mayor Udine said the next thing the Commission wanted was a termination clause; the problem with
this is that, if notice of termination is given, firefighters would start looking for other jobs because
they are concerned about losing their jobs and Coral Springs would have to pay overtime to cover
those hours. He stated that this current offer from Coral Springs provides for a 16-month
termination clause, with or without cause, and if the City contracts with another provider, the City
would agree to make its best efforts to ensure the provider hires these firefighters to fill any
openings. Mayor Udine added that he is in agreement with this clause and feels it is in the best
interest of the City to stay with Coral Springs.

Commissioner Moskowitz suggested that there is another alternative to voting continued
negotiations up or down. He said that he feels there is still time to continue to negotiate and he does
not have a problem coming back for a special meeting next week to deal with this issue.
Commissioner Moskowitz commented that he does not have a problem with the new language of the
termination clause and he feels that all Coral Springs is saying is if the new provider has 33
openings, then these guys and gals should be hired. He added that at this point, he would likely
support the Coral Springs contract, but he still does not understand how the contract has gone up one
million dollars.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young explained the increase.

Commissioner Moskowitz said that he would vote to approve on the $4.8 million number, but not
the $5.2 million and commented that if the Commission is dead set on taking a vote at this meeting,
he will vote to support the Coral Springs contract because he knows the contract and the service.
Commissioner Rosenof apologized to the men and women of the Coral Springs Fire Department and
said this has gone on for too long. He spoke about the caps and fees, and added that the issue for
him is about terms and not dollars. He noted that the City has gotten neither the termination
provision nor the audit provision they wanted. Commissioner Rosenof stated that he would have
voted to move on two months ago, but did not out of respect for the people who are serving the
community and he feels at this point, it is time to make a decision.

Mayor Udine spoke about the termination and non-capital versus labor costs issues.

City Attorney Andrew Maurodis commented that although this contract is not the final version, there
has been a full explanation of Coral Springs’ business points.

Commissioner Weissman said that back in December, the Commission gave Coral Springs the
opportunity to vote on the contract and to respond by January 6"; then the Commission decided to

ADDED AGENDA ITEM: DISCUSSION ON CHINESE DRYWALL

Mayor Udine said that he would like the Building Department staff to come up with a proposal to
waive building permit fees for repairs for original owners, not developers or investors, affected by
Chinese drywall.
There was discussion on waiving permitting fees, including who should be allowed this abatement,
and the appropriate accounting measures to be taken regarding the budget.
Commissioner Weissman said it is important that a waiver be limited to original purchaser and
homesteaded properties.
Commissioner Rosenof questioned whether a re-inspection fee would be included in the waiver of
fees.
After further discussion, City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young confirmed the intention of the
Commission is to propose a waiver for permit review fees and the original inspection.
MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to waive building permit review and original
inspection fees for all original homeowners repairing homes with Chinese drywall, and with the
Building Department determining the original owners. Seconded by Commissioner Rosenof.
MOTION passed unanimously.
