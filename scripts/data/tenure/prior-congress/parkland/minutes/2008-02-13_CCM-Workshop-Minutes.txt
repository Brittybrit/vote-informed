BUILDING
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young indicated that two elevations for the combination Public
Works/Fire Station building would be presented, as stipulated under the contract with Acai. She
described one as similar to property adjacent to that area, while the other was similar to Municipal
Complex buildings; the footprint would not be changed by either elevation. Renderings were
displayed and copies were provided to the Commission.
Mayor Udine questioned whether there was a cost differential, and Ms. Gardner -Young
responded there was none.
Rafael Oranday of Acai Associates stated that the majority of the structure would be kept, with a
15 -foot addition on the north gable end. He noted that the Mediterranean elevation was similar to
Town Centre.
Commissioner Weissman stated that he would like to keep the theme of City structures, with
which Commissioner Rosenof agreed. Commissioner Smith commented that he liked some

Mayor Udine stated that he also preferred the elevation similar to the existing City buildings, and
he felt there unanimity of the four Commissioners present.

4. DISCUSSION: PINE TREE SWALES
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young stated that staff was seeking the Commission's guidance
regarding the maintenance of the drainage swales within the Pinetree Estates area. She offered
that this has been an issue for a long time.

Public Services Director Jim Berkman stated that the Code requires adjacent residents to mow
and maintain the swales, and the Code also provides the City ability to do maintenance subsequent
to a special assessment. He recalled that the NRCS project had been completed about a year ago,
which had cleared Brazilian pepper and tree growth. At its completion, Mr. Archer had sent out a
letter to Pinetree residents informing them of their responsibilities to maintain the swale, and a
subsequent letter was sent on May 30 Mr. Berkman stated that a significant number of swales
had not since been maintained, and he presented photographs.
Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer gave a brief history of the enforcement in that
area. He recalled that, several years back, Pinetree Water Control District had mowed the swales
twice a year, and they would provide the City a list of the properties they felt in violation. The
City would subsequently cite the individual property owners for violations of vegetation,
encroachment, debris, etc. About the time that Pinetree stopped mowing, the Notices of
Violation through code enforcement also stopped.
Mr. Archer indicated that code enforcement of the swales would be a lengthy and expensive
process, taking considerable time and effort of limited code enforcement staff. He cautioned that
the process had in the past taken as long as a year to get everything cleaned up, and then the
process would have to begin again.
Commissioner Rosenof commented that he had served as President of Pinetree Water Control
District for years. He noted the misconception that the District maintained the swales; it does not,
nor is it part of their assessment or responsibility. He stated that Pinetree easements were to the
City, not to the District, and it was the responsibility of the homeowners to maintain them. He
believed the mowing had been an informal "agreement" with the City and residents; as budgets
had tightened and the District became more complicated, the District stopped the maintenance of
areas without agreements.
Commissioner Rosenof personally recalled receiving code enforcement notices about exotics or
overgrowth, and he would usually take care of it. He had assumed that, if he had not done that, it
would be done for him. He noted that the District did not have enforcement provisions. While he
felt that homeowners should maintain their own swales, he believed the City should figure out a
system of cost recovery should City action be necessary.
Commissioner Weissman commented that he had discussed the cost involved, as well as the cost
recovery aspect, with the City Manager, and he questioned whether that would be practical.
From staff's perspective on cost recovery, Ms. Gardner -Young stated that initiation of a code
enforcement case did not force the homeowner to take action. The City could enforce by either
filing a lien against the property, which would more than likely not be collected until the property
was sold or refinanced, or filing against the individual, which would be very expensive. It was her
belief that the property owner perception was that, with code enforcement, the problem would be
rectified and they would expect the City to take correction action; they did not want to know that
the City's only remedy would be to file a lien. Ms. Gardner -Young suggested that 50 percent of
the 700 homes were not in compliance. Substantial funds would need to be budgeted to cover the
City's costs and then try to collect it back.

Commissioner Smith questioned whether the City funds expended, and unpaid by the homeowner,
could be added to the homeowner's non -ad valorem taxes. Mr. Maurodis responded in the
negative.
Mayor Udine reviewed the hypothetical code enforcement process.
Mr. Maurodis suggested that a process for assessment could be created, as other municipalities
had done, and he confirmed that liens on homesteaded properties would not be collected until the
properties either sold or were refinanced. He agreed that it would be a big cash flow problem.
The Mayor felt that the majority of homeowners would fix violations when they received
notification about a prospective lien on their property.
Commissioner Rosenof commented that the Pinetree area had grown from 40 to 450 homes in 15
years, and he believed that many people did not know they had to maintain the swales. While he
acknowledged the issue, he was optimistic that it would not be bad once the homeowners
understood their responsibilities. The Commissioner took issue with the option of special
assessment; he felt that he should not become financially responsible for other properties.
Mayor Udine asked regarding structural encroachments in the swale areas. Ms. Gardner -Young
confirmed their existence and stated that they must also be removed. She noted that code
enforcement would be required to travel the swales on a regular basis to assure compliance.
Ms. Gardner -Young noted that Pinetree did not have a homeowners' association and that,
typically, the homeowner was responsible for their own parcel.
At the Mayor's request of additional explanation, Mr. Berkman added that, as an example,
stormwater in the Ranches must be maintained on the individual properties; it could not flow into
the public right -of -way. He interpreted that the individual property owner would be responsible
for their own stormwater storage and water control.
Commissioner Rosenof commented that there may then be a water table issue; water may be too
high for the septic systems to work properly. Mayor Udine agreed that one property owner's
issue may then affect the surrounding properties. Discussion ensued.
Mayor Udine summarized two possibilities: property owners would be cited for violations
through code enforcement and recovery of costs, or the entire area would be assessed. He
preferred to start with code enforcement to see if it would solve most of the problems, with the
second option pursued at a later time. He believed that a strong code enforcement mechanism
must be in place in order to lien property for non compliance.
Commissioner Weissman noted that, according to Mr. Archer, it would take a year to assess and
cite Pinetree properties. He suggested that one person must be dedicated to this task while the
rest of the City would pay for that, and he did not believe that fair.

5. DISCUSSION: PINE TRAILS PHASE III
REDUCTION IN SCOPE
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young indicated that Parks and Recreation Director Scott Davidoff
and Public Works Director Jun Berkman would make a presentation regarding Phase III of Pine
Trails Park.
Mr. Davidoff reviewed that Phase III had included three softball fields, additional parking, and the
pavilion area, as well as the electrical and storage building. Craven Thompson's cost estimate
was $1 8 million, which the City had budgeted for this fiscal year, but the lowest bid received was
that of West Construction at $2.8 million leaving the City $1 million short. Mr. Davidoff
explained that consideration had been given to moving funds from other capital projects.
However, staff had gone back to the drawing board to see what $1.8 million might achieve, giving
consideration to the needs of current sports programs. He reviewed program participation, and
his report has been incorporated into the Minutes.
Mr. Davidoff recalled that the Commission had approved a multi purpose field for Phase IV. He
noted that Pine Trails was the only park within the City without a playground, which was a chief
complaint of parents, and it was intended for inclusion in Phase IV. Staff recommended the
redesign of Phase III, which would include the multi purpose field, the playground, parking, and
the pavilion. With these elements, the total cost would be approximately $2 million; $1.8 million
had been budgeted, and he anticipated that an additional $200,000 grant from the Land Water
Conservation Fund would be received by the City within the next few months.
Mr. Davidoff explained two options, with their main difference being the locations of the
playground and parking lot. It was believed that, even with swapping out originally- designed
Phase III elements, the City would still meet all of the qualifications to receive the two FRDAP
grants totaling $400,000.
Mr. Davidoff commented that he had been approached by a travel soccer group representing a
major corporation willing to donate $400,000 to the City to build a soccer field at Pine Trails
Park. However, the caveat was that the field would only be used by travel soccer, with the
exception that it could be used for recreational league championships. It would not serve the
multi -use purpose, and it would not alleviate the strain on the other fields.

3. DISCUSSION: ROOF MAINTENANCE ORDINANCE
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young briefly explained that, prior to last year's hurricane season, a
number of residents had called with concern that tiles stacked on roofs awaiting installation could
become projectiles. The City had been limited in its actions, and she had asked Mr. Archer to
investigate.
Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer recalled there had been many calls of concern
regarding roof tiles. With the threat of a storm, the Florida Building Code allows the Building
Department and City staff to take action with new construction. The dilemma has been in the
enforcement of roof maintenance in occupied housing. Staff asked Commission direction based
on two options: to create an ordinance just to deal with loose or damaged roof tiles, taking a
proactive approach through the code enforcement process, if necessary; or to create an expanded
ordinance that would deal with roof tiles as well as other roof maintenance issues, such as holes,
damage, or structural.
Mayor Udine commented that the City needed the emergency -type ordinance prior to a hurricane
or weather issue, but he believed it should be taken further: having some kind of mechanism in
place to protect neighboring property owners.
Commissioner Smith recalled the difficulty in obtaining roof tiles after Hurricane Wilma, and he
noted that a number of his neighbors were just now getting tiles delivered. He believed that the
tarps were warranted under extreme situations.
Mayor Udine agreed, but he noted that a damaged roof or a house in disrepair affects the property
values within a mile of that house. A blue tarp on a roof for an extended time also affects the
property values. He believed there should be some mechanism to assure that the property owners
were at least on a path to having the repairs made.
Commissioner Rosenof noted that re -roofs required a permit, and he questioned enforcement of
the Florida Building Code regarding the roof tiles during hurricane warning. Mr. Archer
responded that it could be enforced and part of that enforcement would be that the roofing
contractor had at a minimum bundled the roof tiles on the roof.
Commissioner Rosenof felt that the ordinance should be expanded to include other construction
materials, with which Mayor Udine agreed. The Commissioner suggested that "blue tarp houses"
should be handled through the County's Unsafe Structure Board. Ms. Gardner -Young responded
that the City does have an agreement with the County, but the case would have to meet the
County requirements.
