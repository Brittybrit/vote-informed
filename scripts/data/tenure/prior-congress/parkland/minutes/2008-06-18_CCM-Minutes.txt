III PUBLIC INPUT
Former Mayor Harold Bockhold spoke regarding the Historical Society, founded in 2000 to
collect and preserve the City history from its inception and into the future. He expressed the
Society's disappointment that they had not been invited to participate in the decisions regarding
Old City Hall and its historical value, and he noted their "eviction" on September 1s with the
rebuilding of the facility. He believed it imperative for the Society's future to have the City's
continuing commitment for space and archive storage.
Mayor Udine recalled that the Historical Society had been included in the discussions about Old
City Hall and had wanted to save the building. He explained that, after meeting with architects
and planners, it was determined that the building could not be saved. He assured that the
Commission and staff supported the Historical Society and that space would be found for them
Marie Gosser of Pinetree Estates spoke regarding the 55+ Club and its activities. She asked that
the City provide the club a steady location for their meetings as the Library meeting room would
not be available in October and November due to elections.
Mary Morris, a former member of the Fort Lauderdale Historical Society and current member of
the Parkland Historical Society, asked the Commission to help preserve the City's past for the
knowledge of its children and future generations.
Dr. Leo Gosser of Pinetree Estates announced the formation of the Broward Beekeeper's
Association. With three registered beekeepers in the City and the potential of two more, the
Association would be an educational organization designed to inform the public of the benefits of
honey bees and the art of beekeeping. He asked that any honey bee situations in the City be
referred to their organization.
Jeff Schwartz, a Ranches resident, spoke regarding an oil spill that had occurred in the canal along
Trotter's Lane. While he thanked the City for its initial prompt response, the clean -up was not
complete. He was concerned that the spill would ultimately percolate down into the Ranches'
wells. The EPA was called and had taken water samples, and the paving company had removed
two 55- gallon drums of tar from the canal. While some surface oils were being absorbed, the tar
was present below the surface in the sand. He asked the City to help the people along Trotter's
Lane and the entire community with well water to prevent future health problems due to the
contamination.
Mayor Udine noted that the City had hired a clean -up company, and he asked for the City
Manager's report on the situation.

City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young stated that, upon receiving the phone call from Mr.
Squillace, staff immediately contacted the County, the local agency responsible for this type of
situation. The County and the EPA had examined the site and at that time indicated that a clean-
up was necessary; the City had utilized their on -call contractors. She agreed with Mr. Schwartz
that additional action was necessary. A partial delay had been in awaiting the County and EPA
ruling as to what further action needed to be taken. Their determination, based on their experts'
tests, was that it had de minimus impact, and they would take no further action. The City was
told that no drinking waters problems exist at this point. She advised that Mr. Berkman was
working on a plan as to the City's next steps.
Mayor Udine asked that the Commission be given a report at the next meeting.
Mike Squillace asked that he be given longer than three minutes to address the Commission, and
the Mayor suggested that he make his three minute presentation. Mayor Udine advised that,
should he have additional comments on the water issue, it should be brought to staff.
Mr. Squillace spoke regarding the oil spill. He commented that Mr. Schwartz had been present
during the road repair and that excess run -offs into the waterways had occurred. He spoke
regarding the use of tar -based product and solvents, and his efforts with the State and the City for
a timely response. He believed that a quick clean -up would be the least costly for all concerned.
Mayor Udine assured Mr. Squillace that the City was working on the situation, and noted that the
EPA had given a clean write -off. The City would go farther: it would be inspect it and reported
upon. He assured the issue was not being ignored.
Ira Goldman also spoke on behalf of the Historical Society, which needed a place to store their
artifacts and work from, and he hoped it would be given on a gratis basis. He spoke regarding an
early-20 century quote by George Santayana: "Those who cannot remember the past are
condemned to repeat it." He believed the Society's continuance a necessity, and he hoped for the
Commission's continued support. The Society also looked forward to meeting regarding the
burial of the time capsule.
Stuart Krantz complimented the City for the repaving of Holmberg Road and for adding the
bicycle lanes and fire hydrants. He spoke regarding the leaking roof in the Liberty Park pavilion,
which appears to be worsening, and pedestrian safety and sidewalk placement along major
roadways. He believed that the City should have a bike advisory committee and that e-Better
Place should provide contact of staff members. He commented that Pinetree Estates roads were
not private; by statute, after seven years of maintenance, the roads were considered public.
Country Acres resident Brian Allen spoke regarding his trash problems. He recalled that, at
annexation, the area should have kept its previous service, which had been good for about a year.
Mayor Udine directed the City Manager to speak with Waste Management regarding the bulk
pick -up in Country Acres. Addressing Mr. Allen, the Mayor believed that Country Acres

interesting that the road was completed in 2007, and yet the oil had not appeared until
June 2008. He clarified that no 55- gallon drums had been pulled from the canal. The
