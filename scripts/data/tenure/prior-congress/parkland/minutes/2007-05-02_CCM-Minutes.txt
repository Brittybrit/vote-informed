IV COMMENTS BY MAYOR AND COMMISSION
Comments made by Vice Mayor Moskowitz:
• Working with former Mayor Bob Marks regarding communications during
emergencies. Satellite phones had been used by the City, police, and fire following the
Hurricane Wilma emergency, but there was a communication gap between government
and the schools. The EAB would examine ways to give principals or administrators
current technology, such as PDA devices, so that they might have constant
communication with BSO and City Hall. He looked forward to an EAB
recommendation.
Comments made by Commissioner Smith:
• Asked that watering days be observed. He noted that BSO patrols gated and non-gated
communities and would issue $500 fines to homeowners who fail to comply.
Comments made by Mayor Udine:
• Asked that the City Manager bring ideas to the Commission about strengthening water
restrictions. He felt certain that the South Florida Water Management District would
implement Phase 3 in the near future, and suggested the City should get a head start to
be sure that the City was doing its part to conserve water.
• Noted that the grass at Pine Trails was really looking burnt, and he asked that Public
Services check it, including the sprinkler system.
• House Bill 1315, the bill for the annexation of the Wedge, had passed the Florida House
of Representatives and had now been incorporated into the Senate bill, which passed
second reading on this date. He expected that it would pass third reading on the special
order calendar on the following day, and then it would go to the Governor for signature.
It appeared the Wedge bill would pass.
• Received word late on this date that the legislature had reached an impasse on property
taxes. A special session would be held June 12`" through June 22°d to try to provide
some type of property tax relief.
• Reminded everyone of upcoming events: Relay for Life would be held over the
weekend and the Arthritis Walk would be held on Sunday morning.

Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2007-37, Meadow
Run Community Signage -CAB Approval.
Planning Director Roberta Moore gave a brief PowerPoint presentation concerning the repainting
of exterior walls and the modification of entrance signs as originally proposed and as modified.
She spoke of the conditions of approval.
Commissioner Weissman indicated that he had received a telephone call from a representative of
the applicant with regard to the signage. At the time, it had not been approved by the Planning
and Zoning Board, which had recommended that it come straight to the Commission after
making certain changes.. The Planning and Zoning Board had not formally approved this plan at
a meeting. While he intended to vote for this approval, he asked that the Board, in the future,
first approve or deny a finished product before its presentation to the Commission.
Commissioner Smith advised that he had received a phone call from a member of the Planning
and Zoning Board. It was explained that the original idea had been the use of shiny, black
granite, which the Board had not liked. The member had spoken to other members of the Board
and had no problems with the toned-down version now presented.
Mayor Udine agreed with Commissioner Weissman. He offered that he had received phone calls
from the losing side of the Board vote, which he did not believe was the best way to handle it.
He believed the sign beautiful and a huge improvement, and he would vote in favor.

Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the second reading of Ordinance No. 2007-12.
Planning Director Roberta Moore recalled the two issues raised by the Commission at first
reading: the duration that election signs may be placed prior to an election and clarification of
the change made to Section 15-40. She explained that the duration of the placement had been
changed from 60 to 90 days and provided information regarding the draft of the Ordinance by an
outside attorney.
Mayor Udine opened to the public.
Robert Mayersohn, 6742 N.W. 80th Manor, stated that he was in favor of advancing any
ordinance that would preserve and enhance neighborhood aesthetics and property values and
promote traffic and pedestrian safety. However, he believed that placing a limit on the duration
of election signs also placed a burden on the resident's right to free speech. Mr. Mayersohn
spoke regarding aesthetics, the exercise of First Amendment rights, and court challenges.
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis commented that, based upon the decisions reviewed, Mr.
Britton, the outside attorney, had concern with the durational limits of political signs. Durational
limits are the rule rather than the exception in sign ordinances that he had seen. He recognized
an element of risk because the courts have been very tough; to mitigate this, there was a
provision in the ordinance that allowed free expression signs -allowing everyone to have a free
expression sign on their property at any time to express their views. Mr. Maurodis
acknowledged that it was a difficult area of the law and that concerns must be balanced.
Mr. Maurodis suggested that the Ordinance be taken back to Mr. Britton -- with the quandary that
the City did not want year-round signs -- to find a solution.
