IV COMMENTS BY MAYOR AND COMMISSION
Comments made by Commissioner Smith:
• Announced that Child Safety Night would be held on Wednesday, October 17th, at the
Heron Bay Marriott from 7:00 to 9:00 p.m. There would be considerable information
disseminated by speakers and exhibitors with particular emphasis on Internet safety. He
encouraged parents and their children to attend this free event.

authorized by Broward County. The Parkland Village developer had been required by
development order to build the street and west sidewalk; there were punchlist items still
outstanding. Additionally, the City must build the east sidewalk. It is in the design
phase and construction will begin immediately following the County's approval.

participate in a traditional Thanksgiving food drive to benefit Family Central.
Collection boxes will be placed throughout the City. The Commissioner invited student
Matt Rothstein to make a brief presentation.
MSDHS sophomore Matt Rothstein thanked the Commission for allowing the City's
participation, and he gave a brief summary of the drive.
Comments made by Mayor Udine:
• Attended the dedication luncheon for the MSDHS Cumber Stadium on Sunday, which
had been a touching and unbelievable event. He thanked the City Manager and Capital
Projects Manager Horatio Danovich for their work. After 17 years, the school would
finally have a stadium. The Douglas football team was doing well, and they would play
Coral Springs on Friday night at the "Pig Bowl"; the losing principal would kiss a pig.

policy statement about bullying -- in schools, on the way to and from school, and on the
sports fields. Mayor Udine offered that the EAB had been funded with $5,000 this
year, and they had indicated that they would want to apply part of that amount toward
this specific issue. He asked that this be discussed at some future Commission meeting
to establish parameters and a plan of action.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2007-22. She
explained that the purchasing provisions were a first step toward bringing the Code to current

Administrative Services Director Barbara Hastings indicated that a provision will be added to
take that into account.

questioned whether the additional steps and work created by Sections 2-141.1, 2-141.2, and 2-

any department head could snake these purchases. He believed that the policy should include
language that the purchase must be specific to that department and its scope of responsibility.

emergency purchase and the authority given to the contractor.

language pertaining to safeguards could be included. Additionally, in an emergency, it should
clearly be ashort-term contract.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested that a real emergency should be defined, beyond imminent
danger to life, safety, health and property, and he questioned breakdowns of equipment. Ms.
Gardner-Young offered City Hall air-conditioning equipment as an example. Mr. Maurodis
agreed that retroactive Commission approval was a mistake and that there should be a reporting

for the next meeting.

4. ORDINANCE NO. 2007-19: FALSE ALARM ZTA
COMMENTS: FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND,
FLORIDA, RELATING TO CHAPTER 11 OF THE CODE
OF ORDINANCES ENTITLED "OFFENSES AND
MISCELLANEOUS PROVISIONS" AMENDING
ARTICLE VI ENTITLED "FALSE ALARMS';
PROVIDING A CONFLICTS CLAUSE AND A
SEVERABILITY CLAUSE; AND PROVIDING AN
EFFECTIVE DATE.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2007-19. She noted
that any comments or concerns expressed at this first reading could be incorporated.
Mayor Udine commented that the alarm ordinance was already in existence and this Ordinance
would be an update.
Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer reviewed that the Ordinance would provide
BSO the authority to enforce false alarms, which had not occurred in 2004. Referring to a
handout left on the dais, Mr. Archer indicated that the Ordinance would also incorporate the fee
structure for the fines, which he had based on one recently enacted by Boca Raton. He spoke
.regarding the handling by other cities. He offered that the Ordinance would also remove the
lengthy permitting process for alarm registration and provide up-to-date definitions. Mr. Archer
indicated that the handout included a tabulation of the number of false alarm calls received for
2006 and most of 2007.

<<PG>>

Commissioner Smith noted that the number one offender in false alarms was the clubhouse in
Parkland Isles. Asking further information of BSO Chief O'Connell, the Chief indicated that it
had occurred for a variety of reasons, but the majority was a technical issue which he hoped
would quickly be addressed.
Commissioner Weissman noted there had been 79 violators having three false alarms in the
previous year. He indicated that he would move to amend the fines to start after the fifth
occurrence, which would reduce the list to 18 violators. He suggested that the subject could be
revisited at any time. A warning would be issued with each false alarm occurrence and, with the
fifth occurrence in the same .calendar year, the homeowner would then be liable for a fine of $250
per call. The Commissioner believed the numbers would drop when that occurred. He did not
believe that the fines should continue to escalate upward.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz stated that he could not agree. He noted that, examining the list of other
cities, Parkland would then be the only city starting at the fifth occurrence. Other cities started
before the fifth occurrence and with lower fines. He would prefer starting fines at the fourth
violation with $150 and the fifth violation at $250. He suggested that even four false alarms
seemed too many.
Commissioner Weissman noted that the Ordinance did address the issue of weather
circumstances, which he felt certain the cause for many, and they would not be counted. He
stated that he would agree with the Vice Mayor's recommendation of four violations, starting
with a lower fee of $100 or $150, and the fifth one and beyond at $250. The Commissioner felt
that, once violators were warned of the fees, there would be a substantial drop in the numbers.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz reiterated his recommendation that the false alarm fee would start with
the fourth occurrence, with a fine of $100; the fifth occurrence and each one thereafter would be
$25G. Commissioner Weissman added that the number of calls would be per calendar year.
Mayor Udine wanted to be certain there was a way to enforce the Ordinance. He did not
anticipate a big revenue generator for the City, but it was not fair that BSO must continue to
respond to false alarms that should have been addressed before reaching multiple occurrences.
He believed that most alarms were monitored through a central station, which first calls for a
code; the police were not dispatched when the code was correctly given. These false alarms
would not then be counted. He suggested it would be difficult to get to four false alarms with
police dispatched, and he agreed that something should be put into place.
Mayor Udine agreed with Commissioner Weissman and the Vice Mayor.. He summarized that
the fourth false alarm should result in a $100 fine and the fifth occurrence and thereafter would
result in a $250 fine. He was glad to be rid of the permitting.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked whether the City had alarm registration. Ms. Gardner-Young
confirmed that only the building permit for alarm installation would be required; there would not

5. RESOLUTION N0.2007-92A: FIRST AMENDMENT TO THE
CITY WIDE FEE SCHEDULE
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, TO APPROVE THE
FIRST AMENDMENT TO THE ADOPTION OF
ADMINISTRATIVE AND USER FEE AND CHARGE
SCHEDULES FOR SERVICES, ACTIVITIES AND USE
OF FACILITIES PROVIDED BY THE CITY OF
PARKLAND; PROVIDING FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2007-92A. She recalled
that, although the Commission had adopted the first schedule, changes had been requested,
particularly with regard to the Parks and Recreation fees.
Parks and Recreation Director Scott. Davidoff recalled that the Commission had directed staff to
establish a wider gap between resident and non-resident fees. The fees of other municipalities as
well as private camps were examined, and an amended fee scheduled was included in the
Commission packet.
Commissioner Weissman wanted to be certain that the fees included all ancillary costs that might
be incurred by the City, particularly with respect to the Building Department and the operation of
City facilities. He hoped that a comprehensive analysis of all fees incurred by the City had taken
place so that it would not be necessary to revisit these fees after the January vote and possible
shortfalls.

<<PG>>

Ms. Gardner-Young offered that she had discussed this with the Building Official earlier in the
day, who confirmed that it had been taken into consideration. She noted this was a "living"
document, and it could not be guaranteed that there may be some other future considerations.
Ms. Gardner-Young noted that the majority of the Building Department's fee revenues last year
had been generated by single-family homes. With the housing slump this year, there were now
more permits for additions and modifications, and this had been reflected in the projection of fees
for this year. She assured the Commission that she had and would continue to keep a close eye
on Building Department activity and acknowledged that the short-term could be very different
from the long-term.
Commissioner Weissman suggested that the portability issue might again turn around, with
which Ms. Gardner-Young agreed. Should the additional $25,000 exemption be enacted, she
believed that the City could lose another $600,000 to $700,000 in revenue. She understood the
Commission concern, and she believed staff had addressed it.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz reiterated that he did not have a problem with the fees, but he wanted
assurance that staff would be consistent in charging them. There should be no exceptions.
Commenting on the summer camp exhibit, Mayor Udine indicated that he agreed with the non-
resident increase. He believed, however, that the numbers for residents should have been kept at
the original amounts. His feeling was that the Parkland Kids Camp was possibly the City's best
program, and most other competing camps had an automatic yearly increase. The Camp was not
meant to be a profit center, but expenses were going up by more than the expenses charged. The
Mayor believed that the original amounts should be presented.
Mr. Davidoff clarified there would still be an increase from previous years. There had been an
adjustment of $50 for the eight-week session in order to encourage people to sign up for the
entire summer, but he reiterated there had been an increase in the fees.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz questioned how many non-residents had participated in the camp last
summer. Mr. Davidoff stated that, including year-round camp, there were 198 non-residents.
Non-resident participants in sports programs numbered approximately 500.
Mayor Udine opened to the public.
Resident Robert Mayersohn, 6742 N.W. 80t~' Manor, questioned that the sports card non-resident
fees equated to the difference in camp fees.
There were no further comments or questions, and the Mayor closed to the public.
Mayor Udine commented that the camps were an unbelievable value for non-residents, and it was
almost supply-and-demand. He supported the differences in the fees.
There was further discussion.
