1. DISCUSSION: BSO STAFFING LEVELS
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young recalled discussion on this subject during the strategic
planning session, and she had asked the Chief for his ideas, suggestions, and what he foresees for
the coming year. Three areas of concern had come to light: a decreased visibility in the
community, an increase in speeding, and an increase in thefts. While she recalled past discussions
about adding a fourth patrol zone, she noted that cost would be substantial. Based upon recent

figures and the funds expected for next year, it may not be feasible without reductions in other
programs or services.
Chief Paul O'Connell reviewed his memo addressed to the City Manager and the statistical
foundations to increase staffing, which was in direct conflict to the budget squeeze felt by all.
Staffing adjustments were required. He firmly believed in a selective enforcement team (SET),
which would give flexibility in hours of work and the ability to target locations and individuals.
The Chief reviewed staffing experiments of the past year, and he discovered that full staffing of
the detective bureau and two less road patrol had proven effective. While this would not help
with visibility since officers were in plainclothes and unmarked cars, it was designed specifically
for crime reduction. Chief O'Connell strongly believed that having a motorcycle officer would
have direct impact on the two priorities of traffic enforcement and visibility. He also strongly
believed in the School Resource Officer program and the positive impact on the children.
Chief O'Connell spoke of three possibilities. Option 1 would be to add to staffing, adding the
motorcycle officer and two deputies to the SET team, keeping the resource officers at the current
level, at a cost just under $500,000. Option 2 would be to eliminate one school resource officer
at the elementary school level, placing the deputy on road patrol; the remaining school resource
officer would float between the two elementary schools. Option 3 would be to maintain staffing
at its current level.
Questioning the school resource deputy option, Mayor Udine felt that deputies in the high school
and middle school were clearly needed. He noted the City's third and fourth elementary schools
would be added in the next couple of years and, at some point with the tax revenue situation and
the lack of reimbursement, it was unclear whether it would be sustainable. He questioned sharing
one School Resource Deputy between two elementary schools.
Chief O'Connell believed the most difficult and critical times were at the start and end of the
school day when the deputies assisted with traffic control. He believed the best way to address it
would be to assign traffic control at the specific school to a deputy taken off the road. He
anticipated that the deputy would be scheduled at one school in the morning and the other in the
afternoon.
Chief O'Connell commented that next Tuesday the Broward County School Board would
consider increasing the monetary coverage for School Resource Deputies to $20,000 starting next
year. The City is currently reimbursed $12,000 per School Resource Deputy. Additionally, they
would "float" a 50 percent reimbursement in the future. He suggested it something to consider in
weighing the options and, while he was optimistic, there was no guarantee.
Mayor Udine noted that it cost the City approximately $130,000 per year per school deputy.
Ms. Gardner -Young offered that the City Managers' group would have a representative at the
School Board meeting. The City's position was that school resource officers should be a school
district cost as they deal with school issues. She believed the roles of the School Resource
Deputy in the elementary versus the middle school and high school very different. She agreed

with the Mayor that the City should consider having one deputy cover two elementary schools,
rather than pulling from the middle or high schools.
Mayor Udine commented that he would not want to pull a School Resource Deputy from an
elementary school if it can be shown that traffic issues could be covered and one deputy could
work two schools. He indicated that, at budget time, he would like to see another way to
increase police presence and what it could cost.
Chief O'Connell noted that, since 1994, the City had had three patrol zones. Response times
suffer as deputies respond to more calls in a larger geographic area with more traffic.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz stated that specific crimes had increased within the past two years, and he
felt it uncertain that three or four additional officers would decrease those statistics. Chief
O'Connell commented that the average response time was between five and six minutes for a non
emergency and noted the fourth patrol zone would directly answer the City's concern about
visibility. He believed that the SET, which would target areas and times, would show results.
The Vice Mayor commented that he would not vote for elimination of a school resource deputy,
and he believed that the two existing deputies should rotate at the third elementary school when
completed.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz believed it logical to add the fourth zone, and he suggested that the City
should consider the use of its reserve funds. With $6 million in reserve, he felt that the cost of
$375,000 for three additional officers would not "make or break" the City in the event of an
emergency. He felt the City at the point of adding more deputies, considering either use of the
reserves or finding a new revenue source.
Ms. Gardner -Young noted that an addition of officers would be a continuing year after -year
obligation; the $375,000 from reserve would not be replenished, and then subsequent years must
be covered in the operating budget. She cautioned that, while the City was better off than most
jurisdictions this year due to growth, there would be none in the coming year; additionally, there
would be lower assessed values and the full effects of Amendment 1. She felt there would be a
substantial downside to this reoccurring expense without a funding source to cover.
Mayor Udine believed all of the Commission felt the need and necessity for at least a couple of
additional officers. He suggested, however, that the Commission give consideration to the total
expenditure, much like a building project, and consider it as an add -on at budget time.
There was further discussion concerning the value of school resource deputies. Ms. Gardner-
Young noted that the high school also had security furnished by the School Board.
Ms. Gardner -Young summarized that staff should examine adding officers whether one, two, or
three, and where the funds might be found to cover.

Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked regarding how the Chief would assign three new officers and how
they would integrate without creating a new zone. Chief O'Connell responded that one would be
a motorcycle officer, dealing with just traffic, and the two additional deputies would be the
permanent SET team for target specific, time specific, and location specific crimefighting.
Mayor Udine recalled that the Commission had questioned the Chief's desire for the motorcycle
officer in the last budget year, and the Chief continued to believe in its importance. He hoped the
dollars would be there so that this discussion would not have to occur again.
The Vice Mayor noted that the Chief attended most if not all of the homeowners' association
meetings. Commenting about the thefts of purses, phones, laptops, and other valuables left in
unlocked cars, he suggested a special initiative between BSO and the City and to educate the

Commissioner Smith suggested that people hear the message, but getting them to listen is another

Discussion ensued.
Chief O'Connell noted that Parkland had the second lowest crime rate in the County. He
commented that he and the City Manager had discussed use of the message boards to remind
residents about locking their cars and garages.
2. DISCUSSION: SPONSORSHIPS /ADVERTISEMENTS
Ms. Gardner -Young stated that this subject had also been discussed at strategic planning. She
wanted to bring the Commission up -to -date on actions in the last year and where additional
opportunities might be found.
Parks and Recreation Director Scott Davidoff gave a presentation. Beginning with the 2007
Annual Concert, sponsorships had been solicited by staff, and Mercedes -Benz had agreed to a
$10,000 sponsorship. In exchange, Mercedes -Benz had received banners throughout the City, as
well as their logo on the City's website and T- shirts at the event, and their cars were displayed at
the event itself The effort had been a success.
With the opening of the amphitheater, Mr. Davidoff stated that the annual concert series had
commenced. Staff again actively searched for sponsorship, and JM Lexus agreed to a $13,500
annual sponsorship. Their logos would be displayed on banners throughout the City and at each
concert, on the City website, and in all e- newsletters, flyers, and the quarterly newsletter. The
City also provided them two canopies and tables and chairs at the event for the sponsor's use and
display of their vehicles. He offered that JM Lexus has been pleased with their sponsorship.
Mr. Davidoff indicated that staff was starting to examine the potential of other events for cash
sponsorship opportunities, including the concert series, the Farmers' Market, the Comedy

Showcase, and SnowFest. Minor events, garnering $1,000 and under, would be Movies in the
Park, the Fishing Derby, and the senior dances.
Staff would also search for in -kind sponsorships, providing products or services, to utilize for
smaller events. Mr. Davidoff gave as examples bounce houses, food, and Sno -cones for the
Bicycle Parade, Zoo Day, and the Gingerbread House event.
Mr. Davidoff indicated that naming rights had also been discussed with the opening of the
amphitheater. There had been multiple press releases and an article in the Sun Sentinel; staff had
spoken with a number of different businesses, and there had been no interest. He noted that
Broward County had not had success with the sale of naming rights to their recently- opened $70
million Central Broward Regional Park.
Mayor Udine commented that "high six figures" for the City's amphitheater was unrealistic, and
Vice Mayor Moskowitz offered that contracts for naming rights were now significantly less.
Mr. Davidoff noted that a sponsorship package would be tailored to the event. He reviewed
examples of the feedback received by staff in their sponsorship efforts: events were too small;
they would only support events that utilize print advertising within major publications; events only
target Parkland residents and marketing dollars must be spent on programs serving more
communities; banners only face within the City; and the return on investment too small when
$10,000 could be spent on traditional marketing and receive greater exposure.
As to the Commission suggestion of hosting additional sports tournaments, Mr. Davidoff advised
that all of the tournaments were held during the recreational seasons. The City's recreational
seasons were already compressed because of the multiple sports and the desire not to have the
sports overlap.
Mayor Udine believed the City should continue to look at sponsorships. He recognized that the
concert series would continue as the City's largest, and he believed its success would continue.
He acknowledged that the smaller programs, such as Zoo Day, could be without sponsors;
however, even the smallest amount of a few hundred dollars, such as from a local veterinarian,
may help to offset expenditures.
Mr. Davidoff believed that the City has a good opportunity to obtain a sponsor for the Farmers'
Market due to its success.
Ms. Gardner -Young noted that considerable staff time has been expended to obtain sponsorships
versus the potential return, and she assured that staff will continue to ask for sponsorships.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz questioned if there were companies or promoters that might take over the
amphitheater operation.
Mr. Davidoff responded that, in order to make money there, tickets must be sold which would
require closing off the amphitheater and the park to restrict access.

The Vice Mayor suggested that many acts loved performing in the open, and temporary restrictive
barricades and personnel could be positioned for an event. He asked if that avenue had been
explored.
Mr. Davidoff acknowledged that staff had been contacted by a number of promoters looking to
do shows, and the feedback has been that they want to wait a year or so: to see the attendance, to
see whether it would be worthwhile to improve the sound, and to see whether parking issues can
be resolved. Promoters had attended shows; they had met with staff and asked questions about
the building.
Mayor Udine asked whether a promoter had wanted to book a specific date for an event, the
proceeds of which could be split with the City. Mr. Davidoff responded in the negative.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested figuring out a way to promote and market the amphitheater in
that direction, as opposed to sponsorship. He believed it unique in that the structure was small
and had lawn space only.
Mr. Davidoff noted that the Commission had wanted events for Parkland residents; banners face
inward to the City and there was no print advertising. He cautioned that promotion of the
amphitheater would open it to everyone. Having run County events and speaking from his
experience, he noted that 50,000 people could attend and the park's "take" might be just $10,000.
He cautioned that these events would totally disrupt other park operations.
The Vice Mayor believed that the decision should be based on the amount of money that could be
brought in. At $5,000 per show, the City may need from eight to 10 shows to make any real
money. He commented that, as the City discusses new sources of revenue, there were only so
many avenues one of which was the amphitheater. He believed the avenue should be explored
to see how and if it might possibly generate some real money.
Mayor Udine believed that the first step had been taken since there had been no sponsorships
prior to the 2007 concert. He acknowledged that events had been scaled down but noted there
had been more. While he understood the additional revenue, his goal would be to see how close
the City might get to break -even. If there is a way to build on these foundations, he believed it
should be done.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that he would like to know how much money might be
generated if operated as a real amphitheater and as a source of significant revenue and if that
might also then attract a sponsorship.
Ms. Gardner -Young cautioned that the City would then be required to deal with traffic, litter, and
other things from the resident's perspective. She believed that the majority of municipal
amphitheaters did not make money.

 Mayor Udine believed these impacts may outweigh the monetary benefits, unless significant

 3. DISCUSSION: COMMUNITIES FOR A LIFETIME STATUS
 City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young recalled that the Commission had adopted a 2006
 Resolution that showed support of the Communities for a Lifetime program, sponsored by the
 State's Department of Elder Affairs.
 Parks and Recreation Director Scott Davidoff recapped the City's 2006 involvement and the
 program's goals. He reviewed facts about the City in the areas of housing, transportation, health
 and wellness, and volunteerism and intergenerational connections.
 Mr. Davidoff stated that the City would continue to work toward full implementation in all areas.
 Based on feedback from other communities, he believed the City well ahead of the curve, and
 staff would continue to improve and add to the program.
