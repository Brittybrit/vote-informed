the Coral Springs Medical Center. Mayor Udine commented that his wife is serving on the Gala
Committee with Mr. Maloney and they will be doing a fundraising gala on February 26, 2010, at
PGCC for the neo -natal intensive care unit.
Commissioner Weissman said he asked for this to be put on the agenda as he visited the medical
center and became aware of some of the needs that the hospital is facing. He stated that the
hospital was built when the area had a lot less residents and it now serves in excess of 150,000
people. Commissioner Weissman commented that the money allocated by Tallahassee to the
North Broward Hospital District, of which this hospital is a part, is the area residents' tax dollars
and is needed here. He asked the Commission to pass a resolution in support of tax dollars from
this area being dedicated to this facility to provide needed services.

renovation of the emergency room, and noted the hospital has a good relationship with fire rescue.

year to address the Commission.
2. DISCUSSION: COLLECTING NON -AD VALOREM
ASSESSMENT FOR SOLID WASTE SERVICES
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young stated that earlier this year a Waste Management
representative was asked to address the Commission regarding sizes of trash containers being
placed outside by the residents and during that discussion, the idea of placing the costs for solid
waste disposal on the tax bill came up. She explained the steps taken since then and said the issue
is before the Commission again in order to take a look at what the costs, benefits or disadvantages
are in implementing this type of process. City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young then reviewed the
contract modifications Waste Management would be willing to consider in return for the
imposition of the non -ad valorem assessment.
Interim Finance Director Nancy Morando gave a power point presentation (a copy of which is
attached hereto and incorporated herein as part of these minutes).

<<PG>>

Commissioner Weissman questioned whether the annual increase was taken into account, what the
increase in the contract is, and the fact that tax receipts are for the prior year. He voiced concern
regarding the potential for paying double as a result of paying Waste Management for the first
three quarters of the year and then paying taxes, in arrears, in the last quarter, and he requested a
legal opinion on this. Commissioner Weissman added that if residents are paying for services into
the future, anyone who sells their home that year would effectively be paying double.
Commissioner Rosenof said he does not feel this is a good deal for the City insofar as the City
would lose money in discounts from early payment of taxes and the City would have to deal with
delinquencies and extra phone calls. He added that the 2% increase in the franchise fee would not
cover the losses.
Mayor Udine commented that there are three stakeholders that need to be looked at: Waste
Management, City residents, and the City budget. He said the residents would receive a benefit, in
particular, 70% will get a savings of over $15 per year because they take advantage of the discount
when paying taxes. Mayor Udine added that the only ones who do not benefit, from the residents'
point of view, are the 700 people who are not paying their bills. He stated that Waste Management
would see a benefit as they would not have to do any of the billing and they would get all of their
money. Mayor Udine said he feels that Waste Management should make up the $69,000 loss the
City would see.
Commissioner Moskowitz stated that the group he is most concerned about is the residents and he
feels the 2% franchise fee increase should be split with them. He added that he would also like to
see an education campaign from Waste Management, in conjunction with the City, to explain to
the residents why this is happening if this goes forward.
There was additional discussion regarding the loss of revenue to the City.
Mayor Udine noted that the three stakeholders he described earlier should all be winners from this
proposal and the City budget should not take a hit.
Tony Spaduccia, Waste Management, spoke and his comments included the following:
• A number of incorrect analogies were presented.
• This is a win/win for everyone.
• Residents will save 60 cents per quarter under tax roll billing.
• Waste Management would be getting what it deserves in light of the resources it has
committed to the City.
• Residents do not get vacation credits for fire or police services, why should they receive it
for garbage pickup.
• Tax roll billing is a method that is used by more and more cities throughout the county.
• No one will pay double.
• The tax roll discount should not come off the City's end or Waste Management's end.
• The idea is to keep the rate whole.
• The additional 2% in fees to the City will not be passed onto the residents.

• Waste Management continues to collect trash from delinquent residents and from oversize
carts as part of the deal they agreed to.
Commissioner Moskowitz gave the background on why tax roll billing is being proposed.
Mr. Spaduccia responded to Commissioner Weissman's question about the 2% fee increase and

11. ADDED AGENDA ITEM: ANNEXATION: UPDATE FROM
DISCUSSION CITY ATTORNEY
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis explained that staff drafted proposed annexation agreements for
the Bruschi and Hendrix parcels, and he has received responses from both of them. He then
reviewed a letter from Dick Coker, representative for Mr. Hendrix, and noted that Mr. Hendrix
wants to be grandfathered in on all the present farming uses.
Commissioner Moskowitz said the only reason Hendrix is talking to the City now is because their
property value may be in jeopardy if the Hometown Democracy bill passes. He stated that he
would like the Hendrix property to come into the City, but there has to be a certain amount of
things that Mr. Hendrix must do in return.

<<PG>>

City Attorney Maurodis commented that he expected a problem with County Line Road, but the
Triple H people accepted the trail system dedication without question.
Commissioner Moskowitz said #4 of the Hendrix response is indicative of what is being dealt with
here and added County Line Road has nothing to do with the Hendrix's ability to farm.
City Attorney Maurodis stated #2 is reasonable and the bottom line on #4 is "no ". He then spoke
about Section 7 regarding the trail system, the school, and the guardrail.
Commissioner Weissman spoke about the school issue and said it is critical for the City's future.
Commissioner Moskowitz agreed with Commissioner Weissman and added that, with what
Hendrix is proposing, he would not vote for the annexation agreement.
City Attorney Maurodis said Hendrix wants the City to make representations that will make it
impossible for the City to deny two units per acre when they come to the City for land use plan
amendments, all the while promising nothing to the City.
Commissioner Moskowitz stated the biggest issue for the City is County Line Road and with that
taken off the table, Hendrix should have agreed to the other issues. He added that the fact that
Hendrix, with 900 plus acres, is saying he will not give 20 acres to the City is insulting.
Mayor Udine spoke about dedication of a park site and City Attorney Maurodis said they will have
to dedicate five acres per 1,000 residents. City Attorney Maurodis also stated Hendrix's response
does not mention public safety requirements whatsoever.
There was discussion regarding inviting the landowners in for a meeting.
Commissioner Moskowitz spoke about the need for another school with the addition of 3,000
homes when the area is developed and noted that the Wedge has to be looked at as a whole area to
figure out where to locate sites for a school, parks and public safety.
Mayor Udine questioned #4 regarding a connector into Lox Road and City Attorney Maurodis
responded. Mayor Udine suggested that if a pre - annexation agreement is impossible, perhaps the
City should pull the local bill since all of the necessary master planning will not be done by next
November.
City Attorney Maurodis said the bill could be pulled or the City could proceed without a pre -
annexation agreement, and if the landowner would like to come into the City, welcome them in but
make no promises regarding their land use plan amendment.
Commissioner Moskowitz stated that this is why he wants to bring the four major landowners in so
that everyone who has land and is thinking of coming into the City understands the City needs land
for parks, schools, and public safety. He added that the details of who gives the land and how, are
details to be worked out with City Attorney Maurodis.

There was additional discussion about bringing the landowners in for a meeting.
Mayor Udine said that he feels it is not necessary to expend staff time and work at this point. He
added that it is of no benefit to the City if Mr. Hendrix is going to farm for the next 20 years and
County Line Road is not coming in. He noted that the City changed the boundary line, has
annexed in what can be annexed in and when there is a state bill, the City will deal with it.
Commissioner Moskowitz commented that he feels Mecca and Huizenga will come back and try to
work something out.
Mayor Udine responded that this is something City Attorney Maurodis can represent to them
without the need for a meeting. He stated that, looking at this agreement, #5 is insulting and the
cost of the guardrail is a done deal.

but they want to come in for free.
City Attorney Maurodis stated that he has gotten a clear picture of the Commission's sentiments
and he will convey this to Mr. Hendrix. He said if they would like to be invited in for a meeting,
he would relay that to the Commission; if not, then at the next Commission meeting, the decision
will be made whether to pull the bill. He added that he will continue to work with Bruschi.
Commissioner Moskowitz mentioned additional negotiation with Mecca and Huizenga, and City
Attorney Maurodis said staff will be working with them as they are coming in for land use plan
amendment.
Commissioner Weissman stated he would like to strengthen the language regarding school
property and City Attorney Maurodis responded that attempts to do that are being made.
Commissioner Moskowitz agreed with Commissioner Weissman, but said they should not be
punished because of Hendrix.
Commissioner Weissman commented that he wants the school site to be set up the same way, that
is, the land be donated to the school district and it reverts back to the City if the district says they
do not want it.
Mayor Udine said additional residential development can not occur unless there is another
east/west solution. City Attorney Maurodis noted that Huizenga's people are trying to do traffic
studies to show they can develop without it.
City Attorney Maurodis said that this is the extent of his comments and he will schedule another
report as the next step in the process.
