III PUBLIC INPUT
Former Mayor Harold Bockhold congratulated Commissioner Rosenof. He spoke of his love of
the City, striving to bring knowledge of the past to assist in its future. He commented that, while
Trotters Lane looked excellent, it should be made a one -way road due to its narrowness.
Additionally, he disagreed that a Deputy Vice Mayor position was needed, and he hoped the
Commission would reconsider their decision.
There were no further comments or questions, and Mayor Udine closed to the public.
IV COMMENTS BY MAYOR AND COMMISSION
Comments made by Commissioner Smith:
Congratulated Commissioner Rosenof, commenting that it was good to have a full
Commission again.
Attended the dedication of the beautiful new YMCA facility, and he invited residents to
utilize it as well as the City's programs.
Also attended "Movies in the Park" last Saturday. He thanked staff for their diligent
efforts in getting everything done.
Comments made by Vice Mayor Moskowitz:
Congratulated Commissioner Rosenof and agreed it was nice to have a full Commission.
Thanked staff for the Trotters Lane and Ranch Road work. Addressing Mayor
Bockhold's comments, he noted that the widening of Trotters Lane had originally been
considered, and he asked the City Manager to again examine the options.
Extended his condolences to the people affected by the tornadoes of Arkansas and
Tennessee the previous day.
Comments made by Commissioner Weissman:
Welcomed Commissioner Rosenof, and he looked forward to working with him.
Nearly 75 percent of Parkland voters favored Amendment 1, which will affect the City's
property taxes by approximately $1 million. The City would tighten its belt, and he
asked that residents recognize these effects as the City balances its budget for the coming
year.
Comments made by Mayor Udine:
Also extended his congratulations to Commissioner Rosenof. He looked forward to
working with him, and he thanked both Commissioner Rosenof and his family for his
commitment to serve.
Noted that the City had already faced budget challenges and anticipated even more
significant challenges in the coming budget year. He agreed that Amendment 1 would
affect the City's finances by $700,000 to $1 million in tax revenue. He commented
regarding the loss of revenue in the Building Department, and he hoped, but doubted,

3. RESOLUTION NO. 2008 MAJID VARIANCE REQUEST
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, GRANTING
PETITION OF MAZHAR BAZGHA MAJID FOR A
VARIANCE APPROVAL FROM SECTION 22 -54 OF THE
CODE OF ORDINANCES TO REDUCE THE SIDE
BUILDING SETBACK FROM TWENTY -FIVE (25) FEET
TO EIGHT (8) FEET FOR A STORAGE SHED, AS MORE
PARTICULARLY DESCRIBED HEREIN; AND
PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; CASE NO. 03 -V -07.
(quasi-judicial)
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Resolution No. 2008 -15.
Planning Director Roberta Moore explained the circumstances of this request. The storage shed
was constructed at eight feet to the property line, while the building permit had required 25 feet.
Mayor Udine noted this proceeding was quasi-judicial.
Ledford Parnell, appearing on behalf of the applicants, waived quasi-judicial.
Ms. Moore stated that, during the Planning and Zoning Board hearing, the applicant had indicated
that a concrete slab was also poured, which had not been requested for review by the Building
Department. The Board had recommended denial of the petition; staff also recommended denial
of the petition and, additionally, asked that the Commission require the removal of the storage
shed and slab.
Ms. Gardner -Young noted that the staff report included in the agenda material reflected the staff
position of denial in that it does not meet the criteria necessary for the variance to be granted.
Attorney Ledford Parnell stated that, while the west side of the storage shed complied with the
setback, the north side did not. The property owners have requested the variance to avoid the
expense of moving the shed. He indicated that the shed caused no injury to the three adjoining
neighbors, who were present, and that it was not detrimental to the public welfare. Mr. Parnell
also proposed that the variance be made temporary with conditions: that it would not be replaced
upon sustaining hurricane damage and, upon sale of the property, the shed would be removed.
Neighbors Stephanie Vieira, Howard Bent, Camilla Comulada, and Julie Karp individually stated
that they did not have a problem with the Majid shed.
Mrs. Majid stated that she had obtained the permit four years before and, at that time, there had
been no indication of a problem. She suggested there was no other appropriate location for the
shed and requested the Commission's favorable ruling.

Mr. Parnell added that the site is heavily treed and that the shed could not easily be moved. He

Commissioner Rosenof asked what had prompted the request for variance. Mr. Parnell responded

Commissioner Rosenof also questioned whether the foundation or slab for the shed had been
inspected by the City. Building Official Peg Bertolami responded that the shed apparently sits on
a slab that had not been inspected; there was neither permit nor inspection requested. The
Building Department would not have asked for a final survey with a shed of this value and a final
inspection would have been impractical. Since the slab was not inspected, Ms. Bertolami was
unable to state that the shed met the Florida Building Code.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz understood the owner's costs and he appreciated the comments made by
neighbors, but the Code was law. He expressed concern that approval of the variance would set
precedent and favored allowing more time to come up with an amicable solution. However, he
would not vote in favor of a variance without inspection of the slab assuring compliance with the
Florida Building Code.
Commissioner Rosenof questioned whether it might be forensically determined that the shed met
Florida Building Code. Ms. Bertolami believed that an X- ray -type of inspection could be done.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested that the matter could be tabled to allow time to determine
whether the shed meets Florida Building Code and noted that the inspection would be at the
expense of the homeowner. Once that was determined, he suggested staff should meet with the
homeowner to try to reach a solution that could be recommended to the Commission.
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis suggested that there must be evidence of conformance with the
Florida Building Code. It should also be understood that, with this evidence, the variance would
not automatically be granted.

Commission meeting.

Codes, and he believed that the concept of vetting should be included in the Ordinance.

have a vetting procedure for individual commissioner's appointments because they had not
