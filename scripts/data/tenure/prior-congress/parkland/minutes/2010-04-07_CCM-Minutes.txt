City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Ordinance No. 2010-07, Adopting

Procedures for CCNA Purchasing Policy, and said that this item was tabled at the March 17

meeting in order for some Commission-requested changes to be made by City Attorney Andrew

Maurodis.

City Attorney Maurodis explained the changes made to the Ordinance, in particular to Section

2E, regarding the Commission being able to re-rank the proposals upon the request of two

commissioners rather than by a majority vote.

Commissioner Moskowitz commented that he was the commissioner who asked for the changes

and he now feels it should go back to a majority in item E since everything else done by the

Commission is by majority.

Mayor Udine agreed with going by majority rule and noted that he does not feel the decision

should be given totally to staff.

There was a brief discussion regarding majority of the Commission versus majority of those

present. City Attorney Maurodis then clarified that a majority of those present would be the

language used in the Ordinance.

Mayor Udine opened to the public, there were no comments or questions, and the Mayor closed

to the public.

MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to approve Ordinance No. 2010-07, Adopting

Procedures for CCNA Purchasing Policy, on second reading, as amended. Seconded by

Commissioner Weissman.

ADDED AGENDA ITEM: DISCUSSION REGARDING CRIME LAB
Commissioner Weissman said that the Commission received notification of the possibility that
the Broward County Commission would not be funding the Broward County Crime Laboratory
under BSO. He noted that BSO is the largest accredited sheriff’s office in the country and stated
he feels that since they provide police services to the City, he does not want to see the crime lab
eliminated. Commissioner Weissman added that he would like to show support for retaining the
crime lab by having the City Commission send a resolution to the County Commission in support
of continued funding of the lab.
Mayor Udine spoke in support of such a resolution and about last year’s County budget hearing
and County taxes, which in part go to fund BSO regardless of whether a city’s police services are
contracted with BSO or provided by a municipal police department.
