1. DISCUSSION: PARKLAND BUSINESS SPONSORSHIP
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young gave a brief introduction of the item being discussed. She
commented that this item was brought up by Commissioner Weissman at the last City Commission
Meeting in which the possibility of additional opportunities for Parkland businesses was discussed.

Commissioner Weissman had brought up an opportunity of a partnership with the Chevron gas
station on 441. Staff has done a comprehensive analysis as to the options that can be offered. She
explained no recommendation was being made at this time. She then turned it over to Mr, Scott
Davidoff, Parks and Recreation Director.

Mr. Davidoff explained what was researched and gave an analysis on the City’s existing
sponsorship packages for the various events. He explained that he contacted a few other cities to
review their sponsorships and advertising efforts. His findings are that the City of Weston is the
only City that does not offer any sponsorship opportunities at all. All other City’s sponsorship
opportunities are event specific. He explained that none of the other cities allow advertising on
their website or in their City publications unless they are official sponsors of a specific event.

He described the efforts that the City of Parkland currently utilizes for its sponsorship and
advertising efforts. He explained that the City of Parkland offers the business spotlight on a
monthly rotating basis; Parkland only allows non-profit organizations to advertise meetings and
events on the City’s television station and E-mail Blasts; Parkland only publishes City business in
the Pipeline newsletter; Non-profit organizations are allowed to put information on the community
bulletin boards; Parkland allows for-profit and non-profit organizations to advertise in the new
resident packets.

COMMENTS BY MAYOR AND COMMISSION

Comments by Commissioner Weissman:

Commissioner Weissman noted that his comment is not just specific to the Chevron station. He
stated that some of the businesses that have taken advantage of some of the opportunities have not
necessarily been located in the City of Parkland. He feels it is to the City’s advantage to help the
businesses be viable given the current cconomic situation and the situation with commercial
properties.

Comments by Mayor Udine:

Mayor Udine commented that he would prefer that the City limit what the City does to a specific
sponsorship. He stated that the events the City provides are events that would take place regardless
of having a sponsor or not. He feels that when specific banner advertisement is solicited on the
City’s website, then the City is in competition with some of the Parkland web-based businesses
that advertise and send business to Parkland.

Comments by Vice Mayor Rosenof:

Vice Mayor Rosenof agrees with Mayor Udine and feels that the prices of the sponsorships are
quite consistent considering how much exposure they receive. He feels the City should maintain
its policy and limit the sponsorship to specific events. He feels by putting ads on the City’s
website, it hurts other Parkland businesses that have publications that are based here. Also he
feels that it cheapens the look of the website.

2. DISCUSSION: LOT CLEARING
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young explained how this item came up for discussion. She
explained that Dr. Leo Gosser suggested that the City re-evaluate its lot clearing Ordinance as a
means to deter coyotes from setting up dens in the overgrowth. She then turned it over to Brian
Archer, Management Analyst.
Mr. Archer explained that Ordinance 2006-26: Nuisance Abatement requires vacant lots less than
Ve-acre to be cleared in its entirety. Vacant lots greater than Y-acte are to be partially cleared with
limitations. He explained the reason the language was proposed in 2006 was because of the great
expense to clear a lot. He stated to clear 1-acre of land it costs approximately $8,000 — $10,000
and possibly more depending on the amount of debris that is generated as a result of the clearing.
He stated many property owners have 2-3 acre lots. He explained there are approximately 111
vacant lots in Pine Tree Estates in the Ranches and 40%-50% percent of the lots are maintained in
a mowed condition. Mr. Archer also explained that the proposed language for the Code revisions
could read as follows: vacant lots are required to be completely cleared of invasives and
overgrowth and maintained at a 12” height. He added there would need to be an exception
regarding preserves and natural areas. Although invasives can overgrow in the natural areas, staff
would like to have them removed as part of the lot clearing process.
Mayor Udine asked City Attorney Andrew Maurodis what would be the process with the amended
Ordinance. Attorney Maurodis explained the City would go through a Code Enforcement action.
Under certain circumstance under Chapter 162, the City could do the lot clearing if there is a
health safety hazard which could be part of the judgment in the Code Enforcement case. Assuming

3. DISCUSSION: TREE/SHRUB INVENTORY
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young gave a brief explanation of how this item came about for
discussion. She explained that the tree/shrub inventory is only in regards to the City’s
responsibilities in the medians. She then turned it over to Brian Archer, Management Analyst.
Mr. Archer gave a brief presentation and explained that his analysis includes missing, dead and
damaged shrubs and trees in the medians on portions of Holmberg Road, Trails End, University
Drive and all of Hillsboro and Mecca Boulevards. He stated the replacement cost is approximately
$50,000 which includes mulch, sod, fertilizer, irrigation replacement, etc. He expressed that it is a
considerable amount of money, but pointed out that it covers the entire City including five medians
and the appropriate size replacement of trees.
Commissioner Moskowitz commented there is a significant improvement on the west side of the
City. He said the medians look better, but some of the berms still need work. He pointed out
several medians in Coral Springs that needs work. He stated he is not suggesting that the City of
Parkland take thc medians over from Coral Springs, but asked City Manager Caryn Gardner-
Young to figure out how to get Coral Springs to show good faith to maintain their medians. He
stated he would like to do the budget transfer to spend the money on the medians and continue to
invest in the City of Parkland’s tree plantings.
Commissioner Weissman agrees with most of Commissioner Moskowitz” comments, but feels it
should wait until next years’ budget year.

Commissioner Moskowitz asked City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young if money could be taken

Commissioner Weissman asked how long it would take to get all the plantings done. Brian replied

make sure the City still has the reserves and do the other half in next year's budget.
