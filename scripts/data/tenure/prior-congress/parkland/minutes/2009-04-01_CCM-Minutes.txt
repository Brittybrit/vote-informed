V DISCUSSION: CHINESE DRYWALL ISSUE
Mayor Udine opened the discussion with a brief explanation of this issue. He had been made
aware of this issue from Parkland residents who contacted him to advise him of problems they

<<PG>>
PARKLAND CITY COMMISSION MEETING MINUTES

PAGE 4
were experiencing in regards to Chinese drywall in their homes. He had visited some of these
homes in the `Banyan Isles' community to inspect the issues directly and noted residents had
reported problems such as: a pungent sulfur -like odor inside the home, copper corrosion, air
conditioning unit problems and in some cases, other appliance malfunctions due to wire
corrosion and respiratory /sinus illnesses.
Mayor Udine spoke on the Tallahassee trip and recognized Holly Krulik, Joe Espinal, Gary
Schultheis, and John Willis for their efforts on this issue on behalf of all the Parkland residents
who are also dealing with this problem. The Mayor accompanied these same residents to
Governor Crist's office in order to speak on this issue and met with the offices of the Attorney
General, Department of Professional Regulation, and Health & Community Affairs. He also
thanked Senator Jeremy Ring, Representative Ari Porth and the City of Parkland lobbyist, Russ
Klenet for their assistance with these meetings.
Mayor Udine advised that Congressman Wexler also planned to send a letter on this issue to
Governor Crist's office requesting this issue be declared a health emergency in order to receive
Federal assistance and likened this situation to a "silent hurricane" with its potential destruction.
Commissioner Moskowitz advised he had first become aware of the issue through a long time
friend who had been experiencing mysterious problems, such as tarnished metals and reoccurring
illnesses, at her home in Coral Springs. He stated he then began researching the issue further and
felt setting up a task force could assist in raising the public awareness on this issue as well as
involve the entire community.
Commissioner Moskowitz suggested the task force have three to five members, including the
Chief Structural Inspector, Paul Wise, to gather information in reference to the problem within
the City of Parkland and report back to the Commission in two weeks for the next Commission
meeting on April 15, 2009. He believed the problem was similar to a natural disaster and the
information compiled will be used for a report that may be useful in the future. He stated he
planned to send the report once completed to every Congressman in the area, our Senators,
Governor Crist office and FEMA He stated he would like to see the City become the advocate for
its residents on this issue.
Vice Mayor Weissman added he believes people moved to the City of Parkland because it is a
city with a `heart'. He noted that while the City is stretched thin financially, the Commission is
committed to offering any resource they can in order to assist the residents during this crisis.
Commissioner Rosenof added while he believed in the City's involvement he felt there is a more
effective method other than a task force to accomplish their goals. He feels it is a Federal
government issue as it involves other States across the nation and noted Senator Martinez is
working on a bill to get the Consumer Product Safety Commission to be the lead agency to
handle this issue.

Commissioner Smith agreed with the Commission comments thus far and noted he is extremely
concerned for everyone who has been affected by the Chinese drywall and that the quicker they
receive some financial assistance the better.
Mayor Udine believed that in two weeks time this issue will have moved up to the next level to
the State and Federal Agencies.
Mayor Udine summarized the comments and added his suggestions.
• Get homes tested, determine if this is a health or property issue and then a declaration by
the State should be made to the Federal government. The City task force will coordinate
with the residents in order to get the testing completed.
• Create a web page dedicated to this issue on the City web site in order to provide all the
information currently available to the residents.
• Determine who is affected, which communities and what years the Chinese drywall was
used.
• Noted the issue is already affecting home sales; Coldwell Banker implemented a
mandatory Chinese drywall rider to disclose the issue on all home sales which affects the
market value of the home and the ability to sell.
• Noted a class action lawsuit may not be the best option for these residents as it can take
years and years to see any outcome.
• Utilize and encourage the local government stakeholders to be aware and involved on this
issue to help move it along a quickly as possible.
• Requested routine reports to be set up.
• Research if any insurance companies will cover this issue.
Commissioner Moskowitz stated the task force will include Murray Zweig, John Willis and the
City of Parkland's Chief Structural Inspector, Paul Wise. He also suggested that residents who
decide to move out of their homes contact their mortgage company or lender to advise of the
situation.
Mayor Udine reiterated the task force will not make any recommendations; their purpose is only
to gather information for the City Commission.
Commissioner Moskowitz requested funding be put aside for the task force operating costs.
Mayor Udine opened to the public.
Michael Ryan, an Attorney and resident of Banyan Isles, advised he is representing several
communities on this issue but also wanted to comment as a resident on the Tallahassee trip with
Mayor Udine. He commended the Mayor for being such a dedicated advocate for the residents
and to the Commission for its initiative in developing the task force. Mr. Ryan also shared
pictures of the copper wires from homes that have Chinese drywall and noted he was concerned
that there are still residents unaware of this situation.
