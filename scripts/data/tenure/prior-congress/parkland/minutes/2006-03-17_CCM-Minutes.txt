III PUBLIC INPUT

Mark Tanner, 6044 N.W. 83rd Terrace, and owner of Adanelle Interiors at 6644 Parkside Drive,
spoke regarding the sign code at Parkland Town Center and distributed hand-outs. He noted that
many things had been promised to them by the Center's ownership, including the use of glade
signs along pathways and store directories, which had not happened. Businesses were having a
difficult time surviving. Mr. Tanner stated they need better signage to let people know they are
there, and he asked the Commission's help.

Joe Vizzini, 6203 N.W. 1 10th Avenue and owner of a salon at Parkland Town Center, stated he
was grateful to Mr. Tanner for his remarks. He indicated this was his third appearance before the
Commission about getting a sign over his store, and he also distributed hand-outs. He noted that
the "Taste of Parkland" had been attended by 2,000 people; merchants were grateful for the
promotion, but people still did not know his shop was there. Mr. Vizzini believed it made no
sense that some stores had two signs and some had one, while his store had none. He noted that
six stores have gone out-of-business, and two more would leave within 60 days, and he hoped for
consideration from the Commission.

Mayor Udine commented that he had received other calls. He asked the City Manager's Office
to meet with the shop owners to try to work something out to help the businesses.

Former Mayor Sal Pagliara presented a book, in which the City was recognized and his
photograph included, for donation to the Library. It had been autographed by David Chang, an
internationally-renowned artist and FlU professor.

Mayor Pagliara thanked City Manager Mark Lauzier and Assistant City Manager Caryn Gardner-
Young for allowing him to participate in the design of Building Department facilities. He
mentioned that he had also met with staff and WCI's Scott Davidson about the design of the
University Drive intersection. He recognized that the decisions were ultimately those of the
Commission, but stressed that the look and design should be compatible with City Hall.

Mayor Udine thanked Mayor Pagliara for the book and his visit.

BUDGET ADJUSTMENT -
HOLMBERG RDIUNIVERSITY DR

COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, AUTHORIZING THE
THIRD AMENDMENT TO THE 2005-2006 FISCAL YEAR
CAPITAL IMPROVEMENT PLAN TO FUND THE
CONSTRUCTION OF ADDITIONAL ENHANCEMENT
FEA TURES FOR THE SIGNALIZATION OF HOLMBERG

THE REQUIRED BUDGET ADJUSTMENTS
NECESSARY TO ESTABLISH A NOT-TO-EXCEED
$200,000 CITY PROJECT BUDGET.

Assistant City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2006-55. She
explained that, after staff evaluation of the proposal, a shortfall was determined and it was felt
necessary to transfer the funds. Additionally, she indicated that staff had received information
that suggested the decision to install stamped concrete should be revisited, with consideration of
the paver option. The intersection design was displayed in the City Hall lobby, as well as at the
Library; additional copies for display in other locations were available upon request.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky thanked staff for bringing this back. Until her vote on the stamped
concrete, she had always felt comfortable with her votes, and she subsequently spent time
thinking about the vision that had been developed. She believed the Commission must strike a
balance between being fiscally responsible and keeping with the vision. She noted only a
$20,000 difference in price between pavers and the stamped concrete.

Mayor Udine recalled the discussion at Strategic Planning about the elegance of the City. He
asked if the $200,000 allocation would include the pavers, and Ms. Gardner- Young answered
affirmatively.

<<PG>>

Mayor Udine explained that this budget adjustment would move funds from another part of the
budget to finish this project, including the pavers; WCI had contributed most ofthe funds.

Commissioner Smith concurred with Vice Mayor Ribotsky. He noted the extensive use of pavers
in homes throughout the community, and commented that stamped concrete became very
slippery when wet.

Commissioner McGilvray stated that she was not concerned with the additional expense. She
agreed that pavers were more attractive, but she was concerned about their maintenance and how
long they would last.

Commissioner Moskowitz said he was neutral; however, he wanted to know where the money
would come from. Ms. Gardner-Young explained this was a line item in the budget, but there
was a reserve for capital improvements, separate from that used for the hurricane.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky noted that staff's recommendation had been asphalt, the least expensive
option; she hoped that future recommendations would also look to the City's vision.

City Engineer Charlie DaBrusco indicated that the construction method for the pavers would be a
concrete base, giving uniformity, stability, and no sinkage. He noted that pavers were more skid
resistant than stamped concrete, and believed they would have a longer life span.

Mayor Udine opened to the public.

Former Mayor Sal Pagliara agreed that the stamped concrete was very slippery and especially
dangerous for people walking in narrow heels. He believed the pavers would be far easier to
maintain, and he suggested using three-inch thick pavers with the six-inch base.

There were no further comments or questions, and Mayor Udine closed to the public.

Commissioner Moskowitz noted that the packet contained information about $113,000 cost to
install brick pavers. He agreed that the concrete base for the pavers was best, but he questioned
that the $20,000 cost for pavers would include that.

Mr. DaBrusco explained that the construction cost estimates contained six or seven options and
included a section concerning Chicago brick pavers, with a concrete header/curb and set on a
concrete slab, at a price of$128,000. Costs for each item were broken out; Chicago brick would
cost $53,000, while the generic brick would cost $22,000. With this substitution, the cost was
reduced to $100,000. He confirmed that $80,000 would have been spent on stamped concrete.

MOTION made by Vice Mayor Ribotsky to change from stamped concrete to brick pavers for
the intersection crosswalks, at an approximate difference of $20,000 in cost. Seconded by
Commissioner Moskowitz.

Assistant City Manager Caryn Gardner- Young read the title of Resolution No. 2006-12. She
recalled this had been continued from the last meeting, and she reviewed that the petitioner had
appeared before the Commission last year concerning entry features when the topic of the
carports was raised. The carports had since been removed and, with this amendment, the subject
of the western landscape buffer had been under discussion.

Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer gave a brief PowerPoint presentation regarding
the landscape issues and photographs of the site.

Mayor Udine announced this item was quasi-judicial.

Nectoria Chakas of Ruden, McClosky waived the quasi-judicial proceedings. She noted they had
been involved in this process for approximately a year. The Commission had authorized the
removal of the deteriorating carports, which were removed for safety concerns, and this process
eliminated the carports from the site plan. At the arrival of Hurricane Wilma, many trees in the
western buffer, which shielded the Winner's Circle development from Country Point, were
destroyed. Ms. Chakas noted that many of the trees were exotic, and they had been asked to
provide a new buffer for a distance of 1,600 lineal feet. Despite the provision of additional
landscaping where the carports were once located, the Planning and Zoning Board asked for an
additional commitment to a buffer along Hillsboro Boulevard and additional landscaping along
441 adjacent to their sign. Their landscape architect was present, and Ms. Chakas indicated they
would be happy to address any concerns.

Mayor Udine opened to the public.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky for letting her know on Monday. Mrs. Wilde was glad that the City had
negotiated with Winner's Circle to replant the buffer, which had first been seriously damaged by
the asphalt trail project in 2003 and then obliterated by Wilma. She complimented the City for
reducing its financial obligation by having Winner's Circle "clean up" the debris on the west side
of the trail, and she questioned the City's intentions for the area. She asked that the area be
incorporated into the tree replacement program, starting with a landscape enhancement of the
entire property west of the trail.

the trees now proposed for removal; the Code prohibited removal of trees from parks or rights-

ADDED ITEM:

CODE ENFORCEMENT DISCUSSION

Commissioner Moskowitz spoke regarding the long-standing code enforcement issues in the
Ranches, an area that had changed dramatically during the past 30 years. He believed the City's
system was broken and that selective enforcement was in use. He proposed the Commission
should direct staff to focus on certain violations, such as those affecting health and safety, and
issue a 30-day abatement for all other minor outstanding violations. Should the Commission not
provide staff direction within those 30 days, he suggested extending the abatement for another 30
days or until some direction was determined.

Mayor Udine asked the City Attorney's opinion on this proposal. Mr. Maurodis acknowledged
that Commissioner Moskowitz had approached him with this idea, and he believed this was
within the discretion of the Commission. He noted the important distinction that the abatement
was suggested for non-health and non-safety issues; it was not a moratorium or a waiver of
violations, but rather a holding off on prosecution.

Mayor Udine asked the status of the outside attorney's work on the Ranches issues and whether
there might be anything definitive on May 24th. Ms. Gardner-Young stated that, since the Code
Enforcement Board had been abolished, staff was working on the Special Master process and the
first hearing would be June. She confirmed the presentation on May 24th and indicated the
attorney would be present.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky stated that she would prefer not to make a decision until the information
was provided on May 24th; it could be part of that discussion. She offered that she had a problem
with abatements, and particularly if one area is singled out. While she agreed the situation was
getting out-of-hand, she hoped that the outsourcing of the Special Master and legal opinions
would make a difference. She did not believe, however, that these types of decisions should be
made until the ordinances and opinions talked about for so long were clarified. In the interim,
she believed that all enforcement should continue.

Commissioner Smith agreed with the Vice Mayor. He was aware that the City had one code
enforcement officer, and he believed there should be discussions about increasing that staff.

Commissioner McGilvray agreed the City needed more than one code enforcement officer, and
she offered that she had discussed this with the City Manager several times. The lack of
resources was definitely part of the problem as the City has relied upon neighbor-versus-
neighbor. She suggested that health and safety should be defined by the City Attorney, as well as
a better explanation of abatement and fines.

Commissioner Moskowitz provided further explanation about abatement, selective enforcement,
and existing issues in the Ranches. He suggested that the City should investigate outsourcing
code enforcement as well. He reiterated his request for a 30-day abatement in order to give staff
a code enforcement policy.

Mayor Udine stated that the Code should be enforced because all neighborhoods should abide by
it. He compromised with a seven-day abatement - until the discussion at the May 24th workshop,
when full direction to staff could be made.

Commissioner Moskowitz agreed with seven days, but suggested that it should also be
retroactive to the date of the fines.

Mr. Maurodis clarified that any existing Notice of Violation would be abated from the date the
violation was given until May 24th.

MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to abate any outstanding violations for seven
days, until May 24th.

Mr. Maurodis provided further clarification: this abatement pertained to the computation or
imposition of fines and was not meant to indicate that no violation exists or that the violation
would not be prosecuted. He noted that the abatement did not pertain to any violation for non-
permitted use or one that directly affects health and safety. Commissioner Moskowitz agreed.

MOTION seconded by Commissioner McGilvray.
