2. RESOLUTION NO. 2006-91A: RECONSIDERING THE
TERMINATION OF THE
COMMUNITY BUS SERVICE

COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, TO TERMINATE THE
INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT BETWEEN BROW ARD
COUNTY AND THE CITY OF PARKLAND TO PROVIDE
FOR PUBLIC TRANSPORTATION SERVICES
THROUGH THE COMMUNITY BUS SERVICE
PROGRAM AND TO TERMINATE THE CONTRACT
BETWEEN LIMOUSINES OF SOUTH FLORIDA AND
THE CITY OF PARKLAND TO OPERATE AND
MAINTAIN THE BUS AS REQUIRED UNDER THE
INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT; AND PROVIDING FOR
AN EFFECTIVE DATE.

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the title of Resolution No. 2006-91A. He reviewed that the bus
ridership had not met the County's criteria to continue the service. The City partially contributes
to the program, and it had been staff s recommendation during the budget process, and also at a
September meeting, to consider its cancellation.

Commissioner Moskowitz stated that he wished to keep the bus. While he had not been present
at the time of the original control, he understood that these things take time to work. He felt it
should be left in place until at least January, when the contract could be reexamined to more
narrowly tailor the route and its times.

Irvin Minney, Community Transportation Office of Broward County Transit, addressed the
Commission. He acknowledged that the agreement would be one year old in January and
acknowledged that it would take at least one year to build up ridership. Based on the numbers,
he believed that ridership was increasing. Mr. Minney agreed that the route may need to be
tailored to serve the areas of higher ridership, perhaps through changes in days or hours. He
stated that their staff would be happy to sit down with the City and be creative to develop a better
route that would serve the needs of the community.

Mark Leavitt, President of Limousines of South Florida, first thanked the City for the staff
support. He acknowledged areas in which the service was working -- Aston Gardens, in
particular -- and he offered their assistance.

Commissioner Moskowitz believed it important to recognize where the bus was successful, and
he believed the question was whether to deal with it now or in January. He felt that an
elimination of the bus would neglect Aston Gardens.

Vice Mayor McGilvray asked regarding the increasing numbers. Mr. Minney read the monthly
ridership figures, ranging from 146 in January to 520 in September and 320 for the first half of
October. He again stated that it usually took time for the ridership to build and the routes
understood, and he offered to provide a further analysis of ridership origination and destination
and to tailor the route or, if necessary, the contract to fit the needs of the community.

Assistant City Manager Caryn Gardner- Young added that the increased summer numbers
reflected the use by students not in school. She noted that residents of Aston Gardens had not
been considered as major riders in the original survey because they have their own transportation;
she had anticipated ridership from Terramar. She offered to do another analysis, if it was the
desire of the Commission, and she outlined the marketing efforts for the bus. The $23,000 cost
to the City was the difference between what is paid the contractor versus the reimbursement from
Broward County.

Mayor Udine questioned if the City's reimbursement would be jeopardized because five riders
per hour had not been met.

Mr. Minney stated that, as per the contract, Broward County Transit staff had an obligation to
assist the City, making sure that the route is successful and doing whatever is necessary to reach
the goal.

Mayor U dine commented that Aston Garden residents had stated that they would be willing to
pay for bus passes in order to keep the bus. From what he had seen, other than Aston Gardens,
the service did not appear a benefit. However, he believed that its future was at the will of the
Commission.

Vice Mayor McGilvray asked at what point the City would lose the reimbursement if the goal of
five riders per hour was not met. Mr. Minney replied that the County would continue to work
toward its success. He suggested the service should be tried another six months or even longer,
with other methods of increasing ridership. At some point, the County and City might come to
agreement that it was not working, but their obligation was to make it successful.

Vice Mayor McGilvray commented that she had originally voted against the community bus,
believing that it would not work in this City. She recalled agreement that the service would be
given six months and, while she acknowledged that ridership was slowly increasing, she would
not want to give it another year.

Commissioner Moskowitz suggested that the bus service should be left in place until January,
when the first year would end and when it could be decided to tailor the route and times for an
additional three months.

Vice Mayor McGilvray questioned that three months would be sufficient for a new schedule, and
Mr. Minney agreed that Broward Country Transit would need more time with a new route. He
suggested six months at a minimum, but it takes at least a year to build a route. He believed the

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the title of Resolution No. 2006-96. He commented that, with
the onset of the new budget year and the revamping of engineering, staff felt it important to
assure coverage in engineering inspections and plan review services. The City would utilize the
services of Coconut Creek's Utilities and Engineering Department, as offered and authorized on
an as-needed, hourly basis by Director Raj Verma, who was present. Mr. Lauzier recommended
Commission approval.

Vice Mayor McGilvray expressed concern about the cost effectiveness and efficiency in the
reporting process. She hoped that the same standards would be followed.

Public Services Director Jim Berkman explained the process, offering that a smooth transition
had already taken place through a blanket order, pending approval of the agreement. He noted
that the City's Code would take precedence; Coconut Creek's Code would apply should some
issue not be addressed. Inspections would be scheduled within 48 hours.

Mr. Lauzier added that the City was using a cost recovery system for the inspections, and Mr.
Berkman noted that the $65 rate per hour is far less expensive than a private firm.

Commissioner Smith asked specifics regarding the cost recovery, as well as the timeframe for the
use of an outside independent company. Mr. Berkman offered that this would be the contract for

AMENDING SECTION 22-4,

COMMENTS: SECOND READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND,
FLORIDA, AMENDING SECTION 22-4, ENTITLED
"DEFINITIONS"; PROVIDING FOR INCLUSION IN THE
CODE OF ORDINANCES; PROVIDING FOR A
SEVERABILITY CLAUSE; PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE
DATE. (1 st reading 9120/06)

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the second reading of Ordinance No. 2006-27.

Mayor Udine asked that the record reflect the presence of Special Counsel Jeffrey Siniawsky for
this and the next several items.

Mr. Siniawsky spoke regarding the Commission's legislative function and the requirement of
rational basis for its decisions. It was his understanding that the City had a traditional definition
of the term "forestry," and he briefly summarized the problems encountered by staff regarding
the definitions of "forest" and "large" and the decision to accept land use plan amendment and
ecosystem terminology. He believed that staff had provided rational bases for their
recommendations.

Planning Director Roberta Moore reviewed the definitions that had changed from first to second
reading. She noted that the Broward County Farm Bureau had provided a wealth of information.

Mayor Udine opened to the public.

Fred Segal, President of the Broward County Farm Bureau, first complimented staff for their
professionalism. He noted that the definitions had been sent to the Department of Agriculture,
Division of Forestry and to the nursery industry, and their responses had not foreseen any
problems. He believed the ordinances good.

Mike Squillace of the Ranches commented that he and other people had purchased their homes,
located on less than 10 acres, with covenants in place that allowed certain uses. He was
concerned that his rights, and those of his neighbors, were being eroded.

Mr. Siniawsky commented that staff has had the ability to reasonably interpret the ordinances.
The term could have been interpreted as 10 acres; they decided instead that the better practice
would be to better define it within the ordinances.

Todd Bates of the Ranches stated, for the record, his opinion that amending the definitions of the
permitted uses for AE-l would not only be a direct violation of his property rights, but also a
direct violation of the City's Charter. He believed the definitions in the zoning classifications
