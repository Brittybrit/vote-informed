1. RESOLUTION NO. 2007-45: SUPPORTING COCONUT CREEK'S
OBJECTION TO ADDITIONAL ACRES
DESIGNATED AS SOVEREIGN LAND
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, SUPPORTING THE
CITY OF COCONUT CREEK'S OBJECTION TO THE
SEMINOLE TRIBE'S APPLICATION FOR THE
DESIGNATION OF AN ADDITIONAL 44 ACRES OF
LAND AS BEING SOVEREIGN LAND.
Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2007-45. She
briefly explained the proposed expansion of the existing Seminole casino and the Seminole
request to designate additional acreage as sovereign. The City has been working with Coconut
Creek, Coral Springs, and Margate to obtain financial or other support due to the impacts this
casino may have on the surrounding cities. Staff had contacted the Governor's Office to try to
convey the casino impacts on the City in preparation for the Governor's compact negotiations
with the Tribe.
Mayor Udine stated that he was in support of the Resolution. He offered that he and the Vice
Mayor had attended the Commission meeting at Coconut Creek, where they had voted to allow
alcohol sales 23 hours per day. While he was personally opposed and the City had taken a
position of opposition, Coconut Creek had taken that action to allow competition with the Tribe.
He .believed it would be anever-ending cycle should more land be placed into trust.
Vice .Mayor Moskowitz also conveyed his support. He believed it important that Parkland
residents understand that the City could not affect the casino expansion. It was hoped that funds
to offset the impact could be obtained through Tallahassee.
Ms. Gardner-Young noted that the 23-hour alcohol sale ordinance in Coconut Creek would be
valid for one year and had a sunset clause. Coconut Creek's intention was to place stricter
regulations during that time period, having heard the concerns of residents and those of
neighboring cities.

City Attorney Andrew Maurodis explained this agreement was part-and-parcel of the work that
had been accomplished on the boundary change. He recalled that the Commission had
ambitiously determined last year that the Wedge property would be more properly developed
within Broward County, and specifically within the City of Parkland, than in Palm Beach
County. This "impossible dream" was coming closer to fruition through the efforts of the
Commission, the lobbyists chosen to act on the City's behalf, and the cooperation of the two
property owners.
Mr. Maurodis stated that approximately 20 property owners comprised the 2,000 Wedge
acreage. The pre-annexation agreement dealt with the two property owners, with an area of
approximately 650 acres, who had taken the lead and paid a majority of the City's costs. He
reviewed the background of the property owners' actions and the evolution of the agreement.
While the property owners had wanted some assurances, they recognized that the City could not
promise any specific land use plan amendment; it was a separate process, requiring public
hearings and other procedural hurdles.
Mr. Maurodis indicated that the agreements provided for a maximum density of 2 units of per
acre and 25 acres of commercial, which were generally acceptable to the City, as well as support
of the elimination of University Drive as a through-road. He believed it significant that the
agreements partnered property owners Huizenga (Triple HHH) and Mecca Farms (Debuys
Property Investment Group) with the City in that common goal. The mix and variety of units
would be similar to that of Heron Bay, as well as the amenities, landscaping, buffering, and
berming. High-quality commercial would be similar to that of State Road 7 and Trail's End;
there would be no strip centers. Additionally, the agreements contained more specific provisions
regarding park dedication, separate trail system dedications and construction, park-like
beautification and community appearance standards, and, most specifically, the donation of land
for a middle school, which would be dedicated as a condition of any land use plan change. Mr.
Maurodis indicated that the maximum the owners would have to pay under the present Broward
County rules was estimated from $4.2 million to $5.9 million.
Mr. Maurodis believed this agreement an important first step for the City. In most cases, the City
would like to be more specific, but this agreement promised the process of the land use plan
amendment, rezonings, platting, and site plan approvals. The property owners must complete
four significant stages.
Mayor Udine recalled the meetings of the past year with the developers, Mr. Maurodis, and City
staff to discuss tons of issues about what had once been considered a long shot. It had been a
difficult and monumental process, with much work involved, and he was happy that these
agreements would bring the City one step closer to achieving its goals. He agreed that it was
extremely important to remember that. these were just two of the property owners of the Wedge
and a small piece of the entire future development. The Mayor thanked everyone involved.
Mr. Maurodis also thanked the Mayor and Commission for their faith. He admitted that he had
been one of the people who thought it would not happen.
