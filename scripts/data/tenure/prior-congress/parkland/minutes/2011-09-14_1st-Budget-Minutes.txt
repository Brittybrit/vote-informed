City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of the first reading of Ordinance No. 2011-31: 
Adopting Millage. 
 
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis stated the following to adopt the millage ordinance into record: 
With the City of Parkland as the taxing authority, the millage rate for the fiscal year beginning 
October 1, 2011 and ending September 30, 2012 to be levied in order to produce sufficient ad 
valorem taxation revenue which when combined with other projected general fund revenues will 
be adequate to pay appropriations made in the budget for fiscal year beginning October 1, 2011 
and ending September 30, 2012 shall be the operating millage rate is 4.0198. The adopted 
millage rate of 4.0198 mils is 2.34% more than the roll back rate of 3.9279 and is computed 
according to the guidelines set forth in Florida Statute 200.065. 
 
Nancy Morando, Finance Director, gave a brief recap of the budget – See attached presentation. 
 
Mayor Udine commented that over the past few years, the City Commission has tried to maintain 
sensitivity about the amount of property taxes each resident pays. He explained that the City of 
Parkland has one of the lowest millage rates in Broward County. He stated that the reason the 
City Commission decided to keep the millage rate the same as last year is because this is the first 
year in many years that the property values have started to go back up. 
 
Mayor Udine also said the solid waste fee was moved to the trim notices as a pass through or 
collection method and it worked out to be slightly lower than the prior year’s fee. He stated that 
it is important to look at the overall tax bill and he pointed out that 20% of the payment is the 
City of Parkland’s portion, however, the remaining dollars go to other taxing entities that the 
City has no control over. He said that he randomly viewed some of the community’s tax bills 
and most of the taxes have gone down. 
 
Commissioner Moskowitz commented that staff has shrunk the size of government employees by 
22%. He stated that he will support the hiring of two new police officers, but asked if staff can 
delay the hiring of one officer until after BSO analyzes the camera surveillance system. City 
Manager Caryn Gardner-Young explained that she would have to bring back a contract 
amendment on October 5, 2011 to account for the two officers. Then at the November 16, 2011 
workshop, the camera surveillance system can be further discussed. She mentioned that these 
two officers were to be part of a power squad in which they will act on an as needed basis. She 
was concerned that hiring one will not serve the function. 
 
Further discussion ensued. 
 
Ms. Gardner-Young suggested that the contract amendment and camera surveillance system can 
be placed on the same meeting. 
 
Commissioner Moskowitz asked if a 6 foot sidewalk is standard. He commented that the City 
should establish a standard for sidewalks. He asked if asphalt sidewalks have specific standards.

Mr. Esmond Scott, Public Works Director explained that there is a Grade A asphalt that the City 
of Parkland uses which is the standard. Commissioner Moskowitz also stated that he was happy 
to see the Cost Of Living Adjustment (COLA) taken out and a merit plan implemented instead. 
 
Commissioner Weissman asked if recycled material could be used instead of asphalt for the 
sidewalks. Mr. Esmond Scott explained that approximately three months prior to his 
employment, some road milling was done in the City of Parkland using Recycled Asphalt 
Pavement (RAP). Commissioner Weissman asked if it was cost prohibitive to use this material. 
Mr. Scott explained that using RAP is inexpensive because it is material you already have and if 
a project needs to be done right away, it holds up well at either 4” or 6” depth and properly 
prepared. 
 
Commissioner Weissman also reiterated that the City of Parkland has the lowest millage rate in 
Broward County at 4.0198 and if our residents lived in any other City, they would be paying 
significantly higher taxes. 
 
Vice Mayor David Rosenof commented that in the future, the City Commission will have to 
think about adding another BSO police zone. He also mentioned that there needs to be a more 
systematic way to put money away in a dedicated maintenance fund for air conditioning repairs, 
painting and other maintenance. 
 
Mayor Udine opened to the public.

rate low, but congratulates the City Commission for doing so. He said this is one of the richest 
communities and the City Commission should increase the millage. He said having a low 
millage does not keep the city sustainable.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2011-76: Fire Rescue 
Assessment. She reiterated that staff is recommending the fire assessment rate of $225 to cover 
the increase in the fire assessment and to provide monies for future infrastructure needs in the 
Fire facilities. She stated that if the City Commission desires to go with the fire assessment fee 
of $210, staff has prepared a resolution based on that decision. 
 
Mayor Udine gave a summation of what the increased rate would cover. He stated the fire 
contract went up $10 and the replacement vehicles went up $5. He asked if there were enough 
votes for going to $225. There did not appear to be enough votes. 
 
Mayor Udine opened to the public. 
 
Stuart Krantz, commented that the fire station should be built when the first Certificate of 
Occupancy (CO) is issued. He referenced that Heron Bay does not have a permanent fire station. 
He feels that based on the amount of money in the community, the fire assessment fee should be 
raised as high as possible to build permanent fire stations now. 
 
Commissioner Moskowitz stated that over the last year, the City has been able to raise $1Million 
towards building the fire station due to development; therefore, the City does not have to pass 
those costs on to the residents. 
 
Mr. John Willis, lives in Heron Bay, asked when will the actual fire station be built, where will it 
be built and when can the residents in Heron Bay expect completion. 
 
Mayor Udine explained that the City is looking into whether the location will remain in its 
current location or if it will be moved to another location within the wedge. 
 
Commissioner Weissman clarified that the recommendation from the Coral Springs Fire Chief, 
Mark Curran, was that the current location of the fire station will change and land has been set 
aside and included in the wedge master plan. His goal is to have the fire station built before the 
residents move in. 
 
There were no others present and Mayor Udine closed to the public. 
 
MOTION made by Commissioner Weissman to approve Resolution No. 2011-76: Fire Rescue 
Assessment with a fire rescue assessment fee of $210. Seconded by Commissioner Moskowitz.
