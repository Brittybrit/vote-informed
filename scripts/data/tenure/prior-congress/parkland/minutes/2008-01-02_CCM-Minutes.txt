Planning Director Roberta Moore explained that the applicant was requesting an amendment to
the previously approved site plan for the mixed use portion of the Parkland Village site, located
just south of, the school site, north of the multi family, and east of the single- family residential.
The petitioner has requested: to reduce the height of the mixed use buildings from three stories to
two stories; to modify the village green parking lot from parallel parking to ninety degree parking;
to modify the building elevations; to approve preliminary civil engineering plans; to approve the
shared parking study; to approve the site plan for the Parkland Village Square and Village Green
area; and to approve the master sign program.
Ms. Moore explained each in more detail. She indicated that staff recommends denial of the
master sign program as the applicant has proposed no uniformity as per Code. They would like
the tenants to be able to utilize their own font, color, and styles. She explained the shared parking
resolution; staff is requesting that 10,000 square feet of restaurant uses be set aside until the
restaurant building obtains site plan approval by the City Commission. This would guarantee that
the restaurant buildings would be built and that parking would be available.
Mayor Udine asked for an explanation for what is meant by the 10,000 -foot restaurant parking
condition.
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young added that the Planning Zoning Board reviewed this
petition; they approved everything except for the master sign plan.
Mayor Udine clarified that the Planning Board approved the shared parking and the reduction of
the height of the mixed use building and recommended denial on the master sign plan.
Patti Hitchcock, representing the applicant, used a PowerPoint presentation to explain their
proposals, and she gave a brief history of the site.
Ms. Hitchcock explained that, when first brought before the Commission in 2005, they did not
present a master sign program, and she explained their proposal in more detail. They would like
to allow for more flexibility to their tenants, rather than having all the same font and all the same
color. She said that when this center was designed the idea was that this would be more of a
traditional town center with individual proprietors. They understand that the City may not be
comfortable with different colors, so they could propose a single color program but at the same
time allow the diversity in the font. They would also like to allow the national trademark retailers
their logos and some color to allow them to be readily identified.
The presentation boards were reviewed by the Commission.
Mayor Udine gave a brief history of this project going back to 2005. He recalled that the original
concern was whether the commercial would do as well as the residential; it had since flipped
flopped because of the real estate market.

City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2008 -06, Waste
Management Agreement.
Mayor Udine noted that this Ordinance would authorize the execution of the same agreement that
had been discussed at the past few meetings, which included the additional bulk and single- stream
recycling but eliminated the 96 -gallon rollout container. Trash and debris would be disposed of as
in the past. While he was in agreement with the additional recycling, "almost" in agreement with
the roll -outs, and the need for additional costs, he believed that residents would have a problem
with the changes all at once. The Mayor did not believe the City was at a point that the service
could be switched without the residents having a problem with the 96- gallon limitation, and he
would like to continue with trash removal as in the past.
Mayor Udine asked regarding the vacation credit, and Waste Management representative Tony
Spadaccia indicated that the same process would continue as that in effect. The Mayor related a
situation of a part-time resident owning two homes in the City, one of which was vacant. Mr.
Spadaccia offered that, while the situation could be examined, Waste Management must have its
equipment and workers available at all times. He suggested that some type of legitimate
exception could be considered and created.
Questioning the City Attorney, Commissioner Weissman understood that an indemnification
agreement was under negotiation with Waste Management, with which Mr. Maurodis responded
affirmatively. The Commissioner asked confirmation that Mr. Maurodis had asked for $2 million
in indemnification due to the action of the Commission in voting for this contract.
Mr. Maurodis recalled past discussion concerning going out for bids for the contract, and his
opinion had been that the "bulletproof' guaranteed route would be to bid the contract, as
indicated through case law and argument. He stated that bidding the contract would be the zero
risk option. He noted that other cities in Broward County had not bid and were not challenged;
he believed that excellent argument also existed that these contracts did not require bidding. Mr.
Maurodis indicated that his concern was to reduce the City's risk, and he wanted Waste
Management to assume it through his suggestion of the $2 million indemnity. Outside counsel
would also be asked an opinion that the indemnity was valid and not contrary to public policy. He
reiterated that he wanted to shift as much of the risk as possible. Waste Management was
confident in its position and had been cooperative.

<<PG>>

At Commissioner Weissman's question, Mr. Maurodis indicated that Waste Management had
made a good start in the negotiation for indemnification, but he had rejected it due to language
and some limitations on the amount, acknowledging that it was not the $2 million he had
requested. Mr. Maurodis reiterated that, because of the issues previously stated, he strongly felt
that he could not recommend going through with the contract without the indemnity. He
acknowledged that, in the past 20 years, he could not recall having previously asked for this type
of indemnity.
Mayor Udine recalled that the City had not bid this out at the last contract, which Mr. Maurodis
confirmed, and Commissioner Weissman noted they were extensions of the original contract. Mr.
Maurodis stated his belief that the original contract awarded Waste Management had not been
bid. He reiterated that case law supported both positions: award of the contract without bid but
with greater risk, or offering the contract for bid with zero risk. He believed the middle course
without offering the contract for bid would be to have solid indemnity and a valid attorney's
opinion.
Commissioner Weissman commented that he would be voting for zero risk.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested that half of Broward cities had simply awarded the contract,
and Mr. Maurodis confirmed that a number had. Ms. Gardner -Young indicated that the norm in
the field was not to go out for bid.
Mr. Spadaccia commented that Coconut Creek and Weston were currently negotiating with their
hauler. Pembroke Pines had completed a new five -year negotiation with their hauler; Miramar,
Wilton Manors, Cooper City, and Lighthouse Point had done this process within the last two
years. He noted that Cooper City and Lighthouse Point had gone out for bid and had thrown the
bids out, choosing to negotiate with their existing hauler.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz believed that Mr. Maurodis had always given good advice and was often
"ahead of the curve." He suggested that the indemnity issue should not hold up first reading;
however, if the City and Waste Management could not see "eye -to -eye" within the next two
weeks, he would choose to follow the City Attorney's advice to limit the City's liability.
Commissioner Smith asked whether residents would be given the new recycling container, which
was answered affirmatively. The Commissioner felt that residents would be more apt to recycle
with larger containers.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked regarding a pilot program, wherein a group might choose to try the
96- gallon rollout container. He believed it might help with the transition. Mr. Spadaccia
responded that it could be created using a defined area and a certain time period. Mayor Udine
suggested this could be considered in the future for specific communities if interest was shown.
The Vice Mayor also questioned Waste Management's commitment to the planned educational
program, and Mr. Spadaccia confirmed their commitment.

City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2008 -03.
Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer explained that the City's arbor street ordinance
has had the requirement that a buffer must be replaced within one year. He had changed the
requirement to replanting within six months from removal; however, he felt it important to leave
the one -year requirement for opaqueness. Mr. Archer noted that the Holmberg Road landscape
theme and the arbor street ordinances were similar, and he felt it important that they be consistent
in their provisions.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that there had been a failure in the original ordinance in that
the buffer at the Parkside Drive circle had been cleared. The ordinance, as it existed, allows the
property owner one year to replant, and the Vice Mayor felt that even six months was too long.
He would like to see replanting within 90 days.
Mr. Archer agreed that 90 days would be adequate. He stated that he had made the change to six
months as it had been the direction of the Commission at that time.
Mayor Udine asked how long ago the Parkside Drive circle property was cleared, and Mr. Archer
estimated that it had been two to three months.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz felt that three months for replanting was adequate and that nine months
should be allowed for opaqueness. While Mr. Archer agreed with 90 days for replanting, he felt
that one year should be allowed for opaqueness.
Mayor Udine indicated that he would support the change to 90 days.
Mayor Udine opened to the public. There were no comments or questions, and the Mayor closed
to the public.

7. ORDINANCE NO. 2007 27: CODE ENFORCEMENT LIENS
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the second reading of Ordinance No. 2007 -27. She
noted that no changes had been requested at the first reading of the Ordinance, and staff
recommended approval as presented.
Mayor Udine recalled that City Attorney Maurodis had recommended this change to make certain
that the City's code enforcement liens have the appropriate priority. Mr. Maurodis commented
that, with increasing foreclosure actions, this change would give the City a better position.
Mayor Udine opened to the public. There were no comments or questions, and the Mayor closed
to the public.
