III PUBLIC INPUT
John Willis, 12334 N.W. 80 Place in Heron Bay, stated that he had previously corresponded
with members of the Commission and the City Manager about the possibility of obtaining a
Parkland post office. He and other residents believed existing post offices too far from their area
of the City. He had been in touch with the offices of both U.S. Representatives Ron Klein and
Robert Wexler, who were receptive. He noted an effort to name a post office for former Parkland
resident Daniel Agami, who died in Iraq, and he would very much like to see the City have its
own post office named after him. Mr. Willis asked that the City put forth a Resolution or
memorandum to support the efforts of the Representatives with the U.S. Postal Service.
Mayor Udine asked the City Manager to agendize a discussion of this at a future meeting. He
believed the effort would be an uphill battle because new post offices were not being built. The
City had gone on record in the past and would willingly do so again.
Stuart Krantz, 6348 N.W. 62' Terrace, asked that the park area near the Old City Hall trail be
called "Cypress Dome." He spoke regarding: a sign at the natural area behind the Wal -Mart in
Coconut Creek; his perception of "hometown" and the response to an e -Better Place complaint;
and the environmental efforts of Plantation showcased in a magazine he distributed to the
Commission.
There were no further comments or questions, and Mayor Udine closed to the public.

2. RESOLUTION NO. 2008-56: INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT
FOR FIRE PROTECTION SERVICES
THROUGH AUTOMATIC AID
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, APPROVING AN
INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE CITY OF
PARKLAND, CITY OF COCONUT CREEK, CITY OF
CORAL SPRINGS AND CITY OF MARGATE FOR FIRE
PROTECTION SERVICES THROUGH AUTOMATIC
AID.
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Resolution No. 2008 -56. She explained this
agreement would codify actions already being taken for the City's eastern fire station. The
intention was to eliminate any boundaries for emergency response, as the closest station would be
dispatched. She noted this agreement resulted in a cost savings, a time savings, and good inter
cooperation. Training efforts would also be coordinated. The agreement would be through
September 2008 as a test of its effectiveness, and she anticipated that, if advantageous, there
would be six month reviews. Ms. Gardner -Young noted that the agreement had been adopted by
the other jurisdictions, and staff recommended approval.
Commissioner Smith commented that faster services would be provided to the Country Acres
neighborhood through this agreement.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked that the number of coordinated responses during the contract be
tracked and provided for a September 30 renewal.
Mayor Udine believed this agreement would improve response times in the eastern side of the
City. He felt it a step in the right direction towards regionalization of Fire and EMS in northwest
Broward, providing a savings to all four jurisdictions.
MOTION made by Vice Mayor Moskowitz to approve Resolution No. 2008 -56, Interlocal
Agreement for Fire Protection Services through Automatic Aid. Seconded by Commissioner
Smith.
Mayor Udine opened to the public. There were no comments or questions, and the Mayor closed
to the public.

City Attorney Andrew Maurodis recalled the discussion of the last meeting. He explained that
January 1, 2009, had been inserted to follow the intent of the Commission, as well as a "waiver"
provision that would allow the City to provide relief should the impact create an inordinate
burden upon the developer. He believed it flexible, but with enough of a standard.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that, while the waiver and flexibility was important, a
variance to the Ordinance could also be approved. Mr. Maurodis offered that the waiver would
be easier and less challengeable.
Mayor Udine noted that this Ordinance would give the City the ability to request solar lighting.
He acknowledged that the Ordinance had been sent to the different shopping center owners. He
asked that, before second reading, the Ordinance also be sent to the four officers of the Chamber
of Commerce, explaining to them that it would not require existing buildings to do anything at this
time; they would have 10 years.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz noted that Hallandale had either recently adopted, or would soon adopt,
a solar lighting ordinance. He reiterated his concern that adequate solar lighting would be
provided, and he asked that the City approve lighting replacements. Mr. Maurodis responded that
Ordinance would become one part of an extensive code section. He noted that a new lighting
fixture, such as a replacement, must come to the Commission for approval; however, the repair of
an existing fixture would not.
Following further discussion, Vice Mayor Moskowitz restated the Commission intent that all
existing lighting be converted to solar power by 2018. Mr. Maurodis indicated that he would
further clarify, and he suggested that the Ordinance be adopted on first reading as amended.
Commissioner Smith noted that the ultimate cost for conversion to solar lighting would be passed
from the landlords to the tenants.
Commissioner Weissman suggested that signage, both individual businesses and monument signs,
should also be included in the Ordinance. He believed that the solar aspect should be covered.
Mayor Udine commented that the sign ordinance changed on a regular basis, and he asked that
this signage be addressed on its next review. He suggested publication of a notice. Mr. Maurodis
responded that a 180 -day Zoning in Progress for signage would be issued.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested some ambiguity occurred since signage was outdoor lighting.
Mayor Udine believed signage should specifically be excluded in this Ordinance. He felt it would
be "cleaner" through the sign ordinance.
Mr. Maurodis reviewed that a Zoning in Progress would be undertaken; he would clarify that this
Ordinance did not pertain to signs; and he would begin the appropriate amendment to have a
separate section to apply in the sign ordinance.

MOTION made by Vice Mayor Moskowitz to approve the first reading of Ordinance No. 2008-
15, Solar Lighting, as amended. Seconded by Commissioner Smith
Mayor Udine opened to the public.
Stuart Krantz, 6348 N.W. 62n Terrace, stated his opposition to the Ordinance. He believed the
City's obligation for public facilities should also be included.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that he agreed with Mr. Krantz' premise, and he noted that
the City was already doing its part. He outlined the projects under investigation.
Mr. Krantz asked regarding the waste of electricity with park lights when fields were no longer in
use. The Vice Mayor agreed, indicating that motion sensor technology and timers have been
investigated. It was, however, a different technology and wattage than parking lot lighting.
Mayor Udine commented that the Parks and Recreation Advisory Board had discussed this
subject many times. In most cases, lights were on for scheduled league play, and they were
nervous about shutting them off immediately following the scheduled use. The Board believed
the lights should be left on for safety while the parks were open. The Mayor acknowledged that
both the Commission and Board had struggled with this issue.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested trying the shutdown of areas not used, or phasing lights on and
off, to save energy.
Ms. Gardner -Young agreed with the Mayor's comments, and she added that the lights took some
time to power up and that it was often difficult to get the fields vacated. Fields were scheduled
for play and games were sometimes canceled.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz believed that the Parks and Recreation Advisory Board should again
discuss ways of energy conservation. He suggested that one park might be designated to close
earlier than the other to save energy, giving Terramar and Pine Trails as an example of the main
parks. He noted that this could also apply to the smaller parks.
Mayor Udine believed that the field space issue would again be debated; he felt it would be a
problem and that there would be significant resistance. He suggested that Mr. Davidoff present
these and other ideas, perhaps including the practices of other cities, to the Parks and Recreation
Advisory Board for discussion and recommendation. The leagues and field usage would need to
be evaluated.
Robert Mayersohn, 6742 N.W. 80 Manor, stated that he agreed with the Vice Mayor that the
City must be more energy conscious, but he believed efforts were convoluted in the sense that the
City would create a mandate for the developer without encouraging other energy savings options
as alternatives. He understood the good intentions, but he believed the Commission should give
consideration to other methods of energy sources and savings.
