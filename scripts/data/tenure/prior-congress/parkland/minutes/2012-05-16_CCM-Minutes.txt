1. PRESENTATION: CHARTER REVIEW ADVISORY BOARD
CHARTER AMENDMENT RECOMMENDATIONS

Mayor Udine thanked the Charter Review Advisory Board Committee members for their participation
in the Charter review.
Mr. Doug Kruse, Charter Review Advisory Board Chairman presented three (3) proposed Charter
amendments to be placed on the November, 2012 ballot.
The three (3) proposed Charter Amendments relate to:

1. The filling of vacancies :

2. Establishing minimum voting requirements for Commission action; and

3. Process of redistricting
Mr. Kruse started his report discussing the Filling of Vacancies - Article 4 Section 4.08 of the Charter.
He explained that the committee came up with two viable alternatives: 1) Prompt Special Election; and
2) Commission Vacancy Appointment. He explained that in the end, the committee chose prompt
special clection to allow the citizens the right to choose who will represent them on the City
Commission. He then explained in detail the prompt special election requirements.
He explained that the Charter Review Board felt the second proposed amendment was a glaring
omission in the City Charter. He stated that the current quorum requirement states there has to be
three members to hold a meeting and conduct business. The Charter committee is proposing that
language be included in the Charter that in order for an action to pass the Commission it needs an
affirmative vote by three (3) members. He stated that the minimum voting requirement would be a
new Article 4 - Section 3.08.
The final arca of proposal is Redistricting — Article 3 Section 3.02. There were three key components
that the Committee felt was essential in the redistricting process. 1) The boundaries to be created is
based on equal population in contiguous borders to eliminate possible gerrymandering. 2) A
competitive process for selecting the redistricting consultant and 3) Allow citizens to be aware of the
options before the Commission takes action.
Other areas that future Charter Review Board members may want to address were attendance
requirements for Commissioners, single member districts vs. citywide elections, monetary threshold
for sale of city property, etc.
Two additional recommendations that’s not Charter related, but in the interest of transparency is to
place salaries and allowances of the City Commission on the website for accessibility to the public.
No recommended changes are being made to salaries at this time. The Charter Review Board also
recommended utilizing the weekly email blast to mention important issues coming before the City
Commission.
Mayor Udine agrees that the City Commission salary information should be posted for residents to see.

business.

Commissioner Moskowitz supports the Charter amendment recommendation that three (3) votes be
required for City Commission to take action, however, he expressed if there was an emergency
situation and two Commissioners were unavailable that the City Commission could still take action
with a 2-1 vote.

Commissioner Willis expressed concerns about vacancies on the City Commission due to resignations
or illness, etc. where two (2) Commissioner’s vote represents the entire City.

Mayor Udine felt that the current Charter requires three members to be present to have a quorum,
however, if one or two members have a conflict, they can walk out and the item dies.

Three members of the City Commission were a “no” vote on the Charter amendment establishing
minimum voting requirements for City Commission action.

Commissioner Moskowitz disagrees on the Charter amendment to fill vacancies. He is in favor of
voting by election, but for good government and to keep costs down, he believes there should be an
appointment, but the amount of time that the appointment should be in place before a Parkland
election is called should be no longer than two years.

 INSTRUCTIONS FOR COMPLIANCE WITH SECTION 112.3143, FLORIDA STATUTES
 A person holding elective or appointive county, municipal, or other local public office MUST ABSTAIN from voting on a measure which
 — inures to his or her special private gain or loss. Each elected or appointed local officer also is prohibited from knowingly voting on a mea-
 sure which inures to the special gain or loss of a principal (other than a government agency)by whom he or she is retained (including the
 parent organization or subsidiary of a corporate principal by which he or she is retained); to the special private gain or loss of a relative; or

 163.357, F.S., and officers of independent special tax districts elected on a one-acre, one-vote basis are not prohibited from voting in that

 For purposes of this law, a "relative" includes only the officer's father, mother, son, daughter, husband, wife, brother, sister, father-in-law,

 enterprise with the officer as a partner,joint venturer, coowner of property, or corporate shareholder(where the shares of the corporation
 are not listed on any national or regional stock exchange).
