11,

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title on first reading of Ordinance No. 2011-36. She

stated that the companion which is the next item Resolution No. 2011-95 that is impacted should

the City Commission approve Ordinance No. 2011-36. She explained that the Resolution is a

dedication to fulfill the City’s park requirements. Ms. Gardner-Young referred to Ms. Roberta

Moore, Planning & Zoning Director for a brief presentation.

Ms. Moore stated that this was a quasi-judicial hearing.

Mr. Dennis Mele, on behalf of the applicant, waived the quasi-judicial procedure.

Ms. Moore proceeded with her presentation on the rezoning and master plan for the Debuys
project. Ms. Moore gave the approximate location of this project and stated that it was annexed
into Parkland in 2009. The rezoning is for 368.999 acres from Palm Beach County special
agricultural to City of Parkland planned residential development and 10.678 acres from Palm
Beach County special agricultural to City of Parkland planned commercial development. She
stated that the master plan includes 526 single family dwelling units, 250 multi-family units, two
public park sites which is part of their dedication, a clubhouse use and a commercial parcel that has
a maximum of 110,000 sq. feet. Ms. Moore noted that the 250 multi-family units and the 110,000
sq. feet of commercial were maximum levels that were set by the Department of Community
Affairs (DCA) during the land use process. She stated that after the second reading of the rezoning
and master plan, the City Commission will be presented with the site plan of the project. She
explained that the developer will be proposing 50° and 65° wide lots. She said that Lennar
Builders is proposing to dedicate park land instead of money. Ms. Moore requested that all
documentation that is part of the application be part of the public record. She stated that the
property owner has not submitted clevations for their single family or multi-family developments
as of yet. The Planning & Zoning Department approved this application 5 to 1 based on staff's
recommendations and there are a number of conditions of approval tied to the rezoning and master
plan.

Mayor Udine commented on the requirement from the DCA and at one time they wanted the whole
project to be multi-family and a lot more density. He said that they were saying it needed to be 2
or 3 thousand units.

Commissioner Moskowitz added that even after it passed the House, the Senate and the Governor
has signed it, it still needed to be approved by DCA (Department of Community Affairs). He
explained that DCA was trying to master plan this area, so the Mayor flew to Tallahassee for an
emergency meeting to convince DCA that this was not what the City wanted and this was not what
the developer wanted and it was not in the best interest of the City. At that point the Wedge parcel
would not have happened because the City would not have agreed to their plan.

Mayor Udine said that DCA was trying to turn the Wedge area into a high density transportation
hub with rapid transit. He said this application was the end result of the negotiation with DCA.
Mayor Udine said he was fine with them donating land for parks.

Commissioner Rosenof questioned the $800,000 ad valorem for 526 units and why did they not
include the multi-family and the commercial. Ms. Moore stated that Lennar does not have a
product for the multi-family at this time so it would be difficult to determine what the ad valorem
would be. Commissioner Rosenof also questioned the need for more commercial in the City and
stated he does not want to see another commercial parcel that the City cannot support. He also
mentioned that there should be some type of regulation as to how they will maintain the land for
the commercial property until the commercial is built. He would like their agreement that it will be
maintained to a certain standard and not left as an ugly vacant lot.
