5. CONSIDERATION AND DISCUSSION ON THE CLEAN-UP OF
TERNBRIDGE PRESERVE

City Manager Mark Lauzier noted this was another Hurricane Wilma-related project. A report
concerning the three-phase clean-up of the Preserve had been provided to the Commission, and
he invited Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer to provide details of the
recommendation and answer Commission questions.

Mr. Archer believed that Ternbridge had suffered the most extensive damage of the City's
preserves, and he displayed photographs. A three-phase approach was recommended. The first
phase would be removal of debris and storm-damaged trees. The second phase would be
treatment of invasive exotic plants and vines that are proliferating with the increased exposure
due to the loss of tree canopy, and an approximate cost of $17,000 was given. The third phase
would be replanting, and staff recommended the use of various size cypress trees and other trees
indigenous to this area at a cost of approximately $30,000.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky thanked staff for the diligent work. However, she expressed concern about
the invasive treatment, questioning environmental impacts with the use of chemicals on the trees
and close to homes, as well as the initial expense and monthly maintenance. She asked if there
might be a better way, such as using crews to cut them out.

<<PG>>

PARKLAND CITY COMMISSION MINUTES
MARCH 22, 2006
PAGE 10

As to the replanting, the Vice Mayor acknowledged the wetness of the area and suggested it may
be too wet to plant for this hurricane season. She also asked regarding the buffering effect of the
species selected.

Mr. Archer responded that cypress trees will provide a taller canopy; other trees, such as Dahoon
holly, will help with mid-story, and the ferns would come back once the exotics were maintained.
He acknowledged that it would take time to fully screen the area.

As to the chemicals, Mr. Archer stated there would be no risk to residents or the groundwater or
trees, provided it was done properly, and he believed it his job to make sure that it was. He
explained the procedures. Removal of the invasives by hand would be much more labor
intensive, and it would require continuing attention. He acknowledged that the monthly
maintenance was "overkill," which he would discuss with the contractor as he felt quarterly care
would be sufficient.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky also asked regarding the third phase, with potential funding through
Broward Beautiful and the Ternbridge Homeowners' Association and the total dollars that may
be spent. Mr. Lauzier anticipated spending between $150,000 and $160,000. With the
Commission's authorization, the hurricane reserve would be tapped for removal, spraying, and
replacement. While it was hoped that community contributions would enhance the project
further, Mr. Lauzier believed that the City should be prepared to foot the entire bill.

Commissioner McGilvray commented regarding the application of chemicals, and asked
confirmation that invasives, and especially pepper, could also germinate from other areas. Mr.
Archer stated this was true, but suggested that the isolation of the area would allow a much better
job of keeping the exotics down. The Commissioner asked regarding treatment of all of the
City's preserves, specifically mentioning Forman and N.W. 8ih Avenue. Mr. Archer indicated
that the 10 acres of the Holmberg Road and N.W. 8ih Avenue preserve received an initial
treatment and three follow-ups, while the 28 acres received an initial treatment and a follow-up
approximately two to three years ago. They must be retreated, but he offered this was part of the
agreement with Broward County for maintenance.

Commissioner McGilvray suggested that all preserves should have a maintenance program. Mr.
Archer responded that maintenance occurred on a regular basis; however, all of the projects have
not yet reached that point. The goal was that follow-up maintenance would take place twice a
year. Using the Forman site as an example, Mr. Archer indicated that the City would take it over
at the end of this year; he wanted the property to be within the five percent required by the
County with twice-a-year follow-up maintenance.

Commissioner Smith questioned if the Phase 1 expense of $85,000 to $125,000 included leaving
30 to 50 percent of the debris or if the expense could be brought down to a more manageable
number. Mr. Archer indicated that he had provided the worst case scenario, with a price range
based upon quotes received from local contractors for 100 percent removal. Both staff and the
County felt that some debris should be left, but there was too much to leave it all.

RESOLUTION NO. 2006-14:

SITE PLAN AMENDMENT TO
RELOCATE REAR LIGHTING
AT THE WATERWAYS SHOPPES
(Quasi-Judicial Hearing)

COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, APPROVING THE
SITE PLAN AMENDMENT AND COMMUNITY
APPEARANCE BOARD APPLICATIONS TO RELOCATE
REAR LIGHTING FROM THE SEAWALL TO THE BACK
OF BUILDINGS BAND C AT THE WATERWAYS
SHOPPES, LOCATED AT 7499 STATE ROAD 7.
(continued from March 1, 2006)

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the title of Resolution No. 2006-14.

Assistant City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young reviewed the project and its issues for the benefit
of the new commissioners. She offered that the rear lighting had been designed for installation
on the sea wall itself and briefly described how they would have illuminated a public walkway
area. Due to an error during the inspection process, the columns with conduits were mistakenly
filled in and the wiring for the lighting could not be installed. As an alternative, the developer
had requested that the lighting be placed at the rear of the buildings. Community Appearance
Board approval was necessary because of the change in elevation of the building design. The
same lighting fixture that would have been placed on the light poles in the seawall would be
used; only the location of the fixtures would be changed.

Ms. Gardner-Young stated that, with respect to site plan amendment approval, there were
concerns with the photometrics and how the lighting would impact the residences, as well as the
change in ambiance. She recalled the Commissioners had been concerned regarding the design
of the light fixtures, and the petitioner was asked to propose alternatives. These were provided in
the Commission packet, and this design issue was the Commission's decision.

Nectaria Chakas of Ruden, McClosky represented the petitioner, Simon & Douglas
Development, and she waived the quasi-judicial process. She introduced Manny Synalovski, the
project architect, and Leonardo Simone, a principal of Simon & Douglas.

Ms. Chakas briefly reviewed the situation, which she described was the result of a contracting
error, for the benefit of the new commissioners. Several design options were offered, but she
believed that the light fixture on the building was more in keeping with the City's country-classic

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2006-09. Recalling the
Commission work session several months ago, he indicated that staff was recommending
adoption of regulations similar to those of Deerfield Beach and Weston, where local overweight
fines were to the benefit of the City. The regulations would help with the safety of commercial
truck traffic through the City, and help preserve and maintain the roadways.

Mayor Udine recognized Chief Carry and Deputy Brian Nicoletti, who were present to answer
the Commission's questions.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky believed it was important to advise agriculturally-licensed residents, as
well as businesses, via letter. She inquired about the proper signage required to enforce this.
Chief Carry did not believe. additional signage was required; there were state statutes already in
place, and the ordinance would be more specific to the City. This ordinance did not prohibit
commercial vehicles, but overweight commercial vehicles could be fined five cents per
overweight pound. Scales would be obtained for this use. Additionally, roadways could be
better regulated, i.e., prohibiting trucks over 10,000 pounds on Parkside Drive or requiring a
special permit. Should the City Engineer find substandard roadways, those roads could be
designated for vehicles less than 10,000 pounds only.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky suggested that the special permit fee should be included within the
ordinance, and Mr. Maurodis indicated that this fee should be set via separate resolution or
ordinance.

The Vice Mayor noted that page 9 referred to a Commercial Motor Vehicle Review Board, and
she asked if it worked well. Deputy Nicoletti responded affirmatively, and added that Coral
Springs was the first to institute this procedure for the violator.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky stated that, if the intent of the ordinance was to protect the City's roads, she
felt strongly that the revenue generated should be dedicated to roadways and their repair. Deputy
Nicoletti indicated that the collection of monies for a city ordinance was already covered by state
statute; it must go directly to the city, specifically tagged for roadway improvement. Coral
Springs, Deerfield, and Weston use the ordinance to funnel 10 percent to the police for training
in this.

Commissioner Smith asked if the ordinance was enforceable, and Deputy Nicoletti responded
affirmatively. As a Parkland police officer, he had issued significant numbers of traffic citations
each month without the use of scales. Deputies would be trained to recognize the signs of
overweight trucks, with flagrant violators targeted. Chief Carry added information regarding
