Mayor Udine opened the meeting at 7:10 p.m. and roll call was taken. All Commission members
were present except for Commissioner Rosenof, who was in transit to the meeting.
MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to take a short recess, to allow Commissioner
Rosenof time to participate in the meeting, and reconvene at 7:20 p.m. Seconded by Vice Mayor
Weissman.
MOTION passed unanimously.
Mayor Udine reconvened the meeting at 7:20 p.m. Commissioner Rosenof was in transit but
present via telephone.
II PURPOSE: 2009 -2010 BUDGET
1. ORDINANCE NO. 2009 -40: ADOPTING MILLAGE
COMMENTS: SECOND READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND,
FLORIDA, ADOPTING THE MILLAGE RATE TO BE
LEVIED FOR THE FISCAL YEAR BEGINNING
OCTOBER 1, 2009 AND ENDING SEPTEMBER 30, 2010
AND STATING THE PERCENTAGE INCREASE IN
PROPERTY TAXES AS SUCH INCREASE IS DEFINED
IN FLORIDA STATUTES, SECTION 200.065, AND
HOUSE BILL 1B, PROVIDING FOR SEVERABILITY,
AND PROVIDING FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE. (9/14/09)
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis read a script into the record which included the title of
Ordinance No. 2009 -40, Adopting Millage.
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young requested that staff provide the presentation for both the
millage and the budget and then introduced Interim Finance Director Nancy Morando, who gave
a brief presentation. Interim Finance Director Morando provided background and scenarios of
what the millage means to the residents utilizing a PowerPoint presentation (a copy of which is
attached hereto and incorporated herein as part of these minutes).
Mayor Udine explained that the increase in police services is not only due to the contractual
increase, but also includes the enhancements of half a motorcycle police officer and the School
Resource Deputy in the new school. He commented about the Wedge piece coming into the City
and said the developer of some of those properties will deposit money with the City to cover
some of the expenses for lobbying and land planning issues. Mayor Udine stated that Centerline
Homes already has people moving into the homes in their new community and he hopes to see

next year's new property numbers increase as a result. He noted that one of the groups that have
been hit hard this year is City employees, who have received no raises or COLA.
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young spoke about the employee pension and said she feels this is
an area that may have to be looked into if things do not turn around next year.
Mayor Udine commented that there is some money put aside through the strategic planning
process for projects listed in the budget, but none are set in stone, such as webcasting, which may
or may not be voted on by the Commission. He noted that if the Commission chooses not to
decide on webcasting, the dollars would stay in reserve.
Mayor Udine opened to the Public.
Cliff Kaufman, 6011 N.W. 62 Ct., spoke about the City survey and said it was not a true
sampling. Mr. Kaufman also spoke about BSO and requested that the Commission establish the
City's own police department. He added that BSO should not be increased more than the
contractual amount.
Dr. Keith Soter, the Colony, Heron Bay, suggested finding ways to get more input from Parkland
residents.
There were no further comments or questions and the Mayor closed to the public.
Vice Mayor Weissman said that the TRIM notices were sent out in August and all the residents
could see what the various taxing districts were proposing for taxes as well as when the public
hearings would take place. He commented on the number of residents who showed up at the two
budget hearings and stated that the public does come out when they feel they are being unfairly
taxed. Vice Mayor Weissman noted that the vast majority of the residents did not choose to
come out, and he congratulated staff for holding the line and watching out for the public's
dollars.
Commissioner Moskowitz stated that when people feel strongly about issues, they come and
voice their opinion. He requested that the slide be shown again listing the millage rate in all the
other cities in Broward County and noted there are 20 municipalities with higher rates than
Parkland.
Mayor Udine commented that Parkland's residents are very much demanding of a level of
service that the Commission wants to continue to provide and they are not willing to sacrifice a
SRO deputy, the library, or a fire station. He added that the residents want to keep the level of
service up in Parkland because they want it to remain a desirable place to live.
Commissioner Rosenof said his constituents are not shy about letting him know about what
services they want and about taxes.

MOTION made by Vice Mayor Weissman to approve Ordinance No. 2009 -40, Adopting
Millage, at 4.0198 mills for the fiscal year 2010. Seconded by Commissioner Smith.
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis stated that the adopted millage rate for the City of Parkland, as
the taxing authority, of 4.0198 mills, is 3.2% less than the rollback rate of 4.1532, and is
computed according to the guidelines set forth in Florida Statutes.
ROLL CALL VOTE:
MOTION passed on a 3 -2 vote.
2. ORDINANCE NO. 2009 -41: ADOPTING BUDGET
COMMENTS: SECOND READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND,
FLORIDA, ADOPTING A BUDGET FOR THE FISCAL
YEAR BEGINNING OCTOBER 1, 2009 AND ENDING
SEPTEMBER 30, 2010, PROVIDING FOR
SEVERABILITY AND PROVIDING FOR AN EFFECTIVE
DATE. (9/14/09)
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Ordinance No. 2009 -41, Adopting Budget.
Mayor Udine opened to the Public.
Cliff Kaufman said BSO may not be putting SROs in the schools any more.
Mayor Udine explained that what Mr. Kaufman has heard about the County and SRO deputies
has nothing to do with the cities that contract with BSO and reiterated that Parkland contracts
with BSO and Parkland pays for the SROs to be in the schools.
Commissioner Moskowitz spoke about police services and coverage.
Bob Marks, 7121 E. Cypresshead Dr., said Parkland is the only city that does not have an electric
franchise fee, which is a big savings to the residents.
