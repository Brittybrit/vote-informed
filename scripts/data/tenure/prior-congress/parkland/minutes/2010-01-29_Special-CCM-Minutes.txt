II PURPOSE:

1. DISCUSSION & CONSIDERATION: FIRE CONTRACT
Mayor Udine said that he requested this special meeting because he felt the decision to terminate
the negotiations was wrong when it occurred and he thinks it is wrong now. He added that he
feels Coral Springs was still negotiating and the numbers provided were not the end-all. Mayor
Udine stated he feels that Coral Springs is the best option for the City and the City should still be
negotiating with them. He reiterated that he feels the motion to terminate was wrong and he is
hoping that one Commission member will make a motion to reconsider.

Commissioner Moskowitz said this meeting was not called to discuss the details; he does not
understand why the City can not continue to negotiate with Coral Springs. He commented that
the Commission can hear both proposals and then make the best decision. Commissioner
Moskowitz requested that one of the three prevailing Commissioners who voted to terminate
make a motion to reconsider.

Commissioner Rosenof stated that at last week’s meeting, the Commission voted to draw the line
in the sand and once they drew the line, his phone rang continuously. He noted that he received a
call from Commissioner Powers and he expressed to Commissioner Powers the two terms that
would make the contract amenable to him: incorporating the auditing and termination clauses, to
which Mr. Powers responded that Parkland would have to go somewhere else. Commissioner
Rosenof said if Coral Springs brings something forward that he could consider, he would make
the motion to reconsider.

Mayor Udine noted that there was a three-vote majority that stopped negotiations, so the City can
not negotiate unless the Commission reconsiders the vote.

Vice Mayor Smith said that he has spoken to a number of residents and they are concerned
whether the City will get the level of service with less equipment. He then spoke about the
implications of having simultaneous calls and dealing with a lack of sufficient equipment.
Commissioner Weissman commented that back in December, the Commission sent a contract to
Coral Springs and they chose to ignore it. He said although it was sent to their mayor, the
problem was that the Coral Springs commission never got it. Commissioner Weissman stated
that the negotiations with Coral Springs were arduous. He said these are the people who want
the City to be their customer, but this is not a welcome relationship for him as it has been a
strained relationship for five years. He noted that during the five-year relationship with BSO, not
once did the City hear the threat of lawsuit or litigation; instead, the City has had a good
relationship with the sheriff’s office. Commissioner Weissman said the fire consultant’s report
says both organizations can provide the same level of service. He then referenced the
spreadsheet which annualizes increases for the term of the existing contract and Finance Director
Nancy Morando responded to his questions. .

Commissioner Moskowitz spoke about the numbers provided by Coral Springs.

Finance Director Morando explained how she came up with the numbers in the absence of any
firm numbers from Coral Springs.

Commissioner Weissman said this contract will cost homeowners $550 more per year over a
five-year period and he can not in good conscience vote for that.

Mayor Udine stated that there was no evidence that Coral Springs is trying to cheat the City and
the consultant was very clear that Coral Springs was the most cost effective and best way to go.
He said that staff on both sides had problems with the negotiations and he spoke about the caps

that were desired in the contract. Mayor Udine added that there are soft caps that BSO could
change if they so choose and in the future, the county will not pay for any loss in regards to a
county contract.

Commissioner Moskowitz spoke about the Commission’s differing viewpoints, on this and other
matters involving bids, and said this is one of the big decisions so the Commission better know
the numbers. He spoke about the Commission negotiating with two entities and stated that the
Commission should look at the details once they have two proposals in front of them.
Commissioner Moskowitz added that the Commission should not rush into this and there is no
harm in speaking with two entities; it is advantageous having both fight for the City’s business.
He noted that he would second a motion to reconsider.

Mayor Udine spoke about the consultant’s findings on service levels, in particular, mutual aid
agreements with surrounding cities.

Commissioner Rosenof commented on the RFP process and then spoke about the termination
clause in the last offer. He questioned how the City can expect a future vendor to hire 33
personnel.

There was discussion regarding the numbers and one-sidedness. Commissioner Weissman noted
that there is a very conservative difference, but he believes the difference would actually be much
greater. .

Commissioner Moskowitz said that the projections were completely one-sided, and the
Commission needs to be sure they know what they are talking about, so more time should be
spent on the matter. He added that he feels that the residents will benefit.

Commissioner Rosenof commented that he would be willing to pay more for Coral Springs, but
this is an unfair and one-sided contract.

Mayor Udine said that the auditor clause is in the contract and Commissioner Rosenof responded
that he wants Coral Springs to pay for the auditor.

Vice Mayor Smith talked about, and read from, the report from the fire consultant regarding BSO
stations not being in close proximity to Parkland.

Mayor Udine commented about the numbers now being given by BSO.

Commissioner Rosenof said he feels that Coral Springs could have voted and sent a message to
Parkland that they were willing to work with Parkland. He then spoke about a negative blog
directed at him and said he felt it was disgusting and not cool.

Commissioner Weissman commented that he asked the consultant for information, but the
consultant did not change numbers for him. He stated that response time is more critical than
transport and mentioned that if an emergency call is under one umbrella, this would improve

response times because the emergency calls go through BSO and are then transferred to Coral

Mayor Udine said that he feels residents would be more disturbed if a different transport

Commissioner Weissman stated that BSO has already made the necessary contacts for the proper

Commissioner Moskowitz said this is a healthy debate to have. He added that he is extremely

MOTION made by Vice Mayor Smith to reconsider the vote to terminate negotiations taken at
the January 20, 2010 City Commission meeting. Seconded by Commissioner Moskowitz.
