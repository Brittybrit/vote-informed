DEPLOYMENT STUDY

Mayor Udine asked the City Manager to explain the fire study.

Mark Lauzier, City Manager, hired Matrix Consulting Group to conduct a study to
analyze the locations of the existing fire stations, their response times and whether or not

additional stations were necessary. They looked at location, the potential impact of build
out of farm areas and examined the contract with Coral Springs. Mr. Lauzier introduced
Travis Miller of Matrix Consulting to review their findings.

Mr. Miller thanked the Commission. They were asked to evaluate where the current fire
stations are, whether or not additional stations would be necessary as development
continues, explore alternatives to the way dispatch is handled, examine details of the
contract with the City of Coral Springs, and present what the City's options are.

Based on the collected information and compiled data, Mr. Miller stated that he believes
the two existing stations are not going to be sufficient as development continues in the
western portion of the City. Currently, the system is set up so that 86% of the calls have a
response time of four minutes or less. Currently, they are delivering a unit in response to
a fire call but that unit may be a non-fire fighting piece of apparatus (i.e. an ambulance).
The City needs to decide what they want the performance standards to be for Fire Rescue
service and delivery. Currently, there is no formally adopted policy. It is reasonable to
assume the City wants calls dispatched quickly, in one minute or less, and less than four
minute drive times. These are common specs for response time. Mr. Miller displayed
maps showing the locations of the existing fire stations and back up locations. There is a
significant gap in the western section of the community for a standard four minute
response time. Mr. Miller displayed a map of what would happen if they added a third
fire station. In addition to the location, road networks and position of canals impact
response time. The City is too long geographically; it could not be covered with only two
fire stations. Three fire stations are required if the goal is to have a response time of four
minutes or less.

Mr. Miller recommends purchasing fire apparatus for the proposed third fire station
because it is the most flexible. An option may be to do something jointly with Coral
Springs; he recommends opening a dialog with them concerning an additional station.
Matrix also reviewed the contract with the City of Coral Springs. He recommended that
management reporting requirements be included in the agreement, also the existing
contract gives away Parkland's EMS revenues; they are applied to Coral Springs but not
paid back to Parkland. This amount can be hundreds of thousands of dollars.
Additionally, the accounting methodology in how Coral Springs charges Parkland is not
defined in the contract, they could change at will. Coral Springs had changed their
budgeted procedures causing the cost for Parkland to increase. There needs to be more
accountability. Matrix recommended specific steps to achieve this as part of their written
report.

Mr. Miller discussed three options to offer fire and rescue services to the City of
Parkland: do it yourselves, contract with the City of Coral Springs or contract with BSO.
It would cost $500,000 more than the current contract for Parkland to provide services on
their own and contracting with BSO has issues in terms of back up.

Matrix also evaluated dispatching procedures. Mr. Miller said that most phone calls get
routed to BSO, they then have to reroute calls to Coral Springs fire dispatch. This may

AG EXEMPTION IMP ACTS - RE: RANCHES

Mr. Lauzier, City Manager, explained that Weiss, Serota, Helfman, Pastoriza, Cole &
Boniske, P .A, represented by Susan Trevarthen, was asked to investigate a lengthy list of
issues related to the Ranches, particularly the agriculture exemption. He then asked the
Assistant City Manager to present the staff report to the Commission.

Caryn Gardner- Young, Assistant City Manager, gave a briefing on staffs
recommendations. She asked the Commission to provide direction to solidify policies.

Ms. Gardner-Young said their intent was to clarify the issues. There are five areas which
they identified that the City needs to address; the first is the agricultural exemption. The
recommended courses of action are based on actual situations the City has been asked to
resolve. They are proposing the following rule: If the property appraiser of Broward

<<PG>>

County has determined that a property is agriculturally exempt, the City will follow that
agricultural exemption classification. In order for the City to determine whether or not a
property has an agricultural exemption, they will be asking for individuals to offer proof
from the appraiser's office when they come in for their licenses. There are some
businesses that do not have to obtain an occupational license, for those, the staff will
investigate to make the determination.

Next, they examined the land use issue. What are the permitted uses in the Ranches area?
The uses which are listed in sections 22-30 ofthe Code of Ordinance are the uses the City
is going to permit. Some of the definitions are not clear so the staff wants to clarify the
terms in order to appropriately enforce the code. Staff will make recommendations to the
Commission and they will rule through an ordinance. The 80120 Rule does not apply
because these will be City wide ordinances.

The third area is occupational licensing. If you have a use that is permitted through 22-
30, the City will process the application for your license. If you are an individual who
sells, grows or produces farm products, under state statute, there is no provision to obtain
an occupational license. Only a business must get a license. Staff will clarify terms
through an ordinance as adopted by the City Commission.

Ms. Gardner - Young said the next issue is general zoning: set backs, lot coverage, hedge
height, etc. If an agricultural exemption has not been issued by the appraiser's office, the
City will enforce all building and zoning regulations. If an agricultural exemption has
been issued, building regulations will not be enforced for non residential buildings.
Zoning regulations will apply unless the zoning regulation inhibits, hinders or interferes
with the agricultural exempt activity. The determination will be made by staff. When the
appraiser's office makes an agricultural exempt determination, it does not apply to the
entire parcel; it only applies to where the agricultural exempt activity occurs. Staff will
ask residents for a surveyor sketch of their property when they apply for an occupational
license so the City has a record of where the activity is going on. If a resident wants to
know what portion of their property is affected, staff will issue a no-fee zoning opinion
letter.

Objects in the right of way are the last issue. Staff is requesting a rule that all objects in
the right of way, except for mail boxes, or those items approved by the landscaping plan
process, must be either removed or a city license must be obtained. Staff needs to
implement a licensing procedure. This will be handled by the City Engineer.

Susan Trevarthen, Special Counsel, stated that cities enforce their codes; they generally

Commissioner Moskowitz thanked everyone for coming out so late. He feels a big
concern is the commercialization of the Ranches and that the Ranches have changed
dramatically over the last ten years. He believes that in the future, there is a natural
progression for it to be less agricultural farms and more homes. The current attitude in the
Ranches is that they do not want interference or code enforcement; however, some
residents have abused their rights. The City has the ability to ban all commercial vehicles
in the Ranches, but he does not think that is what the City should do. He has a few
proposals for the Attorney and City Manager to look into. First is the problem of manure
storage. The City should look into requiring manure to be stored in a dumpster. There has
been a problem with people dumping manure into the canals. Many people are already
using a dumpster. This is a health issue. Second, he wants staff to investigate overgrowth
of vegetation, especially on vacant lots that are becoming dump sites. The City should
look into a maximum height requirement for vegetation. The third issue is speeding.
Commissioner Moskowitz asked the City Manager to look into whether or not the
Ranches have a uniform speed limit and examine the possibility of posting speed limit
signs and perhaps also lowering the speed limit. Commissioner Moskowitz asked if the
City can increase speed controls in the Ranches. Fourth, is the problem with the right of
way. The City should look into an ordinance for work vehicles which would only allow
unloading and loading in the right of way, and not allow parking. The compromise
solution should be for the City to allow work vehicles to park on private property as long
as they are kept out of plain view, perhaps they can be surrounded with vegetation. This
is a quality of life issue.

Commissioner Moskowitz stated that the City should not start strict code enforcement
since there is a history of non-enforcement. The residents in the Ranches should have
respect for each other. These proposals, which are mostly health and safety issues, and
will increase property values as well as the quality of life in the Ranches. The issues at
the Ranches are important, but should not have priority over other issues in Parkland and
the City needs to make a decision and move on. In addition, Commissioner Moskowitz
would like to see Trotters Road paved, especially because school buses travel on it.
