City Attorney Andrew Maurodis went over the procedural guidelines for a quasi-judicial hearing. He
explained that the Commissioners will first disclose ex parte contact with the applicants. Mr.
Maurodis will then swear in all persons wishing to provide testimony which includes staff, applicant’s
witnesses and any members of the public known as participants. He explained that each person can
be cross examined by the applicant on their testimony. He stated the hearing will begin and Staff can
make a full presentation of a factual synopsis about the application. The applicant will then make its
full presentation. The hearing will be opened to the participants who are in support of and in
opposition of the application. Finally, Mr. Dick Coker, Applicant Representative, will rebuttal
followed by a rebuttal from staff.

Ron Gorsech, Attorney with White and Case Law Firm stated he represented WCI Communities in
the Chapter 11 reorganization. He stated he would provide an overview of WCI’s bankruptcy
proceedings and plan of reorganization.

Mr. Gorsech explained that in 2008 when Old WCI filed for bankruptcy under Chapter 11 of the
Bankruptcy Code, it owed its creditors approximately $2 billion. Of the $ 2 billion, over $700 million
was owed to secured lenders who were entitled to priority over all other creditors. Additionally,
against the $2 billion dollars of liabilities, Old WCI was valued at only $615 million. Thus the
secured lenders claims for over $700 million exceeded the total value of the company. Under
ordinary Chapter 11 priority rules, there would have been no value left for any other creditors
including Chinese Drywall claimants. During the Chapter 11 process, Old WCI secured lenders,
negotiated with other creditors to formulate a consensual plan of reorganization would provide some
value to junior creditors. In this case the primary parties negotiating the plan were the old equity
interests in Old WCI, the secured lenders, the general unsecured creditors committee which is the
court appointed committee tasked with acting on behalf of all general unsecured creditors and the
Chinese Drywall claimants who wanted to be treated as a separate class apart from the other general
unsecured creditors. In a case where there are $2 billion of claims and a total corporate value of only
$615 million, the negotiations were difficult. Nevertheless, the negotiations continued as under the

bankruptcy code, the plan must be voted on and accepted by a majority of the claimants and
confirmed by the Bankruptcy Court before it can become effective. Negotiations were ultimately
successful and the plan that all of the parties agreed upon provided among other things that all equity
in Old WCI shares that had worth over $1 billion in 2007 would receive nothing and was wiped out
completely. The company Old WCI would change its name and cease to exist. An entirely new

The Bankruptcy Court confirmed the plan and on September 3, 2009, the effective date of the plan,
New WCI took control of substantially all of Old WCI’s assets and began operating as a new
business. This brings him to the first common misunderstanding that he would like to address:
Namely, the misconception that the current WCI Communities, Inc. is the same company that existed
prior to the bankruptcy filing. He stated it is not. New WCI is an entirely new company with new
owners, a completely new Board of Directors. The new owners are Old WCI’s creditors. All of
WCI’s equity was wiped out completely. The legal reality of the fresh start for New WCI is an
essential part of Old WCI’s plan of reorganization and cach of the three major groups of creditors
including the Chinese Drywall claimants stand to benefit from New WCI success. The second
common misunderstanding is that in some circumstances, the Commission may consider all WCI’s
actions prior to September 3, 2009. This is simply not true. He feels this stems from the failure to
recognize that New WCI is an entirely new company which is legally immune to any claims arising
prior to September 3, 2009. He wanted to be absolutely clear that all claims arising prior to
September 3, 2009 against the company whether based in fraud or any other tort or for any other
reason were discharged by the confirmed plan, therefore you absolutely cannot look beyond the bright
line in the sand on September 3, 2009 and hold New WCI accountable for anything Old WCI did or
failed to do. Doing so for any reason, including in the context of evaluating an assertion of unclean
hands is a violation of the bankruptcy code. The final misunderstanding is that homeowners with
Chinese Drywall including Parkland residents with Chinese Drywall do not want New WCI to build
in Parkland or succeed as a company. This also is not true. Based on the fact that the Chinese
