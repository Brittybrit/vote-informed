1. LEGISLATIVE UPDATE BY REPRESENTATIVE MARTIN HIAR
Representative Martin Kiar reviewed his first legislative session, which he had found personally
satisfying, productive, and amazing:
• Felt Governor Crist had brought a wonderful atmosphere to Tallahassee, where every
legislator felt relevant and' able to make a difference.
• $800,000 in funds had been provided to the Holocaust Survivor Program for in-home
assisted living.
• Passed a bill allowing nurse specialists, which had the complete support of the Florida
Medical Association and doctors.
• Passed through the House (but killed in the Senate) a First Generation Matching Grant
Program, which would give children college scholarships if they are the first in their
families to attend. While it would have applied to Florida state colleges only, he hoped
to include independent schools as well when he re-files the bill next year.
• Helped Senator Nan Rich with a bill that would allow certain cities to reduce or
eliminate their occupational license taxes.
• Led the fight to preserve the uniform school start date.
• Worked with Senator Ring on the comprehensive election bill for avoter-verifiable
paper trail and moved back the presidential preference primary date to January 29t~'. It
made Florida relevant for the first time and put the State on a national level.
• Compensated the family of Martin Lee Anderson, a young man who was killed in a
State-run boot camp program.
• Unanimously passed the cable telecommunications bill, which creates competition to
cable companies. He anticipated that cable costs would drop approximately 35 percent.
• Extended the sales tax holiday for hurricane supplies and school supplies.

• Governor Crist restored the right to vote to certain felons.
• Senator Nan Rich spearheaded a bill that divested Florida investments in Iran and the
Sudan. He hoped other states would follow the lead.
• The Wedge bill was signed into law, and he had been very proud to support that.
• Should have passed the Kid Care Program, providing health care to children whose
families could not afford health insurance. It was not funded due to a glitch in its
Senate passage. Almost 500,000 children were affected.
• Failed to extend the no-fault law. While car insurance rates would decrease $100 to
$200 per year, he believed thousands of people would then drive without car insurance.
• Failed to pass a bill to properly compensate a gentleman for wrongful conviction and 24
years in prison.
• Regarding property taxes, he explained the bills passed and his. feelings about them:
House Bill 1, the largest property tax decrease in Florida history, reduced property taxes
by approximately $15 billion by rolling back taxes to 2006 levels and additional
decreases of three, five, seven, or nine percent by local governments. He believed the
constitutional amendment, a super-sized homestead exemption, was "smoke and
mirrors," particularly with the elimination of Save Our Homes.
Representative Kiar thanked the Commission for the opportunity to speak. He hoped to continue
to make a positive difference and stated that he loved representing Parkland.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that he hoped that the Legislature would consider eventually
consider property tax relief in the form of portability, which he believed a big issue to Parkland.
Representative Kiar agreed. He noted that the property tax bill had been crafted by two House
and two Senate members and had been given to other members of the Legislature the day before
the vote. He believed that, with input from the members, portability would have been included
and would give people the ability to upsize or downsize. If the measure fails on January 29tH _
. and he noted that it needed a 60 percent vote or above to pass, he believed voters should demand
that legislators put something real and meaningful on the ballot to include portability.
Mayor Udine commented that, because the City had been fiscally responsible, only a five percent
rollback had been mandated. He noted that staff had used five percent in budget planning for
months. He believed the City would be able to continue the superior services that people .had
come to expect, although he acknowledged that some cuts would be made in the periphery. The
Mayor agreed that property tax reform should be "done right" at a later time. He thanked
Representative Kiar for his hard work.
Representative Kiar added that, while he represented nine cities in the County, he considered
Parkland a "gem," and he believed the Commission had done an admirable job in their
representation of its residents.

have been taken into consideration, as have the additional costs for the fire station and personnel,
the five percent increase in BSO costs pursuant to the contract, and the addition of the
Community Center.
Ms. Gardner-Young explained that she had developed a hierarchy, which she had divided into
four levels: what the City is required to provide by federal, state, county, and local regulations or
code -all of which would be funded; requested core services, which residents expect the City to
provide though not statutorily required (such as fire and police) -and would be funded; the
services that increase the quality of life for City residents (such as Leisure Services and special
events); and the add-ons -those items that do not add any real benefit but perhaps make things
look more beautiful (such as the pavers at the Holmberg/University intersection). The revenues
and expenditures had been first considered using these categories and then subsequently
examined using the directives of the City's five strategic goals and objectives from strategic
planning.
Ms. Gardner-Young noted that she had asked directors to cut five. percent from their operating
expenditures, which had been met by every department and in some cases exceeded. Operating
expenses were decreased by approximately $2 million. In addition, capital projects had been
reprioritized.
Ms. Gardner-Young stated that she was proud of what had been. presented to the Commission. It
tools great time and effort, and she personally thanked the directors. She asked that the
Commission review the basic budget book during the next month, and she welcomed their
suggestions, recommendations, and changes. She briefly reviewed the upcoming dates for the
budget workshop and public hearings.

review. He questioned the necessity to review the book page-by-page at this time. He
