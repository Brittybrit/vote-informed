1. PRESENTATION: DENNIS MELE UPDATE ON THE SCHOOL
DEDICATION (WEDGE PROPERTIES)

Dennis Mele, 100 West Cypress Creek Road, Ft. Lauderdale, Representative for Triple H Properties.
He gave a brief presentation of the Master Plan for the Triple H property located at the corner of Nob
Hill Road and County Line Road detailing that the elementary and middle school sites are at that
location side by side. He explained that the appraisal process is complete and Triple H has agreed with
all the appraised values with the Broward School Board. He also stated that they have drafted school
dedication agreements for cach site and the agreements have been delivered to the Broward School
Board. The Broward School Board’s Special Counsel is reviewing the agreements with the intent to
place them on a Scheol Board agenda in early April. He explained that the attorney for the School
Board preliminarily has indicated that he would like the City of Parkland to be added as a party to the
agreement and made reference to the Interlocal Agreement for school concurrency. Mr. Mele stated
that improvements are being made to the school sites as part of the dedication agreement and Triple H
will be connecting them to the drainage system and stubbing out water and sewer to the property line,
and fencing off until the School Board is ready to build. He also explained that once the dedications are
done, the sites will be restricted to ensure something unwanted is not built there.
Mayor Udine commented that this item is as a result of an agreement with the different land owners in
the wedge. He explained that the City Commission wanted to ensure that land was set aside for the
appropriate number of schools in the future to be built by the School Board.
Commissioner Moskowitz asked several questions about what would happen if the School Board
decided not to build schools. He wanted to know if the City would have the ability to build a Charter
School.
City Attorney Maurodis explained that the developer has to offer to dedicate the school in the process.
He stated that if the School Board cannot determine if a school is needed, then the property would go to
the City for seven years and if the School Board was ready to build a school within that seven year
period, the property would be given to the School Board.

2; RESOLUTION NO. 2012-13: HEALTH BROKER SERVICES
RFP AWARD
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, APPROVING THE
PROPOSERS’ RANKINGS PER EXHIBIT A AND
AUTHORIZING THE MAYOR TO EXECUTE A CONTRACT
WITH GEHRING GROUP FOR HEALTH BROKER SERVICES
RELATING TO EMPLOYEE HEALTH AND WELFARE
BENEFITS PROGRAMS.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2012-13: Health Broker Services
RFP Award. She explained that the contract with McKinley Financial has expired and the City issued
an RFP in which five companies responded. A selection committee was formed which comprised of
the City Manager, Finance Director, and Human Resources Director who ranked based on the RFP
criteria. The number one ranked company was Gehring Group and staff is recommending approval.
Commissioner Moskowitz questioned why the selected firm is being recommended. Mr. Luis Ferrer,
HR Director gave several reasons for choosing the Gehring Group.
Commissioner Moskowitz expressed that his concern was for employees getting the same or better
service.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young expressed that leverage, more tools, better contacts, a premium
preferred vendor which are afforded better deals are some of the factors for choosing the recommended
firm.
Commissioner Moskowitz expressed his concern that he does not want to get locked into a long term
contract with an agent if their price escalation is higher than what the City is used to. He was ok to
learn that this is a one year contract with the right to renew and the ability to compare the pricing with
what we currently have and if it does not work out, it will be bid out again next year.
Commissioner Willis commented that some of the other presentations showed a lower commission than
the Gehring Group showed and based on conversations with staff, he wanted confirmation that the
Gehring Group is willing to match the existing commission levels being paid to the existing broker.
Ms. Ana Marie Studley affirmed that they are willing to match the existing commission levels which
are 3% and 5%.

5 DISCUSSION: FOR THE CITIES OF MARGATE, CORAL SPRINGS,
COCONUT CREEK AND PARKLAND TO HIRE A
CONSULTANT TO PERFORM A STUDY OF SHARED FIRE
SERVICES AMONGST THE CITIES AND TO ENCOURAGE
THE CITY MANAGERS TO CONTINUE DISCUSSIONS ON
SHARED FIRE SERVICES.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young introduced the above item explaining that a recent meeting was
called by the City of Margate regarding cooperation of the four cities in the northwest quadrant of
Broward County relating to fire services to include Coconut Creek, Coral Springs, Parkland and
Margate.
She gave a breakdown of the entities that service each municipality. She explained that
communications took place amongst the City Managers and fire chiefs over the last several years in
regards to what services could be shared in order to save costs and the City of Margate is looking to
move forward.
Commissioner Rosenof explained that what the four cities agreed to is to bring back to their respective
City Commissions a motion to allow the City Managers to sit in negotiations with the other three cities.
He explained that there is talk of doing study to find out what benefits regionalization may or may not
have in the four cities. He explained there is a cost associated with the study.
MOTION made by Commissioner Rosenof to allow the City Manager to meet with the other three
cities for discussions. Seconded by Vice Mayor Weissman with the provision that there be no cost to
the City of Parkland and to bring back before the City Commission any requests for public dollars.
