1. DISCUSSION: SECOND SYNTHETIC TURF FIELD
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young introduced this item. She explained that during the budget
process the City had received monies through the City's park impact fee requirements. She
clarified that this money does not go into the general fund when received. This is money that

was paid by developers to the City in order for the City to provide the services for the capacity of
those new residents. She stated that at this time there is approximately $500,000 in that account.
She explained that park impact fees can not be spent on anything other than park improvements;
it can not be used for operation or maintenance of the parks. She is bringing forward the
possibility of expending those monies on a second artificial turf field. She added that there was
such a positive response to the first artificial turf field at Pine Trails Park. She explained that it
is not required to spend park impact fees and they could continue to remain in the account until
such time as the Commission chooses to spend it.
The City Manager turned this item over to the Parks & Recreation Director, Scott Davidoff.
Scott Davidoff explained that the City is already reaping the benefits from the first synthetic turf
field at Pine Trails Park. He said the main benefit of the artificial turf field is the ability to play
24 hours a day without the wear and tear of regular fields. He added the Sports Leagues are
overwhelming pleased with the artificial turf field. He explained that the hours of usage is the
way to determine the cost recovery for the turf fields. He said the fields at Pine Trails Park have
three times the usage than any of the other fields in the City. The Soccer, Lacrosse, Flag
Football, and Tackle Football leagues are played at Pine Trails Park and these are the largest
programs. He added that Pine Trails Park would be the park to hold Tournaments for size and
parking issue. For all of the above reasons, staff recommends putting the second synthetic turf
field at Pine Trails Park. He added that the Parks & Recreation Advisory Board voted
unanimously to recommend if there was a second turf field it should be placed at Pine Trails
Park.
Mayor Udine reiterated that park impact fees do not cost the residents and these are dollars that
can only be used for capital improvement at parks. He added that the turf fields would be a
capital improvement; however it would affect maintenance because of less hours of staff time to
maintain. He feels that Pine Trails Park is ready for another synthetic turf field because the
drainage is already there. He said he will vote to put the second field right next to the first field.
Commissioner Weissman said that Terramar Park has had terrible maintenance issues and he
feels putting the financial resources at Pine Trails Park is sending a terrible message. He said
there is still a lot of use for soccer at Terramar especially adults. He feels that since one field is
at Pine Trails Park the next field should be a Terramar.
Commissioner Moskowitz asked which field other than the synthetic turf field has the highest
usage in the City. The answer was field three at Pine Trails. His belief is that the two most used
fields should be the turf fields otherwise they are not serving their purpose. He added that if the
second turf field is put at Terramar then the City would have to make it the most used field.
Commissioner Moskowitz suggested a discussion at the next workshop on a future passive park
in the City that might include a dog park.
Commissioner Weissman said that the largest program is soccer and the soccer league can not fit
at just one park so those parents are still driving back and forth. He mentioned the wear and tear

2. DISCUSSION: WEBCASTING OF CITY COMMISSION MEETINGS
City Manager, Caryn Gardner- Young, gave a brief introduction of this item. She explained that
during the budget process there was a discussion on whether the City would be interested in
implementing webcasting. This would entail broadcasting the City Commission meetings on the
web. The reason for the discussion was because Country Acres does not have Comcast Cable
and therefore can not view the Commission meeting on cable. There were different companies
investigated and they will be presented today. She added that $40,000 was budgeted for this for
the first year.
Public Works Director Jim Berkman and IT Manager Chevelle Crusan addressed the
Commission on this subject. They presented a power point presentation (attached) outlining the
comparables.
Mr. Berkman explained there are different options available with each company.
Ms. Crusan explained that there were two companies that were researched, Granicus and Earth
Channel, and with each company there were different options that could be built upon for
additional services at a later time. She gave all the pros and cons of each.
.Commissioner Moskowitz said that he feels it is a good idea, however at this time he would
rather go with the least expensive.
Commissioner Rosenof agreed that he is ok with the least expensive option at $3,000 because it
will accomplish what he was most concerned with.
Mayor Udine also agreed he would only be interested in the Earth Channel at the lowest cost
option.
Commissioner Weissman asked what other cities besides the Town of Davie uses Earth Channel
He would like to see a list of other cities that use Earth Channel.
There was consensus of the Commission for staff to move forward with Earth Channel.
3. DISCUSSION: PIPELINE OR BRANDING
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young explained that $25,000 was budgeted for two issues of the
Pipeline publication that goes to the residents. The question was raised however if the money

could be better spent towards branding of the City. The City of Tamarac just completed the
process of branding their City. There are professionals in this area and could assist the City to
accomplish this. The research she has done on this subject however shows the cost to be more
than the $25,000 budgeted. She said she is seeking guidance from the Commission and what
their thoughts are on this subject.
Commissioner Moskowitz said that he likes the pipeline and feels it meets one of the strategic
planning goals about communicating and informing the residents as to what is going on in the
City. He feels the pipeline has been a success. He said he would not support getting rid of the
pipeline.
Vice Mayor Smith said he agrees with Commissioner Moskowitz, he also would not vote to
eliminate the pipeline. He does not agree with branding the City.
Commissioner Rosenof said these are separate issues. He said he is not sure he would vote to
continue the pipeline regardless. His perception of branding would be a concerted public
relations effort. He gave an example of the Chinese drywall issue and the convenience of having
messages coming from a concise message standpoint. He was not thinking logos etc. but more a
uniform type statement from the City.
Commissioner Moskowitz suggested they discuss this during the next strategic planning session.
Mayor Udine said that the concept is already something the City is doing to a certain extent with
the Farmers Market and the concerts in the park. These are a type of branding.
Commissioner Weissman commented that he feels that Parkland residents don't necessarily want
to promote the City to people outside the City. Events such as the Farmers Market are for the
residents, not to attract people from outside the City. He said that realtors know that if Parkland
is a good fit they will let people know about Parkland.
Mayor Udine stated that the consensus of the Commission was to continue with the pipeline and
the branding will be discussed at a future strategic planning.
