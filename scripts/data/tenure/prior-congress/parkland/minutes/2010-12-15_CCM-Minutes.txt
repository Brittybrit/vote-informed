II PUBLIC INPUT

Marie Luna Gosser, Pine Tree Estates, expressed concern for the safety of residents in the City of
Parkland due to the coyotes.

Dr. Leo Gosser, Pine Tree Estates, wished everyone happy holidays. He also commented about the
coyotes. He suggested the City make an ordinance that all vacant lots be cleared of underbrush,
garbage and invasives.

David Blessing, 7070 NW 84™ Avenue, requested that the City Commission mitigate his lien further.
He asked to be placed on the agenda for the City Commission to hear his case.

Mayor Udine explained that there was a $400,000 lien on the property and the Special Master
reduced it to $25,000. Mayor Udine stated that Mr. Blessing wants the lien reduced further.
Commissioner Moskowitz asked City Attorney Maurodis what the City’s position was since the
Special Master already made a ruling on the case.

City Attorney Maurodis explained that the current process has been followed and exhausted. He
stated however, when there is a code lien, the City owns the lien and it is completely within the City
Commissions authority to compromise any lien upon its discretion.

Commissioner Moskowitz stated he does not want to establish a precedent where people go before
the Special Master then appeal to the City Commission.

MOTION made by Vice Mayor Rosenof to add the David Blessing lien mitigation item to the
agenda. Seconded by Commissioner Moskowitz. Both Commissioner Smith and Mayor Udine
dissented.

MOTION passed 3-2.

Stuart Krantz expressed several concerns about the sidewalks that are being installed on Holmberg
Road. He feels they should be straight and not meandering.

Mayor Sal Pagliara commented about the Pine Hollow arena. He asked for all of the information on
Pine Hollow since the beginning. He said the Pine Hollow building is not what AE-1 should be. He
contends that the building is commercial structure.

There were no further comments and Mayor Udine closed to the public.

2. Discussion of David Blessing Lien Mitigation Case:
Mayor Udine asked Ms. Caryn Gardner-Young to give a brief history of Mr. Blessings situation.
Ms. Gardner-Young asked Ms. Roberta Moore, Planning & Zoning Director to give the City
Commission a history of what has taken place to date regarding the lien mitigation on Mr.
Blessing’s property. Ms. Moore explained that Mr. Blessings purchased the property knowing
that there was a lien on it. It was scheduled to go before the Special Magistrate’s hearing for a
reduction of the lien amount. The Special Magistrate made a determination to reduce the lien to
$25,000 total. There were two liens on the property at $12,500 each.

<<PG>>

Ms. Roberta Moore continued to say that Mr. Blessings requested that the Special Magistrate or
the City Commission revisit his request for a lien reduction. His request was taken back before
the Special Magistrate during the November 2010 Special Master meeting. Ms. Moore stated
she called Mr. Blessing to request that he be in attendance. Mr. Blessing was not in attendance.
The Special Magistrate still heard Mr. Blessings request even though he was not present.
Ms. Moore back tracked briefly to say that Mr. Blessing was given 30 days to pay the $25,000.
During the November Special Masters Meeting, the Special Master gave Mr. Blessings until
January 2, 2011 to pay the $25,000. At the November 2010 Special Masters Meeting, there was
no additional reduction - it was just a request for additional time.
Commissioner Moskowitz expressed concern for the City Commission weighing into this issue
because it is not the protocol.
Commissioner Weissman stated there were two issues. The first issue is that the reason the City
gave the reductions was to move the properties. Commissioner Weissman said this instance is
not to move a property at all. He said this is not the purpose of what the mitigations were for.
The second point he made is subsequent to the mitigation process starting, there was a discussion
not to reduce the liens as much as had been done at the initial stages. They all agreed that the
time would come when real estate property values were not declining as badly as it had initially
therefore it was important to the other tax payers in the City not to lose the revenue.
Mayor Udine recounted that he asked City Attorney Maurodis to help draft the ordinance to
move short sales. He said the specifics were that it had to be a short sale and the owner was not
getting any equity out of it. He thinks in this instance it is a little different. He asked what were
the City costs.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young stated the costs were around $12,000 or $15,000+. Ms.
Gardner-Young mentioned that the Special Master waived the sale of the property which was
$252,000 for 2 ¥ acres in the Ranches. Based on the lien amount being over $400,000 and the
severity of the violations present on the property which was a house that was a health, life, safety
issue as well as the property not being maintained, the Special Master made a reasonable
determination to reduce the lien from $400,000 to $25,000.
Mayor Udine stated that the City wants compliance and cost recovery. He said this is different
because the buyer knew about the lien before closing.
Commissioner Moskowitz had a concern about residents going to the Special Master and then
appealing to the City Commission.
Mayor Udine stated there is always an appellate right to the City Commission.
Commissioner Smith stated that in his opinion, a 93.5% reduction in lien is a nice reduction and
he is comfortable with the remainder.
Vice-Mayor Rosenof stated he is in favor of collecting at cost.

3. Discussion of Commercial Property owned by Ross Realty:

Commissioner Moskowitz explained that Ross Realty owns approximately 8 — 10 acres of
commercial land that is up for sale. Centerline Homes had shown an interest in purchasing the
property, but has since withdrawn its request to purchase. He feels it is worth it for the City to
look at purchasing the property for future development. At this time, he’s not sure what can be
developed. He suggested that staff do an analysis to see if it would make sense to purchase the
property. He suggested that the City add the question to the master plan that will be provided by
Keith & Associates to see if there is a need for 8-10 acres for a City facility in the next 15 years.
General consensus was given by the City Commission to have staff contact Ross Realty for the
cost of the property, then have Keith and Associates include in the Wedge Master Plan of what
could be built on the parcel.
