II PURPOSE:

1. DISCUSSION: HURRICANE PREPAREDNESS

Mark Lauzier, City Manager, reported on key areas of Parkland's hurricane preparedness. An
emergency operations plan will be presented to the Commission before the hurricane season
starts. In addition, there is a County-wide coordinator to ensure that every city's emergency
operation plan complies with FEMA requirements. The staff is working on the second draft of

the agreement with Broward County for emergency operations. The final draft will be presented
at the June 14 meeting.

Commissioner Ribotsky asked if the City could review Coconut Creek's plan, especially as it
affects the east side of Parkland, in the areas of water supply and traffic issues.

Mr. Lauzier responded that the City is working on improving water and sewer in all areas of the
City.

Jim Berkman, Public Service Director, said he felt that based on his information, Coconut Creek
was prepared with sufficient back up generators.

Discussion by the Commissioners about requiring utilities to purchase back up generators.

Mr. Maurodis, City Attorney, said the City cannot require utilities to buy generators.

Mr. Lauzier stated that the emergency operations plan will be coordinated between the City of
Parkland, the BSO and the Coral Springs Fire Department.

The Commissioners and the City Manager agreed that hurricane preparedness arrangements with
Aston Gardens should be part of the emergency plan.

Mr. Lauzier said that the City will have contracts in place before the storms for clean up which
require heavy equipment. The Commission should also consider entering into tree saving
contracts prior to the hurricane season. They are investigating hiring contractors who are
members of the community in order to insure a vested interest in Parkland.

Commissioner Ribotsky suggested that the City send HOA presidents a letter explaining the plan
in order to provide correct information and set accurate expectation levels.

Mr. Lauzier stated that the City submitted approximately seven million dollars in claims for
hurricane recovery from Wilma. FEMA has reimbursed the City with one million more than was
requested so far.

Mr. Lauzier discussed food distribution points; he suggested using Pine Trails or Terramar Park
rather than the library, which should give the City a better site rating. Another option would be
to organize a City run distribution center, otherwise it is the County and State that determines
what food gets sent where.

Commissioner McGilvray suggested that we have sites that people can walk to, if necessary.

The Commission discussed the necessity of encouraging people to take personal responsibility
for their own provisions, purchasing and storing ice and medicines requiring refrigeration.

Mr. Lauzier advised that the City has little control regarding the restoration of power, however,
they can act as an intergovemmentalliaison with FPL to apprise them of special situations, for
example, explaining that a section of Parkland uses wells that require pumps for water. It would
be very expensive for the City to bury all its power lines.

Commissioner Ribotsky stated that Juno Beach got an estimate on burying power lines in the
amount of 12 million dollars. She is concerned with the "right tree right place" policy and wants
to make sure the City will trim the trees. The water districts are requesting better code
enforcement because there is too much growth in the swales. She recommends that the City
Code Enforcement sweep for vegetation as a high priority before hurricane season starts. This
should be done soon even if they have to outsource it.

Mr. Lauzier stated that the City would need to investigate the legalities of cutting privately
owned trees prior to trimming them or cutting them down. He suggested the City tell its residents
about the concern of trees and branches interfering with power lines. He felt some residents may
voluntarily trim their own trees.

Mr. Maurodis stated that the City would need an ordinance in order to trim privately owned trees
which potentially interfere with power lines. It can be included in the "right tree right place"
ordinance.

Mr. Lauzier stated that if the tree is in the public right of way, the City already has the authority
to trim or cut it as a preventative measure.

Vice Mayor Beck said that Parkside and Riverside Drives have historically lost electricity during
storms. She stated that FPL does not provide a lot of maintenance on these streets. FPL should
have records which indicate where the highest percentages of outages are and address these
issues proactively.

Commissioner Udine asked if the City has the right to contact FPL in order to increase the
priority level of homes that are on septic and well systems.

Mr. Lauzier presented an update on public information dissemination. Last year, the City did not
have an emergency response public communication plan in place. Going forward, they have a
Code Red agreement for before and after a storm. They are emphasizing hurricane preparedness.
The City will also set up the electronic message boards in the west, central and east ends of the
City, which will be updated approximately every 12 hours.

Commissioner Ribotsky suggested that the City investigate posting notices on television which
can be updated regularly. Also, they should look into purchasing or renting satellite radios.

Mr. Lauzier stated that last year they always had use of radios, however there was a short time
with no working phone. He agreed it might be a good idea to investigate the purchase of a
satellite phone.

Mayor Marks asked Chief Dave Carry to comment on fire and police department
communications.

BSO Police Chief Dave Carry stated that during Hurricane Wilma they never lost radio service.
They did order a satellite phone as a back up for future hurricane seasons; however last year they
never lost communications.

Commissioner Udine suggested using AM radio stations to broadcast information to residents.

Commissioner Ribotsky said the City should not send postcards with emergency information
because the mail is too unreliable. She suggested distributing a refrigerator magnet with
hurricane emergency contact information to the homeowners. They would be easy for
homeowners to locate in black out. The City could get a sponsor to offset the cost of the
magnets.

Vice Mayor Beck emphasized getting as much information as possible to residents before the
next hurricane hits.

Mr. Lauzier stated that they would like to purchase a generator in order to get City Hall up and
running in as short a period of time as possible. He also informed the Commission that
Westglades Middle School has been designated as a special needs shelter. The City will also
need to prepare for that. The bid for repairing the roof at City Hall will be available at the next
meeting.

Commissioner Ribotsky stated that they need to advertise the location of City Hall. She
suggested placing signs at the University Drive entrance and at the individual buildings.

Mr. Lauzier said they will be training additional people on staff with EOC. The City is also
looking for CERT members. It is possible they may be able to work with local religious
organizations to recruit additional volunteers. The City is continuing to look at enhancing
resources with the assistance of the police and fire departments.

2. DISCUSSION: SPECIAL EVENTS AND CITY SPONSORSHIP

City Manager, Mark Lauzier, identified three levels of special events. He provided detail in the
written report provided to the Commissioners. First, there are City sponsored events, during
which the city is running the event. Second, there are co-sponsored events, where the City is
providing services or resources in conjunction with an outside entity. Last, there ate non-City
events in which a separate entity is responsible for everything. However, the City still provides
emergency services to non-city sponsored events.

Mr. Lauzier suggested the Commission review the policy regarding the use of city marketing
services and room reservations as it will also have an impact on the procedures at the new
community center.

Mayor Marks stated that he does not want to charge residents for using the meeting rooms, but
would be in favor of a deposit system.

Commissioner Ribotsky stated that the meeting group should cover any costs to the City,
including overtime, otherwise there should be no charge. She also suggested that the City
compile a list of approved organizations.

Commissioner McGilvray agreed that the meeting group should cover costs for overtime.

Vice Mayor Beck felt that the costs are already paid through tax dollars.

Mr. Lauzier reviewed the improvements made to the City's web site. They now have the
flexibility to report on community activities through the community bulletin board. In addition,
there are new links to the library, community businesses, park and recreation and current events.

3. DISCUSSION: MOVIES IN THE PARK

Mr. Lauzier, City Manager, announced that the next movies in the park would be on March 18th.
They are considering using the electronic message boards to announce the date and time.

Vice Mayor Beck stated that she thinks the City should continue to send out the message through
the schools.

Mayor Marks asked the City Manager to increase the degree of utilization of Channel 78 on
Comcast in order to broadcast messages and show highlights of community activities.

III ADJOURNMENT

Motion made by Vice Mayor Beck to adjourn the meeting at 5:05 p.m. Seconded by
Commissioner Ribotsky.

Motion passed unanimously.
