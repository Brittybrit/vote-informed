1. SUNSHINE LAW 
 
 
Mr. Maurodis introduced himself and explained the reason for this presentation. 
 
He gave a brief overview of the public records law and the sunshine law. He explained that he 
wanted to give the Board Members a general knowledge and understanding of the Sunshine Law 
and the Public Records Law that they will know enough to question him if they are unsure of a 
situation. He explained that this training is done fairly regularly especially for new Board 
Members.

Mr. Maurodis referenced Chapter 286 in the Florida Statutes for the Sunshine Laws. He briefly 
quoted that “ all meetings of any Board or Commission of any state agency or authority or 
agency authority or county, municipal corporation or political subdivision at which official acts 
are to be taken are declared to be public meeting open to the public at all times.” No 
Resolutions, rules or formal action shall be considered binding except taken or made at such 
meeting. 
 
Mr. Maurodis stated the following points: 
 
1. Advisory Boards are subject to the sunshine law just like any other board or commission 
- the rules are the same. No excuse that they are only advisory. All those that are sitting 
here must comply. All meetings need to be noticed. Notice should contain the time and 
place of the meeting. The notice needs to provide reasonable notice, which he gave an 
example of at least 48 hours. An agenda is not necessary, however if there is an agenda, 
items may be added to the agenda. 
2. Minutes have to be kept of the meetings; however they do not need to be recorded. All 
official votes have to be kept. Phone participation is allowed, however it is still required 
to have a physical quorum present at the meeting. 
3. When authority is delegated to one individual and that individual is acting as the board, 
there could be some sunshine issues even if they do not speak with another member. 
4. The Sunshine Law essentially states that no two members of the same board may 
discussion any matter that may foresee ably come before that board for action by that 
board. He added that two members of a board can speak with each other, they can go to 
lunch but they can not speak about subject matters that could come before them for some 
type of future action. 
5. Communication between members of different boards is allowed under the Sunshine 
Law. 
6. Further explanation about one member given the authority by the Board to under take 
certain action on the Board’s behalf, that Board Member could be deemed in violation of 
the Sunshine Law. If the one Board member is just on a fact finding action or obtains an 
offer, there is no violation, but if the deal is already prepackaged and decided upon, this 
could be viewed as a violation. 
7. A Commissioner can attend another Advisory Board meeting and he/she can speak. Also 
a member of an one Advisory Board can attend another Advisory Board meeting. Two 
members of an Advisory Board could attend another Advisory Board and speak, however 
they can not then sit with each other an discuss subject matter of those two Board 
members Board. 
8. A Community Organization can have a Commissioner or Advisory Board Members on 
their Board, however they can not use it to get around the Sunshine Law. 
9. On the question on whether an EAB member can never have a conversation about school 
issues, if the Board is going to be making a decision on the issue, and you have a 
conversation about that decision with another Board member then this is a violation. 
 
Mr. Maurodis told the group that if anyone had any concerns they should feel free to contact him 
and he gave his number.

Mr. John Willis questioned if there was a problem having a discussion on what items should be 
on an agenda. 
 
Mr. Maurodis answered that it depends on how the discussion is handled. If it is just a 
discussion about what should be discussed, it is ok, however if the conversation turns towards 
the merits of the item and a discussion that they would have at the public meeting, then that is 
not ok and the discussion should be ended. 
 
10. Mr. Maurodis mentioned a concept that is called a “gratification”, he explained that this 
is where an item is discussed privately and then they go to the public meeting and have 
the same discussion and vote on it. He suggested against this. 
11. Mr. Maurodis also mentioned the concept of using a conduit to work as a go between for 
Board Members in order to see where the other Board Member stands on an issue. He 
cautioned that this was also a Sunshine Violation. 
 
2. PUBLIC RECORDS LAW. 
 
 All documents that a member produces related to their official duty is a public record. 
 
 EMAILS ARE PUBLIC RECORD = he explained that they need to be careful of emails. 
One member can write a position paper of his position on a subject; this in itself is not a 
violation. However the problem is when at the end of email you ask for others opinion 
and invites conversation on the subject that a decision will be made. 
 
Mr. Maurodis quoted from an AG opinion about emails, “the use of a written report by a 
Commission/Board Member to inform other commissioners of a subject which will be discussed 
at a public meeting, is not a violation of the Sunshine Law if prior to the meeting there is no 
interaction related to the report. If, however, the report is circulated among Board Members for 
comments which such comments provided to other members, there is interaction between board 
members, which is subject to 286.,011, Sunshine Law.” 
 
Mr. Maurodis stated that the private correspondence on an individual’s computer is not open to 
the public records law; this also applies to a City computer. 
 
Andrew Garafalo asked about personal notes. Mr. Maurodis said if you are trying to 
communicate then it is a public record. If dictate something to perpetuate information it turns it 
into a public record. Mr. Maurodis stated that the rule on notes is if the note is meant to 
formalize, perpetuate or transfer or communicate information, it would be a public record. 
 
John Willis asked about maintaining emails. Mr. Maurodis answered that emails should be saved 
in files on their computer similar to any hard copy file that they would create. Just as in hard 
copy documents, if it is a transitory document, it can be deleted, otherwise it would be saved as 
long as the public record law requires. 
 
Sebastien Gros asked about applicants calling and wanting to speak with a Board Member. Mr. 
Maurodis said that there is no reason why they can not, however if they are uncomfortable doing

Mr. Maurodis summarized that the Advisory Board Members are governed by Sunshine Law and 
the Public Records Law. 
 Sunshine Law – Any communication with another member of the Board on any matter 
that may come before them to vote on they should avoid. 
 Public Records – All documents that a member produces related to their official duty is a 
public record. 
 E-mails – are subject to the public records laws and should be handled as any other public 
record documents. Remember to avoid using emails that could violate the Sunshine Law.
