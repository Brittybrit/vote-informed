III PUBLIC INPUT
Former Mayor Harold Bockhold related his distress about the plans to demolish Old City Hall.
He believed the historical significance of the building should be recognized and preserved, and
suggested that other buildings be built around it. He asked consideration by the Commission,
and he also asked that the Historical Society be allowed input at their upcoming workshop.
Mayor Udine commented that he had found a copy of a 1970's article about Mayor Bockhold's
election, which he would turn over to the Historical Society.
Leo Gosser of Pinetree Estates added that, since Old City Hall was the first building owned by
the City, it had been the site of many activities worth remembering. He believed it should be
preserved and turned into a museum. Many artifacts had already been collected by the Historical
Society, which should be shown in a respectable manner. Dr. Gosser invited residents to join the
Historical Society to learn about the City's past. He agreed with the comments made by the
former Mayor.
Stuart Krantz of 6348 N.W. 62na Terrace offered that School Board Member Stephanie Kraft had
today presented her reasons why School Site A-3 should be favored over School Site Z. He
personally believed that the City had made the wrong choice four years ago; School Site A-3 was
in a better location. He commented regarding Parkland Town Center, the Commission Strategic
Planning sessions to come, and the City's infrastructure.
Mayor Udine noted, for the record, that Strategic Planning would start Friday at 8:00 a.m.
Thomas White, a North Broward Preparatory School student and CypressHead resident, spoke
regarding a project he had begun the previous summer, in conjunction with the Woman's Club of
Parkland, to interview local veterans. With the help of Rebecca Waldorf at Aston Gardens and
the Parkland Library, he had interviewed six Parkland war veterans, which had been documented
on DVD. To honor them and their contributions to the country, and for the education of other
students,- he would donate the DVDs to the Library and would give copies to a national project
through the Library of Congress. He hoped that the project would be continued by other
students.

Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2007-42.
Leisure Services Director Scott Davidoff advised that the Sports Policy, dated July 24, 1994, had.
been created to enact rules and regulations concerning the use of City park facilities by the sports
leagues. It had not been amended by the Commission since that time, but several informal
modifications to clarify had been made. During the past five months, Mr. Davidoff indicated that

<<PG>>

the Parks and Recreation Advisory Board and its ad hoc committee had reviewed and discussed
amendments to the policy, identifying out-of--date areas, clearing inconsistencies, and setting new
policies. Despite the unanimous vote to bring the policy to the Commission for approval, a
number of concerns regarding participation percentages had been brought forward by the travel
baseball organization, and he handed out some revised sections of the policy.
Ms. Gardner-Young recalled previous Commission discussion regarding the use of sports cards,
which staff planned to again present at the next Commission meeting. Since the policy revisions
also dealt with resident and non-resident participation percentages, it was thought best to address
them at the same time. Staff recommended that the sports policy be adopted, less the
percentages. Although there was prior discussion with the Parks and Recreation Advisory Board,
it would be brought back to them for their consensus before the Commission would hear it.
Mr. Davidoff reviewed some of the amendments: field usage by private schools based on
availability; a special needs component within all recreational sports programs; establishing the
priorities of play-off games, regular season games, and practices; field utilization by private
individuals; an annual meeting- of sports league presidents; participation in multiple sports; and
the sports card.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz thanked Mr. Davidoff for his work. He admitted concern with the
percentages but, more importantly, having adequate field space for the number of participants.
He believed that the leagues with more residents should have first priority to the fields. He also
suggested that the growth of the Lacrosse league should be taken into consideration since it must
be balanced with the soccer leagues.
Commissioner Weissman stated that he also had a problem with the percentages, which he had
discussed with Mr. Davidoff. He questioned setting the percentages, as he believed that the
sports leagues would "fudge" the numbers to make them work. At Mr. Davidoff s response
acknowledging this fear and the fact that it occurred in other cities, Commissioner Weissman
thanked him for this on-the-record statement. The Commissioner wanted it understood that the
City would be encouraging falsehoods, rather than good sports, in order to register children in the
programs. He did not believe this the direction to consider.
With respect to the fees, Commissioner Weissman believed the City should adopt a program of
reciprocity: non-resident participants should be charged the same non-resident fees as their city
of residence charged. He asked that this suggestion be expressed to the Parks and Recreation
Advisory Board prior to their discussion of this issue.
Commissioner Weissman noted there were specific problems with some of the sports,
particularly with the travel program. He suggested that, prior to the Parks and Recreation
Advisory Board passing the policy back to the Commission, the Board should meet individually
with the leadership of those groups where the problems were recognized. The Commissioner
hoped to avoid having to change the policy dramatically in order to know what would work for
the organizations.

3. DISCUSSION: SPECIAL ELECTION DATE
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis noted that the agenda packet contained a memorandum from the
City Clerk that accurately reflected the situation. It was informational; there was no action for
the Commission to take. He recalled that, in summary, the election would deal with the vacancy
created by Commissioner McGilvray's resignation, whichwas effective March 13t~'. The new
Charter provision adopted last November provided that, with no available City election within a
one year period of the submission of the resignation, the election may be held in conjunction with
an available County, State, or Federal election. The Legislature had approved advancing the

<<PG>>
PARKLAND CITY COMMISSION MEETING MINUTES
MAY 16, 2007
PAGE 13
Florida primary to January 29t~', which would be within one year of the date of the resignation.
Based upon the language of the Charter, the election would then be held on January 29th
Mayor Udine questioned whether the City might utilize an earlier election date should the Special
Session call for a vote concerning property taxes. Mr. Maurodis answered affirmatively,
indicating that his ruling would be that the City should avail itself of the earlier date.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz noted that an election might be set quickly, allowing insufficient time for
qualifying. Mr. Maurodis agreed that clearly that would be an exception but, absent that, he
believed the earlier date must be utilized.
Mr. Maurodis stated that, at the end of the Special Session, the dates for qualifying would be set
and everything done that would need to be done.
Mayor Udine asked whether the Charter provisions were self-executing: the vote took place, the
Charter amendments were placed in effect, and nothing further was needed. Mr. Maurodis
agreed, believing that a benefit of the Charter provision was that it had been taken from the
Commission's hands.
Mayor Udine believed this Charter provision should be discussed again in the future. He did not
believe it right, recalling the previous debate and discussion on the dais.

Regarding Resolution No. 2007-58, Mr. Maurodis indicated that he had discussed with Chief
O'Connell and Major Tierney at BSO the issue of forms for prosecuting water violations. He
believed it important to have a schedule of fines and be able to let the first-time violator know
that, rather than going to court, they may pay a fine. The maximum fine is $500; the courts do
not, and the City would probably not, impose a $500 fine for a first violation unless it was an
egregious situation. He believed the suggested schedule of $125 for first violation, $250 for the
second, and $500 for the third and thereafter, severe. The City of Deerfield Beach issues the first
violation as a warning, then $25, $50, and upward, and he suggested $50 for the first, $125 for
the second, $250 for the third, and $500 thereafter.
Chief O'Connell commented that the Commission should consider compliance as the priority; if
the City could not get compliance, then the fines should be imposed. The warnings had already
been issued; violations should now be fines. He believed. that, with compliance, violators should
be encouraged to pay the fine without prosecution as prosecution incurred expenses. There
should be some balance. The Chief believed Mr. Maurodis' recommendation reasonable.
Mayor Udine believed that $50 for first-time violations was too low. He believed the fines
should start at $125. This should be more a penalty than a $50 ticket.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz noted that the BSO signs had warned residents of the fines up to $500
for violations. Chief O'Connell commented that he mentioned the fines for violating the water
restrictions at every homeowners' association meeting he attended.
