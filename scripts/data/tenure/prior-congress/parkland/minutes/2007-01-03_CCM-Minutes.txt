5. ORDINANCE NO. 2006-38: TREE MITIGATION ZONING
TEXT AMENDMENT
COMMENTS: FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND,
FLORIDA, RELATING TO CHAPTER 9 OF THE CODE
OF ORDINANCES ENTITLED "LANDSCAPING AND
VEGETATION", AMENDING SECTION 9-21 ENTITLED
"GENERAL REQUIREMENTS"; PROVIDING A
CONFLICTS CLAUSE AND A SEVERABILITY CLAUSE;
AND PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE.
City Manager Mark Lauzier read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2006-38.
Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer explained that the Commission had requested
this Ordinance in order to address tree removal and non-replacement. He noted that the Code did
not address time limits on tree removal permits, and he took this opportunity to include asix-
month time limit until expiration. Additionally, it mandates that the permit be posted in a
protected but conspicuous area on site.

<<PG>>
PARKLAND CITY COMMISSION MINUTES
JANUARY 3, 2007

Mr. Archer indicated that, presently, the only trees requiring replacement are those considered
"specimen" trees, 16 inches or larger in diameter, and trees removed without a permit. He spoke
regarding inch-per-inch tree replacement, tree-per-tree replacement in construction areas, and
other mitigations, such as cash payment in lieu of replacement and tree credits.
Mayor Udine complimented Mr. Archer on the excellent work. He believed it important to the
City's tree canopy.
Mayor Udine opened to the public.
Natalie Bigio of Pinetree Estates asked clarification regarding the loss of native pines to the pine
bark beetle, and asked 'if this Ordinance then made replacement mandatory. Mr. Archer
responded in the affirmative. She then asked if the tree could be replaced with any tree, and Mr.
Archer stated that, while it was not required in .the Code, he recommended replacement of the
same type of tree.
With Mayor Udine's input, there was discussion why a tree killed by a beetle would be replaced
by the same tree that may suffer the same problem. Mr. Archer stated this was a misconception.
The trees affected by the beetle were those that had existed for 70 or more years and damaged
over time by hurricanes and other factors. He believed that replanted slash pines would grow up
healthier and be more adaptable to their surroundings.
Mrs. Bigio expressed concern that the older sections of the City would be severely impacted by
this Ordinance, where the trees were huge and affected by storm damage. She believed there
should be some method of mitigating the impact.
Mr. Archer commented that the City had a tree replacement requirement in new construction.
There had never been an issue of cost. He believed that tree-per-tree replacement would cost far
less than the inch-per-inch method.
Mayor Udine acknowledged that residents in the older sections may be severely hit by tree
replacement costs. He believed the City should be cognizant of this.
Commissioner Smith asked regarding the timeframe for tree replacement and whether replanting
may be spread out over a period of time. Mr. Archer indicated that the present Ordinance for tree
replacement required 30 days.
Mayor Udine commented that both individual landowners and associations were severely
impacted by the storms. He believed it was important, however, to maintain Parkland and its tree
canopy.
Commissioner Smith stated that he had no problems replacing trees, but he asked regarding an
amendment that extra time would be given for exorbitant numbers of trees lost.

<<PG>>

City Attorney Andrew Maurodis agreed this should be addressed in the situations of natural
disasters, providing additional time. He believed it a good point.
Mr. Archer noted that the Ordinance provided exclusion in the case of hurricane, drought, or

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2006-43. He explained that
the staff had determined the need for a new cell tower ordinance. Because many residents were
complaining about cellular service and the lack of coverage within the City limits in certain
areas, he indicated that the Assistant City Manager was working with a company to site a
potential tower on City property. The Ordinance was necessary so that all provisions related to
the location of cell towers and their equipment were in place. He stated that the Ordinance had
been reviewed by the Planning and Zoning Board.
Planning Director Roberta Moore stated that the existing cell tower ordinance would be repealed
in its entirety and this Ordinance would replace it. She offered to answer specific questions or
review the Ordinance section by section, as chosen by the Commission.
Mayor Udine commented that the considerable expense of outside counsel had been saved by the
City Attorney through his work with other cities.
Mr. Maurodis agreed there were specialty firms that would draft this type of Ordinance at
significant cost, but a number of cities have also been revising their ordinances. Some of the
most recent ordinances were examined and "tweaked" for the City of Parkland. He admitted
there had been no significant changes as it was a standard concept. He believed the version close
to "state of the art," and noted restrictions that had not existed in the old ordinance.
Mayor Udine recalled the zoning-in-progress Resolution, and assumed that this Ordinance would
apply to the company coming in. Mr. Maurodis indicated that anyone having an application after
the zoning-in-progress Resolution was adopted must adhere to the terms of this Ordinance.
Mayor Udine asked regarding inclusion of language to ensure that the .flags flown on the cell
tower were in good condition and not tattered or ripped, as this was the subject of many e-mail
complaints by residents. He acknowledged rules that the property must be maintained, but he
believed that the flag must specifically be mentioned if the tower was used as a flagpole. Mr.
Maurodis agreed there was no specific language and stated that it would be added.
Assistant City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young suggested that the language could be included in
the cell tower lease agreement. Mr. Maurodis agreed that, on City property, it could be handled
on the leases in the future. He believed the companies were fairly cooperative and that
something could be worked out.
Mayor Udine suggested that Public Services could replace the flag and then bill the company,
with which Mr. Maurodis agreed. Mr. Lauzier noted that an issue may be security to the facility,
and the City generally did not have the keys to the gate, fence, or pole. He presumed it could be
charged if the company were willing to give the responsibility of the flags to the City.
Commissioner Weissman suggested that the City have a spare flag on hand.

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2007-01. He clarified that
this amendment had not been prompted by the possibility of any adult establishment or adult
entertainment proposed in the City. Staff was not aware of anything pending or planned, but
there were, however, legal requirements.
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis explained that the sole purpose of this Ordinance was to clarify
those existing. He thought it appropriate to add another definition and clarify a prohibition,
describing them as technical adjustments to the Code. He recommended approval.
Mayor Udine commented that he was aware that Mr. Maurodis wished to keep the City on solid
legal ground each time new cases developed, and he supported these amendments.
Mayor Udine opened to the public. There were no comments or questions, and the Mayor closed
to the public.
MOTION made by Commissioner Smith to approve the first reading of Ordinance No. 2007-01,

VII COMMENTS BY THE CITY MANAGER
City Manager Mark Lauzier noted that a memo about the Wedge had been left on the dais. Staff
planned to hold a Loxahatchee Road property owners meeting. A letter about the meeting would
be sent to the 20 to 30 smaller property owners to let them know directly what has been going on
related to the land use change. Staff had already met with the five major owners, including Mr.
Hendrix and the four planning a land use plan amendment. While staff had hoped to hold the
meeting following the Commission workshop, it was felt at least two weeks' notice would be
more appropriate -possibly the week of January 15th. He asked the Commission for their
suggestions as to the date and time, believing their attendance an important part of the process.
Mr. Lauzier stated that the invitation for the VIP event on February 3rd would be mailed in the
coming week.
Mr. Lauzier indicated he would update the Commission on other subjects in his weekly e-mail.

VIII COMMENTS BY THE CITY ATTORNEY
Mr. Maurodis indicated that the NSID is sponsoring a special act to permit popular elections. He
had spoken with their attorney Susan Delegal. It was proposed that three citizens would be
elected, and he suggested that Parkland should be guaranteed one seat and Coral Springs another.
Mayor Udine was in favor of one seat guaranteed for Parkland.
Mr. Maurodis stated that he would discuss this policy with Ms. Delegal.
