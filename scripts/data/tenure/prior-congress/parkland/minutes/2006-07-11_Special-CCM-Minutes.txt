II PURPOSE:

1. DISCUSSION ON PROCESS FOR FILLING THE VACANCY
FOR DISTRICT 4 COMMISSION SEAT

Mayor Udine stated that he had requested this special meeting pursuant to the City Charter on
Friday, contacting the City Manager, City Attorney, and City Clerk. He commented that the City
Manager was out-of-town due to a pre-planned vacation.

Mayor Udine explained his reasoning for the meeting, with concern about the time limits that
come into play with the resignation of an elected official as well as the budget cycle and business
before the Commission. He believed that the Commission should allow the possibility of an
appointed individual to serve the remainder of Vice Mayor Ribotsky's term.

Mayor Udine noted that he had considered Commissioners' schedules, and July 24th and July 25th
appeared to be common available dates to set another meeting should the decision to appoint
within 30 days be made.

City Attorney Andrews Maurodis commented that the Mayor had accurately set forth the Charter
parameters. He acknowledged the 30-day "window" to fill the vacancy; however, outside the
window was the requirement for a special election, which would correspond with the November
election. He believed the best way to proceed would be to fill the vacancy within that "window,"
by accepting letters of interest from individuals who wished to be considered. Mr. Maurodis
suggested that the candidates could be considered and appointed at the meeting of July 24th or
25th and, at that meeting, the special election could be set.

Mayor Udine explained there would be Parkland questions on the ballot due to Charter Review
and would not be a major cost issue to the City. Mr. Maurodis offered there should be almost no
cost. The Mayor also commented that the candidates must be qualified under the Charter,
meaning an individual residing in District 4. Mr. Maurodis confirmed the special reference in the
Vacancy provision.

The Mayor summarized that a special meeting should be set for July 24th or 25th, and the City
Clerk should prepare an advertisement for that meeting, including a cut-off date for interested
persons to submit resumes. He believed this process made the most sense.

Commissioner Smith commented that Vice Mayor Ribotsky's letter was dated July ill, and he
asked if the process must be accomplished 30 days from that date.

Mayor Udine confirmed the 30 days and anticipated that the resumes could be accepted until
about July 20tll, with the appointment made at the July 24th or July 25th meeting. There would
then be no necessity for an additional meeting.

Mr. Maurodis pointed out that this open process suggested was over and above what the Charter
required.

Mayor Udine agreed that the Commission could appoint now; however, he was not in favor of
the appointment-type process as he believed in letting the voters speak. With important business
coming in the next 120 days, he wished to make certain that part of the City had representation.

Commissioner McGilvray agreed that the District should be represented, and she noted that it
was not the Vice Mayor's intent to allow her District to remain unrepresented. She asked further
information about the process, and specifically about resume review.

Assistant City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young suggested that the Commission's decision could
be announced through the web site. She anticipated a quarter-page ad in the Sunday paper,
announcing the special meeting in conjunction with the acceptance of letters of interest until
Thursday, July 20th, at 5:00 p.m., when they would be distributed to Commissioners.

Mayor Udine suggested Monday, July 24th, at 3:00 p.m. for the Special Meeting. There was
Commission consensus. The content of the ad was briefly discussed, including the six-month
residency requirement and residency in District 4.

Mr. Maurodis confirmed the Commission had ultimate discretion, including its ability to
nominate someone else. He did not wish any prerogatives restricted.

At Commissioner McGilvray's further questions, Mayor Udine acknowledged that the
Commission could decide not to appoint. Should no action be taken within 30 days, appointment
would be completely ruled out; this would give the Commission the, chance to consider this open
and public process.

Mr. Maurodis noted that appointment would require an affirmative vote of three of the four
members.

Commissioner Moskowitz commented that he felt divided as he did not feel it his job to appoint
someone to the dais, but rather to serve his district and the people of Parkland; it was the job of
the voters to elect. However, in view of the upcoming budget process, he agreed it would be a
good idea to appoint someone in these limited circumstances, and he hoped for expeditious
handling ofthe resumes as he wished to either meet or at least speak with candidates.

Commissioner Moskowitz asked clarification regarding approval of the Charter amendments,
particularly in view of the term. Mr. Maurodis explained the unexpired term: if the Charter
amendment does not pass, the candidate would be elected to serve until March 2007 or, with
approval of the Charter amendment, until November 2008.

Mayor Udine offered that, regardless of the Commission's actions, there would be an election in
November for District 4, and he briefly summarized the decisions of this meeting.

For the record, Mayor Udine acknowledged the resignation of Vice Mayor Mimi Ribotsky - with
disappointment. He added that, per the letter written, she offered her assistance for a smooth
transition and would be available to assist the incoming commissioner in moving District 4
issues forward. He suggested that the Vice Mayor's letter be attached to the Minutes of this
meeting.

III ADJOURNMENT

MOTION made by Commissioner McGilvray to adjourn the meeting at 9:25 a.m. Seconded by
Commissioner Moskowitz.

MOTION passed unanimously.
