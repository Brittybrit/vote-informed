3. ORDINANCE NO. 2009 -06: AED'S IN PHARMACY'S ZTA
COMMENTS: FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA,
AMENDING CHAPTER 14 ENTITLED "PUBLIC SAFETY" BY
AMENDING ARTICLE 14 -2 ENTITLED "LIFE SAFETY "; TO
PROVIDE FOR AUTOMATED EXTERNAL
DEFIBRILLATORS IN PHARMACIES, PROVIDING FOR
INSTALLATION AND OPERATION; PROVIDING FOR
CONFLICTS; PROVIDING FOR CODIFICATION; PROVIDING
FOR SEVERABILITY; AND PROVIDING FOR AN
EFFECTIVE DATE.
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Ordinance No. 2009 -06, AED's in
Pharmacy's ZTA and introduced Planning & Zoning Director Ms. Roberta Moore.
Ms. Moore explained the current code requires AED's (Automated External Defibrillators) in
gymnasiums, fitness centers and indoor recreation centers in excess of 1500 sq ft, restaurants
with 100 seats or more, and assisted living facilities. She advised the Planning & Zoning Board
recommended approval of this item on a 6 -0 vote and currently Publix and CVS pharmacies in
the City of Parkland had an AED device.
Vice Mayor Weissman advised he did not believe the CVS Pharmacy had an AED device at this
time. He questioned if the City of Coral Springs required AED's in other locations not listed in
Parkland's code. Staff responded they would look into it and advise.
Commissioner Smith questioned the cost of an AED device; Ms. Moore responded she believed
the units cost approximately $1700.
Fire Chief Mark Curran came to the podium to clarify the cost of an AED device could run from
$1200 up to $3200 depending upon the model and features. He advised the majority of the
models were easy to operate with clear instructions and built in protection against shocking a
person not in need of electrical therapy. Chief Curran also stated retailers were required to
provide in service training with the purchase of an AED device and the Fire department checks
the units for working batteries during annual fire inspections.
Commissioner Rosenof requested staff notify other cities of the adoption of this ordinance with a
letter if approved.
Commissioner Moskowitz requested a review of AED devices in the City of Parkland's parks.
Staff advised the AED's need to be kept in secure locations to prevent loss and Park Rangers
have AED devices in their vehicles.

10. CONSIDERATION: SPECIAL EVENTS AT PINE TRAILS PARK
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young introduced Parks & Recreation Director Scott Davidoff to
present this item.
Mr. Davidoff referred to discussion from the June 11 2008 City Commission Work Shop where
the City Commission requested Staff to develop a fee schedule to allow outside vendors to hold
events at the amphitheater at Pine Trails Park. He advised he had received inquiries from vendors
and promoters interested in this venue.
Mr. Davidoff noted specifically two groups that had come forward. The first named `Driven
Productions' , a group that operates carnival type events and the second was Mr. Casey Gunther,
of Gunther Volkswagen in Coconut Creek, who was interested in offering free concert events for
promotional purposes.
Mr. Davidoff noted some concerns for large events at Pine Trails Park would be insufficient
parking with only 407 parking spaces, the impact to the park, conflicts with sports leagues events
and the impact to members of the YMCA.
Mr. Davidoff stated there was a third party that also showed interest in holding ticketed events at
the amphitheater. He noted this party was a promoter for one of the top national production
companies in the world and a Parkland resident. He advised this company would handle staffing,
parking issues and the City would receive a percentage of the ticket sales.
Vice Mayor Weissman commented that he was not necessarily against the consideration however
he would like a detailed `case by case' analysis to review revenue and expenses involved.
Commissioner Moskowitz commented he was in favor of holding one event on a trial basis. He
added he would not favor any week night events or very low producing revenue events but
advised staff should continue to move forward with their research.
Commissioner Smith agreed with Commissioner Moskowitz's previous statements and added he
preferred the City of Parkland do business with residents of Parkland when possible.
Commissioner Rosenof advised he felt if the event was revenue neutral then even a lower
revenue event would be worthwhile.
Mayor Udine offered he felt the Commission would not want to be locked into a series of events
in the beginning due to various factors. Further discussion ensued.
