City Manager Mark Lauzier read the title of Resolution No. 2006-37. Mr. Lauzier explained
these grants pertain to alternative modes of transportation and the link of public facilities, cities,
and non-vehicular modes of transportation to the public transportation network system. Staff had
identified specific projects; this path would provide a link to State Road 7 as well as to the multi-
use trail system, which would be brought down Parkside Drive, at significant cost. Mr. Lauzier
acknowledged there were minor issues to overcome on the right-of-way with FPL and Camp

Charlie DaBrusco have worked on drawings in conjunction with this application.

Commissioner McGilvray commented about photographs received from a resident about the
condition of the paths on the south side of Holmberg Road. She acknowledged. that this
particular grant probably did not have criteria for repair or improvement of existing pathways,
but she questioned if there might be other grants available.

Mr. Lauzier indicated that Mr. Berkman had closely examined the criteria for this grant, who had
confirmed that this particular grant was for new projects only. While he was unaware of grants
available for repair of existing pathways, he promised to investigate them.

PROVIDING FOR A SEVERABILITY CLAUSE;
PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE; FILE # 12-ZT A-OS.

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2005-32. He recalled the
previous discussions and invited Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer to provide a
briefing. He noted that the City Attorney and Mr. Archer had modified the Ordinance, and that
the agenda backup contained this up-to-date information.

Mr. Archer made a brief PowerPoint presentation regarding the provisions of the Ordinance,
which addressed newly-planted trees and self-seeding exotics as well as non-conforming trees.
He noted that the Planning and Zoning Board had added the permit requirement for tree
trimmers, which followed Broward County requirements and documentation, and had
recommended approval of the Ordinance at its January 6th meeting. Mr. Archer offered that staff
had subsequently reviewed the Ordinance and Section 2, dealing with non-conforming trees, was
amended. It was changed to provide residents with options, as opposed to mandating immediate
pruning or removal, with possibility of replacement, which was felt to cause serious hardship.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky asked regarding the requirement for a tree trimming permit by individual
homeowners and homeowners' associations. Mr. Archer confirmed that individual homeowners
would not be required to obtain the permit, but that homeowners' associations would be so
required.

<<PG>>

Vice Mayor Ribotsky requested that the next issue of the "Parkland Report" include a prominent
notice to residents and associations regarding properties that abut power lines, with a brief
synopsis and perhaps a website link as to the handling.

Commissioner Smith questioned whether this Ordinance applied only to properties in and around
power lines or if it also applied City-wide. Mr. Archer indicated that the Ordinance dealt mostly
with utility areas.

Mayor Udine asked that the word "affective" be changed to "effective" in Section 9-31.1,
subsection 1. Referring to Vice Mayor Ribotsky's questions regarding permit requirements, he
believed that the Ordinance applied to everyone; however, businesses would have to deal with
the permit issue while private landowners would not. Mr. Archer confirmed that firms hired to
perform the work would have to meet the requirements. The Mayor asked the process, and Mr.
Archer provided information to indicate that the burden would be placed on the landscaping
company.

Mayor U dine also requested further explanation of non-conforming trees in Section 9-31.1, and
what had been added post-Planning and Zoning Board consideration. Mr. Archer explained that
the issue of the rule of 20 percent canopy is consistent with both original and revised versions.
The original required that an individual come into compliance within 30 days of the Ordinance
adoption; if they could not prune their trees within 30 days without removing 20 percent of the
canopy, then the Ordinance required that the tree be removed and replaced within 180 days. Mr.
Maurodis added that the original version mandated removal of the trees, regardless of the number
or expense.

Commissioner Smith asked if larger trees that could not be pruned could be replaced with trees
of a smaller caliper. Mr. Archer responded there was a distinction between a street tree and a
specimen tree, and these provisions were contained within another section of the Ordinance.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky questioned if there were any other instances that required permits from
companies but not from individual homeowners. She suggested that this might be a case for
reversing the methodology in that licensed tree trimmers would know proper procedures,
whereas an individual would have no idea and perhaps cause more damage to the trees. Mr.
Archer indicated that he would research this idea and furnish the information for second reading.
Discussion ensued.

Mayor U dine opened to the public.

indicated that Hurricane Wilma had conveniently removed all of the hardwood trees under power

extremely expensive, and some people may not be able to afford it. Many Pinetree residents

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2006-08. He indicated that
Assistant City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young had identified a number of issues related to the
development -- how the development orders were issued and how the development orders were
implemented. In this particular case, the City had published setbacks that were inconsistent with
some of the original Ordinance setback requirements, which rationale could not be explained.
Most importantly, there was concern that the City would be changing the appearance, density, or
uses on the project proposed by the petitioner. Ms. Gardner-Young would discuss the details of
how this came about, the findings, and the recommended solution. While Mr. Lauzier believed
that WCI and the City were on the same page with most of the suggested corrective changes,
there were some areas of differences.

Mayor Udine clarified, for the record, that there can be no rationale for what staff did, if staff
made a rationale on their own. This would have, by definition, had to have been a mistake.
Since the development had been approved by the Commission, he wanted it on the record that it
was either a mistake or not; there can be no "rationale" by staff. Mr. Lauzier agreed.

Ms. Gardner- Young explained this was a joint request by staff and WCI. When she started her
tenure as Planning Director, she had recognized that there were inconsistencies between a staff-
published setback chart and that which was approved by the City Commission. Both she and Mr.
Lauzier felt that these should be addressed, and discussions with WCI had taken place during the
past several weeks. She could not explain how this happened as it had occurred prior to present
staff joining the City; she did not wish to cast blame but rather move forward.

Ms. Gardner-Young acknowledged that it was a confusing issue. She explained a chart which
listed setbacks for all of the pods within Parkland Golf & Country Club, which were relied upon
by residents and contractors for accessory uses. She was confident that compromises could be
reached with WCI before second reading.

Howard Nelson of Bilzin, Sumberg addressed the Commission. He believed that staff had
succinctly explained the issue; a mistake had been made. Roughly half of the community had
been built, permitted, or contracted under a misapprehension of facts, and he believed that the
remainder of the community should be built under similar requirements. He offered to provide a
complete presentation of every detail at second reading.

ADDED AGENDA ITEM:

SELECTION OF CROSSWALK PATTERN

Referring to the pattern sheet he had distributed, City Manager Mark Lauzier noted a wide array
of options for the stamped concrete crosswalks at University Drive and Holmberg Road. The
Commission must provide direction to WCI as to the pattern and the color of the stamped
concrete.

WCI representative Tara Patton noted that, the lighter the color, the more dirt was shown.
Darker colors, such as the reds and terra cottas, held up better.

Commissioner McGilvray asked about the patterns: whether they varied in depth, whether any
one held up better, or whether any required less maintenance. Ms. Patton responded that all
depths were the same. Darker colors were more forgiving, and the quantity of grooves did not
seem to have an impact.

Commissioner Moskowitz offered that he would prefer the European cobblestone pattern in
darker green. He noted a selection could be debated for days.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky suggested the crosswalks should be consistent with the pavers at City Hall
in pattern and color.

Commissioner Smith indicated that, while his preference would be a herringbone brick, he
agreed with the Vice Mayor's remarks.

Mayor Udine agreed with Vice Mayor Ribotsky and Commissioner Smith, and believed the City
Hall pavers were in a herringbone pattern.
