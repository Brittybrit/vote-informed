1. UPDATE: CENSUS 2010
Planning & Zoning Director Roberta Moore said the census is a very important topic in light of the
amount of funding that is at stake, as Commissioner Rosenof identified, if the City does not get all
the residents to participate. She then introduced Pedro Guilarte, Partnership Specialist for the U.S.
Department of Commerce, U.S. Census Bureau, Atlanta Regional Census Center, who provided a
brief update.

<<PG>>

PAGE 7

Mr. Guilarte’s comments included the following:

e The Atlanta office encompasses Alabama, Georgia, and Florida.

e In 2008, a process called Address Enumeration was begun, which basically identifies where
people live, including the homeless.

e Over the last six months, the bureau has been working with organizations such as
municipalities and non-profits to help get the word out.

e In January, an education campaign targeting the public about the coming census began.

e The census questionnaire will be mailed around March 20.

e A couple thousand people in Broward County, many from Parkland, will be hired for
temporary and well-paying positions as census workers. Additional information is available
at Census.gov.

e The census is important because political lines are drawn based on census numbers.

o The federal government distributes $400 billion to communities based on census data, which
is approximately $1200-$1300 for each person counted nationwide. The Florida Complete
Count Committee estimated that the number in Florida is closer to $2000 per resident.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young said that the City provided the meeting room for job
interviews for census positions and staff has created the City’s own census committee, which is
comprised of staff, as well as religious and commercial leaders, in an effort to get the information
out to the public. She noted that the City only had a 66% response rate for the last census, which
has substantial impacts upon the City’s ability to get funding from the state and federal government.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young stated that banners will be put up in the next few months and
the City is coordinating with the schools to put out flyers to help educate people. She added that
there is only one form to complete this year and in addition to the efforts of the Census Bureau, the
City will be a site where forms are available in case some residents do not receive them. City
Manager Caryn Gardner-Young concluded and said the purpose of this update was to get the word
out.
Mayor Udine suggested using a phone message comparable to a code red call to get the information
out and he reiterated the importance of returning the forms so that the City can get its fair share of
funding and boundaries can be drawn correctly.
Vice Mayor Smith suggested utilizing the BSO message boards to get the word out.
Planning & Zoning Director Moore emphasized that the responses provided are 100% confidential
and the information will not be used for any other purpose.

2. DISCUSSION: CHINESE DRY WALL PERMIT

FEE GRANT

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young said this matter was discussed at the last commission meeting
and staff was asked to come up with a process that would allow residents affected by the Chinese
drywall to obtain what she calls a grant, because under state statute, building fees can not be

<<PG>>

PAGE 8

waived. She stated that staff met and additional questions have come up that require guidance from
the Commission. She then spoke about the issue of how to define original property owner and said
a way to address this would be to require proof that the property is homesteaded.

Mayor Udine agreed and then spoke about Chinese drywall disclosure forms used by realtors in
their contracts when the problem became public. He suggested that anyone who signed a drywall
disclosure form should not be eligible for a grant since they were put on notice; a requirement to
produce the original contract will reduce the eligibility pool if there is a disclosure form in there.
Commissioner Moskowitz agreed with excluding those that signed a disclosure form and non-
homesteaded property owners. He stated that he feels this is something the City has to do and he
will support it.

Commissioner Rosenof commented that he was lukewarm on this idea at first, but feels the
Commission should make the leap and do it. He said that the grant should be for homesteaded
property owners and should not apply to re-inspections or to some of the more egregious problems.
A brief discussion was held on treating drywall disclosure forms as disqualification for permit fee
grants.

Commissioner Moskowitz said that regardless of any disclosure forms, he supports the grant for
people who are homesteaded.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young explained how funding of the grants will work from an
accounting standpoint.

Discussion was held regarding setting a specific date by which the property was homesteaded in
order to qualify.

Mayor Udine recommended using January 1, 2010.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young explained that the grant would not apply to a change in the
floor plan nor to re-inspections or additional building permit plans. She noted that proof of Chinese
drywall will also be required and questioned whether those who have already submitted for
permitting should be reimbursed.

Commissioner Moskowitz stated that he feels this reimbursement has to be done.

Commissioner Rosenof said that someone who is proactive should not be punished.

MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to approve the City giving grants to residents
affected with Chinese drywall, conditioned on all of the amendments as given by City Manager
Caryn Gardner-Young on the record as follows: residents have to show a homestead, the homestead
has to be prior to January 1, 2010, and they have to provide a contract which reflects that they did
not know about the Chinese drywall. Seconded by Vice Mayor Smith.

Mr. Backman explained the two separate processes the applicant has to go through and the timeline

density.
