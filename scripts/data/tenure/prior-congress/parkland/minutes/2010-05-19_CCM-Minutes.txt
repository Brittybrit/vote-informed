2. DISCUSSION: PINE HOLLOW DEVELOPMENT
Planning & Zoning Director Roberta Moore gave an overview of the no-fee zoning determination
request submitted last February by NRNS Acquisitions for the construction of a 40-stall barn, a
covered riding arena, and a storage building. She explained that staff reviewed the application
and then sought a legal opinion from Weiss Serota, the City’s outside legal counsel for Ranches
issues; the opinion indicated that the property did have to meet zoning and engineering
regulations, but was exempt from building regulations.
Planning & Zoning Director Moore then explained staff’s review of lot coverage, setbacks,
parking, and engineering, and stated that the development does meet the City’s permitted uses for
the AE-1 zoning district, meets all zoning regulations, and has met the criteria for obtaining an
engineering permit. She said that the heights of the proposed structures are no greater than 25
feet and are within the existing heights currently out there. Regarding maintenance of horse
manure and additional trash, she said that they are required to meet all of the best management
practices.
At Mayor Udine’s request, Planning & Zoning Director Moore explained the steps she followed
upon receipt of this application.
James White, Weiss Serota, reviewed the Florida statutory provisions dealing with agricultural
use or classification and the Florida Right to Farm Act. He noted that the applicant was able to
meet and satisfy all the requirements in the City’s land development code.
Commissioner Moskowitz said that he met with residents last Friday, which is why this matter is
under discussion at this meeting, and then made the following comments:

e When he first took a seat on the Commission, the Ranches was already in the midst of
discussing their future, what it should or should not look like.

o There were two sides in the Ranches: a direction towards more housing and away from
the agricultural, away from more of the commercial aspects that exist today.

e The Commission decided at the time that it would be prudent, considering the direction
the Ranches was moving, to institute some ordinances that would allow the Ranches to
become less agricultural, less commercial, and more community oriented. He feels that
those particular ordinances have not had an adverse affect on the Ranches.

eo The Commission also defined things, including forestry, and talked about what was
permitted and not permitted.

o He was always concerned that eventually these issues would come back up again.

e The economy changed and mansions are not being built in the Ranches; in fact, some of
them are empty.

e Now the direction of the Ranches is going back to more agricultural and commercial.

4. DISCUSSION: NSID LOCATION OF RE-USE PLANT
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young introduced Rod Colon, Director of Operations, North
Springs Improvement District, and said at a previous Commission meeting, Mr. Colon indicated
that there would be a need for a water reuse plant located within the Wedge piece in order to
cover the additional properties that NSID is adding to their district.
Mr. Colon addressed the Commission and his comments included the following:

e NSID’s letter of May 11, 2010, detailing its plans, is in the Commission backup.

e Broward County Commissioner Stacey Ritter wrote a letter of support for this project.

e NSID sends all its wastewater to the County. The county is stating that they have to do a

lot of capital improvement projects to eliminate the ocean outfall.

e NSID is proposing to build a reuse facility to recycle the 2.5 million gallons per day it

currently sends to Broward County.

o Tonight's discussion is about only the wastewater that will be coming from the Wedge.
Mr. Colon explained the reasons for and benefits of supporting reuse, and described the two
proposed sites for the plant: at the northwest corner of WCI Bishops property and property
known as the Saber Land Trust. He stated that even with wastewater from the Wedge, NSID will
still be below its large user capacity number and does not have to build a plant if the Commission
votes against it, but NSID believes recycling this water is the right thing to do.

Commissioner Rosenof said that he was recusing himself because his company does business
with NSID.

Commissioner Weissman asked if there are any negative aspects to this project and Mr. Colon
responded that the plant has to be built and has to be financed, likely through impact fees placed
on new construction in the Wedge. Commissioner Weissman questioned notification to
landowners adjacent to the site and asked if the Commission will be able to approve the site.

Mr. Colon explained that he has met and will be meeting with landowners individually, and
NSID needs approval to do a land acquisition.

Commissioner Weissman commented that he would like some type of confirmation that the
parties who are adjacent to whichever parcel NSID chooses are not only notified, but are okay
with it.

Mr. Colon stated that this is just in the beginning stages and he plans to keep the Commission
informed.

Mayor Udine said that he is supportive as long as the notice requirements are met and he feels
this is an important addition towards keeping water bills as low as possible and providing other
benefits.

 INSTRUCTIONS FOR COMPLIANCE WITH SECTION 112.3143, FLORIDA STATUTES

 WHO MUST FILE FORM 8B
 This form is for use by any person serving at the county, city, or other local level of government on an appointed or elected board, council,
 commission, authority, or committee. It applies equally to members of advisory and non-advisory bodies who are presented with a voting
 conflict of interest under Section 112.3143, Florida Statutes.
 Your responsibilities under the law when faced with voting on a measure in which you have a conflict of interest will vary greatly depending
 on whether you hold an elective or appointive position. For this reason, please pay close attention to the instructions on this form before
 completing the reverse side and filing the form.
 INSTRUCTIONS FOR COMPLIANCE WITH SECTION 112.3143, FLORIDA STATUTES
 A person holding elective or appointive county, municipal, or other local public office MUST ABSTAIN from voting on a measure which
 inures to his or her special private gain or loss. Each elected or appointed local officer also is prohibited from knowingly voting on a mea-
 sure which inures to the special gain or loss of a principal (other than a government agency) by whom he or she is retained (including the
 parent organization or subsidiary of a corporate principal by which he or she is retained);to the special private gain or loss of a relative; or
 to the special private gain or loss of a business associate. Commissioners of community redevelopment agencies under Sec. 163.356 or
 163.357, F.S., and officers of independent special tax districts elected on a one-acre, one-vote basis are not prohibited from voting in that
 capacity.
 For purposes of this law, a "relative" includes only the officer's father, mother, son, daughter, husband, wife, brother, sister, father-in-law,
 mother-in-law, son-in-law, and daughter-in-law. A"business associate" means any person or entity engaged in or carrying on a business
 enterprise with the officer as a partner,joint venturer, coowner of property, or corporate shareholder(where the shares of the corporation
 are not listed on any national or regional stock exchange).
 ELECTED OFFICERS:
 In addition to abstaining from voting in the situations described above,you must disclose the conflict:
 PRIOR TO THE VOTE BEING TAKEN by publicly stating to the assembly the nature of your interest in the measure on which you
 are abstaining from voting; and
 WITHIN 15 DAYS AFTER THE VOTE OCCURS by completing and filing this form with the person responsible for recording the min-
 utes of the meeting,who should incorporate the form in the minutes.
 •
 APPOINTED OFFICERS:
 Although you must abstain from voting in the situations described above, you otherwise may participate in these matters. However, you
 must disclose the nature of the conflict before making any attempt to influence the decision,whether orally or in writing and whether made
 by you or at your direction.
