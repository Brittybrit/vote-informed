3. RESOLUTION NO. 2006-106: INSTALLATION OF SIDEWALK ON
PARKSIDE DRIVE

COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, AUTHORIZING THE
CITY MANAGER TO PIGGYBACK A CITY OF
WESTON CONTRACT WITH TENEX ENTERPRISES
INC. FOR THE PURPOSE OF INSTALLING AN 8' WIDE
CONCRETE SIDEWALK ALONG THE EAST SIDE OF
PARKSIDE DRIVE IN THE AMOUNT OF $317,652.00,
PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE.

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the title of Resolution No. 2006-106. He noted that a capital
project in the CIP had existed for approximately two years relating to the Parkside Drive
sidewalk on the east side of the road, and Public Services Director Jim Berkman had proposed a
piggyback to a sidewalk installation contract by the City of Weston.

Commissioner Moskowitz recalled the last discussion of this project and asked about proper
positioning of the sidewalk in anticipation of a future bike lane. Mr. Berkman indicated that the
eight-foot sidewalks were per the Multi-Use Trails Plan approved by the Commission, and he did
not anticipate that the sidewalk would need to be moved should a bike lane be added.

Commissioner Moskowitz felt that a six-foot sidewalk would allow more room for the bike lane,
and he believed the City should get into the habit of planning for bike lanes when roads were
redone. He wanted assurance that staff would not request funds to move the sidewalk in the
future when a bike lane is added.

Mr. Berkman stated there were others factors in that area: the parking problems at the school and
Quigley Park. He was concerned that cars would park on them, whether or not they were
decreased in width by two feet.

Vice Mayor McGilvray asked about installation of the sidewalk far enough from the roadway.
Mr. Berkman confirmed there were areas with conflicts, such as catch basins and fire hydrants,
that would affect the sidewalk placement.

Mayor Udine stated that he was generally not in favor of the piggybacking process, but he
believed it more important to get the job done in this case. He suggested that the area near the
school should be done when it is not in session, which Mr. Berkman confirmed.

Commissioner Moskowitz asked if the funds saved on a six-foot sidewalk could fund a bike lane.
Mr. Berkman confirmed that roughly $65,000 to $70,000 would be saved, but he could not say,
without doing the math, how much of the bike lane could then be funded.

Vice Mayor McGilvray recalled that the University Drive bike lanes had cost $40,000, and she
was certain that Parkside Drive would cost far more.

6. RESOLUTION NO. 2006-107: STATE ROAD 7 COLLABORATIVE
MASTER PLAN PROCESS

COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, SUPPORTING THE
STATE ROAD 7 COLLABORATIVE MASTER PLAN
PROCESS.

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the title of Resolution No. 2006-107.

Assistant City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young reviewed that the process had been facilitated by
the South Florida Regional Planning Council. She noted that the week-long charrette last
December, hosted by the City, had involved the Treasure Coast Regional Planning Council
design team. Local governments involved in the process have been requested to take part in the
finalization of the Master Plan.

South Florida Regional Planning Council Senior Planner and Project Manager David Dahlstrom
gave an overview presentation of the process. The collaborative involved 16 jurisdictions, with
the State Road 7 corridor divided into nine planning segments. There were three phases in the
Master Plan: organization, which had been started in 2000; planning through the charrettes; and

<<PG>>

PARKLAND CITY COMMISSION MINUTES
NOVEMBER 1, 2006
PAGE 7
implementation, through amendments in Land Use and Land Development regulations. The
goals include improving economic conditions along the corridor, creating local visions, and
promoting transit and connectivity. Some of the local plans have been accepted - in Margate,
Lauderdale Lakes, Lauderhill, Davie, and West Park, and each of these communities have also
submitted corresponding Land Use Plan Amendments to the Department of Community Affairs
to allow mixed uses. Master Plans pending include those of Coral Springs, North Lauderdale,
Miramar, and Plantation, as well as that of the City.

Mr. Dahlstrom offered that a market assessment had also been done by the Urban Land Institute
in 2004 regarding redevelopment potential. He spoke regarding the growth expected by Broward
County in the next 20 to 30 years and starting implementation in other communities.

Mr. Dahlstrom highlighted some of the points of the charrette regarding the Parkland segment,
and the consideration of the ideas expressed by residents and the design team. He displayed
concepts of linear greenways, bus shelters, entranceways, and a mixed use development at
Loxahatchee Road.

Mayor Udine provided additional information concerning the staff and Commission involvement
in the process, and he believed it had been well received by the public. He supported the process
and felt that the wording contained in Section 2 of the Resolution was appropriate.

The Mayor commented that Coconut Creek had not yet "signed on," and he noted that their Wal-
Mart plans had been announced shortly after the charrette had occurred. While he acknowledged
modifications in the plans, he was still uncertain of their buy-in. Mayor Udine also commented
that the City should work with its development community specifically on the II-acre parcel,
and these concepts should be presented to the landowners.

Commissioner Moskowitz stated that he was not in support because he believed that Section 2
should be stronger. He noted that two aspects of the presentation had been the University Drive
extension and expansion in the areas of the power lines, neither of which were "on the table" for
the City.

Mayor Udine recalled that the power line expansion had been offered as a last-minute issue since
the charrette process was meant to offer options; he did not believe that anyone thought it might
happen. As to University Drive, he noted there was not a recommendation made as to what to do
and it was recognized only as a community issue.

Commissioner Moskowitz expressed his concern about approving anything that may involve
University Drive as an option.

Mayor Udine noted that the plan did not have University Drive extended, but connected to
County Line Road - which is similar to the "Wedge" strategy. While it was a point well taken,
he believed it more important that the City be involved in the master planning process.

Vice Mayor McGilvray commented on her view that this was the process and the issues at the
time; things change over time. She agreed that this was a first step for State Road 7.

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the title of Resolution No. 2006-74. He noted that the Planning
and Zoning Board had given their unanimous approval.

Nectaria Chakas of Ruden, McClosky and representing WCI, waived the quasi-judicial
procedure.

Planning Director Roberta Moore briefly explained that the applicant was seeking approval of
water features within the Pod 15 buffer consisting of a five-foot-high stone wall within a
landscaped area. There was no signage proposed as part of this application, and she noted that
landscaping exceeded Code requirements. There were a number of conditions of approval
outlined in the Resolution, but she pointed out an 18-month timeframe. Ms. Moore recognized
the past concerns of the Commission, and this had been addressed during the Board's review.
The previous Resolutions approving PG&CC were still in full force and effect, and none of those
would change based on this application.

Mayor Udine commented that he would like to see some type of penalty clause should the 18-
month timeframe not be met. He did not wish to see the same problems as with Heron Bay.

Paul Angelo, of WCI Communities, understood the concerns over the execution of the features
and, while they would not want a delay, he would agree to an extraordinary site plan application
fee iftimeframes are not met. Ms. Moore indicated that the fee would be approximately $4,000.

Commissioner Moskowitz commented that every project had been delayed well beyond when it
was supposed to be completed. He believed that timeframes should be assured by more than a
$4,000 penalty.

Mr. Lauzier suggested that the City should know long before the expiration of 18 months
whether or not it would be met. He believed that penalties or trade-offs should be determined at
the time of the extensions based upon the status ofthe community; there would be no "free pass."

Commissioner Moskowitz noted that WCI always commenced their projects on time, but these
