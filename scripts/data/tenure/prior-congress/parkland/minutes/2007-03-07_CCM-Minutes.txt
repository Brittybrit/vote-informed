month trial period. While he understood the Board's reasoning and their specific policy,
he believed that staff should turn off the lights if the park was closed due to weather.

morning golf course noise.

reports to the Commission each Wednesday. He recalled that the City had approved
funds in December and that approval at Pinetree Water Control District had just
occurred.

• On the tax millage issues, he had attended all Broward League of Cities' meetings in
the last few weeks, where it had been discussed regularly. With passage, the City
would lose significant revenues. Some cities were forming ad hoc citizen committees
to examine the programs to cut, acknowledging that residents must be educated. He
confirmed that discussions included rollback to 2001 rates with certain modifications,
possible doubling of homestead exemption, and other plans. Residents were
demanding tax relief, and he would take the City's budget to Tallahassee in order to
show legislators the effects of unfunded mandates. He believed it would be a more
equitable system if the State paid for what they should be.
• He would sit at the MPO meeting on the following day, where he believed State Road 7
in relation to the Seminole casino would be discussed, including a study of the traffic it

believed it important that the City have a seat at that table.
• Spoke regarding wellness. He agreed with Commissioner McGilvray's comments -
that the City's vendors and concessionaire must carry healthy choices.

1. RESOLUTION NO. 2007-33: GENERATOR -BID AWARD
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, APPROVING THE
AWARD OF BID NUMBER 2007-03 TO CRISTIANO
ELECTRIC, INC. FOR THE INSTALLATION OF
EMERGENCY GENERATORS AT CITY HALL AND THE
LIBRARY IN THE AMOUNT OF $162,357.50,
PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE.
Mayor Udine recalled that, following Hurricane Wilma, there had been discussion about
generators -before property taxes had become the hot issue -- with the first test of the Public
Safety Building. He agreed that a generator was needed for City Hall because the government
function could then resume faster, but he opposed the $70,000 cost for the Library's generator,
unless it could be stated that contents would be severely damaged. While the Mayor loved that
the Library had been able to provide Internet service to residents without power, he believed it
the wrong time to purchase its generator with impending budget cuts.
Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2007-33. In
response to the Mayor's comments, she stated that she could not think of anything specific at the
Library, such as a rare manuscript, for which there would be concern. Staff s thinking had been
to give residents the opportunity to do something should electricity not be available at other
locations. She agreed it would be essential to City Hall, and she suggested that some future
consideration should be given to generator installation at the Community Center.
Mayor Udine commented that the eastern fire station, as a public safety facility, would have a
generator, as well as conference rooms that might be used. He again stated that he did not favor
a generator for the Library.
Ms. Gardner-Young offered that the Resolution might be amended to include the approval for the
City Hall generator only.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz felt that a generator should be included for the Community Center in
next year's budget so that it might also be an option in an emergency. He suggested that the
funds should be held and appropriated next year to the Community Center.
Commissioner McGilvray questioned that the cost from this company was approximately half of
other bids. Public Services Director Jim Berkman suggested that it may not be so much that their
price was lower, but that the other prices were higher than they should be.

Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2007-22. She noted
that this had been brought back to the Commission at their request.
Leisure Services Director Scott Davidoff recalled that the exclusivity sponsorship agreement for
vending had first been brought to the Commission on January 21St, with both Pepsi and Coca
Cola under consideration. The Commission selected Coca Cola as the preferred vendor and had
asked that they present their contract and proposal to the City. He noted a copy of the agreement
within the agenda package, along with the details of what they would provide. Mr. Davidoff
clarified that this was a beverage agreement; food vending could be pursued later, if the City so
chose. He had familiarized himself with the Broward County School Board wellness policy and
noted that many Broward County schools had exclusivity agreements with either Coke or Pepsi,
which included both Douglas and Westglades.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz expressed concern that the City's concessionaire would be required to
pay $4.00 more per case to purchase Coke through this contract, rather than directly from Coke
in Miami, and that those charges would be passed to the consumer. The concessionaire would
like to purchase Coke under this agreement, but at the price they already paid. If Coke does not
agree, he suggested amending the City's agreement with the concessionaire to allow him to buy it
elsewhere.
The Vice Mayor also asked regarding the availability of other products offered by Coca Cola,
particularly no- or low-sugar, non-carbonated drinks.
The Coca Cola representative reviewed their products, which included Powerade, Coke Zero,
Cherry Coke Zero, Minute Maid Lite, Diet Nestea, and flavored Dasani water. He commented
that they were limited to cans or plastic bottles in park vending machines.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested that a list of all of the choices should be submitted for the
Commission's decision.
The Coca Cola representative indicated that the vending machines placed in the last week had
selections of one Coke Classic, one diet, two water, two Minute Maid juices, and two sports
drinks. He believed that Coke Classic would be the only real non-healthy item. Other healthy
drinks could not be vended in field machines.
Mayor Udine asked regarding the concessionaire's difference in price. The Coca Cola
representative indicated that the concessionaire purchased the product from the Coca Cola
Bottling Company, Hollywood, and his territory, out of the Pompano office, was currently
everything north of Commercial Boulevard. He explained the circumstances and stated that they
could not match the Hollywood cost as they would take a loss.
Mayor Udine suggested that, in reality, the residents would pay for the scoreboards with the
higher price of drinks. He indicated that he would vote no on the exclusivity agreement,
suggesting that the City could build its own scoreboard.

Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2007-05.
Planning Director Roberta Moore gave the staff presentation. She noted that the City already
provides AEDs in public facilities, and this Ordinance would require installation in commercial
indoor recreation centers over 1500 square feet in size, such as in gymnasiums and fitness
centers, as well as in restaurants with ,100 or more seats and in assisted living facilities. She
indicated that staff had worked with the Coral Springs Fire Department, which was in support;
this Ordinance mirrored that of Coral Springs.
Mayor Udine stated that he would vote in favor of this Ordinance. He believed that the only
"negative" may be the perceived burden on business owners, but he would be willing to live with
those complaints. He supported it as a life/safety issue.
Deputy Fire Chief John Forbes of Coral Springs Fire came to the podium.
Commissioner Weissman questioned why only restaurants with 100 or more seats would be
required to install an AED as opposed to those restaurants with 50 seats, as an example. He
believed that smaller facilities provided faster service and, therefore, served more people.
Chief Forbes offered that AEDs were a fairly expensive item, and a restaurant with 50 or less
seats would feel the financial burden more than that of the larger restaurant. He acknowledged
that the seating capacity did not preclude the need.
Mayor Udine commented that AEDs might be required for any size business, but this Ordinance
was comparable to that of Coral Springs.
Commissioner Weissman asked regarding the seating capacity of the City's existing restaurants.
He felt that any new restaurants would know of their requirements and build the cost into their
budget. He believed the burden would be on existing businesses.
Ms. Moore confirmed that this regulation would not affect the majority of restaurants, and she
noted that existing businesses would be given 180 days to comply. She indicated that she would
provide a breakdown of the restaurants in the City with valid occupational licenses.
Commissioner Weissman believed that the Ordinance should be amended to reduce the seating
capacity to 50.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented regarding his desire to have the AEDs strategically placed in
the parks to minimize response time, and he had discussed this subject with Ms. Gardner-Young,
who had indicated that a grant would be pursued for additional defibrillators.
