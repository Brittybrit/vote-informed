2. DISCUSSION: WATERING OPTIONS
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young introduced Landscape Architect Planner Brian Archer and
Public Works Director Jim Berkman. Landscape Architect Planner Archer gave a presentation
on the watering of trees and landscaping in areas without automatic irrigation systems. His
comments included the following:
o The three specific areas are portions of Holmberg Road, Riverside Drive center median,
and portions of Trails End.
o Each area is unique with specific conditions that have different costs associated with
them.
e Staff is proposing three options to provide adequate supplemental watering for newly
planted and/or replaced trees:
-Manual watering by the Public Works Department.
-Manual watering by a private contractor.
-Installation of automatic irrigation systems in these locations.
Landscape Architect Planner Archer said staff is recommending phasing in automatic
irrigations systems over the next ten years and then provided detailed information on the above
options and locations, and related costs and staff recommendations.
Commissioner Moskowitz spoke about tree replanting and relocation, and noted that the newer
trees on Holmberg Road, mainly in front of Cypress Head, were less mature and some have
died due to the water issues under discussion. He questioned the cost of manual watering by a
private contractor and Landscape Architect Planner Archer responded that the cost is for a two-
year period. Commissioner Moskowitz stated that he would like to see a sprinkler system
phased in over a period of time on Holmberg Road or perhaps providing for it during the
budget cycle. He suggested trees be purchased that are slightly older than those planted in the
past, although he recognizes the limitations due to root issues, and added that it makes sense to
invest in the infrastructure rather than pay yearly recurring costs to do hand watering.
Commissioner Rosenof agreed that the automatic irrigation system is the way to go and
suggested this be done in an environmentally friendly way, such as solar powered pumps. He
also suggested drip irrigation be utilized to reduce water usage.
Mayor Udine said he is in agreement with the comments made about automatic sprinklers and
suggested, in light of planning for the next budget cycle, to start looking at this and coming up
with a plan over the next couple of years to get this accomplished, beginning with the most
needed areas and going from there.

1. DISCUSSION: IMPACT FEES
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis gave background information and general rules governing
impact fees, and his comments included the following:

o [tis a heavily litigated area.

o There are a lot of laws governing it.

e An impact fee can only be levied for capital facilities and not operating costs

o By definition, an impact fee can only be levied on new development and can not be
levied for the purposes of making up a facilities deficit that already exists.

o There are very specific requirements, including a “rational nexus test”; there must be a
rational basis between the impact fee and the impact upon the development, and the
expenditure has to be reasonably connected to mitigating the impact of that
development in the area.

e In order to implement an impact fee, generally a study to support that impact fee is
required; there are firms that perform such studies, as it can not be done internally.

o The City has a park impact fee.

e A road impact fee for local roads is a possibility, but very few cities have them due to
their complicated development. Implementation requires a sophisticated traffic
division; Parkland does not have a traffic division.

e The most likely impact fee would be a public safety impact fee; impact fees are not a
panacea to solve all the City’s needs.

Commissioner Weissman spoke about a study that he recalled being done in the past and
questioned the implications for large sections of the City that are not yet complete, such as
Heron Bay.

City Attorney Maurodis responded that the study Commissioner Weissman referred to would
not have included the Wedge and said the most likely area for the impact fees would be the
Wedge as that is where the significant development is going to occur. Responding to a
question from Mayor Udine, City Attorney Maurodis suggested focusing on a public safety
impact fee and possibly having a consultant that does these studies try to identify other types of
impact fees, although that is difficult to do.

Mayor Udine spoke about the provisions for land for schools with reverter clauses in the
annexation agreements and City Attorney Maurodis noted there is specific language in the
agreements that land given to the City is not a credit against any present or future impact fees
other than a school impact fee.

Commissioner Moskowitz questioned road impact fees, in particular for County Line Road,
and City Attorney Maurodis said he would need to do further research.

There was discussion about the future development of County Line Road, as well as eminent

not for county roads.

the City having control over the medians.

City Attorney Maurodis said that it may not be a 100% assessment against Hendrix because
County Line Road is going to benefit the entire transportation system.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young spoke about Lox Road, by way of example, and the
obligations relating to it if someone would come to the City for a land use amendment.

developers at the platting and land use stages what is needed as far as the type of ingress and
egress.

Commissioner Rosenof noted that there are multiple developers in the Wedge and an impact
fee would equalize the cost among developers.

City Attorney Maurodis said he would conduct a further review to determine if there is a way
the City can do impact fees on County Line Road.

Mayor Udine reiterated that the City already has the park impact requirement and the need is
for the public safety impact in the Wedge.

3. DISCUSSION: IMPLEMENTING SPORTS FEES

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young said this item was discussed by the Commission during

strategic planning and staff was asked to bring it back during the year. She then introduced

Parks & Recreation Director Scott Davidoff, who gave a presentation.

Parks & Recreation Director Davidoff commented that as part of the City’s Strategic Plan,

sports program fees and revenue neutral programs were designated as top priorities. He said

that currently there are 3,000 participants utilizing City facilities for sports activities and the

City receives no revenue from these programs. Parks & Recreation Director Davidoff stated

that the majority of surrounding municipalities either run the programs themselves and collect

the revenue, or they charge fees to the independent sports organizations for the use of City

facilities.

Parks & Recreation Director Davidoff then reviewed the various options, fee structures, and

current practices in the City. He noted that the City facilities, which are over-used, have

substantial maintenance costs.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young spoke about usage of the fields and said, if the

Commission desires staff to implement it, a per participant fee would be a new funding source

to address the problem of over-usage of the fields.

Commissioner Moskowitz commended Parks & Recreation Director Davidoff for doing an
excellent job and said people in the community are very happy. He stated that this is one area
that should not be run like a business and although he understands the reasons given, now is
not the time to implement these sort of fees.

Mayor Udine said that Parks & Recreation Director Davidoff is doing a great job and there are
a lot of compliments on the department. He commented that, at this time, he is not in favor of
the City running the leagues. He stated that he would like to see non-resident fees looked at
again to ensure the City is getting fair compensation from non-residents who are playing and
using City fields. Mayor Udine added that he would like to see the City continue giving as
many benefits as possible to the special needs programs and they should be exempted from any
non-resident fees whatsoever. He said he feels the leagues are doing a great job and the
volunteers are to be commended; he wants to make it easier for them rather than adding more
financial burden.

Mayor Udine acknowledged “Mayor for the Day” Brandon Marks who said he would be in
favor of a minimal increase in the annual fee because the kids playing on the fields add to the
maintenance costs.

Mayor Udine said that residents are paying through their tax dollars and explained how the fees
and costs are currently distributed.

Commissioner Moskowitz explained to Brandon that there are needs and expenses in regard to
the fields and this discussion has been about a direct line of funding for that.

Mayor Udine spoke about the leagues and noted that the City has nothing to do with their
internal operations or accounts. He reiterated that he feels the City should maintain the status
quo, the non-resident fees should be looked at again, and every exemption possible should be
given to the special needs programs, including non-resident special needs.

Commissioner Rosenof said he feels that any fee structure, whether it is a discount for special
needs or anything else, should be brought before the Parks & Recreation Board for discussion
as he would like to hear their input.
