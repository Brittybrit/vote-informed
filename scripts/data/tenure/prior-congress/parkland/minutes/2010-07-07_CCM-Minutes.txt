1. CONSIDERATION: POLICE PENSION BOARD CHAIRMAN
TO DISCUSS ORDINANCE 2008-18, SHARE PLAN

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young introduced Frederick Ferrara, Chairman of the Police Pension
Board, who appealed to the Commission to approve the second reading of the share plan ordinance, or
if the Commission chooses not to move forward with the share plan, give some direction to the
pension board.

Steven Cypen, the pension board attorney, spoke about the insurance premium tax monies for the
share plan that can only be used to enhance benefits and said there is no cost to the City. He stated
that if there is another benefit the City would like to utilize instead, it can be done, but it might result
in costing the City if the assumptions are not accurate.

Michael Merlob, 5 member of the pension board, spoke about the share plan, noting that it is a good
idea, and explained that if the City does not act, the State will take away the money for future benefit
improvements and use it; in addition, the State will take away the $69,000 that is currently being used
and the City will become responsible for paying that amount. Mr. Merlob questioned why there has
not been action taken for the second reading of the ordinance.

Commissioner Weissman said that there is going to be an ordinance proposed in August that will
address the overall situation with the pension and may include an annuity factor.

Commissioner Moskowitz stated that he voted for the share plan ordinance on first reading and is
going to continue to support the share plan.

Mayor Udine commented that when the plan was enacted, it was cumbersome because it was
supposed to have been picked up entirely by BSO. He said that there were four people who could not
transition into the state retirement plan and any shortfall was supposed to be picked up by BSO. He
further explained the background of the current pension plan and noted that he is not going to vote to
increase pension benefits at this time. Mayor Udine added that the City’s pension attorney will be at
the Commission meeting on August 18" to discuss options with the Commission.

There was a brief discussion regarding having the City’s pension attorney present at the August 16"
pension board meeting and providing to the board the backup that will be brought before the
Commission.

Discussion was held regarding why the officers were put into a defined benefit pension plan and the
potential consequences of meeting a new benefit level.

Gina Orlando, administrator for the pension board, spoke about the need for open dialogue and
pension members’ concerns regarding their pensions.

Mr. Merlob explained how pension assets are invested.

Additional discussion was held regarding annuities.
