III PUBLIC INPUT
Matt Katz, 7920 Sequoia Lane and President of the CypressHead Homeowners' Association,
spoke regarding the elimination of the crossing guard located within the CypressHead
community. Residents felt this was a mistake; the post was located in a high pedestrian area
because the school was located in such close proximity. The community requested that the
Commission reconsider their decision.

Andrea Schaja, 7301 E. Cypresshead Drive, also asked that the Commission consider returning
the crossing guard on an interim basis, allowing sufficient time to work something else out.
Sheryl Hashagen, 7820 E. Upper Ridge Drive, spoke in support of the crossing guard position.
Susan Kelton, 7420 Dover Court, commented that people do not stop at the stop sign and a police

Fred Musallam, 7220 Pamlico Lane, asked the Commission to reconsider their decision about the

COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, AUTHORIZING THE
CITY MANAGER TO EXECUTE A CONTRACT
AGREEMENT WITH SEATING CONTRACTORS USA
FOR THE .PURPOSE OF INSTALLING ALUMINUM
HOME BLEACHERS WITH PRESS BOX FOR THE
STONEMAN DOUGLAS HIGH SCHOOLS FOOTBALL
STADIUM IN THE AMOUNT OF $515,799.37,
PROVIDING FOR SEVERABILITY; AND PROVIDING
FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2007-96.
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis read changes to the Resolution and contract into the record,
which would be furnished to the City Clerk. He explained that the City would be held liable for
the $515,000 contract when signed by the City. He noted that the previous situation had been a
specific contract with the Broward County School Board in which funds had been committed,
and this situation had "skipped" that step. In order to move forward but still protect the City, he
believed there should be assurance that the money was there before the City enters into the
contract. Mr. Maurodis also wanted written authorization from the Broward County School
Board stating their approval, since the construction would take place on their property. He
understood that time was of the essence, and he asked that the item be approved with these
conditions.
Ms. Gardner-Young advised that the City had received a check for approximately $181,000 for
the first phase of the construction. She further explained Mr. Maurodis' concern that the City
would not be held responsible for the full amount without first having the entire $515,000. She
understood that the only other issue was that of the bond, which was expected within two weeks.
Mr. Maurodis commented that the contract provided that the bond must be provided within 15
days of the agreement. Since the City would not sign the agreement until the $515,000 was in
the City's account, he believed there time for the bond.
Ms. Gardner-Young indicated that the plans were underway.
Capital Projects Manager Horatio Danovich confirmed that the $181,000 check was provided on
this date and that the other $300,000, which would come from a private donor, was expected
within the next two weeks. He also anticipated that the permit would be issued by the School
Board Building Department within the next two weeks, as would the performance bond from the
contractor.
Mr. Maurodis reiterated that he did not want the City to sign the contract until the full $515,000
was received.

Vice Mayor Moskowitz questioned why the City could not sign a contract for the amount of the
first phase, and Mr. Maurodis responded that the City could do that with a phasing plan as part of
the contract. The Vice Mayor asked if that would be acceptable to the applicant.
Dave Grad, Douglas High School Athletic Director, stated that the contractor had given a bid of
$515,000, which would include the public address system and the $15,000 bond. He indicated
that the contractor would phase the project and not bill the school for the entire $515,000.
Giving an example, Mr. Grad stated that the school had been invoiced for the slabs, both visitor
and the home side, which were within the $181,000. The next invoice they will receive would be
for the bleachers and the press box, but not the sound system. Due to the $15,000 bond, which
took them over the limit of their funds, they cannot afford the sound system right now and the
contractor was aware of this. The School Board has issued a purchase order to the school so that
a portable sound system can be purchased. Mr. Grad noted that the School Board had invested
$600,000 into the track.
Mayor Udine explained that the City wanted to facilitate and help, but it had been stated from the
beginning that the City could not be placed at risk for anything more than what had been raised.
He noted that the City had already contributed dollars and would now only facilitate.
Mayor Udine asked Mr. Maurodis regarding the insertion of additional language to make him
more comfortable. Mr. Maurodis responded that the contract should then be amended to include
a .phasing plan and "the City shall not be obligated for any phase until it gives written
authorization to construct the phase." Without the funds, the City would stop the project.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested that the contract should be for the concrete slabs for $182,000;
then, with receipt of the $300,000 for the bleachers, another contract should be signed. The City
could then approve the contract today, with responsibility only for the $182,000 already in its
possession, and not wait the two weeks for the remainder.
Mr. Maurodis stated that he would amend his language to indicate that the City had received the
$182,000, and that the contract was approved, subject to inclusion of a phasing plan that would
not obligate the City to pay for any phase that it does not specifically authorize.
MOTION made by Commissioner Weissman to approve Resolution No. 2007-96, MSDHS
Contract for Bleachers & Press Box, as amended. Seconded by Vice Mayor Moskowitz.
Mayor Udine made additional comments. In Section 12, page 2 of the contract, he requested
additional language that the City would not be held liable: should the contractor be prohibited
from completing work due to Acts of God or acts of negligence by the owner, or should the
School Board halt the work for any reason. On the notice provision, Mayor Udine asked that, in
addition to the City Manager and Capital Projects Manager, the City Attorney also be noticed.
Mayor Udine commented that he would be in favor of the Resolution, subject to the
amendments.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2007-17. Noting
that the City was not responsible for water or sewer installations since services were provided by
Coconut Creek, Parkland Utilities, or NSID, staff felt it inappropriate to have the fees within the
Code and recommended their deletion. Additionally, she noted that every department had a Code
provision that addressed administrative or user fees, and she believed it made more sense to have
them in one section so that they may more easily be found. This change did not authorize the
imposition of fees; they would be implemented only with the Commission's approval through a
resolution. Ms. Gardner-Young admitted that some things had been "slipping through the
crack," offering the example that there was no existing administrative charge for an NSF check.
Commissioner Weissman recalled that he had voted against any raises during these economic
times, but he was not necessarily in favor of removing fees. He asked how the removal of water
fees from the Code would affect new development.
Capital Projects Manager Horatio Danovich responded that the City does not inspect water or
sewer facilities, and he believed it unfair to charge a fee for something done by Coconut Creek,
Parkland Utilities, or NSID.
Commissioner Weissman asked if those fees had been collected in the past. Mr. Danovich
responded in the affirmative, .but noted that the City had also performed the inspections in the
past. The City would no longer inspect or collect fees.
Commissioner Weissman indicated that he was not in favor of this change:
At the Mayor's questions, Ms. Gardner-Young indicated that the City generally did not do
inspections as the City did not install or distribute. If a resident had a problem with their water
service, they were to call their provider and not the City.
Commissioner Weissman commented that, if the City would no longer inspect and no longer
collect fees, the City would then be entitled to collect fees when the City inspects. Ms. Gardner-
Young agreed.
Mr. Maurodis asked, if a separate governmental entity such as NSID was doing inspections,
whether there were Code requirements for their system. Mr. Danovich indicated that Appendix
B of the Code contained requirements specific to design and installation, but they conflicted with
each and every utility. The City could not enforce the contractor to meet the City's criteria when
the permit was issued through one of those utilities.
Commissioner Weissman suggested that the City could make certain there was no shoddy work.
Mr. Danovich agreed, should the City continue to request contractors to notify the City for the
same inspections.
Mr. 1Vlaurodis believed the standards may need adjustment.
