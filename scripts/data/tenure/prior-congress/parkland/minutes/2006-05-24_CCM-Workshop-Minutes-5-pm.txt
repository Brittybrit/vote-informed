CHARTER REVIEW BOARD
RECOMMENDATIONS

Mark Lauzier, City Manager, explained that the Charter Review Board reviewed the
Charter over the last year. Their report has been given to the Commission. There are eight
charter amendments recommended by the Charter Review Board. Mr. Lauzier introduced
City Attorney, Mr. Maurodis, who drafted the amendments.

Mr. Maurodis praised the Charter Review Board on their dedication and diligence. He
then explained their recommendations. Exhibit A deals with the redistricting process;
they believed that it needs to be redefined in an off year to separate it from the politics.

The Mayor opened to comments and questions from the Commissioners.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky expressed concern that while the term of two districts run
concurrent with the term of the Mayor and those Commissioners would not have to resign
to run for the Mayor's seat, the Commissioners in the other two districts, because of the
staggered tenns, would have to resign if they decided to run for the Mayor's seat. Both
districts (with the staggered terms) are on the east side of Parkland. She would like to
find an equitable solution to this situation.

The Mayor stated that he agrees with the Vice Mayor and will entertain a discussion
about this issue following the presentation of the Charter Review Board's
recommendations.

Mr. Maurodis explained the recommended changes to Section 3.05, which deals with
Commission salaries. The Charter Review Board would like to see fair compensation for
the Commission. Their recommendation provides for a specific salary but allows for cost
of living increases. The Charter Review Board also recommends offering the
Commission a straight salary which they felt was easier to regulate than salary plus
benefits.

Mayor Udine stated that the job of Mayor and Commissioner is a tremendous time
commitment; it is not done for the money. There is an extensive amount of work and
attendance at various events.

Commissioner Smith stated he was not doing it for the money.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky said she attended some of the Charter Review Board's meetings.
The Charter Review Board's suggested salary is about average of City Commissions in
the County per survey information provided. She feels the Commissioners in Parkland
put in an above average amount of time. She is comfortable with putting it out to vote to
allow the residents to decide.

Mayor Udine agreed that people should vote on the raises, however he is concerned about
the negative public backlash.. The majority of the Commission ultimately decides
whether or not it is put on the ballot for residents to vote on.

Commissioner McGilvray asked when the salaries would be put into effect. She said that
the Charter Review Board did an amazing job in reviewing this issue. She feels it is a
long overdue raise because there are many out of pocket expenses for the Commission.
She also feels that offering a higher salary may encourage more people to run. She would

like to entertain raises beginning after the next election so the current Commissioners are
not giving themselves a raise.

Mr. Maurodis moved on to the next amendment which is the residency requirements. The
Board recommended changing the minimum requirement of residency for the Mayor
from six months to one year and requires a Commissioner to reside in a specific district
for a minimum of one year.

The Commission had no comment.

Mr. Maurodis next presented Section 4.06, which deals with vacancies on the City
Commission. The current language is confusing and the recommendation is to clarify the
process. The Board suggests that they hold an election to replace a Commissioner;
however, if that is not a viable option, then an appointment is made by the Governor.
This amendment is far superior to the existing one.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky expressed concern that the Governor be involved m an
appointment.

Commissioner Moskowitz feels that an appointment by the Governor should be done in
an emergency only; for example, if there was no functioning Commission.

Mr. Maurodis said he will look into seeing if there is an alternate process.

Mr. Maurodis said the next recommendation addresses the language concerning the
removal of the City Manager. Mr. Maurodis agreed with the clarifying language.

The Commission had no comments.

Mr. Maurodis explained that Article 13, which covers the 80/20 Rule, is not actually a
part of the Charter; it was adopted as a separate referendum prior to the Charter being
adopted. The Charter Review Board recognized the importance of the 80/20 Rule and
wanted to clarify it.

Mr. Maurodis explained that Article 14 provides for a set timeframe for the Charter
Review Board to become active to review the Charter and also for emergencies. It is
similar to the procedures used by Broward County.

Commissioner Moskowitz commented that the appointments to serve on the Charter
Review Board should not coincide with the timing of an election.

Mayor Udine requested that Mr. Maurodis work out a mathematically acceptable way for
appointments to occur during an off year. He agrees that the basic concept is an excellent
idea.

Mr. Maurodis reported that the Charter Review Board also had additional
recommendations for future actions. He stated that the Board worked very hard and did a
great job.

Mayor Udine recognized the Charter Review Board members that were present: Richard
Josepher, Sal Conza, Marilyn Krantz, and Ira Goldman, and thanked everyone who
served on the Board.

Mayor Udine asked Mr. Maurodis to investigate options concerning Commission terms
and how they would coincide with Mayoral elections.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky discussed the possibility of four year terms. She said she received
the results of a survey conducted by the Broward League of Cities which reported that the
overwhelming majority of City Commissioners have four year terms. The Vice Mayor
feels a four year term would add continuity, cause fewer election diversions, and allow
the individual Commissioners to serve with more experience. The new policy should not
extend current terms but come into effect after the next election. Another trend they
should look at is to move the elections to November. Lastly, the City should establish a
term ofthe vice mayor; it should be specifically defined.

Mayor Udine asked to discuss the November election issue first and believes it would
force the issue of even terms. He is concerned the Charter Review Board did not
recommend these changes; however there is a cost savings to hold the election in
November. The Mayor requested that the City Attorney further examine this issue and
present the Commission with some ideas.

Commissioner Moskowitz said they should avoid having the entire Commission elected
at one time. He thinks three years is a better term length, it increases accountability with
more elections.

Mayor Udine further stated that the vice mayor should be a one year term and may serve
a maximum oftwo consecutive terms.

Commissioner McGilvray clarified that a person could serve again as vice mayor after at
least one off year.

Mr. Lauzier said the Commission would need to decide on the amendments by July 5th in
order to submit to the Supervisor of Elections in the specified timeframe.

Mr. Maurodis will bring back additional ideas at the first meeting in June.

17 FOOT MULTI USE EQUESTRIAN TRAIL -
WEST OF WINNER'S CIRCLE

Mark Lauzier, City Manager, proposed that the Commission move forward with both the
site plan amendment for the Winner's Circle apartment Complex and fixing the landscape
buffer between the Winner's Circle and Country Place communities along the multi use
pathway. There is a choice of five different actions for the Commission in order to
address these issues. City staff recommends going with the original approved plan which
includes installation of a buffer, clean up and mulching of City property. The property
along the water is to be cleaned up by the water district. Mr. Lauzier introduced Brian
Archer to present more details about the plan.

Brian Archer, Environmental Resource Director, researched the use of multi - use trails
which are dedicated to the City. He presented the trail and landscape plan at Winner's
Circle and displayed diagrams of the 17 foot trail to the Commission.

Mayor Udine asked the Commission if they had any questions for Mr. Archer.

Commissioner Moskowitz asked if both options D and E would allow for the 17 foot
equestrian trail.

Mr. Archer confirmed that was correct.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky expressed concern that the trail was almost 20 years old and
apparently the City had never officially adopted it.

The Mayor opened to comments from the Commission.

Commissioner McGilvray asked if the City knew who was usmg this trail. Local
residents say they only see a horse once a month.

Mr. Archer stated that currently the equestrian portion of the trail is barely useable; it
looks like it was never actually cleared.

Commissioner Moskowitz said there has been a lot of developer dollars invested into the
west side of Parkland; the Commission does not favor one side of Parkland over the
other. He likes option E; however he wants to make sure there is some visibility to the
sidewalk because there could be security issues.

Mr. Lauzier clarified that the buffer on the east side is part of every option. There are no
plantings on west side.

Vice Mayor Ribotsky stated that the only difference between options D and E is the
irrigation system. She asked if there was funding for this plan and suggested they use the
