Mayor Udine commented regarding letters he had received from County Commissioners about
the State's desire to remove County oversight on land use. He believed that the County had
cooperated with municipalities through appointments to the Planning Council and other efforts,
and he noted that the County had supported the City about the Wedge through legislative priority.

Resolution with the caveat under Municipal Home Rule that the City would not make a decision
on the County fight regarding land use.

too protective, and too anti-density and, for that reason, they were "on the same page" as the
City. He believed the Mayor's point well taken.

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2007-03. He recalled the
Commission recommendation for an EAB safety committee, which had resulted in the City
Attorney's draft. It was proposed that four of the Board members would serve on the committee,
plus three additional individuals would be appointed.
Commissioner Weissman commented that his recent campaign had included the proposal to
create the subcommittee that would provide information, suggestions, or programs to enhance
school safety in Parkland and, hopefully, lead the way for the entire school system. There were
currently no members of the EAB with experience in public safety or security, and he believed
that people of this expertise were required. The Commissioner spoke about the favorable
response he had received from the electorate during his campaign. He believed the City had the
people and resources, and he urged the Commission's support of the subcommittee.
Commissioner Weissman stated that he would also like to amend the proposal: changing to "up
to four members" of the EAB and leaving all of the Commission-appointed positions at-large.
Commissioner McGilvray suggested that the committee should be expanded to a board broader
than the schools to include pedestrians and bicycles. She was concerned that the City may not be
able to dictate to the School Board, and she noted that members of the EAB had opposed the
formation of the subcommittee. She felt that further discussion was warranted.
Following his questions of clarification, Vice Mayor Moskowitz believed the number of
meetings per year should be mandated, and he suggested one per semester. While he agreed with
Commissioner McGilvray's comments, he felt that bicycle and pedestrian safety could be
included in subcommittee responsibility since the majority was students. The Vice Mayor also
suggested that the subcommittee meet with the school principals and school resource officers,
believing there maybe issues that could be addressed by the City if the proposal goes forward.
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis asked clarification of Commissioner Weissman's amendments.
It was noted that, should no member of the Education Advisory Board wish to serve on the
subcommittee, all appointments would then be made by the Commission. Mr. Maurodis
indicated that the revisions would be made.
Mayor Udine believed that, in concept, the safety committee was a good idea. He commented
that one of the things mentioned at the EAB was that incidents in schools were a reflection of
what was happening in the community. He• suggested that a problem could be that EAB
members also serve on other boards in the community; they should theoretically control the
committee, which would enable the input of public safety experts. The Mayor .also favored a
minimum requirement of one meeting per semester since the EAB met monthly; issues could be
workshopped with the committee. He believed it would accomplish the goals envisioned by
Commissioner Weissman, while not overworking the EAB and assisting staff.

4. RESOLUTION NO. 2007-09: PEPSI VENDOR AGREEMENT

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the title of Resolution No. 2007-09. He explained that staff was
trying to take advantage of opportunities that would be mutually beneficial and result in revenue
as well as items of use. It was found that Pepsi had provided the best business opportunity.
Leisure Services Director Scott Davidoff stated that both Pepsi and Coco-Cola had submitted
proposals.. He explained that there were many .similarities in a comparison of the two products,
but noted that Pepsi had offered some additional value to the City.
Commissioner McGilvray hoped that the vendor would also concentrate on the healthy
alternatives for sports drinks, and Mr. Davidoff noted that Pepsi had healthy snacks and drinks as
part of their product line.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz believed that healthier alternatives -without the caffeine and sugar -
should also be offered in vending machines.
Mayor Udine admitted that one of his first concerns had been the concept of commercialization,
noting that the scoreboards were basically "sold" for advertising, but he believed the City had
evolved enough to accept it. He also commented that the City currently had an RFP out for the
concession, and he asked whether this agreement might affect that due to the exclusivity.
Mr. Davidoff responded that the concession RFP included the stipulation that the concessionaire
would abide by any agreement held by the City with a beverage company; concessionaires were
familiar with this concept. He noted that the Pepsi pricing was discounted, which was expected
to equate to lower pricing at the concession stand.
Mayor Udine stated that he preferred Coke and would like to see their proposal. He felt it may
be a better brand for the City.
Commissioner McGilvray suggested that flavored waters should also be vended. Mr. Davidoff
responded that each company had their own water brand and that Pepsi also had fitness water.
The concessionaire would be able to sell Smoothies and things of that nature that would not
involve exclusivity. Mr. Davidoff indicated that the City could determine the product.

residents, he had assembled a table that, for every five trees, 30 days were given to replace them.

Mayor Udine opened to the public.
Natalie Bigio of Pinetree Estates commented that the Ordinance concerned trees taken down by
the homeowner, but she questioned the exemption of trees killed by a natural disaster, such as a
hurricane. She noted that pine trees, rocked by the hurricane winds, were dying at a prodigious
rate but had already been vulnerable because of the beetles. She believed it should be considered
related to the natural disaster. She asked that a timeframe provision be inserted into the

not feel it would be an issue.
Commissioner Weissman indicated that he had discussed large tracts of land with Mr. Archer.

portion thereof. Property owners would be responsible for the larger tract in the same time, and

Commissioner McGilvray commented that, with 10 acres of more, there would probably be a
developer involved, and she noted that trees were not usually planted until a certain level of
construction was completed.
Mr. Maurodis suggested that leniency would apply only to three acres or less, and Mayor Udine
added that, with more than three acres, this chart would not apply. At Commissioner
Weissman's suggestion that a sentence be added, Mr. Maurodis offered that it would be clarified.

Mr. Lauzier offered that there had been comments from constituents about park lighting
operation when not in use. It had been discussed by the Parks and Recreation Advisory Board,
and he read from their meeting minutes. The Board suggested that the lights should be left on
until 10:00 p.m., unless the park was closed earlier due to inclement weather. Staff would follow
the recommendation of the Board unless the Commission objected.
While stating that he agreed with the recommendation of the Parks and Recreation Board, Mayor
Udine suggested that the situation should be monitored and revisited next year. He agreed that
the parks should be operational but, should it be shown that fields were not used in later hours,
then perhaps the lights should be turned off. As an example, he suggested that the Quigley Park
lights might be turned off earlier on non-league nights and Terramar might be designated the
park with lights until 10:00 p.m.
Commissioner Weissman commented that motion detectors were used in some environmental-
friendly buildings, and he suggested that these be investigated for park lighting. Mr. Lauzier
stated his doubt that this technology would work with the sodium vapor lights.
