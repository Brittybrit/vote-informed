II PURPOSE:

Mayor Udine first outlined the procedures and noted this was an open public meeting.

1. PRESENT A TIONS & INTERVIEWS:

ENGINEERING & CONSULTING SERVICES
BID # 2006-11

The first presentation was made by R. J. Behar & Company, Inc., a Broward County-certified
minority firm, which was represented by Juan Vazquez and Robert Behar, principal. It was
indicated that a comprehensive team had been assembled for the contract -- covering civil

engineering, structural engineering, construction management, and transportation consulting, and
the experience of team members was reviewed.

Commissioner Weissman questioned the number of professional staff located in Broward County
and whether there might be conflicts. Mr. Behar indicated that all 35 staff members were located
within the County and that, with 98 percent of their work in the public sector, they anticipated no
conflicts.

The second presentation was made by CDM, represented by Jon Goldman, officer-in-charge of
the Fort Lauderdale office, and Stewart Magenheimer, proposed project manager. The office was
comprised of 35 people and had operated locally for 40 years, and Mr. Goldman offered
information about the experience of the team in all consulting disciplines.

Commissioner Smith questioned whether there might be any conflicts of interest, and Mr.
Goldman indicated that the firm had no private interests at the present.

Commissioner McGilvray asked regarding the percentage of their business in the public sector,
and Mr. Goldman indicated that their office was all public. Another division worked
internationally and throughout the United States in the private sector.

Commissioner Weissman noted that Keith & Associates had been mentioned for roadway work,
and he asked whether that staff had been included in their total of 35 people. Mr. Goldman
stated that the Keith & Associates staff would be in addition to their own.

Mayor Udine questioned the number of staff dedicated to the City's projects. Mr. Goldman
indicated that the full gamut of their Florida resources would be available to include Mr.
Magenheimer's leadership, the 35 in-house staff, and the design center in Orlando.

The third presentation was made by Carter Burgess, primarily represented by Steve Williams;
other members of the team introduced themselves and reviewed their experience. Their office
was comprised of nearly 60 professionals locally, with roughly 150 in the State of Florida; the
home office was located in Fort Worth, TX, with 3,000 professionals nationwide.

Commissioner Weissman questioned the number of professional full-time staff in the Fort
Lauderdale office and whether there may be possible conflicts. It was indicated that 57 people
were in the office, with 15 to 20 engineers, six landscape architects, and six planners. No
conflicts were anticipated.

Mayor Udine asked regarding their existing projects for Coral Springs. The response was that
the firm was engaged in studies concerning recreational survey, design, and drainage, as well as
traffic studies and illuminated street signs.

Vice Mayor Moskowitz questioned their position on the extension of University Drive. It was
indicated that they were not specifically consulting as to the city's goals, but the firm did

represent Coral Springs as it related to a current design project that affects the intersection of
Sample Road and University Drive and their "downtown" concept.

The fourth presentation was made by CPH Engineers, represented by Robert Walker.
Established in 1981, there were 11 branch offices in Florida and a total of 350 employees,
including Texas, Connecticut, and Puerto Rico. He briefly reviewed the qualifications of their
staff and its experience.

There were no questions of the firm.

The fifth presentation was Craven, Thompson & Associates, represented by Tom McDonald and
Bob Cole. As a full-service, multi-disciplined firm located in Fort Lauderdale with local
knowledge, they had been in business 45 years, and their continuing contracts included the
School Board of Broward County and other Broward County cities. Mr. McDonald reviewed
their past projects and relevant experience with other municipalities; 70 to 75 percent of their
work had been in the government or quasi-government arena. There were 90 people in Broward
County, with 25 registered professionals.

Commissioner Weissman asked regarding conflicts with developers in Parkland, and Mr.
McDonald responded they were not currently working on any development projects in the City.

Commissioner McGilvray asked regarding their work for Coral Springs and Coconut Creek. It
was indicated that they were on a list of continuing consultants for Coral Springs, but there were
no active projects at this time. They had been selected to do the park in Coconut Creek; there
was no continuing relationship.

The sixth presentation was made by Kimley-Horn and Associates, represented by Gary Rattay.
As a full-service firm, they had served municipal clients for nearly 40 years. There were
approximately 2,300 staff around the nation and 800 in Florida; their local office was in nearby
Fort Lauderdale. Mr. Rattay reviewed a list of municipalities for which they had served as
general and specific consultants, as well as the qualifications and experience of the staff to be
involved in their services.

Commissioner McGilvray asked the definition of "geotechnical." Mr. Rattay explained that,
performed by a subcontractor, it was boring into the soil to investigate and analyze its conditions.

Commissioner Weissman questioned the number of full-time employees at the Fort Lauderdale
location and whether there might be any conflicts with developers in the area. Mr. Rattayoffered
there were 42 people in the office, and he acknowledged there had been issues in other
municipalities where potential conflicts had been addressed and resolved. There were no
potential conflicts with the City.

The Commission took a brief recess at 5 :40 p.m.
The meeting resumed at 5:47 p.m.

The seventh presentation was made by Nutting Engineers of Florida, represented by Rick
Wohlfarth, a local engineering company with interest in the geotechnical, construction materials
testing, threshold, special inspection, and environmental. With 80 employees, including four
professional engineers, he reviewed their experience, internal processes, and diverse client base.

Commissioner Smith asked whether the firm could handle the City's engineering needs, and Mr.
Wohlfarth gave an affirmative response.

Mayor Udine questioned the number of Broward County employees and whether there may be
any potential conflicts with developers. Mr. Wohlfarth estimated the number of employees at 30,
and stated that they were currently working with Kol Tikvah. Any potential conflicts would be
identified, discussed, and resolved.

The eighth presentation was made by GF A, represented by Bud Cobb. He introduced the firm,
founded in 1988, with five offices and 150 employees statewide. The contract would be
administered through the Boca Raton office. Mr. Cobb detailed the firm's certifications, the
qualifications and experience of its staff, as well as resources, equipment, and numerous and
notable project involvements.

Mayor Udine asked if the firm had been retained for Coconut Creek's new "downtown" area. It
was indicated that their portion of the work had been completed.

The ninth presentation was made by Calvin, Giordano and Associates, represented by Eric
Penfield. He introduced other members of the firm, and he noted that their office was local. The
team had been selected based upon the individual capabilities and availability, and their work
would be tailored to the needs of the City. Mr. Penfield reviewed the experience of the firm with
other municipalities in the area.

Commissioner Weissman asked the size of the full-time professional staff in Fort Lauderdale.
Mr. Penfield stated there were approximately 150 full-time staff members in Fort Lauderdale,
and approximately 30 in the West Palm Beach office. He confirmed there would be no conflict
of interest with any developer in or around the City, and they were not a municipal provider for
any adjacent city.

Mayor Udine questioned the firm's position with Weston. Mr. Penfield stated that his firm was
contractually the city's engineering staff, and had been since its incorporation in 1996, with full-
time personnel in those roles. For the City of Parkland, separate individuals had been identified
and their roles would be explicitly and exclusively for the City.

The Commission recessed at 6: 15 to allow the last presenter's scheduled presentation.
The meeting was resumed at 6:20 p.m.

The last presentation was made by T. Y. Lin International, represented by Gino Valderrama. The
firm had been formerly known as H. A. Ross, headquartered in Coral Gables for 58 years. He
described it as a multi-disciplined firm with considerable experience with municipalities, and the

III ADJOURNMENT

MOTION made by Commissioner Weissman to adjourn the meeting at 6:28 p.m. Seconded by
