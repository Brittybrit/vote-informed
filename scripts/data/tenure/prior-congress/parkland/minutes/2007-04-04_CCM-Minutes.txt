1. DISCUSSION: CSFD CONTRACT ENHANCEMENT
Mayor Udine believed there had been some misreporting about the last meeting and noted there
had been two separate issues: the staffing of a temporary trailer at the new Western Fire Station
site and the permanency of the project. He explained that the Commission had asked the Interim
City Manager to investigate the change in staffing numbers, the concerns of FPL, and the issue
with the trailer itself. The Commission understood there would be no detriment to holding off
the decision until this evening's meeting to allow the issues to resolve.
Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young stated that, since the last meeting,. she had met twice
with Coral Springs staff, and she had provided a memorandum from the Capital Projects
Manager outlining the ability to proceed with the temporary fire station. She was pleased to
advise that .the requirements could be met and the anticipated completion date was April 30tH
Trailer paperwork had been clarified by Coral Springs. Unfortunately, FPL could not move more
quickly, and she recommended that the City proceed with the use of a private electrical
contractor. Coral Springs provided a contract credit of approximately $54,000 with the reduction
of staff to 12. There was sufficient budget to cover the expenses to October.
Mayor Udine commented that he had spoken with Mayor Brook, Vice Mayor Gold, and
Commissioners Mena and Boccard. There were legitimate issues that needed to be worked out
prior to moving forward as partners and not as provider/customer. The Coral Springs
Commission also viewed the relationship as a partnership benefiting the residents of both cities.
He was in favor of the temporary staffing and fire station.
Commissioner Smith offered that he had received a number of phone calls and e-mails during the
past days. He acknowledged that the fire station was needed and, with the details worked out, he
was in favor of moving forward to open the temporary station as quickly as possible.
MOTION made by Vice Mayor Moskowitz to approve the temporary fire station. Seconded by
Commissioner Smith.

COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND,
FLORIDA, AMENDING THE CITY CODE BY

THE BENEFIT FORMULA MULTIPLIER TO 3% AND TO

Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the second reading of Ordinance No. 2006-45.
She noted that the changes requested by the Commission at first reading had been made. Staff
still recommended denial due to the concern with potential cost to the City.
Chairperson Fred Ferrara spoke regarding the members of the plan and its funding and formation,
with assurance that Section 185 monies would be spent for enrichment or enhancement to the
basic plan. $194,000 had been obtained in the first year, and he explained that the Board had
unanimously agreed to wait until the funds for the second year were credited - if they were the
same or higher -before moving ahead with plan enrichment. He spoke regarding the "norms" of
20 years and out in numerous municipalities, as well as the cost-of-living increases of three
percent. Sergeant Ferrara felt these enhancements offered something to each of the four
members; the monies were there and there would be no liability to the City now or in the future.
Assurances have been built into the Ordinance.
Attorney Stephen Cypen reiterated these were premium tax rebates and would not be affected by
any ad valorem tax rollback. The rebates were not in jeopardy -- they had been in the statutes for

more than 30 years -and the funds were traditionally used for extra benefits. He emphasized
that the funds accumulated could not be used for any purpose other than enhancements. There

Commissioner Weissman questioned the shortening of the retirement requirement, suggesting

2. CONSIDERATION: UNIFORM SIGN PROGRAM
Planning Director Roberta Moore stated that staff, at the direction of the Commission and in
conjunction with WCI, had hired Urban Design Studio to create design concepts for the Uniform
Sign Program.. A charrette, attended by residents, had been held. Urban Design Studio was
present to make a presentation on the workshop findings, and she introduced Anne Booth and
Allen Borza.

<<PG>>

Ms. Booth reviewed that their firm had started working with WCI on a program to develop a
unified sign program; conceptual sign designs were created and later presented to the
Commission. The Commission had asked that staff work with the community for input, and a
workshop was held January 17th. In addition, a website questionnaire had been posted to solicit
input from the community on the four sign designs. Ms. Booth commented that the primary
benefit of a uniform sign design was to create a consistent and cohesive theme throughout the
City, creating a desirable living space that, in turn, increases property values and generates
greater tax revenue.
The three components of the program were directional signage, points-of-interest signage, and
entry monuments. Ms. Booth noted there were several current opportunities for major entry
signage, as well as public facility signage. She explained that existing signage was a
combination of styles and types; the ones given focus at the workshop were primarily the
monument signs, which would create the underlying theme for the balance of the signs.
Ms. Booth noted consensus at the workshop for rustic but simple, elegant signage, using natural
stone in keeping with the theme of the City. Landscaping was considered a key element of the
signage program. She described the preferences for a single theme and for sign locations and
their components, including the lighting and lettering. The expressed. concerns included: that the
input of western communities should be solicited, which were not represented at the meeting;
that the funds allocated toward signage should be carefully considered; that the signage of
communities and developments should be individualized; and that roundabout signs should be
consolidated.
Ms. Booth presented and described their conceptual drawings, and she asked for Commission
preference and direction.
Mayor Udine opened to the public. He noted that the three-minute rule would be waived for
workshop participants.
Dodie Keith Lazowick, 8400 Trotters Lane, felt the program wonderful. She offered that this had
been discussed in the past, and consensus had never been reached. She believed the uniformity
would be great for the community.
Former Mayor Sal Pagliara commented that he agreed with many of the items described by Ms.
Booth, and he confirmed that considerable input had been given at the meeting on January 17th
It was decided that the signage must be unique and unlike. any other city. While he commended
the conceptual drawings, he believed they resembled those of WCI too much, and he agreed that
the communities should be individualized. Mayor Pagliara distributed some ideas and believed
the signs must have continuity throughout the City. He suggested that he, Mario Mangone, and
Brian Archer had considerable experience in this .area, and he asked that they be given the
opportunity to put something unique together and work with Urban Design. He suggested that
another workshop could then be held to review the designs.

alcoholic beverage establishments not part of a restaurant, such as a bar or lounge facility, would
be a special exception for Commission approval. This Ordinance proposed some additional
regulations mandating a 1,000-foot distance between alcoholic beverage establishments and a
schoal site, a day care center, or an educational center, as well as 500 feet between alcoholic

County with one exception to the definition area. Staff had worked with an attorney representing

that the Commission had the language of both versions included in the packet.

his understanding that the Board had believed that its passage would tacitly approve bars and

Mayor Udine indicated that he had asked for this discussion. He believed the City had received a
