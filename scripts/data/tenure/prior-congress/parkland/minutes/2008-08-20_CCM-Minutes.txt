 1. UPDATE: SCHOOL Z
 Planning & Zoning Director Roberta Moore introduced: School Board member Stephanie Kraft
 and Project Manager Marshall Washington; Skanska Project Manager Chip Penn; Roger Bolling,
 Song & Associates, Architects; and Engineer Robert McSweeney of Keith and Associates. Ms.
 Moore noted that groundbreaking for School Z had occurred the previous week.
 School Board member Stephanie Kraft commented regarding the parking situation at Park Trails.
 She noted that, with the School Z opening next year, there would be less people at Park Trails
 and some of the portables may be moved out. In addition to getting some of the green space
 back, she noted that a few more parking spaces could be added. Ms. Kraft commented that the
 School Board was excited that School Z would be one of the first environmentally-conscious
 schools in Broward County. It appeared to be on time and on schedule.
 Mr. Washington indicated that Skanska USA, the construction manager for the project, was
 issued their notice to proceed on July 18th; they have 365 days to complete the work. To assure
 that the school opens on time, they were present to discuss the working hours.
 Skanska Project Manager Chip Penn requested that construction hours start at 7:00 a.m., Monday
 through Friday, and on Saturday from 7:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. When slabs were to be poured, they
 would notify the City one week in advance so that they may start at 4:00 a.m.
 At the Mayor's request for guidance, City Attorney Andrew Maurodis suggested that the School
 Board occupied a different status. He believed that a motion by the Commission approving those
 hours would be appropriate. As a governmental entity undertaking a project for a public purpose,
 the School Board would be exempt from many of the building permit requirements.
 Vice Mayor Moskowitz wanted assurance that, by allowing the construction hours, the school
 would open on time in August, and Mr. Washington indicated that July 18, 2009, would be the
 substantial completion date. The Vice Mayor suggested that the City would entertain even longer
 hours to assure completion on time.
 Commissioner Rosenof expressed concern that the slabs would be poured before the rush hour,
 and particularly that concrete trunks would parade on University Drive while school carpools
 were en route. Otherwise, he would rather start the pours at 9:00 a.m.
 Ms. Moore offered that staff has been diligently working with the School Board, Skanska, Keith
 and Associates, and Song & Associates to ensure that the school is constructed. Any issues

 City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the second reading of Ordinance No. 2007-25.
 Planning and Zoning Director Roberta Moore waived quasi-judicial. She offered that staff had
 since 2006 worked with Calvin, Giordano to process these amendments in response to concerns
 voiced by DCA and FDOT regarding University Drive. Staff had proposed that policies be
 added to the EAR to indicate that, once University Drive had been resolved, the City would
 reevaluate its Comprehensive Plan. It would then be brought back to the Commission for review
 and adoption.
 Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked clarification. Ms. Gardner-Young explained that the Broward
 County Plan reflects University Drive as a certain condition, which the City is not in favor of;
 however, there must be a consistency throughout the County. If this was not done, the City's
 Plan would be held deficient; Comprehensive Plan Amendments could not then be passed
 without penalty, including losing funding. Ms. Moore added that the policies were very clear:
 the City will reevaluate once University Drive has been addressed.
 Mayor Udine commented that, with $2 billion in debt, FDOT was not building new roads,
 particularly those opposed by the public.
