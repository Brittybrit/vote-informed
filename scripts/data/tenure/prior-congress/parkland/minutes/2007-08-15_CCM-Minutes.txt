1. DISCUSSION: COMMUNITY CENTER -YMCA
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young recalled the previous discussions concerning the possibility
of an outside agency operating the Community Center.
Leisure Services Director Scott Davidoff recalled that options had been presented to the
Commission at the June 13tr' meeting. There were two organizations capable of offering the full
range of programming, and the Commission. had authorized that staff commence negotiations
with the YMCA. Representatives of the YMCA were present; after a short presentation, they
could answer any questions.
Sheryl Woods, President and Chief Executive Officer of the YMCA of Broward County, gave
brief background information about her experience with the YMCA .and its history. Her
specialty has been the opening of facilities in partnerships -- with local governments, as well as
in school settings and private business, and she credited their success to communications, the
partnerships, and the understanding of a common vision. She applauded the City's investment in
the Community Center for the sake of strengthening the community and an active lifestyle. She
was also pleased to state that she was a resident of Parkland and loved its hometown feel.
Ms. Woods gave a PowerPoint presentation about the YMCA and its expertise, reputation and
programs.
Commissioner Weissman indicated his understanding that the YMCA would not compete with
the City-run camp program, which Ms. Woods confirmed. He asked the YMCA's intentions
with respect to early child care, and explained that his concern was that not-for-profit and for-
profit enterprises already offered these services in the City. With the less-than-market lease with
the YMCA, he would not want to put those entities at a disadvantage. Ms. Woods assured that
the YMCA would first investigate the needs of the community, and she believed that their
success was dependent upon collaboration and not duplication.

Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked confirmation that the proposal was afive-year contract. Noting a

Commissioner Smith remarked that he had spoken with Ms. Woods at the "Passport to

Mayor Udine also wanted assurance that the City's summer camp program would not be diluted

would work together. Mr. Davidoff indicated that this and other commonalities had already been
discussed. He assured there would be no duplication of programming; however, there would be
things that the YMCA could offer that the City cannot, such as special needs, specialty and sports
camps, etc.
Mayor Udine also wanted to be certain that Parkland residents would have an earlier window to
register for programs and to have a different membership structure since it was resident taxpayer
dollars that have paid for the facilities. He hoped that the City could maintain some involvement
or have an open line of communication for the input of ideas so that the facility could remain
tailored to the community.
Mr. Davidoff commented that a committee would be formed, comprised of YMCA and City
staff, which would meet on a monthly basis.
Ms. Wood offered that aCounty-level committee had met monthly and had become very
important. She indicated that she was not at all concerned about communications.
Mayor Udine offered that he had spoken with Weston and had visited the facility; he wanted to
make sure these issues were addressed in the proposal. He believed the YMCA's expertise in
running this type of operation would be of huge benefit to the City. The Mayor believed it the
appropriate way to be a steward of taxpayers' dollars; it would go from being a negative to a

2. RESOLUTION NO. 2007-79: WINE & CHEESE STORE
SPECIAL EXCEPTION
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, FOR SPECIAL
EXCEPTION APPROVAL OF THE RIVERSTONE WINE
SHOPPE, LOCATED AT THE .SOUTHWEST CORNER
OF STATE ROAD 7 AND MECCA BOULEVARD, CASE
NO. O 1-5E-07.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2007-79.
Planning and Zoning Director Roberta Moore explained that this request was for a special
exception fora 2,200-square-foot wine and cheese store within the Riverstone Shoppes at 7329
State Road 7. The applicant has requested a special exception in order to be able to offer. wine
tasting within the retail setting of the wine store. Very specific hours of operation have been
indicated by the applicant, which were identified in the staff report, as well as a number of uses,
which Ms. Moore explained. The Planning and Zoning Board had reviewed the application and
had recommended approval on a 4-2 vote, .and she indicated that the Board had concerns
regarding the consumption of alcohol on the premises. She added one condition of approval to
those already identified: that the number of tables be limited to four and the number of chairs be
limited to eight (two chairs per table). Ms. Moore offered that she had spoken with the applicant
concerning this additional condition of approval, who had indicated his agreement. The
applicant was present to answer any questions.
Mayor Udine noted this was a quasi judicial proceeding. The applicant, Scott Novik, who
identified himself as a Parkland Isles resident, asked explanation of quasi judicial, which was
provided by City Attorney Andrew Maurodis. Mr. Novik indicated his waiver of the process.
Mr. Novik acknowledged the City's concern regarding his venture. He assured the Commission
that it would not become a local bar or tavern with extended store hours. His intent was that the
store would offer something different and unique to customers.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz offered that the apprehension was that the City had recently passed an
ordinance regarding bars, but he did not believe this wine and cheese store was what had been
envisioned. He indicated that he would be in favor of this request. He suggested that the owner
should be careful of liability.

<<PG>>

Commissioner Weissman commented that there were six issues raised with the special exception
-- four of which he had no problem with. However, he did have a problem with customers
purchasing a bottle of wine and then consuming the entire bottle on the premises, and a problem
with sale of wine by the glass. The Commissioner asked if the applicant would be willing to
exclude those two from the request.
Mr. Novik responded that he would not advertise wine-by-the-glass or being able to drink a
bottle on the premises; it would not be that kind of place. He explained that he wanted to be able
to offer cheese or an appetizer while customers were awaiting their reservations at Cafe Bella
Sera or at Japango. He assured that the on-premise consumption would not be their main focus;
it would be an option, but an important part of his goal to offer a different experience.
Commissioner Weissman noted that, as a retail wine establishment, someone would substantially
save by having a bottle of wine at their store versus purchasing that bottle of wine at the nearby
restaurants. Mr. Novik acknowledged that could be true, but he noted that his store would not be
open past 9:00 p.m.
Ms. Moore confirmed that the approval included the store's closing at 9:00 p.m., and Mayor
Udine noted it would be 8:00 p.m. during the week.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz disagreed with Commissioner Weissman. He believed that. the
elimination of those two abilities would significantly limit the owner's chance to succeed.
Commissioner Weissman noted that the owner had stated that the consumption was just a small
part of his business. He suggested that, if the success of the business were based on the
consumption, it could not be a minor part of the business.
Commissioner Smith commented that Parlclanders and their businesses should be supported. He
wished Mr. Novik success.
Mayor Udine commented that a similar shop had opened at Heron Bay. He believed there should
be wine-tastings so that people might buy the wines. He admitted that he had a larger problem
with the wine sold by the glass and consumed on the premises but, with only eight chairs, he did
not see it as a big draw. He indicated that he would vote in favor.
Mayor Udine opened to the public. There were no comments or questions, and the Mayor closed
to the public.
MOTION made by Vice Mayor Moskowitz to approve Resolution No. 2007-79, Wine & Cheese
Store - Special Exception, as amended subject to all staff conditions. Seconded by
Commissioner Smith.

APPROVAL AT OLD CITY HALL
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, TO AUTHORIZE THE
APPROPRIATE CITY OFFICIALS TO EXECUTE THE
ATTACHED LEASE AGREEMENT FOR INSTALLATION
OF A STEALTH CELLULAR TOWER AND
ASSOCIATION EQUIPMENT CABINET AT OLD CITY
HALL BY FLORIDA TOWERS LLC; PROVIDING FOR
CONFLICTS; PROVIDING FOR SEVERABILITY; AND
PROVIDING FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2007-88. She offered that
this matter had been discussed in the past and some terms had been reached. She recalled that
the Commission had first considered the entry and testing arrangement. This action was the next
step, but not the final step, as the applicant must still apply for the development petition
approvals of site plan, CAB, and perhaps a variance following .the signing of the lease agreement.
Ms. Gardner-Young indicated that she had met with the petitioner, who was present, and she had
examined agreements of other municipalities. The 103-foot tower would be able to handle five
carriers, and she had placed on the dais some photographs. The tower would be "stealth" in that
it would resemble the nearby large trees, and staff felt the tower would better blend with the
redesign of Old City Hall.
Ms. Gardner-Young indicated there were three proposals. She recommended that the City enter
into the option that would entitle the City to a rental fee each year and revenue-sharing based on
the carriers and their number. The petitioner was comfortable with this arrangement. The
agreement was typical in its terms; however, the requirement for a generator had been included,
one which would be capable of servicing the cell tower as well as the Public Services building
and fire station. Ownership of the generator would be turned over to the City, but they would
share in maintenance costs. In addition, the petitioner had also agreed to work with the City in
landscaping beyond the base of the tower itself. Ms. Gardner-Young recommended Option 3.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz made the general statement that cell phone service in the City was poor.
While it may not be on the "budget radar," he suggested that other sites in the City be examined
at the same time, such as Pine Trails Parka He asked that the Commission be advised. of the
interested companies.
John Ruth indicated that T-Mobile was definitely interested, having walked the site just days
before. He offered that the site selected would be at the middle of the north property line.
Verizon and Metro PCS were interested, but he believed that Sprint would be the second tenant.
He felt confident that the tower would fill.
Commissioner Weissman understood that this contract was similar to that in other municipalities.
He asked whether a clause might be included that would allow the City's lease to be updated
