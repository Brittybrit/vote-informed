will require the YMCA, as the tenant, to be responsible for all costs associated with any repair.
Mayor Udine said that he would vote to support this request and then spoke about the City,
possibly on a temporary basis during these economic times, re-looking at some of the City sign
codes as they relate to some of the commercial buildings to make it easier for businesses in
Parkland. He stated he will request in the future that this be discussed in a workshop or
Commission meeting as this is an important issue to future tenants understanding what their
rights and responsibilities are going to be. Mayor Udine added that he knows there are new
tenants wanting to come into the Waterway Shopping Center who do not understand the City’s
strict sign codes.
Commissioner Moskowitz commented that signage is not the only reason businesses are having
problems, but is perhaps part of the reason.
Commissioner Weissman spoke about the Bella Sera sign and said the problem with businesses
is well beyond the signage issue; the problem is the economy and the amount of rent that is being
charged.

ADDED AGENDA ITEMS:
1. DISCUSSION: WASTE MANAGEMENT FEES

Commissioner Weissman spoke about Waste Management’s proposal that will be coming to the
Commission in the next few weeks and stated that there is an approximate $20,000 difference in
the numbers at this time; one suggestion Waste Management is offering is to pass the cost on to
commercial properties. He added that he is opposed to this suggestion because of the current
economy and the fact that Waste Management will be saving so much with the new tax roll
billing. Commissioner Weissman said that he would like to know the consensus of the
Commission regarding how they feel about sending a message to Waste Management that Waste
Management should absorb the $20,000 and should not penalize the commercial properties that
are struggling to survive.

Commissioner Moskowitz commented that he agrees with Commissioner Weissman in that the
Commission does not want to pass the cost to the commercial properties, but he does not feel that
the Commission is going to reverse the decision to put garbage fees on the tax rolls if there is not
an agreement reached regarding the $20,000.

Mayor Udine questioned the original proposal and City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young gave a
brief overview.

Discussion was held regarding the provisions in Waste Management’s proposal, in particular, the
revenue stream to the City and the significance of the $20,000 to the overall deal.
Commissioner Moskowitz said the Commission was successful in accomplishing a cost savings
for the residents with this deal and the City needs to do its best effort to get Waste Management
to absorb the $20,000, but he is not willing to walk away from the deal if they do not absorb it

Mayor Udine commented that the Commission does not want to see these costs passed on to
anyone else.
Commissioner Moskowitz added that, at a minimum, Waste Management has to come back with
a better number.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young clarified that the Commission does not want any costs
passed on to anyone and Waste Management should absorb the costs or come back with a better
number.
2. DISCUSSION: PUBLIC SAFETY IMPACT FEES
Commissioner Weissman said the Commission has previously discussed public safety impact
fees and the hiring of a professional to determine the appropriate amount for such fees. He stated
that City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young has received a cost figure of about $25,000 for a study
and suggested that the Commission give her the authority to hire the professional to begin the
process. Commissioner Weissman noted that this impact fee would be charged when building
permits are pulled and the City is issuing those building permits now; a delay in starting this
means lost revenue to put aside for public safety facilities that need to be built.
A brief discussion was held regarding funding and cost/benefit of the study, and how impact fees
work.
The consensus of the Commission was to support undertaking the study.
