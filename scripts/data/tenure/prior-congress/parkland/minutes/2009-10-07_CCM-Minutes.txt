Vice Mayor Weissman said that all of the setbacks are within code, but he is concerned about the
flag pole.
Commissioner Moskowitz commented that he has no problems with the car wash at this location
or with flag poles, but the Commission has held to a stringent standard regarding signage and he
feels it is important to be consistent. Commissioner Moskowitz noted that he can not vote for the
flag pole at 35 feet high, but may be agreeable with a shorter pole, and added that he would not

environmental impact and stated that this particular car wash is state of the art and 100%
environmentally green.

pole.

the flag is advertising but would accept a shorter flag pole if the Commission deems it
appropriate.
Commissioner Smith asked what height would be acceptable.
Vice Mayor Weissman asked about the height of the car wash building, to which Mr. Vogel
responded 21 feet.
Commissioner Rosenof stated that he does not object to the flag or flag pole, but to the lighted
pole at night.
There was discussion regarding the height of the flag pole, possible approval of the car wash with
or without a flag, the possibility of the applicant coming back to the Commission at a later date
to address the flag pole request, and possible approval of a 21 foot flag pole that is not lit and
with the flag removed daily at sundown.
Vice Mayor Weissman said specific provisions can be put into the approval, in particular a
provision regarding replacement of the flag if, at the city's sole discretion, it is determined that
the flag is faded, torn or tattered.
Commissioner Rosenof stated that if the flag is lit, it has to be photometric lights and Vice Mayor
Weissman added solar lighting should perhaps be required.
There was additional discussion regarding lighting for the flag pole and for the entire facility.
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis suggested that the Commission assign a flag pole height of 21
feet without lighting.

4. DISCUSSION: FIRE CONTRACT
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young said that the fire consultant is ill and she will have him
come to the next meeting. She noted that she met at the beginning of the week with her
counterparts in Coconut Creek, Coral Springs, and Margate.
Mayor Udine commented that he would like to wait until the consultant is available and Vice
Mayor Weissman is available after the October 21 meeting.
Vice Mayor Weissman stated that the city manager of Coral Springs made it clear he has no
interest in a fire district and he does not intend to give up control of his fire department. Vice
Mayor Weissman added that with respect to the lack of requested information and lack of
negotiations, he does not think there will be a change in Coral Springs' philosophy.
There was discussion regarding going out for bid and preparation of a bid package, as well as the
interlocal agreement.
Commissioner Moskowitz spoke about the matrix used for garbage and suggested doing
something similar for an RFP for fire services. He discussed the options available and his
concerns regarding going out for an RFP.
There was discussion regarding the existing fire contract and going out for an RFP.
Vice Mayor Weissman commented that a contract that gives no guarantees or does not state what
the costs for future years will be does not make business sense.

VIII COMMENTS BY THE CITY ATTORNEY
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis spoke about issues related to annexation and said there is an
October 22 deadline for any local bills. He stated that discussions have been held with time
property owners with regard to possible annexation and filing a local bill to annex these
properties is being contemplated. City Attorney Maurodis noted that these owners agree to being
annexed, but there are no annexation agreements or cost recovery agreements in place
Vice Mayor Weissman said that absent a pre - annexation agreement with the property owners,
there is no reason for discussion at this time.
Mayor Udine commented that there are two landowners with whom the City has pre - annexation
agreements and they made a lot of concessions to get this accomplished. He added that he feels
these new landowners should come forward with similar concessions.
Vice Mayor Weissman stated that the new landowners should come up with land for a school and
ensure that if the school board decides they are not going to use the land, then the land would
revert back to the City.
Further discussion was held and Commissioner Moskowitz said he agrees with Mayor Udine
regarding the need to get cost recovery from the big parcels.
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young spoke about the 10 -acre parcel and explained that the owner
indicated there is no reason to change the use of the property; he wants to be in the City because
he feels his property would be worth more, in addition to having a governing body closer in

City Attorney Maurodis explained the two -step process that is going on simultaneously with the
parcels; they are seeking annexation at the same time they are moving ahead with the land use
plan amendment process. Regarding the land use, he stated they are going to apply through the
county and they will also apply through Parkland. City Attorney Maurodis then spoke about the
parcel owners trying to get their land entitled and confirmed that the Commission's greatest
amount of discretion is at the pre - annexation stage; the second most is the land use plan stage.
Mayor Udine commented that he is agreeable to a local bill to get the Palm Beach Farms piece
into the City, however, he is uncomfortable with the two large parcels absent a pre - annexation
agreement. Mayor Udine then spoke about the time crunch involved in this matter.
City Attorney Maurodis suggested that the City could file a bill and if it is felt the City has
enough control over that bill, the City can arguably buy another month because it is the City's
bill. He said he would check with the legislative delegation and if the bill is filed and it can be
withdrawn at any time, then the City could negotiate into November.
Commissioner Moskowitz reiterated that he does not have a problem with cost recovery or
getting dedication of land, but is more concerned about City dollars being spent on the process.
