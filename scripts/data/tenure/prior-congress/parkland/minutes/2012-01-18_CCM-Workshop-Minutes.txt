1. PRESENTATION: PINE TRAILS PARK MASTER PLAN
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young introduced Mr. Scott Davidoff, Parks and Recreation
Director. Mr. Davidoff gave a brief presentation regarding the Pine Trails Park Master Plan.
He explained that since the creation of the original Master Plan, there had been multiple
changes. He explained that based on population, new needs and trends have been identified by
staff, thc Parks Board and residents. He also stated that many of the originally claimed

alternate elements to be considered. He presented three options for the City Commission to

Mayor Udine had questions about the wetlands mitigation. He feels that the amenities such as

Organization leaders to meet as many of the requirements as the City possibly can. He also had
concerns about parking for occasional use at Pine Trails Park and asked if pervious parking can
be put in instead of asphalt.
Commissioner Moskowitz had comments about the significant cost involved with the wetlands
mitigation. He asked what the timeline is. Mr. Davidoff replied that there is not a sct timeline.
Commissioner Moskowitz also asked how much money is there for the next phase. City
Manager Gardner-Young explained that currently there isn’t any money although there is

It was determined that as the budget process gets underway, there will be monies that will
become available in addition to park impact fees and other development project initiatives.

2. PRESENTATION: RED LIGHT CAMERA IMPLEMENTATION
Mayor Udine explained that currently the City of Parkland does not have red light cameras.
The cameras that are at the intersections are Broward County traffic cameras.
Chief Marvin Stoner gave a brief presentation on red light cameras and gave some statistical
data on how the system works and its usefulness. (See attached presentation for additional
information on the system).

Mayor Udine asked about the cost to the City for installation of the red light cameras. Chief
Stoner replied that there is no cost for installation or maintenance, however, there is a fee per
camera which will come out of the generated funds from violators of the red light cameras.
Commissioner Rosenof reiterated that the company is speculating that there will be two
violations per day in each town. Commander Cowart explained that the 2.1 violations per day
is required in order for the company to break even, however, before installation of a camera,
there will be a video verification study done to determine if a traffic camera needs to be
installed at a particular intersection.

Vice Mayor Weissman commented that the way the company is being paid is in odds with what
the legislative intent was which says that they cannot be paid based on the number of
infractions that occur. He also asked if the City does not reach the cost of $4,750 per month, is
the amount carried over to the next month. Chief Stoner clarified that if at the end of the year
there is a deficit, then it will be wiped away.

Commissioner Rosenof had questions about the termination of contract.

Commissioner Moskowitz referenced a page from the presentation and expressed his support
of the red light cameras which could have proven useful during his personal experience in a
recent hit and run car accident. He stated that he wants staff to look at the two companies and
bring it back to the City Commission.

Further discussion ensued.

Mayor Udine stated he would be inclined to look into the red light cameras in greater detail
provided there is no cost to the City.

Commissioner Rosenof expressed concerns with people being issued citations in instances of
following funeral processions. He feels that if red light cameras make people safe in general
then he is in favor of having them, however the details of the agreement must be reviewed
closely.

Commissioner Moskowitz clarified that staff direction will be to put out an RFP to bring back
before the City Commission.
