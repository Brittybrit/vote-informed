1. DISCUSSION: ENERGY INCENTIVES
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young recalled discussion regarding the City of Parkland's Energy
Incentive Program during a previous City Commission meeting. At that time it was indicated that
some changes were required and staff was asked to review the program and to advise

recommendations. Ms. Gardner -Young introduced Environmental Resources Director, Brian
Archer to give a brief power point presentation.
Mr. Archer reviewed the original incentive program and new proposals for 2009 -2010. He
suggested that 9 of the original 12 programs remain unchanged, including: hybrid vehicles,
incandescent light bulbs, solar powered landscape lighting, ultra low flow toilets and shower
heads, energy star program, exotic invasive tree replacement, high efficiency A/C replacement,
A/C duct system test & repair and solar panels for the home. He noted the solar water heater
program had been modified, the Florida Power & Light (FPL) Sunshine Energy program and the
plug load control device were eliminated. The FPL program was eliminated due to problems
between FP &L and the State of Florida and the plug load control was eliminated due to lack of
interest from the public.
Mr. Archer reviewed new programs he proposed for 2009 -2010 which included: carbon foot
print credits, automatic sprinkler rain sensor, sustainable landscape design, photovoltaic solar
pool pump heater and programmable thermostat retrofitting. He noted the costs and reward
amounts to the City's residents in the power point presentation.
Mr. Archer advised the expenditures for this program for the 2008 -2009 fiscal year cost the City
of Parkland $5,647.98. He estimated expenditures for the 2009 -2010 fiscal year would cost the
City $13,400.00 based on the proposed additions and modifications. He advised an amendment
to the original Ordinance No. 2007 -29 would be presented at the February 4 2009 City
} Commission Regular meeting reflecting the program criteria.
Commissioner Moskowitz stated he felt the program was worthwhile for the City.
Commissioner Smith stated he liked the updates to the program.
Commissioner Rosenof noted his concerns with the program and advised he preferred rewards
such as permit fee refunds for sustainable landscaping over incentives that dictate what type of
energy method a resident should use. Overall, he stated he was behind the intention of the
program but encouraged staff to research other methods and to incorporate more public
education.
Mayor Udine stated some of Commissioner Rosenof's points are well taken and the issue was
how to put the City's funds to their best use. He advised the City had budgeted $50,000.00 for
the incentive program for 2008 -2009 fiscal year and $5,000.00 was actually utilized. The
remainder was moved back into the City's general operating fund. He suggested the budget
shouldbe reduced to $25,000.00 for this fiscal year based on this presentation.
Mayor Udine also stated he agreed with the additions to the program and advised he would like
to see the energy plug load control device left in the program just in case a resident became
interested. He questioned if rewards could be given to residents for other energy saving methods
not listed in the Ordinance.

The City Commission discussed avenues that would allow residents to come up with other
innovative ways to conserve energy and resources as new or better technologies evolve. Also
discussed was the need for a monthly report from staff updating the Commission on the program,
using the city's website as an educational resource for residents and clarifying carbon footprint
credits and how if it would benefit the City of Parkland's residents.
Mr. Archer informed the Commission the money source for this program comes from landscape
permit fees and cell tower revenues, not the City's tax payers. He then concluded his
presentation.
2. DISCUSSION & DIRECTION: PARKS & RECREATION
INFRASTRUCTURE
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young recalled discussion on, this subject during the strategic
planning session, and she had requested the Parks & Recreation Director, Scott Davidoff, to give
a presentation regarding infrastructure, programming and staffing needs.
Mr. Davidoff prepared a power point presentation which he reviewed with the City Commission.
Mr. Davidoff advised the primary point would be to review what functions the Parks &
Recreation department provides, including athletic fields, playgrounds, picnic areas, special
events, trail systems and open space. Then to review who the stakeholders are: City of Parkland
residents, City recognized sports leagues, area schools, YMCA and `pick -up' sports leagues. He
noted the largest user groups are the City recognized sports leagues.
Mr. Davidoff noted the following items: 241 non - resident sports cards had been sold to date,
fields are used daily by the residents and by area schools from the beginning of the school year
through June. He advised the City's Public Works department performs field maintenance during
winter break and during the summer months when the sports leagues are not m season.
However, this maintenance is not sufficient based on the amount of usage that occurs. Due to the
new `Rec -Trac' software, field use can be tracked by hours. From August 1, 2008 to January 14,
2009 the following usage had occurred: multipurpose fields 7,881.5 hours, baseball fields
1,569.5 hours, basketball courts 710.5 hours and softball fields 122 hours.
From the presentation, Mr. Davidoff reviewed the City's current facilities, addressed current and
future needs at the City's Parks: Quigley Park, Terramar Park, Pine Trails Park, Liberty Park,
Equestrian Center, Covered Bridge Park, Doris Davis Foreman Park and Six Acre Park.
He presented options to accommodate the City's future needs.
• Option #1 listed the City to take over the preserve adjacent to Terramar Park, construct 2
baseball fields, move softball field #5 to the preserve area, construct a restroom building,
move basketball courts #4 & #5 to Pine Trails Park, construct a new parking lot for a cost
of 2.7 million dollars.
