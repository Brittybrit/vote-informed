II PURPOSE:

1. FILLING THE VACANCY FOR DISTRICT 4 COMMISSION SEAT

After summarizing the purpose of the meeting, Mayor Udine suggested following the usual
meeting process, with comments from each Commissioner followed by their motion. Per his
discussion with the City Attorney, he noted that an appointee would need their name placed in
the Resolution and require three affirmative votes.

Commissioner Moskowitz indicated that he had come to the conclusion that the Commission and
the City as a whole would be better off leaving the seat vacant for the next three months and
going straight to a special election. As a student of the political process and democracy, he
believed the power of election has been conferred on residents. While the Charter may grant the
Commission the power to appoint, he believed it was not in the best interests of the residents.
Despite the argument that it is budget time and District 4 needs representation, Commissioner

Moskowitz noted that Commissioners were elected City-wide, with responsibility for the entire
City, and, in theory, the Commission had input into the budget in the Strategic Planning process,
during which the Vice Mayor had been present and District 4 represented. He believed the
Commission should resolve to work harder in the next three months, pick up the slack - if there
was any, and press forward with four members.

Commissioner Moskowitz added that he felt that the departure of the Vice Mayor had been a real
loss. He commented that the Commission had received a letter from the Vice Mayor following
her resignation, which indicated her willingness to be appointed until November so as to
maintain representation for her District. He felt that the Vice Mayor was responsible for the
position the City and her District had been placed in. He apologized to the residents of District 4
if they felt "orphaned," which was not the case, and he urged District 4 residents to contact him
or any member of the Commission about any problems until the special election was held.

Commissioner Moskowitz felt that the four candidates were well qualified, and he wished them
well in an election. He believed a direct appointment would question the integrity of the
government; the voters of Parkland should fill the seat.

Commissioner McGilvray complimented Commissioner Moskowitz on his speech, but admitted
a difference of opinion on the integrity of the process. She believed the Charter gave the
Commission the power to appoint for the very reason that it left the option to the Commission,
dependent upon the matters before the Commission and how long the process might take. Most
of the upcoming meetings would be about the budget, and she believed that someone with budget
experience would be the most helpful. She noted that one of the candidates had more experience
than the present Commission combined.

Concerning the power to appoint, Commissioner McGilvray stated she did not feel that way, but
suggested that the Vice Mayor's offer to be reappointed for the three months should be
considered since she had been elected by the people. She offered that the presence of a fifth
person on the dais made votes' more equitable and would make a motion at the appropriate time.

Commissioner Smith commented that he had resided in the City for 23 months and, within his
first month, had made contact with then-Commissioner Udine and had asked to become
involved. There were a number of applicants, and he had telephone conversations with several.
He found it difficult to know any of them well enough to consider an appointment to a position to
which they would normally be elected by voters.

Mayor Udine stated that he felt this issue the most difficult decision he has had to make as an
elected official. He noted that residents had expressed to him their confidence in this
Commission and this government to move the City forward. He personally thanked, and
expressed his support of, City Manager Mark Lauzier, believing that he has helped to bring the
City to the next level and in a positive direction. He was equally proud of the Commission as he
believed its members were truly delivering to constituents - more efficiently and more cost
effectively, and for that he thanked each member of City staff. He admitted that Commissioners

experienced frustrations -- with each other, with staff, or with a member of a different outside
board - yet they had persevered, and he believed the City was a better place for their decisions.
The Mayor stated he had spent considerable time rationalizing the Vice Mayor's actions and had
reached an epiphany that he was angry that this situation had occurred.

Mayor Udine believed there were public misconceptions that must be cleared up. He noted that
the proposed budget was completed July 5t\ prior to the resignation; the appointment was not a
"requirement" of the budget process. He noted that each and every member of the Commission
was elected City-wide and was responsible to each District of the City. Additionally, the budget
is a document that flows from the Strategic Planning process; the meetings took place throughout
the year, were attended by all elected representatives, and all of them contributed to the process.
No one district of the City benefited more than another based on advocacy. The final budget
hearings would be held in mid-September, after the candidacy qualifying period began.

Mayor Udine indicated that, at the time of the Vice Mayor's resignation, he had been advised of
the process to fill the vacancy by the City Attorney, who explained that the dates coincidentally
coincided with the November election. With Charter questions already on the ballot, there would
be no cost to the taxpayers. The Mayor indicated that the City Attorney had further explained
that the Charter allowed, but did not require, that the seat be filled within 30 days of the
resignation. Other than residency, there were no qualifications, no public input, or interviews
necessary. The Commission could appoint anyone they wished to serve. The Mayor believed
that the process should be opened to applicants and that the Commission should select the most
qualified applicant to fill the position until the special election. He noted that the Commission
had gone above and beyond Charter requirements.

Mayor Udine offered that his personal procedure had been to interview each applicant with the
same questions. He thanked the applicants, and stated that he sincerely appreciated their
willingness to help their City. He believed that all applicants had the potential to become an
outstanding public servant, but he admitted that it had been difficult to reconcile their willingness
to now serve the City when they had been unseen during his four years of service at City and
other civic functions. With the exception of one applicant, the Mayor felt that the applicants'
learning curve would be well past the time of the special election. In his mind, the only person
able to step right in and do the job would be Mark Weissman. No matter who was chosen,
Mayor Udine believed that he and the Commission would be open to major criticism.

Mayor Udine spoke of the partisanship in the election four years before, when e-mails and
anonymous political website attacks became the norm, and he believed that voters had expressed
their distaste of the fighting and political tricks in the results of the most recent election. He felt
that voters had placed an awesome trust in him and the members of this Commission, and he
cannot and will not allow the infighting to continue or be tainted by partisan politics. He
indicated that his vote would be to leave the job of selecting the representative for District 4
where it belongs - with the voters.

Commissioner Moskowitz summarized that, with two Commissioners now wishing not to
appoint, there would be no point in voting "no" for each individual; he would rather make a
motion not to appoint. City Attorney Andrew Maurodis stated this was within their authority.

Commissioner McGilvray questioned, with the Charter provision, when the Commission might
feel it appropriate and agree to appoint.

Mayor Udine responded that his feeling was that he did not want to "cloak" someone with
incumbency. The qualifying for the special election will be the first week in September; the
qualifying will be before the September budget meetings. He agreed that the people who had
applied for the seat would make excellent City Commissioners, but he felt uncomfortable making
the appointment. He did not feel the need to take the right away from voters, and he would
prefer to let them decide.

Commissioner Moskowitz commented that, to him and in a situation with one resignation, the
opportunity to appoint would be never. He noted the difference between the words "may" and
"shall," and he believed the provision was included to face calamity.

Commissioner Smith, responding to Commissioner McGilvray's question, noted that one
nominee had the qualifications for appointment. However, since two officials have already
stated that they would not appoint anyone at this point in time, he felt the question was moot.

Commissioner McGilvray admitted her difference of opinion; she felt the District should be
represented and she would like to take the option afforded the Commission in the Charter.

MOTION made by Commissioner McGilvray to appoint Melissa Heller to the District 4
vacancy. Seconded by Commissioner Smith.

Mayor Udine acknowledged the qualifications of Ms. Heller, but he noted that she had not been
involved in the issues within the City. He did not believe her appointment would add to the
process, and he would vote "no."

Mr. Maurodis clarified that the motion would be to adopt Resolution No. 2006-72 with Ms.
Heller's name inserted.

ROLL CALL VOTE:

Commissioner Moskowitz No
Commissioner McGilvray Yes
Commissioner Smith Yes
Mayor Udine No

MOTION failed on a 2-2 vote.

Mayor Udine asked for other motions to appoint. There were none.

Mayor Udine opened to the public.

Christine Hunschofsky, 9976 N.W. 64th Court, agreed that it was important for the City to move
forward and everyone to work as a team. She acknowledged that the Commission worked hard,
whether residents agreed with their decisions or not, but she did not enjoy the comments made
about former Vice Mayor Ribotsky. While the personal reasons for her resignation were not
known, and until they were, she felt it better to respect the job she did and thank her for it.

Mayor Udine agreed, and he apologized for the comment he had made. His comment had been
based on the reason that he had thought written in the resignation letter, and he had not meant it
to sound like that.

Marilyn Krantz, of Pine tree Estates, recalled that, in Charter Review, one of the issues that came
up regarding this circumstance was how deficit the current Charter was. She believed their vote
of 2-2 demonstrated the need for another vote. Additionally, she commented that applications
had been requested and she did not understand why their minds had now been changed. She also
found it interesting that three Commissioners had come to the meeting with the same intention of
delaying the vote, reminding them to be careful of their outside conversations. She commended
the Commission for taking applications, but she believed they had come to do a job and she
encouraged them to do that.

Debra Rowars, of Ternbridge, offered that she lived in the District now missing representation,
and she encouraged the Commission to appoint someone. She noted that two of the present
Commissioners had not been previously involved in City matters prior to their election, and she
did not believe that this should be held against the applicants. Noting that former Vice Mayor
Ribotsky would return on the following day, Ms. Rowars believed that the Vice Mayor and other
former commissioners should have been consulted for their opinions, and she came to the
defense of the former Vice Mayor in her decision to resign.

Mayor Udine stated, so that the record was clear, he spoke to former Mayor Marks twice about
this appointment, and he had also solicited the advice of people in the District - David Ofstein
and Howard Talenfeld. As to why this meeting had been set for this date, the Charter dictated
that this process must be done within 30 days and gave explanation of Commission vacation
plans; this was the only time that the Commission could meet. In response to Ms. Krantz'
comments, he noted that it had been a 2-2 vote, and he again apologized for any
misunderstanding about his remarks as he was referring only to the City Manager issue which
had been spelled out in the Vice Mayor's letter.

Commissioner McGilvray encouraged the applicants to consider running In the November
election, noting that the Commission would be honored to have any of them.

Former Vice Mayor Debbie Beck, a resident of CypressHead, believed it was the Commission's
obligation to appoint someone to fill the seat. It was a Charter provision; she believed the
Commission should do their job, even though it was a difficult choice and even if there was
political payback or other implications in the air. She took issue with the Mayor's comments
regarding the election four years before, and she reminded him that the true dividing line within
the City came with the BSO vote. She wished him the best in unifying the City. Ms. Beck hoped
that, without a decision at this meeting, it would not be an added item at a later Commission
meeting.

Ms. Beck also spoke regarding the public trust, and what she perceived as a correlation between
the change in millage rate and Vice Mayor Ribotsky's resignation and her concerns about
effectiveness as a member of the Commission. Ms. Beck commented regarding the most recent
election, and a palm card campaign done by Mark Weissman for Commissioner Moskowitz
falsely indicating support by the police and firefighters.

Commissioner Moskowitz responded that it was not a hard decision politically, but rather would
jeopardize the integrity of the government in that one nominee had worked on his campaign and
another nominee had contributed to the Mayor's campaign. He believed, that by not appointing,
it was the best way to keep the public trust. He suggested great disrespect had been shown in
some of the comments made. Referring to Ms. Rowar's earlier remarks, he apologized that his
comments had been perceived as an attack and admitted his frustration.

Mayor Udine agreed that they were frustrated. He spoke regarding the four week break that
should have occurred and the necessity to meet on this particular day for the 30-day window.
Qualifying would take place the first week of September; speaking of several nominees, he
believed that the voters should decide.

At the Mayor's request for further nominations, there were none.

III ADJOURNMENT

MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to adjourn the meeting at 4:00 p.m. Seconded by
Commissioner McGilvray.

MOTION passed unanimously.
