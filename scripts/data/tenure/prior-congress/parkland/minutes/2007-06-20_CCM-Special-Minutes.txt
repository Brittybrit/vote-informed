CALVIN, GIORDANO & ASSOCIATES
Vice President Shelly Eichner introduced John Weber, Director of their West Palm Beach Office,
and Lisa Dykstra, Director of Transportation Planning Services. She noted that their firm
currently provided engineering services to the City and that they were also working diligently to

bring forward the City's EAR-based Comprehensive Plan amendments. She believed the
Commission knew their background, skills, and personnel.
Ms. Eichner reviewed their work with other. municipalities and the Florida Department of
Transportation and had received letters of recommendation in testament to their past performance
and professional competence. This project would be based out of their West Palm Beach office
with staff available to the City at all times. The workload would be balanced among the traffic

through proven their proven municipal experience..
Commissioner Weissman asked if the firm now had a relationship to ,any city or unincorporated
area contiguous to the boundaries of the City. Ms. Eichner responded in the negative.
CPH ENGINEERS
Project Manager John Melendez indicated their primary office was located in Miami, and he
viewed this as a "fresh" contact into the area. Their firm, began in Sanford, Florida, had a strong
and extensive history there. Todd Hendrix would be the primary contact in the Miami office.
They would utilize a team approach in the development .review area, and he believed their
transportation expertise would provide both basic and detailed attention. The firm had extensive
experience with the Florida Department of Transportation, and Mr. Melendez indicated that he
was very familiar with the issues that could arise between local municipalities and the FDOT.
He looked forward to helping the City meet its objectives while balancing the safety and welfare
of its residents.
Commissioner Weissman asked confirmation that CPH did not have any connection to any city
or county directly surrounding Parkland. Mr. Melendez stated that most of their .clients were
located in central and northern Florida, and they served both the public and private sector. The
closest city in South Florida would be Key Largo.
DAVID PLUMMER & ASSOCIATES, INC.
Juan Espinosa indicated that their engineering and transportation planning firm was located in
Coral Gables. In business since 1978, they had offices in Fort Lauderdale and Fort Myers as
well. He indicated their firm had a large number ofhighly-skilled and experienced transportation
engineers and planning professionals, and they had assembled a very qualified team. The firm
had served as traffic consultant for different cities in South Florida, including Boca Raton,
Miami, Coral Gables, and Miami Springs. They were proponents of transit and had extensive
experience in traffic calming and pedestrian solutions. He spoke of their computerized resources.
Mr. Espinosa stated that they were firmly committed to quality control and had strong corporate
support, with President Tim Plummer in charge of the day-to-day operation of the contract. A
West Broward resident, Mr. Espinosa stated that he would be the project manager, and he

FLORIDA TRANSPORTATION ENGINEERING, INC.
'The representative stated that his firm welcomed this opportunity and appreciated the chance to
work with the City. He offered to answer any questions.
Mayor Udine asked if his firm worked with any other Broward municipalities. The
representative indicated that they worked only with the Florida Department of Transportation at
the present time. They were very familiar with District 4 and their work with municipalities had
been indirect and in conjunction with FDOT projects. He understood that the City had a sizeable
annexation underway and that political and traffic situations must be addressed, particularly with
the long-range transportation plan.
With regards to FDOT, Commissioner Weissman asked whether the firm had done work on any
portion of the State Road 7 corridor. The representative indicated that their involvement had
been in terms of design, minor improvements, and some work regarding traffic operations. He
indicated that, while the firm specialized in data collection, they were also in the process of
designing minor traffic calming devices, roundabouts, and medians for some small
municipalities.
KEITH & SCHNARS
Eric Penfield, designated consultant and project manager for this contract, introduced the
executive management team: Vice President Andre Groenhoff, Director of Transportation
Planning John Krane, and Executive Vice President Michael Davis. He spoke of their strengths,
including: extensive municipal traffic engineering experience; the corporate office located in
Fort Lauderdale and additional professionals located in West Palm Beach; and an unrivaled
continuity of professional staff. Mr. Penfield indicated they were ready, willing, and able to
begin work on this contract, and he offered to answer any questions.
Commissioner Weissman noted that the firm had refused to provide any information concerning
litigation. Mr. Davis responded that it had been their corporate policy not to provide that
information. He stated there was no litigation pending against Keith & Schnars that would have
any affect on this contract. Cases against the firm at the present time were in the context of
construction inspection work for the Florida Department of Transportation.
Commissioner Weissman commented that, since cases filed became public information, he did
not understand the firm's refusal to provide that information. Mr. Davis stated that he could
revisit that policy with the firm's general counsel and his colleagues. The Commissioner recalled

RS&H (REYNOLDS, SMITH AND HILLS, INC.)
Jeff Easley, Vice President and South Florida Region Director, introduced Project Manager
Winston Harris and Lead Design Engineer Sam Gonzalez, and he thanked the Commission for
the opportunity to make. this presentation. He recounted their experience around the State, and
especially in South .Florida, for municipalities, counties, and the Florida Department of
Transportation. They offer a wide variety of services, including data collection, traffic
engineering, GIS,: and design and environmental services and reviews. Mr. Easley stated there
would not be any conflicts of interest with bordering municipalities, FDOT, or the county. Their
offices are approximately 12 miles away, and he assured that any assignment received from the
City would be given attention by their professionals and turned around in a timely manner. I-Ie
briefly reviewed the team's combined experience.
Commissioner Weissman asked regarding the location of FDOT District 6, and Mr. Easley
responded that District 6 was Miami/Dade and Monroe counties.
Mayor Udine questioned if the firm represented any Broward cities. Mr. Easley indicated there
were-none at the present time.
STANLEY CONSULTANTS, INC.
Freddie Vargas stated that he was the designated Project Manager for this contract. He gave a
brief history of the firm, established in 1913, and its projects. Mr. Vargas indicated he was a
Tamarac resident and frequented this area. The main office is located in West Palm Beach. The
firm would be able to provide full service in traffic engineering, transportation planning,
intersection geometric element, roadway and bridge design, water resources and environmental
services. Mr. Vargas introduced engineer Mark Weber, who would administer the contract, and.
Deo Chimba, a specialist in transportation engineering, and he spoke of their education and
experience. They currently have a contract with the City of Boca Raton, and they were recently
awarded a contract with the City of Margate. They do not represent private industry in Florida.
Mr. Vargas believed they have a proven track record with local and state government and other
agencies.
