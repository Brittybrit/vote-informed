8. ORDINANCE NO. 2006-45: AMENDING PENSION PLAN TO
PROVIDE BENEFIT
ENHANCEMENTS
COMMENTS: FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND,
FLORIDA, AMENDING THE CITY CODE BY
AMENDING CHAPTER 2 "ADMINISTRATION,"
ARTICLE X "PENSIONS," DIVISION 2 "THE POLICE
PENSION PLAN AND TRUST FUND" BY AMENDING
SECTION 2-226 TO PROVIDE FOR NORMAL
RETIREMENT UPON THE COMPLETION OF TWENTY
(20) YEARS OF CREDITED SERVICE, TO INCREASE
THE BENEFIT FORMULA MULTIPLIER TO 3% AND TO
PROVIDE AN ANNUAL 2% COST-OF-LIVING
ADJUSTMENT FOR RETIREES; PROVIDING FOR
SEVERABILITY; PROVIDING FOR CODIFICATION;
AND PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE. (1St first
reading 12/20/06)
City Manager Mark Lauzier read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2006-45.
Mayor Udine noted that both the City Manager and Finance Department recommended denial of
the Ordinance.
Sergeant Fred Ferrara, Chairman of the Police Pension Board, spoke regarding the experience of
the Board and its administrative staff.
Pension Attorney Steve Cypen explained the program under Chapter 185 whereby premium taxes
were assessed against insurance companies doing business within the City which are then

<<PG>>

PAGE 8
credited against their general state taxes. The City must meet certain minimum criteria to claim
that money, which must be used for improved police benefits, and he spoke of the selected
options for its use. In all probability, the City would not bear the cost of these benefits; the
"bottom must drop out" in revenues for there ever to be a problem. Should a decrease in revenue
occur, the Pension Board would incrementally repeal the cost-of-living.
Actuary Charles Carr presented a simplified chart regarding the use of the "Chapter" money. He
reiterated that, if the "Chapter" money does not fall, there would be no risk to the City; with the
2006 level of $106,000, revenues must decrease to a level of $14,184 before the City would have
even one dollar of added cost. Mr. Carr believed it extremely unlikely this would occur.
Mr. Cypen offered that the State had enacted a special provision. to provide atwo-year guaranteed
protection period for cities.
Responding to the Mayor's question, Mr. Lauzier explained his concerns regarding the funding
of the multiplier increase and the potential problems related to the City's contract with BSO and
pension contributions, with over $300,000 in dispute.
Mayor Udine suggested that any new obligations would clearly be the responsibility of the City.
He asked if there might be a risk to the City should there be another actuarial study.
Mr. Carr responded that the bottom line would be whether the $14,000 level would continue as
the annual cost without escalation.. He noted that, from an actuarial standpoint, there would
always be a risk to the employer in defined benefit plans; anything that would cause a deficiency
in the plan would be a risk. He offered that, while actuaries tend to be conservative when plans
are evaluated, there was always a risk that the benefit improvement had not been priced properly.
Mayor Udine asked whether the risk would be lessened should the City decide to wait one year.
Mr. Carr replied that it would take the risk away for one year but, by waiting a year, the numbers
would have to be recalculated and have a different result. The rules mandate one pricing at the
time of Ordinance adoption; the City might win or lose.
Mr. Cypen noted that over 300 cities participated in this program and, despite many amendments,
none had expected an extra layer of protection. The money must be used for improvements; if
not used, he believed that the State would stop the money.
Mr. Lauzier suggested -and he noted that it had been discussed by the Board -that the City buy
what it could now afford: $26,156- worth of benefits. Mr. Carr agreed, but stated that the risk
was still there. He noted that all of the numbers were the actuary's best estimate of the cost at a
certain point in time. Mr. Lauzier believed there would be less risk spending real existing dollars
versus future dollars that the City did not have. Mr. Carr offered that the only difference in risk,
which he termed "slim" -- would be that the future Chapter money would drop from $106,000 to
$14,000. At Mr. Lauzier's further explanation of his concerns, Mr. Carr indicated that actuarial
studies were based upon assumptions about the future and were priced to minimize the risk of the
pension program or its improvements.
