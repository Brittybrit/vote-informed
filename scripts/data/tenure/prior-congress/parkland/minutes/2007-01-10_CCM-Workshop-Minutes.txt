Coconut Creek City Manager John Kelly gave a brief background, recalling Coconut Creek's
past invitation to the Mayors and City Managers of Parkland, Coral Springs, and Margate for a
presentation about their "Main Street" area.
Mayor Udine asked that the record reflect that Commissioner Smith had entered the meeting.
Mr. Kelly introduced Coconut Creek Deputy City Manager Dave Rivera and Development
Services Director Sheila Rose.
Ms. Rose gave a brief history of the concept for the 400+ acres, bounded by Wiles Road, Lyons
Road, Sample Road, and State Road 7, which started approximately four years ago. The land has
been used for farming; however, the comprehensive plan dated from the 1970's and the land use
for the area was industrial. Ms. Rose stated that, with development interest in the perimeters of
the property and industrial-flex in the area, the city had the opportunity to conceptually create a
unique mixed-use development. A Regional Activity Center had been presented to and approved
by the County Commission and the Department of Community Affairs. Ms. Rose noted that the
design guidelines won an award from the American Planning Association last year because they
had been based entirely on the concept of green and sustainable architecture rather than
duplicating styles of other projects.
Ms. Rose displayed graphics of the site, showing existing buildings and how staff envisioned the
future development of the property. She noted that building permits were in process for 300,000
square feet of lifestyle-type retail with residential and office components, plus three upscale
restaurants. Pedestrian-oriented,. there would be higher-end retail outdoor shopping.
Ms. Rose indicated Coconut Creek had an amendment to the Regional Activity Center in process
to correct some inaccuracies but, in the midst, they had also been working with the Seminole
Tribe on their potential development at the opposite end of the property. The Tribe was currently
in the rezoning process and their Development of Regional Impact provides for the development
program. The Tribe also has a potential application to take the property into trust.
Ms. Rose stated that rezonings, plats, and other ministerial approvals were underway, and some
of the traffic issues along Sample Road and State Road 7 would be addressed.
Mayor Udine commented that he and Commissioner Weissman .had heard some of this
presentation at the Northwest Council of Elected Officials but, for the benefit of others present,
he asked explanation of the Seminole proposal to put the land intrust.
Mr. Kelly explained that the Tribe had been given the 4.8 acres in 1984 by the County and State
when the Turnpike had been built through their reservation in Hollywood. The zoning was
changed to commercial, the smoke shop was built, and the Tribe started the casino project in
1996. Mr. Kelly commented that the 4.8 acres of trust land was actually a sovereign country, and
the Tribe could do anything on that land. Coconut Creek provides water and sewer to the
property, which is the key to a municipal services agreement with the Tribe that gives their city

$1.5 million per year, including acost-of-living escalation each year. The Tribe also pays
franchise fees, utility taxes, and other surcharges.
Mr. Kelly stated that the Tribe has proposed a large and upscale facility: a hotel three times
larger than the Hard Rock Casino, potentially 1,000 to 1,200 rooms and 24 stories high. On top
of the expanded casino will be another 500 rooms, and the parking garage will have nine stories
and 6,500 parking spaces.
Before the proposed expansion of the trust, Mr. Kelly stated that he had encouraged Margate,
Coral Springs, and Parkland to consider meeting with the Governor to support a compact for the
Seminoles to have full Class 3 gaming. He noted that "racinos" in the State have Class 3 gaming
for slot machines only. Past governors have opposed the compact, and he hoped Governor Crist
would not. Using establishments in. Connecticut, Mr. Kelly illustrated the money that could be
made by the State and. municipalities, and he commented that the Seminoles' first draft had
pledged 30 percent of their profits. The Tribe planned retail shops, restaurants, and
entertainment venues. With the purchase of the Hard Rock chain for $965 million, he believed
the Tribe had the wherewithal to do anything they wanted. He noted that the Tribes had become
a financial empire in the State of Connecticut.
Mr. Kelly expected the development to have a tremendous impact on State Road 7 and Sample
Road. While he admitted that only Coconut Creek and Broward County had any standing with
regard to the 43.7 acres the Tribe wishes to add to the trust, he believed that other impacted
communities should approach the Governor for support. He commented regarding the extension
of Wiles Road over the Turnpike to help alleviate Sample Road congestion and improvement in
interior roads. Coconut Creek has requested a six month delay in the process -- until July 1St -- in
order to do studies of economic impact and traffic engineering, and the Tribe had given their
agreement.
Mr. Kelly indicated that Coconut Creek officials would attend a conference on gaming in the
Keys starting Friday, at which Max Osceola would be the main speaker. They hoped to meet
with a Bureau of Indian Affairs official reviewing the land-into-trust application at the
conference to obtain his commitment that the BIA would not process the application until
Coconut Creek accomplishes their six month review.
Mr. Kelly spoke regarding the potential impacts of the trust lands expansion on his city.
Mayor Udine recalled that their previous presentation had been prior to the application for the
additional trust land. He noted that the casino could continue to exist on the five acres and
everything else could be built around it, whether in trust or not.
Mr. Kelly confirmed that the Tribe could not have gaming on any lands owned unless it was
owned prior to 1988 when the National Indian Gaming Regulatory Act was passed. The
Seminoles have owned the 4.8 acres in Coconut Creek since 1984; they could gamble on that
land only, unless the regulations were changed at the federal level.

Mr. Kelly hoped for the commitment of Parkland officials and other cities directly impacted to
visit Tallahassee and Governor Crist when the opportunity for input about the compact is
presented. He expected that Coconut Creek would be in some way represented or at the least
referenced in compact negotiations.
Mayor Udine opened the meeting to Commission questions.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked if the planned facilities would include the concert venue and
nightclubs, expressing concern that the site was located in an already congested area and the
roads would not be able to handle the traffic.
Mr. Kelly agreed about the congestion, particularly with the existing road design. They had been
told by the Tribe that significant funds had been set aside to address the congestion at Sample
Road and State Road 7, but there were other considerations. He acknowledged that light rail and
bus transportation from remote parking facilities were being considered just to accommodate the
estimated 5,000 to 10,000 round-the-clock employees.
In terms of the venue, Mr. Kelly stated that the Tribe had always stated it would be an upscale
project due to the higher demographics and financials. Their Hard Rock 6,000-seat venue sold
out for a recent concert in 15 minutes, but the Tribe had advised they would consider more of an
intimate nightclub setting here. However, there was nothing in writing. He believed the business
would dictate their decisions, and their business would be successful as they do not do low-end
projects.
Mayor Udine suggested that no matter what is done there, the traffic issues would be huge for the
entire area.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz questioned the time line of development.
Mr. Kelly responded that the Tribe builds very rapidly on trust land, working 24 hours per day.
The Tribe had hoped that the 1,000-room hotel would already be operational.
Mayor Udine believed that Governor Crist would ultimately sign the gaming compact for the
potential proceeds to the State. If the State did not sign the compact, the Seminole Tribe would
apply to the Department of Interior; while it would delay the event, he believed it would happen
nevertheless. Since the racinos had opened, the Tribe had been at a competitive disadvantage,
and he suggested the Tribe wanted it done as quickly as possible.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked regarding the funds set aside for the roads, and whether the Tribe
would first build the facility and then deal with the roads. Mr. Kelly indicated that he could not
.provide an answer. Coconut Creek staff would soon discuss this at a meeting with the Tribe,
which did not want other cities present at the meeting.

City Manager Mark Lauzier briefly described Granicus, a system to provide video web streaming
and video archiving of the City's Commission meetings. He noted that the City's next action
would be to enter into a software agreement with Granicus at a future Commission meeting.
Mayor Udine questioned information contained on the agenda summary about the dwindling
cable audience. Mr. Lauzier admitted that he had not questioned the statement and that it could
be unsupported. The Mayor stated that he was curious as to records kept.
Commissioner Smith commented that, perhaps if the audience could hear the meetings, they
would watch them, as he had received complaints about the volume.
Mr. Rynders gave an overview of their technology and services. Based in San Francisco and
begun in 1999, the audio and video of the meetings would be published on the City's website,
both live and in on-demand format. They work only with local governments, with 225 clients
throughout the country. With 20 clients in Florida, he expected that number to increase to 30 in
the next 30 to 45 days. Mr. Rynders noted that the webstreaming became an integrated public
record in that it would be searchable, with software tools for the clerk to record actions through
the meeting and, through that process, link the agendas and minutes directly to the audio and
video archives. He gave a demonstration using the City of Naples and examples of the website
functionality and the tools available.
Commissioner McGilvray recalled discussion about webstreaming several years ago, with
concerns about advertising and the ability to send out agendas to subscribers. Mr. Rynders
indicated there would be no advertising and no e-mail blitzes as part of the technology.
However, he offered that users could subscribe via a tool that could automatically update them
regarding a specific subject or issue, which would generate an e-mail notification.
Commissioner McGilvray also asked regarding the archiving and whether the presentations
shown at Commission meetings would be included. Mr. Rynders responded that the
webstreaming was dependent upon camera placement and would essentially be the same as
currently shown on cable. He offered that any type of electronic file could also be published.
Settings within the software would be done on site during staff training.
Commissioner McGilvray questioned how recesses during meetings would be edited. Mr.
Rynders indicated there were several, but also a "pause" feature in the application until the
meeting was reconvened.
Commissioner McGilvray asked the City Manager if this system would be in place of minutes.
Mr. Lauzier suggested that the minutes process would be much easier. He stated that the City
Clerk would like to do summary minutes that would be much shorter since the verbatim would
always be available. He noted that DVDs of the meetings could be sent out or accessed at the
Library. City Clerk Sandra Couzzo added that she hoped for streamlined minutes. The minutes
were now summary; it would be a better situation for individuals to be able to hear whole
conversations. Mr. Lauzier noted that a request for verbatim would be everything.

Mayor Udine indicated that he would only consider this if the Minutes "disappear" and would
comprise only motion, maker, second, and vote. He asked if this action would replace any jobs.
Mr. Lauzier responded in the negative. There were a number of major initiatives to be

and workshops. Some time must be given to connecting and paraphrasing the agenda and tying it
to the videostream. While it could be expanded later as the next phase, he admitted that level
had not been planned.
While the Mayor noted the money in the Information Technology funds, he asked regarding the
monthly recurring costs. Mr. Lauzier indicated that the City would work from the IT funds for
the first year and the future $800 per month would have to be budgeted. With the Mayor's
further questions, Mr. Lauzier indicated no budget for public records requests, which were
handled on an as-needed basis. He expected that the system would assist with those.
Mayor Udine asked if furnishing a DVD or giving web access would legally satisfy public
records requests. Mr. Maurodis stated that the City would still be required to have written
minutes, whether summary or shortened like other cities. If anything more substantive is
requested, the DVD could be made available.
Addressing expansion into departments, Mr. Rynders offered that the City could stream as many
meetings as it wished. The limitation would be in the ability to link actual minutes documents to
the video. There must be training of the staff to the technology and customized templates must
be set per each meeting body. Additional fees would be involved.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked regarding other companies with this service. Mr. Rynders offered
there were a number of video streaming companies, but they were the only one working with
local governments to provide automated legislative workflow. Mr. Lauzier commented that he
had not found one company as sophisticated as this.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz questioned the goal for the system. Mr. Lauzier commented about the
goals: service to the public, giving easier and quicker access to records for information requests;
Clerk and staff time and efficiency; and a useful tool for the Commission and staff.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested that, with this technology, meetings and minutes across-the-
board should be handled with this system. Mr. Lauzier commented that there would be
advantages to Planning and Zoning use.

At the mention that tutors charge a minimum of $50 per hour, Mr. Lauzier offered that he had
been at the Library this past Monday;. there were three tutors and four tables in the room.
Mayor Udine suggested a computation of three days a week, two hours each day, .multiplied by
$45 per hour, and then divided by 15 tutors that each would pay a yearly fee. Mr. Lauzier offered
that amount might be the occupational license fee to tutor in the Library, which should be
enacted by ordinance.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested that there may be overflow into the new Community Center.
He believed that the policy should also be uniform as to residents and non-residents: if the
individual teaches in the City or if the teacher is a resident, there should be no fee. He felt that
teachers should be encouraged to remain here through the additional income.
Commissioner McGilvray suggested a limit of two days per week, and discussion ensued. It was
noted that nothing would be placed in effect until the Community Center opened.
Mayor Udine believed there plenty of time to decide the policy before the beginning of the next
school year. It was not about making money or penalizing; the problem was that it infringed on
the Library use. The fees would not be meant as a deterrent, but rather as a control of the space.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz also suggested limiting the number of tutors per day, and there was
further discussion.
Mr. Lauzier suggested that the Library Director speak with the tutors about their ideas, and then a
separate item would be brought to the Commission.
At the Mayor's question, Ms. Hayes indicated that the possibility of fees had been discussed at
the Friends of the Library meeting. They did not have a problem with charging an occupational
fee per semester or per year.
Mayor Udine asked whether non-resident students who attend Parkland schools could obtain
library cards, and Ms. Hayes responded that the Library policy was that individuals must be a
resident, a teacher, a business owner, or the on-site manager of a business within the City. The
Mayor asked that the Friends be asked their thinking about allowing non-resident students who
