4. RESOLUTION NO. 2008
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, OPPOSING THE
BROWARD COUNTY COMMISSION'S PROPOSED
RETAIL ESTABLISHMENT PARKING SECURITY
ORDINANCE, MANDATING RETAIL BUSINESSES TO
INSTALL SECURITY SURVEILLANCE CAMERAS
THAT WOULD COVER THE ENTIRE PARKING LOT
TWENTY -FOUR HOURS A DAY, SEVEN DAYS A
WEEK.
This item was pulled from the consent agenda.
OPPOSING BROWARD COUNTY
COMMISSION'S PROPOSED
RETAIL ESTABLISHMENT PARKING
SECURITY ORDINANCE
Mayor Udine explained that he requested this item to be on the agenda by the Broward League of
Cities and other interested groups with concern for the expense of this project to the cities and its
residents.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested sending a letter to Broward County stating the City of
Parkland concern with the home rule issue. He suggested tabling this item and to send a letter
stating the City's concerns. Vice Mayor Moskowitz does not feel it prudent to inject the city into
this issue.
Commissioner Weissman asked the Police Chief to come up to the podium and asked his opinion
on the issue, specifically if this requirement could prevent crimes in the City of Parkland. The
Chief said this issue stems from the homicide at Walgreen's store on Powerline Road. There were
no surveillance cameras at that store. He said this could have two positive effects. First it has the
potential to act as a deterrent and secondly, It has the potential to help solve the case. He agreed
that Vice Mayor had a good point regarding home rule.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz feels that the issue of who pays for the project and what type of
equipment that would be used is for another day. As of right now the issue is the home rule.
Commissioner Smith said that having been in the banking industry there are still robberies's in
banks even though they know there are cameras in the banks.
Mayor Udine said that the City could do their own ordinance on this subject in the future but they
do not appreciate the County overstepping home rule.

REGULAR AGENDA
1. CONSIDERATION: ENERGY INCENTIVE REVIEW
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young explained the "Energy Incentive Review" item. Brian
Archer, Environmental Resource Director, presented to the Commission a review of this program.
He said there was a lot of participation and enthusiasm for this program. A total of (46)
applications were approved totaling $5,467.98 in incentive awards. (29) were for Air
Conditioning system replacements and Hybrid cars with (17) remaining for light bulb
replacements, low flow toilets, energy star appliances and different solar energy programs. Mr.
Archer recommended revising or eliminating from the plan: the exotic/ invasive tree replacement,
solar powered landscape lighting and the plug strip load control as there was little or no interest in
these programs. He then recommended replacements or additions to the program: rain sensor
device maintenance, sustainable landscape irrigation and design.

Mayor Udine questioned the City Attorney Andy Maurodis to clarify wording on Ordinance
2008 -28A. He accepted the explanation.
Mayor Udine questioned wording on Ordinance 2008 -28. The Mayor thought that the current
appointments for the boards were required to be residents of the City of Parkland instead of
"residents and/or business owners He suggested that we delete the "and or business owners
portion. Staff advised the Mayor that this is a first reading of the ordinance so it would need to be
amended and readdressed at second reading. Discussion ensued regarding board appointments
being filled by residents of the City of Parkland.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz questioned ordinance 2008 -28A regarding electronic copies to be
provided to the public for no charge versus hard copies and fees required for this service.
The Mayor advised that this service is provided already but the ordinance is addressing the costs
involved for gathering information requested, hence wording "administrative" fees. Discussion
ensued.
Mayor Udine opened to the public.
Robert Mayersohn, 6742 N.W. 80 Manor addressed the subject of resident's appointments in
Ordinance 2008 -28, Chapter 2, Section 2.39. Mr. Mayersohn's suggestion was to make resident's
concurrently with the City Commissions, such as, when a Commissioner or Mayor seeks to be
elected the candidate needs to be a resident of Parkland for a certain period of time. The board
advisory applicant would also need to be a resident of Parkland for a certain period of time as
well. Mr. Mayersohn also suggested that if the business owner was kept in place this should also
have an imposed residency term and to limit the number of outside members permitted to maintain
balance on the board.
There were no further comments or questions, and the Mayor closed to the public.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz addressed Mr. Mayersohn's suggestions and clarified the residency
restriction to run for the City Commission you must be a resident for one year, changed from 6
months previously. He suggested that 1 year may be too long for an advisory board applicant and
the limit to be considered for these boards be 6 months Mayor Udine advised that this could be
taken into consideration at the time the applicants are reviewed but to not include a residency
restriction at this time that would limit the pool of applicants.

COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE

STANDARD RIGHT OF ENTRY AGREEMENT FOR
EXECUTION BY THE CITY MANAGER.

purpose.
