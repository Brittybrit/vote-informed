2. DISCUSSION: REDISTRICTING
ADDITIONAL PLANS
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young recalled the workshop discussion about the redistricting
map to be attached to the Ordinance, which requires two public hearings. Two versions had
previously been provided by the University of Florida, and the Commission had requested that
two other versions be investigated for sufficiency. Documentation had been placed on the dais
indicating that neither would work, and she indicated that Ms. Maureen Tartaglione was present
to explain the justification, if necessary. Ms. Gardner-Young stated that the available options
would be Plan 1 or Plan 2, but they should not be construed as final. For clarity, only one map
should be attached to the Ordinance, but the map might still change.
Mayor Udine provided explanation that the Commission had discussed this at a workshop, where
public comments had been invited; two public hearings. on the ordinance, which would attach
one map, would be held.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz questioned why Plans lA and 2A were not viable. He expressed concern
that a map included with the ordinance could be amended. Since the Commission's proposals
were not sufficient, he suggested that other options could not be proposed without information
about the census blocks.
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis indicated the Vice Mayor was correct; the Commission was at
the point that action must be taken fairly quickly. There would be only minor tweaking from first
to second reading. He agreed that, to a large extent, the options had been narrowed.

Giving an historical perspective, Mayor Udine commented that this had in the past been a simple
process. He noted that Plan 1 was similar to that existing, and Plan 2 was more radical -and
closer to what the districts would eventually look like in the future. The Charter Review had
given the City a new process, and he respectfully offered that it may have been "overkill" to fund
a process that might have been done simply and in-house.
Consultant Maureen Tartaglione spoke regarding Pine Tree, indicating that they had tried various
options to include Pine Tree in one district. While limitations were the standards in districting
for equal population, contiguity, and compactness, she suggested that census blocks were the
more driving factor. She referred to a report sent this date, which noted that almost all of Pine
Tree north was one census block comprised of 700 people and, additionally, wrapped around
another neighborhood. She agreed that Plan 1 was the least disruptive in that it followed what
already existed, and she explained other movements considered.
The Vice Mayor reiterated his comments that he had hoped to keep Pine Tree together as much
as possible, but Plan 2 had split Pine Tree into three districts. He indicated that, while he
probably favored Plan 1, he asked that it be noted that Plan 1 would probably not work in four
years' time since all growth would occur to the west.
Mr. Maurodis clarified that the attachment to the ordinance would be Plan 1. He suggested that
the City Manager include Plan 1 in the back-up as well.

user and/or administrative fees. Some were required statutorily, such as those for the Building
Department in Exhibit B, while others were administrative, such as a returned check charge. She
assured that the fees were in line with those of other municipalities, and staff members were
present to address any concerns of the Commission.
The Commission did not question Building Department fees. Mayor Udine believed the
Building Department was providing better service; less complaints were heard, and it had moved
forward in a much more positive and user-friendly way.
As to the administrative fees, Mayor Udine commented that he did not have a problem with
these. For example, there should be charges for bounced checks; it was a standard business
practice. There were no other Commission comments.
As to Leisure Services fees, Mayor Udine noted that the non-resident sports card had been
implemented for basketball registration. He assumed that it had run smoothly, and Leisure
Services Director Scott Davidoff agreed. The Mayor believed that the fees charged for park and
pavilion use were reasonable.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that the City was not raising or instituting fees as a result of
tax reform; the City was merely catching up with what every other Broward city had done for
years.
Mayor Udine offered that most fees were already in place. There were costs to the City for an
event stage, as an example, and he agreed that those costs should be passed to the user rather than
subsidized by the taxpayers.
Commissioner Weissman questioned the differential between the resident and non-resident rates
for camp and activities. He asked the percentage of non-residents in those programs. Mr.
Davidoff responded that camps at: Westglades had 22 percent non-residents; Riverglades had 16
percent non-residents; and Teens had 17 percent.
Commissioner Weissman asked why there was only a $50 difference when the City charged $150
for a sports card. Mr. Davidoff indicated that staff had tried to stay in line with other municipal
and private camp programs in the area, and he noted that not all municipalities distinguished
between residents and non-residents. The Commissioner also questioned why costs were not
uniform based upon the length of sessions, and he believed the difference between residents and
non-residents should be more, noting a difference of $180 used by Boca Raton.
Mayor Udine agreed. He suggested-that the fee schedule could be passed and, in the interim,
some of the Leisure Services fees reexamined. He agreed with Commissioner Weissman's
comments and, noting that the City's camps were among the best in the County, he believed that
the City's residents should be given the first opportunity to sign up for them. Mr. Davidoff stated
that this would now occur. The Mayor suggested that every procedure be examined, assuring
that City residents were given early priorities.
