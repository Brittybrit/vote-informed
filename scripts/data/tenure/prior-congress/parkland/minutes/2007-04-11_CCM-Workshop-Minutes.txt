Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer gave a brief presentation about the Climate
Action Handbook. He indicated that the City had already taken steps to recognize the program

3. DISCUSSION: "MIXED USE" LAND USE CATEGORY
Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young indicated this matter had been brought to the
Commission at its last meeting, and she had pulled it in order to make certain that all were on the
same "wave length." She noted that discussion concerning the addition of this land use category
had not before occurred, and she had agendized this to answer any Commission questions.
Planning Director Roberta Moore offered that the intent of the mixed use land use designation
was to take into consideration the report given by Treasure Coast Regional Planning Council
with respect to the State Road 7 corridor, in which the concept was greatly discussed. Based
upon that report, staff had researched other communities which had adopted mixed use
designations. The designation would give property owners the opportunity for a combination
commercial and residential component during the land use process.
At the Mayor's question, Ms. Gardner-Young indicated that, under general planning practices, a
residential land use designation could not have commercial uses within it and, conversely,
commercial uses could not have residential. The mixed use category was developed to allow
uses to be integrated.
Mayor Udine questioned whether the mixed use category would then be City-wide or if certain
areas could be designated. Mr. Maurodis responded that the category would have no application
when first adopted. Only when a landowner wished to make use of it, and'the Commission felt it
appropriate, would the land use then be designated in a certain area. If the Commission did not
feel it was appropriate, it would not be approved. Having the category might encourage a
property owner to ask for it, but it would give them no right to it.

Mayor Udine asked about the 11 acres on State Road 7 and whether this designation was
specifically for that area. Ms. Gardner-Young stated that it was not specific to that parcel?
however, either the Wedge piece or that parcel would be the two more likely areas wishing to
utilize that designation. The 11-acre parcel has a commercial designation at the present time, and
any inclusion of housing would require a land use amendment. She suggested that adoption of
mixed use was a policy decision.
Commissioner Weissman questioned whether any project that would require this type of
designation had been proposed for the City. Ms. Gardner-Young indicated there had been a
couple of proposals for the 11-acre site; however, a formal petition had not been submitted.
At various questions, Commissioner Weissman stated that he was not in favor of adopting this
designation, preferring to first see the project proposed before allowing it. He believed the
Commission should have the ability to first decide whether a project would fit into their vision of
the City -and only with enough restrictions that no other project could easily adapt. He
acknowledged that some area of the Wedge could be a potential location for this type of
development.
Mr. Maurodis noted that the Commission would be creating a category to the Comprehensive
Plan and that the process would be a lengthy one. Ms. Moore added that the zoning district for
mixed use must .also be created, commenting that this would be the first step of many taken.
Ms. Gardner-Young offered that, through adoption of this land use amendment now, future
Commission decisions would be controlled. Additionally, specifics could be adopted when
projects were proposed.
Commissioner Smith asked whether decisions were made on a case-by-case basis, asking
specifically about the classification of the Parkland Village site. Ms. Gardner-Young responded
that Parkland Village had required a land use amendment, site planning, zoning, and a plat; it had
been zoned commercial. Mr. Maurodis noted that flexibility units were also assigned.
Commissioner Smith suggested that the multi-step process gave the City more control. Ms.
Gardner-Young indicated that, even though staff has suggested the creation of the mixed use
category, the Commission would still have the option to determine whether that designation
would be correct -just had been the case with Parkland Village and its land use. She believed
the mixed use land use category would give the Commission more flexibility and full discretion,
and that it would not limit the City's ability to give and take.
Mayor Udine recalled the commercial zoning of the Shield property, the review of development
proposals, and what now appears a successful venture. With his understanding of the issue, he
noted there had been several proposals for the 11-acre site along State Road 7, and the Planning
and Zoning Board, the Treasure Coast group, and the Commission had favored mixed use
development. He believed it would be difficult to attract a mixed use proposal if the City did not
have that category.

Commissioner Weissman commented that landowners had come to the City years ago with all
types of proposals, including one individual with 16 different ideas; the Commission had not
liked any of them, but they had not created a new category to accommodate. He believed that the
developer must have a program that would make sense financially and not one that the City
might dictate. It could be a mixed use plan, or a commercial plan, and at that point the
Commission would have to make a decision. The developer would come to the City with their
plans, and he felt it did not make sense to create a land use category because that would be what
the City would like to see the landowner build. He was not in favor.
Ms. Gardner-Young believed the Mayor's opinion was correct. She believed that the petitioner,
without an existing land use category, would not wait for the process to change it.
At Commissioner Weissman's question of submitted rough-draft proposals, Ms. Gardner-Young
confirmed there had been plans of mixed use; she believed the landowner was also exploring
commercial. She felt it could be economical either way, but that it would be a question of the
easiest way to go. Commissioner Weissman suggested that, in light of the existing residential
market, the landowner may not see the same return as quickly.
Discussion ensued.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz entered the meeting.
Mayor Udine summarized that, without enough support on the dais, there would be no reason to
go further. He agreed that developers could bring in any type of proposal they wished.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz stated that he did not wish this to be the policy for the Wedge as he
believed it contained the possibility for mixed use, which he felt attractive to retail and
commercial business.
Commissioner Weissman restated his position: he did not oppose mixed use, but he did not want
to create the designation without the Planning and Zoning Board and this Commission seeing
what is proposed and by whom. Projects would be examined on an individual basis. He was
concerned that, once created, it would be available to any property owner in the City.
There was further discussion. The Shield was compared with that of the 11-acre area on State
Road 7. Mayor Udine recalled that the process for the Shield had taken ayear-and-a-half, and
Mr. Maurodis confirmed there had been a land use plan change and flexibility units. He noted
that the Broward County Land Use Plan allowed mixed use only under very limited
circumstances, which could be accessed.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz admitted his worry that the City would be sending a signal that mixed
use would not be welcome.
Discussion followed.
