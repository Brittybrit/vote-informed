 1. RESOLUTION NO. 2012-106: TOWN CENTER SPECIAL EXCEPTION 
 
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF 
PARKLAND, FLORIDA, FOR SPECIAL EXCEPTION 
APPROVAL FOR A CHILD CARE CENTER TO BE LOCATED 
AT 6604 PARKSIDE DRIVE IN THE PARKLAND TOWN 
CENTER, CASE NO. 02-SE-12. (quasi-judicial)

Ms. Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution 2012-106, Town Center Special Exception, and noted 
that Michele Mellgren would be making staff’s presentation this evening. Ms. Mellgren entered the staff 
report and applicable attachments into the meeting record, and summarized the request for a special 
exception for the approval of a child care center, for a maximum of ninety-two children, between the ages 
of one and four. Ms. Mellgren stated that the City’s traffic consultant had reviewed the applicant’s 
proposal and concluded that there would be no net traffic impact. She also noted that the proposed 
development would trigger the need for a total of twelve parking spaces, and that the applicant had 
agreed to provide nine parking spaces. Ms. Mellgren stated that the third issue was potential noise from 
the play area at the day care center and the potential impact on neighboring properties. She noted that 
play area would only be open between 7:30 a.m. and 6:30 p.m., Monday through Friday. Ms. Mellgren 
briefly reviewed the proposed conditions of approval for the special exception, including the fact that the 
facility itself would only be open between 6:30 a.m. and 6:30 p.m., Monday through Friday. 
 
Commissioner Rosenof inquired about the character and materials for the fencing adjoining the proposed 
child care center. Ms. Mellgren said this particular would be taken up in the future by the City 
Commission, acting in their capacity as the Community Appearance Board. 
 
Bonnie Miskel, representing the petitioner, waived the need for quasi-judicial proceedings on behalf of 
her client. Ms. Miskel noted that the Parkland Town Center property was currently in the 
receivership/foreclosure process, with an existing 67% vacancy rate. Ms. Miskel said that the existing 
tenants in the Town Center was hopeful that the City Commission would approve the proposed day care 
center this evening. She noted that day care facility would have a total of six classrooms, for children 
between the ages of one and four. Ms. Miskel briefly described about the building footprint and layout, 
including the need for attention to security-related measures. She also said that the one-year old 
playground facility would be physically separated from the playground for other children at the child care 
facility. 
 
Carl Peterson, the applicant’s traffic engineer, briefly reviewed the estimated trip generation for the child 
care facility, noting that the proposed use would generate approximately 368 less vehicle trips per day 
than a traditional retail use. 
 
Ms. Miskel said that some residents of the Mews community were concerned about traffic generated by 
the proposed child care facility and the alignment of proposed driveways. She stated that the project’s 
architectural consultant had reviewed potential options to address these concerns, but this was not 
possible due to existing infrastructure and related easements. Ms. Miskel noted that she had two letters 
expressing support of the proposed child care facility and submitted them for the record. 
 
Mayor opened the floor to public comments. 
 
Robert Marks inquired about the nature of proposed fencing for the facility. Commissioner Rosenof 
noted that this would be taken up by the Community Appearance Board in the future. Mr. Marks also 
inquired about proposed outdoor lighting of the facility. Ms. Miskel said it was not the facility 
operator’s intention for children to play on the playground after dark. 
 
Ricky Gordon spoke in favor of the proposed child care facility as a very suitable use for the property and 
encouraged the Commission to support the proposal.

Charles Morrell, a resident of the Mews, noted that the Board of Mews community voted against a 
proposed day care facility for this same site about eight or ten years ago. He requested that the Mayor 
and Commission not approve the proposal, principally due to traffic-related concerns. 
 
Gayle Easterling, a resident of the Mews and a certified city planner, noted that the Mews had vehicles 
from 78 homes which exit on to Parkside Drive and Holmberg Road on a daily basis. She suggested the 
concept of the south entrance possibly only having an option for right-turns only. 
 
Katherine Morrell, a resident of the Mews, said she was concerned about traffic from the circle on 
Holmberg Road and the potential for back-ups during peak times. Ms. Morrell inquired about the times 
of day when traffic counts were taken for the proposed project. Carl Peterson, the applicant’s traffic 
consultant, noted that the traffic study counts were conducted between the hours of 7:00 and 9:00 a.m. 
and 4:00 and 6:00 p.m. 
 
Sam Cornelius spoke in favor of the proposed day care facility at Parkland Town Center. 
 
Joy Milligan also spoke in strong support of the proposed day care facility/academy. 
 
Mary Esther Levine, a resident of Heron Bay, said that she knew the Morgans, the owners of the existing 
day care facility in Coconut Creek, and that they were wonderful people, business persons and Parkland 
residents. Ms. Levine stated that she supported approval of the proposed day care facility. 
 
Noting that there were no additional speakers to address this matter, Mayor Udine closed the public 
hearing and opened the floor for Commissioner’s comments and questions. 
 
Vice Mayor Weissman inquired whether the property owner/applicant would be willing to put signage in 
the building about which exit to use when leaving the parking area. Ms. Miskel said she her client would 
be pleased to accommodate this request as a condition of approval. 
 
Commissioner Rosenof said the concept of making the southern entrance a right-turn only was a very 
good idea which had some potential merit. He requested that the City Manager work with the applicant’s 
traffic consultant to evaluate the practicality of implementing this approach. 
 
 
Commissioner Willis noted that patrons of the day care facility dropped off their children at different 
times in the morning, unlike traditional schools, were there was a specific set time for drop-off times for 
students. Ms. Miskel said this was correct. Commissioner Willis inquired whether any of the other 
business in the plaza were open at 6:30 a.m. or during the early morning hours. Ms. Miskel said the 
earliest the other businesses in the Town Center opened was typically about 8:00 a.m. 
 
Mayor said he was extremely supportive of the applicant’s proposal. He said this day care center 
could potentially be helpful to the overall shopping center’s future tenant mix. 
 
MOTION made by Vice Mayor Weissman to approve Resolution 2012-106. Seconded by 
Commissioner Willis.
