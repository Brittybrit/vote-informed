City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young recalled during past discussions there had been issues about
facilities located within the private communities, such as a clubhouse, playground, or tennis
court, which were created at the time of development. It had recently come to the City's
attention that a plamied playground had been removed and another facility had not taken its
place. If a site plan had been approved for the facility, she believed the question became how the
City should be involved and to what degree.
Commissioner Weissman suggested that, apart from amenities, there was also the question of
signage. He gave, as an example, the repair of a sign that may become different than what was
originally approved. He believed that the City should maintain annual contact with homeowners'
associations and management companies to keep abreast of what was going on in each
community in order to maintain the originally-approved amenities and the signage.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested that residents should hold the associations accountable, rather
than the City accepting this responsibility.
Commissioner Weissman commented that an amendment to the site plan originally approved
should be the decision of the City.
Ms. Gardner-Young offered that staff had taken a strong position when developments were under
construction. As far as those existing, there had been no steps taken because staff did not
specifically look for modifications. Unless there was some mechanism or a specific request, it
would not come to the City's attention. In the case of signage, however, it was much easier. She
offered that she routinely attends homeowners' association meetings, and there had been cases of
reminder letters sent to management companies regarding signage.
Mayor Udine believed that,. in the case of a green space conversion, the City should have some
input. While he was concerned that a substitution of one recreational amenity for another might
be an extreme case, he believed that the City should at least be placed on notice about the change.
The Mayor noted that his second issue with developer amenities was, in the case of WCI. He
believed it time to check the process and the estimated times for completion of the amenities.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz added that the process should be checked since WCI had also pledged
funds, such as the traffic signal at the school.
Ms. Gardner-Young indicated that she would provide a list of current amenities. She noted that
Planning was in the process of reviewing the conditions of approval, and these would be
provided to the Commission when complete.
Mayor Udine commented that certain things were bonded or there were letters of credit. He felt
the City should stay on top of the situation and work toward close out of those different accounts.
Ms. Gardner-Young noted there were ties to building permits or Certificates of Occupancy
issued. If WCI was not coming in for the CO's, they could not be forced to meet the conditions.

1. DISCUSSION: PINE TRAILS PARK CELL TOWER
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young indicated that cell phone service in western areas of the City
came up during the discussions regarding the proposed Old City Hall tower. The Commission
asked staff to obtain letters of interest on the possibility of a cell tower installation at Pine Trails
Park. The letters of interest had been included in the Commission's packet. She indicated that
Grain had on this date indicated they were no longer interested.
Ms. Gardner-Young reviewed that three companies were interested -- Verizon, Sprint, and
Florida Towers, and she asked the Commission's direction.
Mayor Udine commented that he did not believe there was any question that Florida Towers
would be the best for the City, and he felt the City should move toward that contract. He noted
that the relationship with Verizon and its Liberty Park tower had not been good.
Ms. Gardner-Young offered that Verizon had recently filed its first co-location application.
Representatives of the three companies were present to answer questions.
At the Vice Mayor's question regarding the cell tower design, Ms. Gardner-Young noted that
these were only letters of interest; the next step would be to negotiate a contract. With the
Commission's direction, there were options that could be investigated and negotiated.
Mayor Udine commented that all of the applicants had submitted either a flagpole or stealth pole
without specific design, and he assumed that the design could be worked out.
Ms. Gardner-Young indicated that the tree design at Old City Hall was felt to blend in with 'the
large trees in the area. At Pine Trails, there were no trees and the tower might be used for park
lighting. She acknowledged that the important factors would be the costs, the escalation clause,
and provision of a generator.
John Ruth of Florida Towers LLC briefly spoke about the generator. He stated that he did not
want to mislead the City about back-up power for the entire Community Center.
At the Mayor's question, Ms. Gardner-Young offered that a generator would be provided for the
new facility as well as service for the cell tower at Old City Hall. It could be argued that co-
locators could pay a portion of the costs, or the City could also contribute toward the generator.

3. DISCUSSION: PARKSIDE DRIVE ARBOR STREET

Environmental Services Director Brian Archer stated that the CypressHead Board of Directors
had requested that the Commission consider creating a landscape berm ordinance with regard to
Parkside Drive and the Parkland Lakes PUD. Mr. Archer suggested there were two options
within the City's existing Code: to amend the Parkland .Lakes PUD to add landscape buffer
requirements, or to add Parkside Drive as an arbor street.
Mr. Archer indicated that amendment of the PUD would impact CypressHead, Cypress Cay, and
The Mews because they were part of the PUD.
Should an arbor street be created, Mr. Archer stated that all developments along Parkside Drive
would be impacted and each development differently. Only Holmberg Road is presently
considered an arbor street in the City. The only exempt property along Parkside Drive would be
the school.
At the Commission's request, Mr. Archer presented photographs of Parkside Drive and offered
descriptive information. If the option to amend the PUD is chosen, he recommended that the
Holmberg Road landscape theme be used as a guide as it gave specific numbers of trees and
accent shrubs for every 1001inear feet.
There was discussion concerning a Holmberg. Road property where its buffer hedge had- been
removed.
Mayor Udine summarized that there were currently no ordinances in effect and the homeowners'
association had no rules in effect. Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested that the association
promulgate rules under its declaration.
John Jarvis of CypressHead indicated that the particular area was not within their jurisdiction,
nor was it addressed in the covenants and restrictions. He recalled that Gino Scialdone had
spoken to the City about creating an arbor street prior to Hurricane Wilma.

Mayor Udine questioned how the Parkland Lakes PUD might be amended, recalling an issue
with voting. Ms. Gardner-Young stated that she would do more research if the Commission was
interested in pursuing this. She noted that, while CypressHead may want it, other developments
would be affected.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested that the idea was great in theory, .but the cost may be
significant to other property owners in order to create the berm for the benefit of CypressHead.
While he agreed that it should be investigated, the cost would be important.
Mayor Udine asked regarding Town Centre, and Mr. Archer indicated that it would be in
compliance as long as a berm was not required.
At the Vice Mayor's question, Mr. Jarvis recalled the destruction of Hurricane Wilma and the
work done by the majority of property owners, and he confirmed that only one property had not
complied.- Mr. Jarvis noted that the Parkland Lakes Planned Unit Development required the
80/20 rule referendum condition, which he believed impossible. Ms. Gardner-Young recalled
that the PUD regulations had followed those of CypressHead.
Mr. Jarvis noted that the arbor street had been mentioned as an option.
Mayor Udine asked what an arbor street might entail. Mr. Archer anticipated that the cost in
100-foot sections -- with the berm, four trees, six accents, and the continuous hedge -- about
$8,000 for every 1001inear feet.
With Mr. Archer's further explanation of the arbor street ordinance, there was further discussion
regarding the expense to accomplish the work.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested that it might be less complicated and less expensive for the
homeowners' association to install the berm and landscaping on the one property in question,
rather than enacting something that would affect other associations and areas and cost them
money.
Ms. Gardner-Young indicated that it would be her recommendation not to do the arbor street.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz believed there were ways that the homeowners' association and the City
could put pressure on anon-compliant property owner. He felt it unfair to burden other
properties in order to get one property owner to comply.
Mayor Udine felt it clear that the City should not invest staff time and resources to try to amend
the PUD for this issue, leaving only the arbor street ordinance -which would present a number
of problems to enforce.
There was discussion concerning other possible solutions, and Mr. Jarvis asked if the property
owner might be cited under code enforcement.
