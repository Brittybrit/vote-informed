III PUBLIC INPUT
Mayor Udine opened to the Public.
Mr. Stuart Krantz, 6348 NW 62 Terrace, offered his comments on the following topics:
• He felt the City of Parkland Library should not be considered a leisure activity and there
for should not be combined with the City's Leisure Services Department and urged the
Commission not to discount the community's need for academic services provided by the
library.
• He suggested the City add street lights to the intersections between Park Trails Road and
Pine Tree Estates.
• Requested the Parkside Drive circle be redesigned. He believed it had been hit by a
vehicle on three occasions at least due to poor design issues and expressed concern that
pedestrians are at risk in that area.
• Requested the pedestrian sidewalk to be relocated further away from the street at the
Northern section of Holmberg Road.
• Spoke on invasive vegetation issues, such as the Brazilian Pepper and Australian Pine,
which destroy native plant life and wildlife habitats. He suggested the City encourage the
natural growth of native plants and utilize them in place of invasive vegetation.
Mayor Udine explained that by adding the Library to the Leisure Services department, it would
ensure the library received continued funding for the services they provide, rather than remaining
an isolated department at budget time.
Mr. Mike Squillace, 7250 NW 84 Terrace, spoke on the oil -like substance that seemed to
remain an issue at Trotters Lane in The Ranches and thanked Commissioner Moskowitz for
coming out to observe the situation.
Mr. Squillace also spoke on the Commissions' view to discourage over commercialization in
The Ranches neighborhood however, the City has allowed a "Fair" (the City operated Farmer's
Market) in front of his home.
There were no further comments or questions and the Mayor closed to the public.

IV COMMENTS BY MAYOR AND COMMISSION
Comments made by Commissioner Rosenof:
• Advised the EAB and the Parkland Garden Club will hold their first meeting on Tuesday,
June 23 at 10:00 a.m. at the City of Parkland Library to discuss a new `Victory Garden'
project on the City's property.
Comments made by Commissioner Smith:
• Addressed Mr. Squillace's comments and noted the City had done everything they could
to correct the problem at Trotters Lane and would continue to diligently work on the
issue until it was resolved.
Comments made by Vice Mayor Weissman:
• Addressed the need of completion of the right of entry agreements from all of the private
communities before a hurricane hits the area. He stated without these signed agreements
the City would not have the authority to enter individual communities to proceed with
post hurricane clean up efforts in the event of a storm. He suggested the Commissioners
contact the Home Owners Associations and/or Management Companies for the
communities located within their districts and request these agreements on behalf of the
City.
Mayor Udine commented that the City would need to reach out to the sub - associations directly
regarding the right of entry agreements.
Comments made by Commissioner Moskowitz:
• Briefly reviewed the oil spill issue at Trotter's Lane in The Ranches. He noted that
Florida Highway Products (FHP) had been hired to pave the road. He stated that in the
beginning the City may have had difficulty comprehending the cause of the issue,
however, once the City had become aware of the situation he felt they responded
appropriately.
• Spoke on the clean up efforts that were needed and referenced a sample of material
obtained from the clean up site which revealed possible defective material that appeared
to not have dried properly.
• Suggested the City Attorney, Andrew Maurodis arrange a meeting with the consultant,
Sphere Environmental Services, Litigation Attorney Stuart Michelson and the
Commission in order to educate them further on this development.
Comments made by Mayor Udine:
• Noted for the record the City had filed a lawsuit against FHP, the road contractor they
felt was responsible for the issues at Trotters Lane. The City has provided their resources
in the meantime to help rectify the matter and hope to recoup these costs via the lawsuit.
• Addressed the comments Mr. Squillace raised concerning the Farmers Market over
commercializing the City. The Mayor advised the market is located on City owned
property, is well maintained by staff with traffic routed by BSO, and is considered to be a
positive event for the neighborhood.

2. DISCUSSION: FIRE CONTRACT w/FIRE CONSULTANT
City Manager, Caryn Gardner -Young explained that per Commission direction, she has been
negotiating the City's contract for fire protection services with the City of Coral Springs and to
also discuss the potential of fire protection services to be provided by the Broward County
Sheriff's Office (BSO). Ms. Young introduced Mr. Steve Savia with the Sage Group Consulting
Services, who had been engaged to assist her in this endeavor.
Mr. Savia, CEO of the Sage Group, explained the Sage Group is a consulting firm specializing in
emergency services. He was engaged by the City to assist in the evaluation and negotiations of
the fire rescue and emergency services contract with the City of Coral Springs. The contract is

City Manager, Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Resolution No. 2009 -46, Concert Promoter
Contract and introduced Parks & Recreation Director, Mr. Scott Davidoff to provide a brief
presentation.
Mr. Davidoff explained Mr. Doug Isaac of EXI had sent his apologies for not being able to
attend tonight's meeting. He addressed the Commission's comments from the last meeting and
noted staff had evaluated other contracts and felt most comfortable with the City of Fort
Lauderdale/Live Nation contract for their beach festival/concert series. Staff modified the
contract to fit the needs of the City of Parkland and had the City Attorney, Andrew Maurodis as
well as EXI's Attorney review.
In addition he met with the Fire Department and EXI, onsite at the Pine Trails Amphitheater, to
determine the size and scope of the event with an initial estimate of approximately 7,500 person
event maximum. They discussed the safety and traffic plan, which will require approval by BSO
& the Fire Department and reviewed the insurance requirements.
Mr. Davidoff advised EXI has planned the first show for late summer, possibly the weekend of
August 24` 2009 prior to the start of school.
Mayor Udine explained the Commission decided it would be in the City's best interest to
determine if the Amphitheater had the potential to generate revenue which could help offset
other City special event costs and increase income.
Further discussion ensued regarding standard contract details, such as event insurance coverage,
inclement weather refund policies and the special event application process.
Mayor Udine questioned if the Commission could approve the contract subject to the City
Attorney's approval of the agreement.
City Attorney, Mr. Andrew Maurodis, responded that the Commission could approve the
resolution substantially to this form.
The Commission agreed to approve substantially to this form, subject to the approval of the City
Attorney and the Attorney for EXI.

FLORIDA, AMENDING CHAPTER 4 ENTITLED
"BUILDINGS AND BUILDING REGULATIONS";
AMENDING ARTICLE V ENTITLED "PROPERTY

FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE.
City Manager, Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Ordinance No. 2009 -26, Open Air Storage
and introduced Environmental Resources Director, Brian Archer to provide a brief presentation.
Mr. Archer explained the proposed Ordinance had been created in order to establish a process
that would adopt reasonable regulations relating to open air storage and to ensure that the storage
of loose items, such as, junk, debris or abandoned property would not be permitted to be stored
outside of businesses or residences within the City of Parkland.
The adoption of the Ordinance would assist City staff with hurricane preparation year round as
well as maintain the welfare of residents.
