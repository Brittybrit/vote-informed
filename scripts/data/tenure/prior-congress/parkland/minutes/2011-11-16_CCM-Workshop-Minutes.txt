1. DISCUSSION: ENTRANCE SIGNS
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young referred to Mr. Scott Davidoff, Community Development
Director for a brief presentation. See PowerPoint Presentation attached.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young asked for guidance from the City Commission.

Vice Mayor Weissman spoke about uniformed signage at all the entrances to the city and asked
what the approximate cost would be.

Mr. Davidoff explained that the cost depends on the type of signage. He stated that the sign
needs to have the ability of changeable text or a monument sign. He said that the sign company
can do whatever is desired.

Vice Mayor Weissman asked the reason for changeable text as opposed to a sign indicating
entrance into the City of Parkland.

Commissioner Rosenof explained that the Douglas High School coaches and band directors
expressed a desire to announce accomplishments of schools within the City.

Commissioner Weissman had a concern about having more things to celebrate than there was
room cnough to display on the monument sign. Ile reiterated that there should be uniformed
signage.

Commissioner Rosenof felt that University Drive would be the best location for the sign.
Commissioner Moskowitz commented that he understands what Commissioner Rosenof wants to
do but the real purpose of the sign is for recognition purposes. He felt that doubling the price in
order to go solar does not make sense therefore he suggested LED lighting so the bulbs would
never have to be replaced.

Vice Mayor Weissman stated that he is ready to move forward with just an entrance sign and stay
away from the message signs since there is access to the BSO digital signs.

Mayor Udine asked staff to look at maintenance to the entrance sign at 441 and Holmberg. He
commented that the digital sign is a perfectly good sign. but not for the Parkside Drive location.
He also commented that the BSO message board is becoming a problem. He feels if the sign
should go anywhere, it belongs at City Hall.

Further discussion ensued.

The City Commission decided that a signage program should be discussed further at the next
strategic planning workshop.

DISCUSSION: DISTRIBUTION OF 5K RUN MONIES
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young referred to Scott Davidoff for a discussion on how the
monies raised from the Sk run should be distributed. He then mentioned several possible
options.
Vice Mayor Weissman spoke about the two non-profit organizations that the City currently funds
and how the dollars can be leveraged. He suggested each non-profit organization receive $2,100
and to be presented at a City Commission meeting.
Commissioner Moskowitz stated that he would like to see the application process used in order
to assess the real need for the money.
Commissioner Rosenof agreed with the application process, but he would like to see some
criteria put in place.
Mayor Udine said he would like to see a few dollars set aside as seed money to help with next
year’s race so more money can be given away next year. He also likes the idea of having the
Area on Aging and Family Central as the only two non-profits that the City gives to. He would
also like to see some of those dollars used for Parkland schools. Hc would like the EAB to come
back with a recommendation as to where the $5,000 can best be used.
Mayor Udine felt it would be a mistake to go through the application process because there will
be more applications submitted than there is money to fund.
This item will be brought back before the City Commission on a future agenda after it goes to
EAB for their recommendation.

DISCUSSION: PODS IN SINGLE & MULTI-FAMILY DISTRICTS
Roberta Moore gave a brief presentation on pods in single-family and multi-family districts
which are currently prohibited. She explained that pods are permitted within community
facilities.
Commissioner Moskowitz felt that nothing should be done at this time. He stated that HOAs
already have rules regarding PODs.
Commissioner Rosenof stated that he is ok with leaving the ordinance on pods as is for now
since it is already prohibited, but feels there should be a time limit regulation and require a
permit.
It was decided that no changes would be made to the current code regarding pods and if jt
becomes an issue in the future, the City Commission will revisit it.
