1. DISCUSSION: LOCAL GOVERNMENT INVESTMENT POOL
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young recalled the Commission's request to discuss the City's
participation in the Local Government Investment Pool. Finance and Administrative Services
Director Barbara Hastings had outlined that participation and potential concerns in a memo
addressed to the Commission. Ms. Gardner -Young recommended that the City's position at this
point should be that the situation be closely monitored; it was felt too early to seek legal action.
For the benefit of the public, Mayor Udine gave a brief background of the issue.
Ms. Hastings offered further information about the investment pools, labeled Pool A and Pool B
the latter comprised of 14 percent of the total funds and deemed "under stress." The City has
been able to withdraw approximately $10 million without redemption fees, and these funds have
been reinvested in Treasury notes. To date, the City had $8 8 million in the pool, including $1.6
million "under stress." She also noted that, with the run on the pool in November, payment of
interest was suspended and instead reinvested in the pool to help alleviate the losses. According
to the law firm representing Pasco County, a lawsuit should deal with the loss of the interest since
November and the fact that funds cannot be withdrawn. A potential lawsuit in behalf of the pool

has been discussed by the governing board; they were unsure, however, as to who would be
liable. Ms. Hastings felt certain that, with the filing of a lawsuit, there would be a sure run on the
bank and the participants left would not have any money to withdraw. She recommended that the
City continue to daily monitor the situation. Funds were being withdrawn when available, and no
new deposits had been made. Since property taxes were distributed early in the year, she assured

Commissioner Smith commented that, having been in the investment world, he would rather pay a
two percent redemption fee to get something back than pay no fee and possibly get nothing.
Commissioner Weissman asked an estimate of the loss in earnings since October/November. Ms.
Hastings reviewed the City's two separate pool accounts, and she estimated that the City had lost
approximately $60,000 for the month of November only the only month of forfeited interest.
The Commissioner asked whether there was some intention to make up the funds lost. Ms.
Hastings indicated there was considerable discussion in the Florida League of Cities, which has
been attending all of the pool meetings. The League is arguing the fact that participants should
get back their November earnings.
Mayor Udine agreed that the League wanted to get those dollars back, but there has also been
discussion about the League starting their own pool with 100 percent guarantees.
Ms. Hastings noted that Holland Knight suggested that all elected officials put appropriate
pressure on the State Legislature to back the pool. Should that happen, lost earnings would be
recouped by the municipalities.
Commissioner Weissman questioned the risk to Pool A with total redemption of Pool B. Ms.
Hastings clarified that redemption of Pool B was not an option. There is no stress to Pool A, and
it had been recently rated by Moody's. Federated has taken over management, while the analysis
had been done by Blackrock.
Mayor Udine believed the question was whether the City should file a lawsuit for the $60,000 in
interest lost. He summarized the situation, and stated his belief that the City would get its money
back from both Pool A and eventually Pool B, with interest. With the tax revenues coming in, the
City was not hard pressed for cash. He recommended that Finance should continue to monitor
the situation and keep the Commission advised of issues.
Ms. Hastings confirmed that the City was not cash strapped; the City currently had $7 million,
plus $5 million in the operating account. Commissioner Weissman asked whether a need for cash
was anticipated during this fiscal year, and Ms. Hastings responded in the negative. With a
budget of $22 million, half -way through the year and spending at a 50 percent level, there was
more than $11 million in cash available.

City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Resolution No. 2008 -12.
Parks and Recreation Director Scott Davidoff commented that Phase III, as designed, consisted
of three softball fields above the T -ball wheel, the pavilion at the east end of the lake, and
additional parking. Craven Thompson had estimated a cost of $1.8 million, which was budgeted
by the City. However, the lowest bid from West Construction was $2 8 million $1 million
short. He believed the City must reevaluate its needs.
Mr. Davidoff explained that sports league participation had been reviewed. He noted that softball
numbers were down 45 percent and baseball numbers down 18 percent, and these were consistent
with national trends. Recreational and travel soccer, tackle football, flag football, and LaCrosse
all utilize the multi purpose fields; there were approximately 3,000 participants in those combined
programs, and the fields had few breaks.
Staff believed that the addition of a multi purpose field, which had been planned for Phase IV, as
well as a playground would cost approximately $2 million comprised of the $1.8 million
budgeted and a $200,000 Land and Water Conservation Fund Grant, which should be received
shortly. Mr. Davidoff reviewed the proposed locations in the park and the areas left for future
development. This Resolution would authorize staff to pursue the design change, as well as to
terminate the existing contract with West Construction.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked about parking that would be added, and Mr. Davidoff estimated
approximately 100 spaces. The Vice Mayor commented that there had been no intent that the
"temporary" lot would become permanent. While adding the field made sense, he believed,
especially with the Amphitheater, that more focus should be placed on adequate parking.
Mr. Davidoff noted that the field would not occupy the entire space of the temporary lot; a
number of spaces would still be available. He also offered that the future cell tower may lower
the cost of field lighting, thereby freeing some funds. He felt confident that, with the future
$400,000 grant, at least one Little League field as well as additional parking could be added.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked regarding the tennis facility planned for the park. Mr. Davidoff
offered that clay courts had been planned as part of Phase IV, and he confirmed that Phase IV
was currently not funded and that it may not happen for many years. The Vice Mayor noted that
the Terramar facility may also not see an infusion of money for quite some time.

that a revised copy of the Ordinance had been placed on the dais. While it contained the same
provisions as the original version, the City Attorney had reworded it differently to assure that the
City would be on its best footing for the Ordinance adoption.
Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer noted that the Ordinance would require
commercial establishments to recycle the same materials as residences. All commercial centers
have been notified, and he stated that some clarifications have been made to them regarding the
Ordinance intent. He noted that, with the exception of BJ's, all other commercial centers use
Waste Management for solid waste and cardboard; businesses would be able to use the cardboard
dumpster for single- stream recycling, which would eliminate the need of additional roll -offs. Mr.
Archer anticipated additional costs to the businesses, which Waste Management would handle.
Mr. Archer noted that State statute does not allow the City to require a commercial entity to use a
specific hauler for recyclables, and it had just happened that the businesses, with the exception of
BJ's, use Waste Management. No matter the hauler, businesses would be required to recycle the
same materials.

Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer recalled the recent workshop discussion. He
stated that the City could not presently enforce the repair or maintenance of a roof unless the
threat of storm was imminent, which was provided by the Florida Building Code. Contractors
and most property owners were well aware of this regulation and had been very cooperative.
With hurricane warnings in effect, this Ordinance would allow staff to become more proactive
about broken roof tiles, decay, gutters, and other debris or trash on the roof.
Commissioner Rosenof recalled that other building materials used during a renovation or
construction project had also been discussed at the workshop. Mr. Archer confirmed these were
covered by the Florida Building Code, and he noted that language pertaining to materials on the
ground, including barbecue grills and furniture, was also addressed. The Commissioner expressed
concern about construction materials that were placed temporarily.
Commissioner Weissman questioned that the Ordinance addressed year -round roof maintenance.
Mr. Archer indicated that the Ordinance had been somewhat designed for the storm season, but
was proactive in that the requirements were year -round and enforceable. The Commissioner
commented that homeowners' associations addressed most of these issues, but Mr. Archer noted
that the associations have been coming to the City for assistance with this.
Commissioner Weissman commented that a neighboring city was specifically addressing roof
pressure cleaning, and he asked if that should also be included in the City's roof maintenance
policy. Mr. Archer responded that most associations do address the pressure cleaning of roofs,
but to cover other areas without associations, such as Pinetree, some standard language could be
included for second reading to make the requirements City -wide.
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis noted that the liens may be unenforceable.
Commissioner Rosenof commented that a loose roof tile could damage his own home, but a dirty
roof could not. He felt that the Ordinance was meant to keep people safe and disagreed that a
mandatory City -wide roof cleaning should be included as City -wide. Mayor Udine agreed and
stated that he would support the Ordinance as written.
Mayor Udine opened to the public. There were no comments or questions, and he closed to the
public.

 VII COMMENTS BY THE CITY MANAGER
 In follow-up to the subject of code enforcement, City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young stated that
 she had discussed the process with Chief O'Connell and Mr. Archer, and she recommended that
 a change be made in the City's policy. She briefly reviewed the current process: with
 compliance before being heard by the Special Master, there was no charge to the violator.
 However, she proposed that once set for hearing before the Special Master - even with
 compliance-the City would seek some recovery of its costs, such as $25.
 Mayor Udine paraphrased the explanation and indicated that he was in support. There were
 affirmative responses from other Commission members.
