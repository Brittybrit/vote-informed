II PURPOSE:

1. PRESENT A TIONS BY LOBBYISTS FOR THE WEDGE PARCEL

Mayor Udine summarized that the 10-minute presentations would be made by the firms who had
submitted proposals or Letters of Interest to perform lobbying services for the City. He noted
that the meeting was being televised.

Kingsley Ross, representing Associated Advocates, made the first presentation and distributed a
handout. He introduced co-principal Michael Cusick, who was also present, and indicated a
support staff of three in Tallahassee. Mr. Ross noted that he and Mr. Cusick had over 40 years of
combined lobbying experience, and he gave a brief summary of their backgrounds and technical
legislative involvement. He believed their firm unique; they would provide highly personalized

services and would be immediately and fully accessible to the City's needs. He noted that any
need for additional help would be absorbed into their negotiated price.

Mr. Ross briefly outlined their approach. He offered that his initial research indicated that the
annexation would not be a local bill, but a general bill, and that it may require a referendum.

Bernie Friedman, representing Becker Poliakoff, made the second presentation. He stated that,
as partner-in-charge of the governmental practice group, he would be the City's day-to-day
liaison. They had already begun legal research of the issues and what was important to the City;
they had met with leaders one-on-one and had visited the "Wedge" and Hendrix Farm area.
Their analysis suggested that protection of the City, with the right representation, was imperative
during the annexation process, and he stated their loyalty would be to the City.

Mr. Friedman introduced other members of the team present, and he reviewed their respective
backgrounds and experience. Additionally, he spoke regarding their political connections,
believing they had the political muscle to get the job done in both local government and in
Tallahassee. He noted they would have no conflicts.

Mayor Udine stated, for the record, that attorney Neil Schiller, now with Becker Poliakoff, had
run the campaign of his opponent.

The third presenter, Dutko Poole McKinley, was not present at their scheduled time.

Jack Tobin made the presentation in behalf of his firm, Jack Tobin & Associates, and the
proposed partnership with Tew Cardenas in this matter. Mr. Tobin reviewed his legislative
experience, particularly with annexation, and noted that the existing Broward County laws on
annexation were as a result of his efforts. He noted that he had a local office, as well as an office
in Tallahassee.

Mr. Tobin acknowledged these were complex issues and very important to Parkland. He knew
the dynamics of the University Drive issue, as well as the positions of other cities. He would
have no conflicts. He spoke regarding his political connections in Broward and Palm Beach
counties, as well as in the House of Representatives, and knew the property owners. He believed
it important to be able to bring people to the table and make compromises.

Representing Tew Cardenas, Mary Lou Tighe spoke, indicating that Al Cardenas sent his regrets
that he had not been able to attend. In partnership with Jack Tobin & Associates, she believed
their team, the members of which she listed, would best represent the City's interests in
Tallahassee and in Broward and Palm Beach counties. She stated they had extensive experience
in legislative areas, as well as in local government, with expertise in advocacy and land use, and
would be able to move the City's legislative agenda through both sides of the aisle with their
team approach.

Mayor Udine noted that representation for Dutko Poole was still not present. With three
proposals on the table, he opened the floor to Commission questions.

Commissioner Smith asked regarding the political ties of Associated Advocates. Mr. Ross
indicated that, in Tallahassee, it would not be strictly a Republican issue and must be handled on
both sides of the aisle. Mr. Cusick stated that, while they had not been involved in local politics,
they had involvement in fundraising events in the area, particularly with the Crist campaign.

Vice Mayor McGilvray had no questions, and Commissioner Moskowitz indicated that he would
prefer to ask general questions of the candidates before the vote.

Mayor Udine commented that he had done considerable research and had ranked the firms in his
mind prior to the meeting: #1 was Dutko Poole, which was not present, and #2 was Becker
Poliakoff. He noted that he had personally worked on charitable issues with Bernie Friedman.
He believed it could not hurt to have 14 or 15 lawyers working on this in Tallahassee; they were
the most cost effective and included bi-partisan representation and legal acumen at their
fingertips.

Commissioner Moskowitz felt it important that the dynamics of the situation should be
understood. He believed the landowners would lead the charge, and the team hired by the City
would represent and protect its interests. He recalled that the "Wedge" had been anticipated as a
two-year issue, but he noted that the landowners were pushing for this legislative session. The
Commissioner suggested that the City's lobbyist must start immediately, and he personally
believed this the most important issue since the City had incorporated.

Commissioner Moskowitz specifically asked Becker Poliakoff regarding the need for additional
staff and associated costs. Mr. Friedman responded that the Becker Poliakoff fee was very
competitive; he acknowledged they were "hungry" to come into Parkland, that they liked the
issue, and that the fee would protect the City's interests.

City Manager Mark Lauzier commented regarding the RLI process. Stating that the City wanted
the best qualified firms, he noted that Mr. Rubin had withdrawn and that Dutko Poole had been
expected to make a presentation. He asked the City Attorney about the process to continue.

City Attorney Andrew Maurodis noted that each of the firms had been sent a letter regarding the
time of their presentations.

Commissioner Moskowitz expressed his understanding that this meeting was for presentations
only, and that the vote would be taken during the regular meeting.

Vice Mayor McGilvray asked whether that might be done. Mr. Maurodis replied that the vote
may be taken now or at the 7:00 p.m. meeting.

MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to vote at the 7:00 p.m. meeting.

Vice Mayor McGilvray questioned the legality of voting later. Mr. Maurodis responded that the
Commission's vote would be postponed. At the Vice Mayor's further question about a late

MOTION withdrawn by Commissioner Smith.

Javier Soto, representing Dutko Poole McKinley, apologized for his tardiness and explained that
1-75 had been at a standstill. Giving a short presentation, he indicated that the parent company,
Dutko, headquartered in Washington, D.C., was a federal governmental relations and public
policy firm that had branched out into state and local governments. In Florida, they were Dutko
Poole McKinley, representing a 14-year merger with a Tallahassee law firm, serving both public
and private clients. Partners Van Poole and Will McKinley were originally from Broward and
their Miami office had been opened last year. Mr. Soto spoke regarding his personal credentials
in Miami-Dade government and a session spent in Tallahassee. He thanked the Commission for
their time consideration and again apologized for his late arrival.

Commissioner Moskowitz asked the location of Messrs. Poole and McKinley, and Mr. Soto
responded they had obligations in Tallahassee with the election cycle.

Mayor Udine commented that the Commission had now heard four presentations, and he stood
by his original comments.

Commissioner Moskowitz suggested that it may be easier to first limit the selection to two firms
and then decide between the two.

Mayor Udine believed the Commission should try to move forward with a motion to see if the
three votes were there.

MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to hire Associated Advocates.

MOTION died for lack of a second.

Vice Mayor McGilvray commented that this decision was very difficult due to the various
relationships, and Mayor Udine agreed. He offered that he was not as worried about the
partisanship issue as he was about the "nuts and bolts." He again stated that he had done
considerable research and reiterated his first choice of Dutko Poole McKinley and second choice
of Becker Poliakoff.

Directing a question to Mr. Soto, Commissioner Moskowitz asked regarding allegiance. Mr.
Soto stated that the City would be their client in a contractual relationship and all allegiance
would be to the City. They would want a cooperative working relationship with the landowners
and would work toward building consensus in both Broward and Palm Beach delegations.
