II PURPOSE:
1. DISCUSSION: 2007-2008 CITY OF PARKLAND BUDGET
Mayor Udine noted. that this workshop would be the first opportunity the Commission had to
discuss and evaluate the budget as a group and that the Commissioners had previously met with
staff on an individual basis. He recalled that two meetings were held for public input. For the
record, he noted two members of the public present at this meeting and that these individuals had
also attended the public input meetings.
Mayor Udine proposed that the City Manager first give her presentation and that questions be
held until the end. This meeting was the Commission's chance to discuss the budget, and he
would not open the meeting to the public. There would be two future public hearings on the
budget as required by Florida law.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young thanked the Commission for the opportunity to present the
budget to them, and she thanked Barbara Hastings, Nancy Morando, and the Department
Directors for their hard worl~. She commented that, with the ongoing discussions about property
tax reform, it had seemed that every week had required different numbers.
Ms. Gardner-Young noted that the budget book had been placed on the City's website at the
beginning of July. She recalled that it had been distributed in preparation for the July 5th meeting
in order to discuss the millage as well as the fire assessment. Two budget meetings had been
held for public input: on July 31St, which was attended by three people, and on August 1St, which
was attended by two people. The public hearings for the budget would be held on September
14th and September 24th at 7:00 p.m.
Finance & Administrative Services Director Barbara Hastings reiterated appreciation of the hard
work of Department Directors and their staff to get the budget done on a timely basis. She made
a PowerPoint presentation concerning tax reform and the City's anticipated loss of $1.9 million
in revenue. She spoke regarding the millage rate and. the recently-mailed TRIM notices. With
respect to the "super exemption" vote in January, staff cannot determine how much the City
would lose in ad valorem revenues as it would depend upon each resident's election.
Ms. Gardner-Young commented regarding a $1.1 million reduction in revenue from the Building
Department due to the effects of the housing slump. However, there had been some increases:
more accurate ad valorem figures would result in an additional $763,000 and, with the adoption
of the fire assessment, an additional. $230,000 would be obtained. In preparation for the potential
revenue reductions, Ms. Gardner-Young reviewed what steps were underway. As an example, a
schedule of City fees would be presented at the September 19th Commission meeting, which
would include both revised and new fees not before implemented but allowed by statute, such as
those for false alarms. The potential for revenue from cell towers and grants was also reviewed.

Ms. Gardner-Young spoke of: the increases for fire and public safety services; cost containment
through realignment of the departments and outsourcing; reductions in employee COLA to two
percent and merits ranging from zero to three percent; reduction in the Library hours to 46 per
week; and an elimination of eight non-essential personnel positions.
Ms. Gardner-Young additionally spoke regarding the service enhancements of the Western Fire
Station and the anticipated opening of the Community Center and Amphitheater in December.
Budget and Strategic Planning Manager Nancy Morando made a PowerPoint presentation
concerning the net budget of $29.9 million. She reviewed revenue sources and major
expenditures.
Ms. Hastings spoke concerning the Capital Projects Fund. A $2.3 million fund balance was
projected for the upcoming budget year, which would be available for the future year of 2009.
Following that, there would be no fund balance and the City would need to rely on any transfers
from the General Fund.
Ms. Gardner-Young reviewed the planned and existing capital projects and the anticipated
expenditures.
Mayor Udine asked to revisit the map slide, and he noted that almost all of the capital projects
were on the east side of the City.
Ms. Morando continued with explanation of the departmental detail, including information about
the departments with reductions as well as increases and their reasons. She also spoke regarding
the resultant savings to homeowners as a result of the tax reductions.
Mayor Udine commented that, throughout the tax reduction debate, residents had wanted the
dollars spent on general government to be reduced but without losing the services provided. He
noted that all general government departments had reduced expenditures, with some drastically.
Mayor Udine asked regarding a slide that concerned crossing guards. Ms. Gardner-Young
clarified that, while there had been some confusion in assembling the material, there would be a
crossing guard at Riverglades. However, there were too many posts and not enough personnel,
and ads had been placed to recruit for that particular spot. As to CypressHead, she would leave
the decision up to the Commission, which she suggested could be discussed at the September
19t1i meeting. Again, she noted insufficient personnel to fill the post.
Mayor Udine acknowledged that the issue was different: the CypressHead crossing guard was
the only one not recommended for funding with City dollars since it was within the CypressHead
community and on private property. He noted that all of the crossing guards on public streets
would be funded, except for the one post at the closed street which would eventually be
reopened. The Mayor believed it important that crossing guards and school resource officers not
be cut. Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that surrounding cities were doing so, with which

Commissioner Smith asked that residents consider the question of super-exemption very
carefully, and he suggested visiting the Broward County Property Appraiser's website to
calculate what that super-exemption may mean to the individual homeowner. He cautioned that
there may be tax savings for the first few years, but then property taxes would go higher than
ever because there would be no three percent limit per year.
The Commissioner also commented regarding the $30 increase in the fire assessment fee, noting
that another city had raised its fee by $200 in order to keep their fire/rescue in place. He thanked
staff for accomplishing the same objectives at only $30 per home.
Commissioner Smith questioned spending nearly $400,000 on the repair of Ranch Road and
Trotters Lane. He asked confirmation that the City owned those roads and was responsible for
their maintenance, which Ms. Gardner-Young confirmed.
Commissioner Weissman recalled the original. discussion concerning the resurfacing of
Holmberg Road in phases. Since the area west of Riverside Drive was done not that many years
ago, he believed afive-year life was left on the existing road. He asked how much the City might
save by not resurfacing that area.
Public Services Director Jim Berkman estimated that approximately $200,000 would be saved by
elimination of that overlay area.
Mayor Udine questioned how long the City might hold off on the resurfacing of that area. Mr.
Berkman stated that, typically, overlays should take place in the 9- to 12-year range to avoid
long-term problems. He believed that 13 years would not make a huge difference, but he was
concerned that a habit of putting this type of work off could lead to reconstruction issues.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz recalled that the presentation regarding Holmberg had indicated that it
should be done within the next five years, which Mr. Berkman confirmed.
Commissioner Weissman also asked the thinking behind the recommendation for a motorcycle
officer. Ms. Gardner-Young offered that, in her discussions with the Chief, there were two areas
of major concern: burglaries and car break-ins. She noted that BSO had asked for a fourth patrol
zone for some time and, with the increase in population, she believed the City needed more
patrol officers.
Chief O'Connell added that they wanted an individual with expertise in traffic enforcement,
noting that the number one concern at homeowners' association meetings was traffic safety.
There is approximately a five percent cost difference between a motorcycle officer and a patrol
officer; however, a motorcycle officer would be dedicated to traffic enforcement -with work
efforts pinpointed to specific areas of concern.

Commissioner Weissman asked confirmation that $132,000 covered one 40-hour shift, with
which Chief O'Connell agreed. The Commissioner asked how one motorcycle officer could
cover the entire City all of the time. Chief O'Connell indicated that it had been their experience
that traffic issues diminished significantly on weekends; the position would probably be Monday
through Friday with some flexibility in the work schedule so that both timeframe and area could
be targeted for enforcement.
Commissioner Weissman noted that a vehicle had considerable mobile equipment, and he asked
if a motorcycle would be capable of carrying the same. Chief O'Connell indicated that a motor
cycle officer would not have the laptop and modem; they would be dispatched over the radio,
which is not uncommon. He believed a motorcycle gave more flexibility and visibility as it sent
the message that this deputy was dedicated to traffic enforcement.
Ms. Gardner-Young asked confirmation that the motorcycle officer could do traffic and more.
Chief O'Connell indicated that the deputy could also be assigned to a special project, such as the
investigation of the increase in car burglaries that had occurred in July.
Commissioner Weissman questioned the cost difference between the motorcycle deputy and one
in a vehicle. Chief O'Connell indicated the difference, called assignment pay, is about five
percent more for the motorcycle deputy and equipment.
At Ms. Gardner-Young's question of the "going rate," Chief O'Connell indicated that the price
given would include the salary and all of the benefits for the officer, as well as the motorcycle
and all of its equipment, such as stationary and moving radar.
Mayor Udine summarized that it would cost $126,000 for a patrol .officer, with which Chief
O'Connell agreed.
Commissioner Weissman asked if a vehicle officer would have the same traffic expertise, and
Chief O'Connell responded in the affirmative. The Commissioner felt that this should be a
decision of the City Manager. Ms. Gardner-Young felt that, if the same functions could be
performed by a patrol officer with $6,000 less, the decision was fairly easy.
Vice Mayor. Moskowitz noted that the City would have more equipment with a car than a
motorcycle.
Mayor Udine offered that, in previous years, a fourth patrol zone had been urged, which the City
did not wish to do at this time. The City's big issues appeared to be with traffic and, while he
would prefer the most cost effective, he would rely on the Chief s opinion that the motorcycle
officer would be of the most benefit.
Commissioner Weissman commented that he had noticed on State highways and other
jurisdictions that drivers responded to the "decoy" vehicles left parked in the medians. He asked
if BSO had such a motorcycle that could be moved around the City.

Mayor Udine recalled that Chief Carry had tried this smart law enforcement technique, but the
City had been "killed" on it. There had been hundreds of e-mail complaints, and the Mayor
suggested that it may have occurred too soon after the merger. Commissioner Weissman agreed
that it may have been timing.
Mayor Udine summarized that the Commission agreed with having another officer, but with
concern whether the expenditure of an additional $6,000 would be the most effective. Ms.
Gardner-Young indicated that she would work .with Chief O'Connell .and determine which way
to go. Commissioner Weissman indicated that he would prefer to have an officer with the same
expertise plus the vehicle for less, and Mayor Udine stated that he did not disagree.
Commissioner Weissman also commented that he had received his estimated tax bill, which had
shown a slight increase due to the $30 fire assessment increase, and he suggested that the
rollback in taxes had been offset. He noted that the City could save $200,000 by not resurfacing
a portion of Holmberg Road, and he believed that a fire rescue assessment increase would be
more appropriate next year.
Mayor Udine asked that the Commission be given recommendations concerning the Holmberg
Road project in order to save on the fire fee; he believed options should be considered. He
anticipated a major media blitz about cities setting fire fees in exchange for lowering taxes.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz commended Ms. Gardner-Young on .the amount of departmental
decreases. However, he wanted to be on the record that he would like to keep the crossing guard
in CypressHead.
The Vice Mayor questioned Chief O'Connell concerning the increase in car burglaries, and he
asked whether BSO might launch some sort of education program to raise awareness about
locking car doors and not leaving valuables in plain view. Chief O'Connell responded that,
beyond the homeowners' meetings attended and the articles written for various publications, he
had not explored other programs but would do so. He noted that cars were unlocked in 75
percent of the car burglaries in the City in 2006. The Vice Mayor felt that a serious educational
campaign should be conducted by BSO.
Mayor Udine hoped that BSO deputies would continue their contact with the vehicles at the
parks and the "night watch" cards. Chief O'Connell indicated that he and Ms. Gardner-Young
had discussed the purchase of electronic message boards that they have been borrowing from
other districts. He suggested their purchase with Law Enforcement Trust Funds, and one of the
messages could be about locking doors.
Commissioner Smith commented about the number of people leaving their garage doors open at
night, and he suggested that people should caution their neighbors about it. He felt this effort
would also help educate the City.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz noted there had been no discussion concerning adequate reserves should
another hurricane event occur.

Vice Mayor Moskowitz reviewed the Holmberg Road resurfacing project. He agreed with
Commissioner Weissman in that they should continue with the original plan: to resurface west to
Riverside Drive, to do the bike lanes from 441 to University, and curbing from Holmberg onto
Pinetree streets. He .stated that, if the dollars saved for not paving Holmberg Road west of
Riverside equate to the raise in the fire fee, he would then agree with Commissioner Weissman
and not want to raise the fire fee. He noted, however, that raising the fire fee was absolutely
justified in that there had been a 25 percent increase in the fire contract but less than a $3 per
month increase in the fees.
Mayor Udine thanked the City Manager and staff for their work on the budget in this extremely
complicated year. He believed the City would increase its services and yet would roll back the
taxes. He noted that the City's taxes were 18 percent of the total tax bill and yet the cities took
that greatest brunt of the tax hit. He spoke regarding the property valuations, believing that next
year's valuations would more accurately reflect the stagnant market. The Mayor also commented
that the City had lowered its millage rate late year, which was one of the reasons why the City
only had to reduce an additional five percent.
Mayor Udine asked concerning the rollback, and he wanted to make sure that the City would get
the full benefit of construction coming on the tax rolls. He asked specifically for the increase in
residential from last year to this year and the new properties added. Ms. Hastings indicated her
understanding of his request.
Mayor Udine commented about the sufficiency of meeting room space, believing it should not be
an issue for keeping the Library open another night. He suggested that the Library building
should have a second inner door lock so that the lobby, meeting room, and restroom areas could
be accessed in the event it was decided that the meeting space was needed. He believed there
would be sufficient meeting space once the City's new facilities came on line.
As to the Quigley acquisition, the Mayor believed .the City would be in a good position to
renegotiate the price since property values have fallen. He commended staff for the appropriate
handling.
Regarding Legal Services, Mayor Udine commented that the City Attorney had not requested any
adjustment, but he believed the expenses should be reexamined mid-year. While he was not
proposing a change, he believed Mr. Maurodis' fees low in comparison to those of other cities,
and he suggested examination. He acknowledged that the litigation budget was going up because
the City was trying to address issues that had been left unattended for years: code enforcement in
the Ranches and the resulting lawsuits, as well as the addition of the Special Master. He believed
there had been some improvement in the area.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that it was important to note that the increase in Legal.
Services had been spent in defense of a suit later dismissed. He also agreed with the Mayor's
suggestion that the Legal Services fee should be examined mid-year. He noted that Mr.
Maurodis' fees had not increased in a decade.

Mayor Udine commented that he had been glad to see the reevaluation of the Community Center
and Amphitheater furniture, fixture, and equipment and that he was happy that the fire hydrant
issue would be addressed in conjunction with the Holmberg Road project.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz hoped that Coral Springs would have a pump truck available for the City
in the event it was needed in the Pine Tree area. Fire Chief Mark Curran nodded his head in
confirmation. Ms. Gardner-Young believed that the pump truck was parked at the eastern station
and that capital funds had been earmarked.
Mayor Udine spoke of his concerns at State-level cuts, such as the elimination of funding for
D.A.R.E. and other such programs, and he noted that municipalities would be blamed for
something beyond their control. He commented that the super-exemption vote in January had
been a topic of discussion at the League of Cities. While the City had been able to adjust this
year, he anticipated that city budgets would be devastated should that measure pass.
The Mayor noted that $150,000 had been budgeted for Special Events. He acknowledged there
would not be a major concert in the coming year, but rather a concert series and a larger concert
in March in the new Amphitheater. He believed that the $50,000 expenditure for the one day of
"Parkland Days" should be examined: whether it is necessary, or whether it might be considered
for every other year. Mayor Udine noted that it was this type of program that, with the super-
exemption, the City would have a difficult time providing in future years.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz agreed. He hoped that the Amphitheater would generate some revenue
so that, while the City might eliminate the annual concert, there may be more events going on
there which would be open to residents. As to "Parkland Days," he agreed that it might be
considered for every other year, and he suggested that it be held off this year while waiting to see
what might happen in January.
Mayor Udine reiterated his concern about spending $50,000 for just one day, and he recalled that,
at the last concert, there had been considerable expense for the fireworks -which were being set
off as the rain came down and people were dashing to their cars.
Commissioner Weissman noted that, with the January vote, the City would have an even harder
time justifying the expense of "Parkland Days" if it were eliminated this year. He suggested that
the City might want to consider cutting back, perhaps without the parade and letting Kol Tikvah
or Mary Help of Christians use City property to hold a carnival on separate days. There would be
very little cost to the City. Should the City not be able to budget for "Parkland Days" next year,
at least something different would have been tried for this year.
Mayor Udine agreed that these ideas should be considered. Ms. Gardner-Young indicated that
.staff would look at different options, but she suggested that perhaps multiple events could be
held for the same money -- rather than just one day.
Acknowledging concerns about jobs in government, Mayor Udine commended the City's
employees. He believed the City had a quality staff and, unfortunately, they sometimes bear the

savings" in time for the September budget hearings. Despite the reduction in the millage from
3.95 to 3.4, it was really not reflected since the property values increased. He asked that these
facts be explained.
Mayor Udine agreed. He noted also that the City's portion was small in comparison to the
