City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Ordinance No. 2009 -30, Background Checks,
and introduced Parks & Recreation Director Scott Davidoff, who gave a brief presentation.
Parks & Recreation Director Davidoff reviewed the following amendments to city code proposed
in this Ordinance:
• Change minimum age for background checks from 16 to 18
• Make an exemption for active law enforcement officers, exempting them from criminal
background screenings
• Change the time frame that the background checks are valid to coincide with the sports
seasons, which run from July 1 through June 30 annually
• Require photo ID badges be part of the background screening
Commissioner Rosenof said he favors a majority of the changes, but he wants to pull the photo ID
badge component for further research to determine how other cities have handled the matter.
Mayor Udine agreed with Commissioner Rosenof and stated that the leagues feel the photo ID is
going to be expensive and a logistical problem. Mayor Udine also commented on the raising of the
minimum age for background checks, noting the particular significance this has for student
volunteers, and on the exemption for active law enforcement officers.
Commissioner Rosenof questioned whether the City would require active law enforcement to
provide a letter from their agency verifying a background check had been done and City Manager
Caryn Gardner -Young responded in the affirmative.

3. RESOLUTION NO. 2009-70: LOCAL BILL TO ANNEX CERTAIN
PROPERTY INTO THE CITY OF PARKLAND
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, SUPPORTING A LOCAL
BILL TO ANNEX CERTAIN PROPERTY INTO THE CITY
OF PARKLAND.
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Resolution No. 2009 -70, Local Bill to Annex
Certain Property into the City of Parkland, and introduced City Attorney Andrew Maurodis.
City Attorney Maurodis explained that the county boundary change provided for a complete
change of the boundary and resulted in all of the property being in Broward County, but only two
parcels are now in the City of Parkland. He stated that this resolution is in support of an
annexation of four properties: the Palm Beach Farms property, the Bruschi property, the Hendrix
property, and the WCI property.
City Attorney Maurodis said he has been in contact with the attorney for the legislative delegation,
who is confident that, since the bill is being done with separate parcels, as things progress and if
appropriate annexation agreements can not be worked out, a particular parcel could then be
withdrawn. City Attorney Maurodis confirmed the deadline to submit a bill is tomorrow, and
suggested moving forward with the four parcels and working with the landowners' attorneys on
annexation agreements.
Mayor Udine mentioned a letter received from Mr. Coker, who represents Mr. Hendrix, and said it
looks like they will move forward in a positive manner. Mayor Udine added that he feels this is a
positive step; Mr. Hendrix is an honorable person and comes to the table as such. Mayor Udine
said Mr. Hendrix wants to continue to farm, but realizes the City has certain needs that fall to his
property, such as the eventual need for County Line Road to go through there when the farming
operations are done. Mayor Udine stated that Mr. Hendrix also recognizes that there are cost
recovery issues, in particular for attorney's fees for drafting pre - annexation agreements.
Commissioner Moskowitz commented that the City has been consistent with the major landowners
insofar as everyone has to contribute and added he is pleased that they have decided to do cost
recovery, which is consistent with what has been going on in the Wedge.

6. DISCUSSION: BID PROTOCOLS
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young said this item came from a previous Commission request that
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis present a procedure for bids.
City Attorney Maurodis explained the procedure and said it mirrors what the Commission has done
previously; it provides for a scoring with a fail safe provision to avoid low balling and a procedure
in the case of a tie. He added that it is easy to understand, is a good concept, and can be relied
upon.
Mayor Udine commented that, on the bids that this is applicable to, staff will have the scoring
sheets based on this policy.
City Attorney Maurodis noted that the score sheet can be included in the backup so that the
Commission can look at it ahead of time.
Commissioner Rosenof said he has two problems with this; first, there should be a mechanism that
if the Commission wants to accept staff's ranking, they should be able to do that without going
through the scoring process.
Mayor Udine stated that a provision could be added allowing this unless state law requires
Commission scoring.
Commissioner Rosenof said the other problem he has is that three votes automatically prevails; it
does not require a compromise and can marginalize the two no votes.
There was discussion about the scoring and voting process in the past and as outlined in the
procedure.
Commissioner Rosenof said he does not see the need for having the automatic win and feels that
the top vote getter should receive the award.
