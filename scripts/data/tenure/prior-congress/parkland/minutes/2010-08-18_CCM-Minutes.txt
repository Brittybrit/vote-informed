II PUBLIC INPUT

Mayor Udine opened to the Public.

Marie Gosser, 5893 NW 63rd Way in Pine Tree Estates, commented about the Coyotes eating their
animals and pets. She asked the City to help resolve the problem.

Mayor Udine set the record straight. He explained there were two reported incidents that confirmed
the coyotes had attacked. Other incidents occurred with animals, but was not confirmed that it was
related to the coyotes. Staff called the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission to ask
how to best handle these types of issues.

The Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission suggested it was best to first educate the
residents about how to deal with the coyotes. They did not recommend trapping be done. Recently,
the coyotes have become more brazen, therefore the City hired a trapper.

City Manager, Caryn Gardner-Young confirmed that 20 to 30 traps would be placed in the Pinetree,
Ranches and Cypresshead areas and a daily report would be given by the trapper of his progress.

Leo Gosser, 5893 NW 63rd Way in Pine Tree Estates, congratulated the City for hiring a trapper. He
asked if the trapper’s contract could be placed on the website or receive a daily update on the
trappers activities.

Don Anthony, Communications Director for the Animal Rights Foundation of Florida commented
that killing the coyotes will not end the problem. Residents must be told not to panic and be
responsible pet guardians.

Bill Reicherter, Jr. lives in the Ranches, disagrees with trapping the coyotes. He believes they should
be killed.

Mayor Udine expressed that the City is not allowed to humanely move coyotes.

City Manager, Caryn Gardner-Young stated staff will provide updates, but will not provide locations
of the traps so not to compromise the trapping efforts.

Cindy Navarro, resident in the Ranches for 25 years, has lived with the coyotes, but feels it has
gotten out of hand. She also asked if signs could be posted for missing animals.

Commissioner Moskowitz advised her to get with Stuart Krantz who sends email blasts to roughly
6,000 people announcing missing animals.

Jean Katz, resident in Pine Tree Estates asked if the property owners cleared off their land, would
that deter the coyotes and is it a requirement for property owners to keep their properties cleared.
Mayor Udine commented that residents get cited for violations, but it is difficult to enforce. He
reiterated that educating the residents is as important as the trapping.

LEGISLATIVE UPDATE: SENATOR JEREMY RING
Mayor Udine introduced State Senator Jeremy Ring, who gave a legislative session update.
Senator Ring commented on the following topics:

eo State Budget — with no stimulus money, early estimates show the state budget at 6 billion
dollars short this year. He explained about TABOR and how it affects the City. He
expects it to be placed on the ballot and warned that all cities need to plan for it.

o The second most significant issue is the state and local pensions. As the chair of
government operations in the state senate, he expects to deal with this issue directly.

e He would like to see cities take on more influence from the counties especially as it relates to
zoning issues. He wants the Cities to have the responsibility, but have more of the influence
to go with that responsibility. He plans to attend the League of Cities meeting to start talking
about this issue.

Mayor Udine commented that the millage rate was not raised and property values are starting to turn
around. He also commented about the bill passed giving a zero valuation to homeowners who had
Chinese Drywall, but understands and supports the justification.

Mayor Udine believes we have to find other revenue streams in the State of Florida where tourism
and growth seem to be so cyclical that when both take a down turn, we see the effects. He supports
Senator Ring on his efforts and maintains a good relationship with the Senator’s office.
Commissioner Moskowitz agrees with Senator Ring on the issue of the City having more influence

6. RESOLUTION NO. 2010-69: AGREEMENT WITH CORAL SPRINGS
FOR FLEET MAINTENANCE & REPAIR
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE

CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, AUTHORIZING THE
APPROPRIATE CITY OFFICIALS TO ENTER INTO AN
INTERLOCAL AGREEMENT WITH THE CITY OF CORAL
SPRINGS TO PROVIDE FLEET MAINTENANCE AND
REPAIR SERVICES ON AN ONGOING BASIS, PROVIDING
AN EFFECTIVE DATE.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2010-69: Agreement with Coral

Springs for Fleet Maintenance & Repair.

Jim Berkman, Public Works Director explained that fleet includes our vehicles as well equipment,

backhoes, trailers, etc. This was necessary because our City is not big enough to have its own

mechanics or its own garage yet we have fleet that needs maintenance and repair. Currently, we

bring the equipment to different places for repair which is not very efficient and quite time

consuming in using staff time for transport. To be more efficient, the City of Coral Springs has a

fleet division and is capable of doing all the work for all the vehicles and equipment in one location.

The down time of staff is minimal due to the close location of their facility and the hourly repair rate

is much less. It is a non-exclusive contract and the City is not required to take it to Coral Springs.

Mayor Udine asked if we had used either Margate or Coconut Creek in the past and what were their

costs.

Jim explained that Margate did approach us years ago and then backed out about doing the work for

us. Jim detailed the location of the repair facilities in the other municipalities and explained it’s more

cost effective to keep it close by.

Commissioner Weissman expressed, since the contract is non-exclusive, he would like staff to check

with other cities to make sure the City is getting the lowest cost possible for repairs.

MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to approve Resolution 2010-69. Seconded by Vice

Mayor Smith.
