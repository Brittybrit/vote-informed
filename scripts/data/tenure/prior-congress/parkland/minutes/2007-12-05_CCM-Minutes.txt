III PUBLIC INPUT
Former Mayor Harold Bockhold spoke regarding the Historical Society proposal to bury a time
capsule - to be opened in 50 years -- with remembrances about the founding of the City and its
evolution as part of next year's Parkland Days celebration. They will have meetings to decide its
contents, and he hoped for the input of the Commission.
Ranches resident Mike Squillace spoke regarding -the bridge in front of his property, which he
believed had been damaged by the City while compacting the roadway in 2006. He noted that the
problems of neighbors had quickly been addressed by the City, and he felt that the City's failure to
resolve his issue was in retaliation for the positions he had taken on other matters.
Harvey Krupp, 6191 N.W. 98t'' Drive, spoke in support of senior citizens and the 55+ Club. He
commented regarding the recent Sun-Sentinel article concerning the City's use- of CDBG funds for
senior programs. He believed the City's seniors needed these services, and he hoped the City would
continue to provide them.
Marie Gosser of Pinetree Estates also spoke regarding the senior program. She complimented the
City for its attention to seniors and criticized the Sun-Sentinel reporter.
Dr. Leo Gosser also complimented the Commission for its fiscal responsibility and its support of the
grant applications; he hoped the City would continue to seek as many as possible. He also extended
kudos on the presentation of the City's news magazine, which he believed a significant
improvement over the newsletters previously distributed.
Aston Gardens resident Joan Farb stated that she had been greatly disappointed at the attitude of the
Sun-Sentinel reporter. She spoke regarding the limited income and budget. of seniors, who
appreciate the opportunities the grants have extended, and she encouraged the Commission's
continuing support.
Alex Maldonado of Sable Pass cited statistics regarding children hit by cars in North America,
which she blamed on speeding cars and the lack of neighborhood speed bumps. She encouraged
their installation.
Ira Goldman spoke regarding the traffic signal at State Road 7 and Hillsboro Boulevard and,
specifically, the northbound left-turn signal from 441 to west on Hillsboro. He anticipated more
problems at that intersection with the opening of the new Wal-Mart, and he hoped the City would
talk to the County about its operation. He also noted that it was also very dark traveling west on
Hillsboro from 441.
Terry Wedzik, Parkland Isles resident and a member of the 55+ Club, believed the Sun-Sentinel
reporter was uninformed. She also spoke regarding taxes for fire and police, believing it unfair that
small homes pay the same as the large ones.

The Commissioner hoped the Commission would support an RFP to at least give the City an idea as

Commissioner Smith commented that, during his time on the Commission, he had not received any
complaints from constituents about trash pickup., While he did not disagree with Commissioner
Weissman's comments regarding the limitation of the 96-gallon container, he felt that the week's
second collection could accommodate overflow.
Responding to Commissioner Weissman's comments, Mayor Udine noted that. increases in the
previous franchise agreement were tied to the Consumer Price Index and had kept prices down. He
compared the cost of gasoline in 1996 versus that of today and noted that all costs, including that of
labor and insurance, had also increased significantly since then. He commented that the City would
benefit because franchise fee receipts would increase; however, while he was not happy about the
cost increase to residents, he believed it a fact of life.. In his opinion, there were only two companies
capable of doing Parkland's garbage collection -- Waste Management and All Service, and he
believed that both companies' costs were also going through the roof.
Mayor Udine acknowledged that the Waste Management agreement was a change in service and
that some residents may have a problem with this. He offered that many of the residents he had
spoken with are excited about single-stream recycling and are anxious to start. He noted that he had
been driving around checking out garbage cans, and he estimated that 97 or 98 percent of residents
would not have an issue. He expected that one 96-gallon for trash and a 64-gallon for recyclables
would suffice for most people, except on rare occasions. The Mayor noted that Mr. Spadaccia had
indicated that the City could have left the garbage collection as it exists, changing only to single-
stream recycling. However, it was felt time for the City to move into the future and that Waste
Management was the Company to do this.
Briefly addressing costs, Mayor Udine agreed that $1 a week may be a lot of money to many people,
but the cost structure had increased significantly. Unfortunately, the City must consider this new
method of providing the service.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the second reading of Ordinance No, 2007-26. She
commented that this Ordinance would fill the "hole" in the City's previous ordinance, and she noted
that Chief O'Connell was present to testify regarding the impacts.
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis briefly reviewed the intent and findings, and he questioned Chief
O'Connell. Chief O'Connell indicated that he had 30 years' experience in law enforcement in
Broward County, with three of those years working Internet crimes against children. He testified
that, based upon his experience, the recidivism rate for sexual offenders and sexual predators was
higher than with other crimes, and it was his opinion that the measures taken in this Ordinance were
a reasonable way of trying to lessen the danger to children. The Chief recommended adoption of the
Ordinance and concurred with the findings of fact.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz acknowledged the work of Christine Hunschofsky, and he thanked her and
the members of the Education Advisory Board.
