REGULAR AGENDA
1. PRESENTATION: FLORIDA TURNPIKE ENTERPRISE
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young indicated that Florida Turnpike Enterprise staff had
requested the opportunity to make a presentation regarding the proposed installation of a sign on
University Drive.
Dale Cody of Metric Engineering, principal in charge, spoke regarding their project to install.
digital changeable message signs on the arterials leading onto the Sawgrass Expressway. His
PowerPoint presentation gave a general overview of the project, including its impacts, benefits,
and the timeframe for construction of the signs proposed within the City. The scheduled
completion of the overall project was anticipated for Summer 2008. From a public safety
standpoint, the signs could potentially be used for traffic rerouting due to accidents, Amber
Alerts, and hurricane evacuation.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz questioned use of the signs for Amber Alerts, and he asked the specific
location of the signs. Ms. Gardner-Young stated that the only sign within Parkland's jurisdiction
would be located on University Drive. The signs located at 441 and at Nob Hill would be in
neighboring cities. Mr. Cody indicated that operations were the responsibility of the Turnpike
Enterprise and stated the sign would be located outside Aston Gardens.
The Vice Mayor asked the City's authority: whether the City might either turn down the request
or elect the fiber-optic version for the smaller pole. Ms. Gardner-Young responded that the City
had regulations that must be abided; she was unsure that the City could veto the Turnpike
Enterprise's. decision, but there were certainly aesthetics that they could be made to follow.
Mr. Cody assured that the shorter pole would be utilized. Their presentation had been an
opportunity to present both plans.
Sonyha Rodriguez-Miller and Juan Kuthy, ITS Construction Manager, were present for the
Turnpike Enterprise.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked whether the three signs -those on University, 441, and Nob Hill -
would also be used for other information, such as the major alert that occurred on this date with
the school lockdown and massive manhunt. Mr. Kuthy responded affirmatively, indicating that
there would be interagency communications.
Ms. Gardner-Young stated that she had received a phone call from The Landings, and there was
resident concern that the sign would flash at night and impact their area.
Mayor Udine commented that he had also received some phone calls, and he believed this was a
different issue. Comments had been made about the sign actually placed on the Sawgrass. He
understood that there may have been engineering requirements, but the suggestion had been that
the sign would have been better placed adjacent to the school rather than to the community.

Ms. Gardner-Young offered that the sign had already been designed; its location was fixed
specifically for efficiency and to safely notify drivers to exit. She acknowledged that staff had
tried to have it relocated. She explained that, as a result, she was very sensitive to the proposed
sign -its location, purpose, and size -and there would be no exceptions to the City's standards.
Mr. Cody stated that the intent was that motorists would be given enough notification so that the
option to exit could be safely taken. If signs were placed on the overpass as had been suggested,
the decision to enter onto the ramp had already been made. He also commented that it would be
a much smaller sign and that luminescence was typically reduced in the evenings. He noted that
the sign would be roughly the same height as a traffic signal head.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz again asked whether the City had the ability to say "no." Ms. Gardner-
Young responded with "probably no," but she felt strongly that, from an aesthetic perspective,
the City should have some say and that the communities most strongly affected, such as Aston
Gardens, Mary Help of .Christians, and The Landings, should have some communication.
The Vice Mayor felt that some better look for the pole should be designed. Mr. Cody responded
that, at this point, the structure had not been finalized. He indicated that they had worked with
other cities and had received their input. He stated that the design/build team would be willing to
listen and consider the City's ideas.
Ms. Gardner-Young asked that the City be furnished what had been done to accommodate other
cities for their ideas on what could be proposed. While Mr. Cody agreed to do so, he offered that
the only request they had to date involving the structure was to paint the arm black. There had
been no other requests relating to design of the structure.
Mayor Udine expressed concern that the location was already complicated to motorists with the
Mary Help of Christians' school zone iinlnediately there at the exit. While he believed the sign
would be good on many levels, he asked that some consideration be given to incorporating the
school zone sign.
With the understanding that the sign would be installed, Vice Mayor Moskowitz summarized that
the City Manager was being directed to work with the necessary parties to come up with a
solution agreeable to all parties.

3. DISCUSSION: VICE MAYOR MOSKOWITZ'S
CITY OF PARKLAND ENERGY TAX REFUND
AWARDS PROGRAM
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young briefly introduced the item, which was for discussion
purposes only. She offered that an ordinance to implement the program would later be drafted.
Environmental Services Director Brian Archer gave a brief presentation concerning the program,
which would reward residents for taking extra steps to be more environmentally responsible.
The program's total cost was estimated at $104,000.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz thanked Mr. Archer, Mr. Maurodis, and Ms. Gardner-Young for their
input and ideas in this team effort. He believed the City had been fiscally responsible for the past
two years and the Commission had recently taken steps to become more environmentally
responsible through its waste and recycling programs. He personally felt that the City could go
even further, which had led to the development of this program.
Mayor Udine summarized that nothing would be .adopted at this meeting; the Commission would
give direction to staff to prepare specific ordinances.
Commissioner Weissman. stated that, while he was fully supportive of the ideas, he had some
questions regarding costs. Mr. Archer offered that some of the programs would require no
inspections; while there would be no cost for staff inspections, Building Department fees would
be incurred by the residents. The Commissioner suggested that use of energy-efficient light
bulbs and low-flow shower heads could easily be modified after an initial inspection, and he
asked whether a second random inspection during the first year had been considered.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz indicated that he and Mr. Archer had discussed possible loopholes which
could theoretically happen. He believed the program must be administered in good faith and
noted that Mr. Archer had other responsibilities as well. He suggested that, upon inspection, Mr.
Archer could keep the proof of purchase receipts, and he invited the consideration of other ideas
during the formulation of the ordinance. The Vice Mayor offered that the idea was that people be
motivated to install these energy-saving devices.
Commissioner Weissman asked whether owners of rental properties, including large complexes
such as Winner's Circle, would also be eligible. Vice Mayor Moskowitz stated that he viewed
the funds as tax dollars; renters did not pay into the tax system and owners of the .units did. He
believed that the program must be uniformly administered, and he provided further explanation
as to the one-time only credits.
Commissioner Weissman suggested there may be questions regarding the registration of hybrid
vehicles and whether purchased by a resident or a business. The Vice Mayor indicated that this
issue must be investigated further, and Mr. Maurodis assured that the best thinking would be
presented.
