COMMENTS: FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND,
FLORIDA, PROHIBITING THE ACT OF TEXTING
WHILE DRIVING; PROVIDING FOR
IMPLEMENTATION OF THE PROHIBITION; PROVING
FOR A PENALTY; PROVIDING FOR A SEVERABILITY
CLAUSE; PROVIDING FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE.
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Ordinance No. 2009 -43, Banning Text
Messaging While Driving, and said this item was requested by Commissioner Moskowitz.
Commissioner Moskowitz gave a brief explanation and requested that the following steps be
taken:
• Marjory Stoneman Douglas High School students give a presentation on what they have
learned and why this is so important
• The Commission passes the Ordinance on first reading
• The Commission passes the Ordinance on second reading
• City Attorney Andrew Maurodis asks for an Attorney General's opinion on the adopted
Ordinance
Angela Lane, Legislative Assistant to State Senator Frederica Wilson, read a statement on
Senator Wilson's behalf regarding texting while driving.
Mayor Udine recognized the students.

<<PG>>

Michael Ross, Joshua Feinzieg, Justin Levy, Danielle Levy, Adam Feinzeig, and Max Fegelman
each spoke about texting while driving, and presented a video depicting reaction times as well as
a video public service announcement filmed and produced in Wales.
Commissioner Moskowitz asked that the students provide their contact information to City staff
and said he would like to see them give their presentation at hearings to be possibly held in
Tallahassee. Commissioner Moskowitz noted that there were two changes he would like to be
made to the Ordinance: 1) change the terminology to "motor vehicles" so that motorcycles are
included, and 2) change the fine from $250 to $100.
MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to approve Ordinance No. 2009 -43, Banning Text
Messaging While Driving, as amended, on first reading. Seconded by Vice Mayor Weissman.
Commissioner Rosenof commented about enforcement and questioned how BSO could
differentiate between this and any other distraction while driving. He noted that there are other
Ordinances in effect that deal with distractions while driving and said regardless of the outcome
of this Ordinance, BSO should keep enforcement up against distracted drivers no matter what the
cause of the distraction.
Commissioner Smith said the presentation was very informative and thanked all the presenters.
Mayor Udine requested that the presentation be put on the City's website.
Commissioner Moskowitz said he would like to find out how other municipalities or states are
carrying out enforcement and added that it does not need to be addressed until receipt of the
Attorney General's opinion.
Vice Mayor Weissman said he understands enforcement concerns, but BSO Chief O'Connell
could instruct his deputies to give warnings until such time as enforcement issues are resolved.
Mayor Udine commented that this is a great example of how local government works and he
hopes the students spread the word to their friends.
Mayor Udine opened to the public.
David Gobio, 7624 N.W. 70 Way, Cypress Cay, spoke about enforcement and said negligent
driving is the real issue.
There were no further comments or questions and the Mayor closed to the public.
Commissioner Moskowitz suggested that the Ordinance could be changed, based on some of the
comments made, prior to it coming back for second reading. He reiterated that if 17 states are
actively enforcing it, there must be some sort of protocol that they are using.

3. RESOLUTION NO. 2009-56: WOODHOUSE DAY SPA CAB
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, FOR COMMUNITY
APPEARANCE BOARD APPROVAL FOR
MODIFICATION TO THE MASTER SIGN PROGRAM
FOR THE BAKER AND ZIMMERMAN OFFICE
BUILDING LOCATED AT 6991 N. STATE ROAD 7,
CASE NUMBER 12- CAB -09.
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Resolution No. 2009 -56, Woodhouse Day
Spa CAB, and gave a presentation. Her comments included the following:
• One sign is proposed to be located on the south side of the street, fronting Hillsboro and
the second on the east side of the building fronting State Road 7.
• Both signs would state "The Woodhouse Day Spa ".
• Staff is recommending denial of both signs based upon the 2002 Commission site plan
approval.
• The Planning and Zoning Board approved the petition as long as the proposed signage
meets the City code requirements. The south signage would not meet the sign code
requirements as it relates to the location, since signage needs to be located where there is
pedestrian access to the tenant's space.
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young said that this is a quasi judicial hearing and asked if the
petitioner would waive the procedural aspects of the quasi-judicial hearing.
Susan Richards, owner of the Woodhouse Day Spa, waived her quasi-judicial rights, and
provided background information and the rationale for her request. Ms. Richards commented
that one of the most important aspects of any business staying viable in this economic timeframe
is the ability for it to be visible. She asked the Commission to reconsider the recommendations
that have been put forth and said they are based on a vision of a building in 2002.
Robert Baker, partner in Baker and Zimmerman, spoke about the present state of the economy
and in support of the signage.
Mayor Udine opened to the public, there were no comments or questions, and the Mayor closed
to the public.
Vice Mayor Weissman said that he represents the 2,000 people that live in the area and these
residents do not want the Fifth Avenue look at the entranceway to their homes. He stated that
there is plenty of signage on the building now to identify it and Woodhouse Spa can advertise
that they are in the Baker and Zimmerman building; no one would miss it.

<<PG>>

Commissioner Rosenof commented that he served on the Planning & Zoning Board when the site
plan was approved and he was one of two dissenting votes; however, he is not against approving
this request in order to be consistent with the strip center across the street where each store has its
own sign. Commissioner Rosenof spoke about his concern if the first floor was to be subdivided
and additional signage was then desired.
Commissioner Moskowitz said this is an example of a larger issue and he is not going to support
it because the City has consistently turned down shopping centers who have asked for monument
signs or additional signage. He stated that if the Commission is going to change its direction on
signage, then they should approve this request; but if they are not, then the Commission should
remain consistent and deny the request. Commissioner Moskowitz added that if the Commission
is now going to reexamine all signage as a result of the economy, he is okay with that, but this
should be a broader policy decision and not just one variance.
Commissioner Rosenof noted an additional sign was added at Parkside Drive, so approval of this
would not be without precedence and he added that the discussion is not about monument signs,
which he would oppose.
Commissioner Smith said both Commissioner Moskowitz and Commissioner Rosenof make
valid points, but he feels the best advertising the spa could do is advertise the fact they are on the
ground floor of the Baker and Zimmerman building, because it is a landmark on State Road 441.
Mayor Udine commented that he would like to accommodate the spa in some way to give them a
level of visibility, but he does not favor the sign proposed for the Hillsboro side. Mayor Udine
questioned the possibility of Baker and Zimmerman removing one of their signs and replacing it
with a Woodhouse Day Spa sign.
Vice Mayor Weissman said he would not object to switching out the signs as long as the letters
of the spa sign are no larger than the Baker and Zimmerman sign.
Robert Baker, owner of the property, said this would penalize him and spoke in support of Ms.
Richards. Ms. Richards further explained her spa's need for visibility.
Commissioner Moskowitz reiterated the need to be consistent and said there are a lot of
businesses that are struggling. He added that to be fair, what is done for one should be done for
all.
Ken Zimmerman spoke in support of one spa sign and Mr. Baker agreed there are limits to the
amount of acceptable signage.
Further discussion was then held by the Commission.

Mayor Udine said this item is a very key part of the City's fire situation; it could be that the
solution is a northwest fire department. He stated that if there are costs involved, the
Commission needs to be cognizant moving forward that they do not want to bear too big of a
burden.
Vice Mayor Weissman reiterated that Parkland would be paying 25% of the costs involved in the
study unless at some point in the future the Commission agrees to terminate the agreement.
Commissioner Smith said the cost sharing should be based upon the population of each city.
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young clarified how the arrangement would work and said the
cities' staffs are going to do the majority of work, so consulting fees would not be incurred at the
beginning of the process.
There was additional discussion regarding the agreement and cost issue, and City Manager Caryn
Gardner -Young requested direction on a cost amount the Commission is willing to pay.
Mayor Udine said that the problem stems from paragraph #4 in the agreement, which divides the
costs equally. He stated that the Commission could give its approval with paragraph #4 stricken;
the city managers could work the costs out among themselves, with City Manager Caryn
Gardner -Young advising the other cities the cost sharing should be based on population.
Commissioner Moskowitz withdrew his original motions.
MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to approve Resolution No. 2009 -64,
Memorandum of Understanding — Fire Services Study Agreement, with an amendment deleting
paragraph #4 in the agreement and with a maximum initial exposure of $1,000. Seconded by
