Senator Jeremy Ring offered that he had sponsored a bill for mandatory background checks for
sports coaches for the past two years, and he pledged a third year. He commented that they had
found an inexpensive way to accomplish them, and he offered to talk with the City about it.
Senator Ring provided an update of the past year and related his expectations of the coming year:
The budget had been cut $5 billion last year, and he anticipated as much as $3 billion this
year. Cuts in services would be beyond those "nice to have." Creating new sources of
revenue and raising fees would become a necessity.
Indications were that property taxes would again be an issue. While he had supported
past Senate legislation on property taxes, he now believed that the State and cities could
not make more cuts.
Anticipated that another catastrophic natural event would increase property insurances
by a potential 10 percent per policy per year for 30 years and increase sales taxes. In the
absence of a national catastrophic fund, the State was on its own. He expected that rates
could only be controlled through the State's acceptance of risk through reinsurance. The
choices were higher premiums, lower risk or lower premiums, higher risk, and he
supported the latter. He was working on a bill to "tweak" the Florida catastrophic fund,
and he believed the risk must be taken from the State.
Focus attention and invest in innovation for future economic development growth.
Will focus on insurance and economic development, and he believed the big issues were
the budget, insurance, and property tax. Other areas would be children's vaccinations, a
parents' bill of rights, background checks on sports coaches, some condo legislation,
stopping the use of computer spyware, and stopping diploma mills
Commissioner Weissman asked about bringing technology to the State, and how to get a first
class educational system to attract high technology. While Senator Ring believed the State's
public schools must be world class, he noted that industries and economies were built through
universities. There must be focus on the university aspect and building business; children must be
given something to strive for. He spoke regarding venture capital and attracting talent.
Mayor Udine believed that the bill regarding investment of pension dollars was one of the most
innovative things to come out of the State Legislature for a long time. He would like to see more
of a pursuit in that area. Senator Ring believed Tallahassee was ready for creative answers.

Unlimited. The pumpkin sales would take place from October 20 to October 31 and the

Mayor Udine recalled that the events had been approved last year, with some concern as to

of the tent. The Mayor remembered that the tent had been used only for the Christmas tree sales.

there be an impending storm. Ms. Gardner -Young gave confirmation, and Ms. Moore added that
enforcement of this provision would occur through the Building Department, including the right
for cost recovery should the City require its removal. Commissioner Weissman asked whether a
bond would be posted.
Mayor Udine suggested a $1,000 bond to cover the tent removal, and he asked that the permit
could earmark those funds for the band should it not be utilized. He believed it should be so
structured so as to maximize the band profits.
