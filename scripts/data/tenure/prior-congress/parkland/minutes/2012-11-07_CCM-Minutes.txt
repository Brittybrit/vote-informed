1. ORDINANCE NO. 2012-22: PARKLAND RESERVE POD C
LAND USE AMENDMENT
COMMENTS: FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA,
AMENDING THE FUTURE LAND USE MAP OF THE CITY OF
PARKLAND, AS DESIGNATED IN EXHIBIT “A” CONSISTING OF
9.355 ACRES FROM COMMERCIAL (BROWARD COUNTY) TO
PARKLAND RESIDENTIAL 3 DU/AC (9.355) CONTAINING A
SEVERABILITY CLAUSE; CONTAINING AN EFFECTIVE DATE;
CASE 01-LUPA-12.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Ordinance 2012-22 on first reading: Parkland Reserve
Pod C Land Use Amendment. Ms. Gardner-Young introduced Michelle Melgren to make City staffs
presentation. Ms. Melgren noted that a total of 28 residential units would be permitted, if approved. She
noted that this was a small scale land use plan amendment. Ms. Melgren also said that the Planning and
Zoning Board, at their November Sth meeting, had unanimously recommended approval of the proposed
amendment to the Parkland City Commission.
Dennis Mele, the applicant’s representative, stated that the property was currently approved on the plat note for
45,000 square feet of commercial development, 5,000 square feet of office development, and ten residential
units. Mr. Mele said that the proposed revision to thirty-three residential units would result in a net reduction
of over 6,000 daily vehicle trips. Mr. Mele said the lot sizes for the proposed 33 homes were the same size as
those in Pod C of Parkland Reserve. Mr. Mele noted that his client was amenable to making contributions to

<<PG>>

PAGE 7
the Parkland Education Fund in the total amount of $250,000.00. Mr. Mele requested the opportunity to
respond to any public comments.
Mayor Udine invited interested parties to make public comments relative to the applicant’s proposal.
Robin Day, speaking on behalf of the Board of Directors of Parkland Reserve, said her association favored the
possibility of utilizing the subject parcel as a community park, rather than as overflow parking for Heron
Heights or the applicant’s proposed land use amendment. Ms. Day said that she had received positive feedback
to date regarding her association’s proposal to seek a different and progressive scenario for this land parcel.
Michele Millgren entered all applicable documents and exhibits into the meeting record. She made a brief
presentation. Ms. Millgren noted that City Staff recommended approval of proposed land use amendment, as
did the City’s Planning & Zoning Board.
Commissioner Willis noted that he had met with Ms. Day and members of the Parkland Reserve Association
about their desire to have green or open space. Commissioner Willis said although he concurred with the
resident’s preference for green space, the property owners had the ability to proceed with either a commercial
or residential project in the future. Commissioner Willis stated that the residents of the Parkland Reserve had
the option of buying the land parcel from the owner in order to preserve it as green space. Commissioner
Willis said that the builder had laid down some gravel into the overflow parking lot earlier this week.
Commissioner Willis also noted that he had requested that the City Manager include, in the next phase of
design for the park across the street, some type of overflow parking for Heron Heights.
In terms of traffic-related matters, Commissioner Willis requested that City staff investigate possible options
relative to the difficult nature of making left-hand turns out of Parkland Reserve on to Pine Island Road,
towards Heron Heights during school hours. Commissioner Willis said without any means to preserve this
parcel as open space, his vote this evening would be to change the zoning, since he much preferred lower
density residential development homes, rather than having commercial usesadjoining homes or schools.
Commissioner Rosenof said he had reviewed the site plan and felt that closer attention needed to be paid to
guest parking arrangements and related parking issues for landscaper trucks and similar vehicles.
Vice Mayor Weissman said he had heard from a number of residents about traffic related issues.
Vice Mayor Weissman also stated that he was supportive of the thirty-three residential unit alternative
proposal being presented by the applicant.
Mayor Udine noted that there had been a significant change in economic conditions since the builder's
original proposal for commercial and office uses in the middle of Parkland Reserve Mayor Udine stated that
he criginally favored having an open great lawn, back in 2003 or 2004, but there was no way to make this work
at present due to economic-related factors. Mayor Udine also noted that there were no funds to preserve this
parcel as open space. Mayor Udine stated that this was a vacant piece of land used for overflow parking at
present, as a convenience for the school, only for a certain period of time. Mayor Udine said that he would be
supportive of downzoning from commercial to a much less intensive residential land use.
MOTION made by Commissioner Weissman to approve Ordinance 2012-22.
Seconded by Commissioner Willis.

Ms. Gardner-Young read the title of Ordinance 2012-23, Standard Pacific Master Plan Amendment, on first
reading. Mr. Maroudis noted that a quasi-judicial procedure would be required for this matter at second reading.
Michele Mellgren made a brief staff presentation, noting that there would be no change in the clubhouse layout
or the amenities associated with the facility. She also stated that there would be minor roadway and guardhouse
changes which would be reviewed during the site planning process. Ms. Mellgren said the Planning and Zoning
Board had unanimously (6-0) recommended approval of the proposed master plan amendment. Ms. Mellgren
also said that City staff recommended approval of the proposal.
Dernis Mele, the applicant's representative, said that the proposal would relocate the clubhouse to the larger of
the two lakes in the development.
