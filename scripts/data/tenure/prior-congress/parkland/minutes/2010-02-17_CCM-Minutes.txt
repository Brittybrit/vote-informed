City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2010-29, Authorizing Calvin
Giordano to Design, Certify and Perform Environmental Study Relating to Riverside Trail Project,
and introduced Public Works Director Jim Berkman.
Public Works Director Berkman gave a brief presentation and said that the City’s intent was to
complete the Riverside Trail Project all the way up to Trotters Lane by using the $225,000 federal
grant received as the City’s match to a Broward County MPO grant, which was applied for but not
awarded to the City. He stated that the $225,000 is still available for the trail, but it is not enough to
reach Riverside Drive. Public Works Director Berkman noted that the DOT requires the sidewalk
to be a minimum of ten feet wide, which is much wider than what the City usually does, and it will
dead-end between Riverside Drive and Trotters Lane.
Commissioner Rosenof commented that he feels the money for this item should not be spent at this
time since this is not a project the City is going to do right away and the design will likely be
obsolete by the time the project is undertaken.
Mayor Udine agreed and said that it does not make sense to spend $41,000 in order to keep a
$200,000 plus grant that will not even complete the project half way.
Commissioner Weissman stated that returning the grant money will be a mark against the City for
future grants and he feels as much of the project as possible should be finished at this time. He
added that other grants can be sought; the project may not take as long as thought to complete and at
the same time, the City will not lose its opportunity for future grants.
Commissioner Moskowitz said he believes this will be the second time the City has turned down
federal dollars and confirmed that points are taken away when a grantee has turned down federal
dollars before. He stated that he agrees with Commissioner Rosenof, but it should be understood
that this will be a mark against the City for future federal dollars.
Mayor Udine reiterated that the City can not afford to do the whole project at this time.
There was no action taken on this item.

PARKLAND UTILIZES COMPETITIVE CONSULTANT’S
NEGOTIATIONS ACT.

This item was pulled from the Consent Agenda.

City Attorney Andrew Maurodis explained that this item needs to be changed to an ordinance in

order to amend the City’s purchasing code because a number of federal projects require that the

code not only reference the Competitive Consultants Negotiation Act, CCNA, but also sets forth the

procedures by which the City is going to implement the CCNA. He stated that this is a more staff

driven system than Commission driven and is part of the trend towards having professional staff do

the rankings. City Attorney Maurodis noted that it does provide for Commission action when they
"believe the rankings are inappropriate and he added that if the Commission does not favor the exact

process as set forth, changes could possibly be made, but something like this needs to be embodied

in City code and as soon as possible.

Commissioner Moskowitz said that he pulled the item because he thought it was moving too

quickly and he is concerned about simply rubber-stamping the federal government’s suggestion. He

asked if making some changes to it, so long as it mirrors the models, would be acceptable.

Mayor Udine said this would be done between first and second reading of the ordinance.

City Attorney Maurodis explained that this is the procedure he is recommending, but he does not

feel that the federal government recommended that it had to be done in one particular way, only that

it is a requirement to have it in the regulations. He noted that it is not enough to just say the City is

going to follow the CCNA.

Commissioner Moskowitz questioned which procurement projects this would apply to and City

Attorney Maurodis responded that he recommends that the City have the same CCNA process for

everything. Commissioner Moskowitz asked if there is a certain timeline in which to have this done

and Public Works Director Jim Berkman said it is required for the Holmberg Road project.

Mayor Udine suggested approving the ordinance on first reading and then tweaking it before second

reading to make sure all requirements are met in order to ensure that the City can apply for any

federal grant and that it is all done in accordance with applicable federal regulations.

Commissioner Moskowitz said that he does not disagree and he wants to comply with the CCNA,

but he would like to see what changes could be made so that aside from staff’s input, the

Commission will still have some sort of input.

City Attorney Maurodis reiterated that changes can be made between first and second reading of the

ordinance.

MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to approve first reading of (Resolution No. 2010-30,

Adopting Procedures for CCNA Purchasing Policy) to be changed to Ordinance No. 2010-07.

Seconded by Commissioner Weissman.
