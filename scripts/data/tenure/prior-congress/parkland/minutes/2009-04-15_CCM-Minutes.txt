1. STATUS: CHINESE DRYWALL TASK FORCE ACTIONS
The Chinese Drywall task force members include: Paul Wise, City of Parkland Chief Structural
Inspector (Building Department), John Willis, resident of Banyan Isles, and Murray Zweig of the
City of Parkland Planning & Zoning Board. Mayor Udine commented these members are
volunteers and he thanked them for their diligence and the amount of time this group has put in
on this issue.
John Willis spoke on behalf of the task force; he explained the task force was created to provide
a source for information for residents on the issue of Chinese drywall. They met with the City
Manager within a day of being appointed and had a web page constructed for the task force to
compile information about Chinese drywall and list useful links to other websites such as, the
Florida Department of Health (http: / /www. doh. state. fl. us /environment/community /indoor-
air/drywall.html) He advised this is the only governmental agency thus far that has provided
instructions for people to determine whether their homes meet the criteria for Chinese drywall
and designed a `Frequently Asked Questions' page addressing questions in relation to Chinese
drywall issues.
Mr. Willis explained the task force created a short survey that can be accessed from the City web
page for people who believe their homes may be affected by Chinese drywall. He noted there
were (137) responses so far, with the majority of responses coming from residents of Heron Bay
and PGCC (Parkland Golf & Country Club).The survey showed none of the respondents that
have contacted their insurance company have had a claim approved or have had a lender willing
to work with them on their mortgage terms as of yet, further more, no reports of assistance or
communication has been received from WCI except to advise that someone would come out to
document their issues.
Mr. Willis acknowledged Viviana Coriat, Cindy Abaunza and. Joeseph Espinal for their
contributions and reviewed the results of their `Chinese Drywall Census' of the Banyan Isles
neighborhood. According to their research they found 32 out of 91 homes have confirmed cases
of bad drywall and there are 22 homes that are still not sure, but which about half do fall within
the prime time period. They feel based on these numbers, that between 40 to 50% of the homes
in that neighborhood are affected by Chinese drywall and the majority of those residents are
complaining about health issues as well. He said some have already decided to leave the
community. He also advised the Commission that the Heron Bay Board has decided to
deactivate resident's automatic security gate openers if they are not current on their HOA fees
and these residents would need to wait in the visitor's lane for the security guard to let them
through. He had questioned if an exception would be made for victims of Chinese drywall but
the answer is unknown at this time.
Murray Zweig spoke about an email Paul Wise . had received from Dr. Tim Townsend, a
researcher from the University of Florida and former Parkland resident, who has requested to

3. CONSIDERATION: CORAL SPRINGS FIRE CONTRACT
City Manager, Caryn Gardner -Young briefly explained the purpose of this item: The City of
Parkland entered into a six year contract with the City of Coral Springs for emergency medical and
fire protection services. Ms. Young noted the contract is due to terminate on September 30, 2010
with an automatic renewal for an additional five years with the same terms and conditions, unless the
City chose to terminate the contract, in which case, a twelve month prior notice is required
(September 30, 2009).
Ms. Gardner- Young advised she had prepared four options for review and requested further
direction from the Commission.
1. The City can take no action and renew the contract as written under the automatic renewal
provision.
2. The City can negotiate new contract terms prior to the need to provide termination notice of
the contract.
3. The City can provide termination notice of the contract and seek bids from other entities.
4. The City can provide termination notice of the contract and then negotiate new contract
terms.

Vice Mayor Weissman felt it was prudent as the guardians of the City's financial resources to open
discussions regarding bid options for these services. He noted no derogatory opinion regarding Coral
Springs Fire Rescue Services, however, he spoke on the tactical improvements he felt were required,
such as; a multi -year contract with accurate figures in advance for budgetary decisions and caps on
the cost of services.
Commissioner Rosenof suggested a hybrid of options that would entail researching other potential
service providers while simultaneously open negotiations of contract terms with Coral Springs.
Commissioner Moskowitz suggested addressing the City's contract concerns with Coral Springs ..
before going out to bid or researching for potential interest. He felt cost of services caps needed to be
one of the main items for negotiation.
Mayor Udine spoke on the partnership between the City of Parkland and the City of Coral Springs;
He advised residents have reported satisfaction with the services provided, he believes the current
contracted rates to be fair, and the geography of the City boundaries dictates that Coral Springs is the
best provider for these services. He stated he was in favor of Option #2, to negotiate fair and equal
contract terms with the City of Coral Springs.
Commissioner Moskowitz felt service from a business client perspective could be improved and was
in favor of negotiating more equitable contract terms.
Vice Mayor Weissman felt the City would need to go out to bid for new service providers in order to
receive the best possible contract terms.
The Commission agreed on Option 2: to negotiate new contract terms prior to the need to provide
termination notice of the contract and if acceptable terms cannot be reached then the City will go out
to bid for a new service provider.
MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to move forward with Option 2, as noted.

ADDED AGENDA ITEM: UNIVERSITY DRIVE EXTENSION ISSUE
City Attorney, Andrew Maurodis presented an update on the University Drive extension issue:
Mr. Maurodis spoke on this unprecedented undertaking which would amend the Palm Beach and
Broward County boundaries and remove a major roadway (University Drive) from the traffic
ways plan. He advised that if the proposed settlement agreement between Palm Beach County
and the DCA (Department of Community Affairs) was approved then the DCA would issue an
`order of compliance' to delete University Drive.
He noted the Cities of Coral Springs, Tamarac or Margate could still appeal this decision;
however they would bear the burden of proof which is difficult.
MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to approve the signing of the DCA Order of
Compliance Agreement. Seconded by Vice Mayor Weissman.
ROLL CALL VOTE:
MOTION passed unanimously.
