DISCUSSION:

PUBLIC SERVICE BUILDING
LOCATION OPTIONS

Mayor Udine recognized Commissioner-Elect Mark Weissman and congratulated him on
winning the election.

City Manager Mark Lauzier stated that staff had investigated different sites on which to
build a new Public Services building and a new fire station. City staff reviewed the

options and determined that, out of the four possible sites, the Old City Hall site was most
cost efficient and best suited to the City's needs for the fire station, Public Services
building and a community room at the same location. He presented a design to the
Commission. After input from the Commission and Fire Department, staff will estimate
the construction costs. It was hoped to make the new facility two-story, similar in
appearance to Parkland Town Center, to allow more land for parking and storage. Old
City Hall would be demolished to make space for the new facility, although the Historical
Society would like to keep old City Hall.

Public Services Director Jim Berkman added that he wanted the facility to be
aesthetically pleasing and as non-commercial looking as possible. Parking for the site, at
the corner of Parkside Drive and Holmberg Road, would be behind the building to hide it
from view.

Mr. Lauzier recommended using this site for the new facilities. A plan for the transition
period would need to be devised. He believed it would be possible to temporarily locate
Public Services staff at the Community Center, which would probably be completed in
August or September 2007. He stated that the design money and construction costs are in
the budget for the current fiscal year.

Mayor U dine opened to the Commission.

Vice Mayor McGilvray stated her apprehension about the noise impact on the
surrounding area, especially given the size of the vehicles, and expressed concern about
the distance to the homes behind the buildings and across the street. She also asked
whether all of the City's vehicles could be accommodated there.

Mr. Lauzier commented that the City may need to decrease the size of the community
room to allow more room for Public Service vehicles, and Mr. Berkman added that the
facility would address future needs for Public Services as well.

Vice Mayor McGilvray suggested having bricks or pieces of Old City Hall incorporated
into the new design plan.

Commissioner Moskowitz shared his concern about traffic problems during construction
in this high traffic area. Additionally, he believed that excessive noise during
construction and operation would disturb the nearby residents. He suggested preserving
Old City Hall and moving it to another location. He also expressed concern over the size
of the community room, suggesting instead more office space.

Mr. Lauzier stated that most of the construction would not involve roadway use and
major traffic problems should be avoided. He noted that Public Services personnel and
trucks were already located there; it would not be a change of use.

Commissioner Moskowitz suggested that construction time be restricted so as not to
disturb the nearby residents. Additionally, the property should have sufficient buffers.

Mayor Udine also expressed concern about the noise during construction. He agreed that
the site was appropriate for Public Services because the location is already used. He
suggested that the City might lease space in the adjoining commercial center to
temporarily house employees and get the construction done as soon as possible.

Commissioner Smith said that potential growth should be considered while building the
fire station, and Mr. Lauzier stated that the Fire Chief had made those provisions.

Mayor Udine believed that the facility would modernize the existing location and give
the eastern section of the City better services.

Mr. Lauzier reiterated that there are substantial savings by doing one larger project
instead of two smaller projects. Over the next few months, he would present design
concept options to the Commission. The two-story building would allow garages on the
first floor and offices on the second, while freeing up more space for parking. He
believed that directive to the designer could be provided to make the scale fit into the rest
of the community.

Commissioner Moskowitz asked about placing the facilities in the Wedge.

Assistant City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young responded that it may be three years
before anything could be built there. In addition, she believed that the economies of scale
would be lost.

Mr. Lauzier offered that staff will be looking for money for a western fire station from
landowners in the Wedge.

Mayor Udine stated that, at this point, the City Manager and staff had the consensus of
the Commission to move forward with examination of the site for the Public Services
building, fire station, and the community meeting room.

2.

DISCUSSION:

WEDGE AREA DENSITY REVIEW
AND GUIDELINES FOR
PRE-ANNEXATION AGREEMENT

Mr. Lauzier stated there were two new issues. He reviewed a conversation with a Palm
Beach County representative; they want an overlay district as per the Lox Road study.
Palm Beach County's Comprehensive Plan for unincorporated areas is huge, covering
many issues, and Palm Beach County wants the City to continue with their
comprehensive plan policies. He noted that this could be a potential deal killer as it
would not be appropriate for Palm Beach County to dictate how the land would be used.
Additionally, Palm Beach County would probably issue a land use change only for the
land owned by the applicants and not for the entire Wedge site. Palm Beach County
wanted an interlocal with Parkland regarding these issues. Mr. Lauzier suggested that the

City should try to annex the Wedge with as little land use planning and zoning in the
Palm Beach system as possible.

Mayor Udine questioned whether Mr. Maurodis could go to the landowners for cost
recovery, including legal fees and lobbyist expenses.

Mr. Maurodis agreed with the Mayor and stated that the issue of cost recovery was a
given, but timing is still uncertain. Initially, the Wedge landowners wanted the Palm
Beach land use; however, Palm Beach wished to micromanage what would happen after
the annexation, which he believed absurd. He noted that the landowners recognize this
and, as a result, were switching their focus to a pre-annexation agreement with Parkland.
The deal was becoming very complicated due to these issues.

Mr. Maurodis asked to bill separately on this item from his usual contract with the City as
it would allow him to bring in an associate, Sharon Cruz. He offered that Ms. Cruz
headed the land use division for 12 years in the County Attorney's Office and had also
acted as County Attorney on two occasions; she was extremely well qualified. Mr.
Maurodis stated that he would bill the City at the same rate as the Ranches attorney,
which is $185 per hour.

Mayor Udine asked the Commission's consensus to Mr. Maurodis' request, and the
Commissioners unanimously agreed.

Commissioner Moskowitz asked the negatives to entering into a pre-annexation
agreement.

Mr. Maurodis noted there are many issues. As all the landowners are not represented,
they can not promise zoning in an agreement and, so far, the developers do not have any
specific ideas on how they want to develop the land. Additionally, Broward County
would never agree to an agreement. He suggested that the City might determine what the
impact fees would be, per unit, at this time as the landowners may be interested in paying
fees up front. He offered, for example, funds to finish Pine Trails Park now. He noted
that it is hard to plan for something without having the known details because the City
could sell itself too cheaply. These are high-quality landowners; they recognize the costs
associated with this type of land deal and they are willing to pay for them. The City
should give the landowners some assurances while insuring Parkland-quality
development.

Commissioner Moskowitz suggested holding a workshop with the Commission to get a
detailed update regarding the landowners. Mr. Lauzier indicated that staff should be able
to present more information at the next workshop.

Mr. Maurodis said this process could speed up over the next few months. Going forward,
the City should try to quantify impact fees, keeping in mind that they may want to enter
into a pre-annexation agreement. Also, it should be indicated that the Commission prefers
not to be burdened by a Palm Beach County land use designation.

3.

DISCUSSION:

RFP DRAFT REVIEW
FOR CODE ENFORCEMENT SERVICES

City Manager Mark Lauzier gave a copy of the draft RFP to the Commission. The initial
proposal includes hiring one full-time code enforcement officer and one possible part-
time officer for the weekends; they would build toward two full-time officers based on
need. While two officers have been budgeted, staff would like to be able to increase as
needs dictate. The length of the contract would be one year with an option for a second.

Vice Mayor McGilvray believed code enforcement officers should be without ever
having had a criminal conviction - not just in the last two years as stated in the RFP. She
asked regarding advertising and how long the process might take.

Mayor Udine agreed, and he suggested they change the wording regarding a criminal
record to be the same as the BSO requirements.

Mr. Lauzier identified two private entities that provide code enforcement services. He
intended to solicit those firms and BSO for bids, and hoped for other responses to the
newspaper notice.

Ms. Gardner-Young said the process usually takes about four weeks.

Commissioner Moskowitz agreed with the idea of starting out small with the option of
expanding and with the one year contract. He wanted to make certain that the code
enforcement company would follow through on the City's direction, and Mr. Lauzier
stated that the contract included a section on coordinating efforts with the City.

Mr. Maurodis said he believed the contract covered the key issues, and Mayor Udine
stated his agreement with the contract terms.

Mr. Maurodis noted that the process did not call for short listing. The submittals would
be brought directly to the Commission.

Mayor Udine noted that Martin Kiar had been elected the State Representative for this
area, and he expressed interest in having Mr. Kiar's district office in Parkland. It would
be of benefit to the City to have his office here.
