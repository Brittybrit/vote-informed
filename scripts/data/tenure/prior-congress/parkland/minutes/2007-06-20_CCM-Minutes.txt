Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2007-66.
Leisure Services Director Scott Davidoff indicated that the amendment's purpose was to clear up
any confusion that resulted from the difference in hours between the pro shop and the courts.
Staff hoped to ensure that administrative staff from the United States Tennis Federation would be
available during all hours that the pro shop is open and, in return, they would be responsible for
40 percent of replacement items and maintenance.
Commissioner Weissman recalled that this issue had been raised when there had been damage
during court off-hours. In reviewing the contract, he had found the amendment pertained to
ongoing maintenance. He noted that the contractor received 85 percent of the revenue and would
now be responsible for 40 percent of ongoing maintenance; however, there was no provision for
damage that occurs on the property during the contractor's "watch." Mr. Davidoff agreed. The
Commissioner commented that his larger concern with the tennis facility was that the City would
have 100 percent responsibility.

5. RESOLUTION N0.2007-67: NON-RESIDENT SPORTS CARD
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, TO IMPLEMENT
AND CHARGE A FEE FOR ANON-RESIDENT SPORTS
CARD FOR NON-RESIDENTS TO PARTICIPATE IN
CITY RECOGNIZED SPORTS LEAGUES; PROVIDING
FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE.
Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2007-67.
Leisure Services Director Scott Davidoff offered that this subject had been discussed by both the
Commission and the Parks and Recreation Advisory Board over a number of years, piggybacking
on the sports policy. The Board recommended instituting anon-resident sports card, similar to
that adopted by many other municipalities. The proposed cost would be $155 per family per
fiscal year, covering all family members and all sports throughout the fiscal period. Two
different options had been examined: a flat rate fee and a system of reciprocity. The Board
preferred the flat rate as it would assist in lowering the number of non-residents participating in
the program. It was felt that the system of reciprocity would become an administrative
nightmare. Mr. Davidoff reiterated that most municipalities had these fees, which were included
in the back-up material as well as a breakdown by sport. He estimated that the non-resident
sports card would be purchased by approximately 200 families.
Commissioner Weissman recalled previous conversations about the policy and the expectations
of dishonesty. He suggested that the least expensive city to claim to live in would be Parkland.
Mr. Davidoff noted that a driver's license or utility bill must be produced to verify residency.
The Commissioner continued to favor a system of reciprocity with other cities, whereby those

<<PG>>

children would pay the same as what Parkland children would be made to pay in other cities to
play in their sports programs.
While Vice Mayor Moskowitz acknowledged the Commissioner's argument, he felt that the
reciprocity system would also be unfair based upon the geography of where a player lived.
Mr. Davidoff commented that the Parks and Recreation Advisory Board had realized this
potential and had wanted a flat rate that everyone would pay. They also felt that, with the higher
rate, it would assist with the issue of non-residents, rather than instituting a cap or percentages.
The Vice Mayor asked the accuracy of 196 non-residents playing recreational soccer. Mr.
Davidoff indicated that the numbers were based upon the past year's rosters, without distinction
to resident and non-resident. He acknowledged, however, ways to circumvent the system. Vice
Mayor Moskowitz felt that the policy would only work i.f the City could keep it honest.
Mayor Udine commented that the City must realize it could never catch every individual who
chose to cheat the system. With what had gone on in Tallahassee, he believed it time for the City
to set the non-resident fee since the City's taxpayers pay for the parks. He believed it necessary
and reasonable, and he suggested that residents of other cities expected to pay a fee since
neighboring cities charged non-residents. The Mayor stated that, while he had originally agreed
with Commissioner Weissman and the system of reciprocity, he believed it would be a logistical

Moskowitz suggested that "special needs" should be defined. Mayor Udine commented that

like to see this break given. While the Vice Mayor agreed, he suggested there may be situations
of economic special needs as well. The soccer program and its fees were discussed, and Mr.
Davidoff offered that other communities also had special needs programs. The Mayor suggested
that, in the case of a special needs child, staff could use its discretion; he did not believe that it
would involve great numbers. While Vice Mayor Moskowitz agreed, he noted that reciprocity
would give the excuse.
Mr. Davidoff commented that, currently, the only special needs program in Parkland was the
soccer program; other leagues were working towards that.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz stated that he felt uncomfortable using higher fees with the hope that
non-resident participation would be curtailed and using money as the criteria of whether or not a
child might play in the City.

Board reexamined the issue and, based upon a suggestion made by Pete Manso, they
recommended instituting a 50 percent rule: that any league, travel or recreational, that has fewer

discussed in January, which he believed in line with other municipalities, and the only program

Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that he was not concerned about a program with 40 total

Vice Mayor Moskowitz noted that the City would institute a policy that would affect one sport.

the policy addressed the number of people playing on the City's fields and the "field crunch" at

the leagues have acknowledged that, with expansion, they must schedule on days and times they

even better. He noted that the City's leagues were not marketing to out-of--city families, and he
estimated that 90 percent of the non-resident participants were those living near Heron Bay and
attending Park Trails. He did not consider non-residents coming to play in Parkland's
