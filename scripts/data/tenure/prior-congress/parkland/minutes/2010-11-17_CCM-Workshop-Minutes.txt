In PURPOSE:

1 DISCUSSION: SECURITY CAMERAS
City Manager, Caryn Gardner-Young gave a brief introduction of the item being presented and
then turned it over to Chief Paul O’Connell. Chief O’Connell introduced Dan Williams,
Patrolman from Winter Springs Police Department in Central Florida and also a representative
for NDI Recognitions Systems. Mr. Williams showed a 4 minute video and then gave a brief
PowerPoint presentation on license plate recognition security cameras.

The video presentation stated that BSO uses this system and Chief O’Connell, clarified by
saying BSO currently uses a portable license recognition system.

Comments by Vice Mayor Rosenof:

Vice Mayor Rosenof said he spoke with the Chief of Police in Lighthouse Point that morning
regarding the security system. He questioned whether a person sat all day checking license
plates and Chief O’Connell answered that it all depends on how the City wants to pay for it.
Chief O’Connell said the human interaction is necessary because of the misreading of the
license plates by the system. Vice Mayor Rosenof’s concern was that he does not want an
innocent person in hand cuffs because of a misread. He also stated that all the license plate
reads are public record and would have to be available on request. He asked about a forced
multiplier. He said Parkland needs another zone and this may be a good stop gap until then.
Chief O’Connell explained that this is another tool for police officers to help them do their
jobs. This could be an investigative tool as well.

Vice Mayor Rosenof said he is leaning towards approving this, but he has some questions of
the City Attorney. Chief O’Connell agreed that the City should explore the system and concept
of using the system.
Comments by Commissioner Moskowitz:
Commissioner Moskowitz stated that he read an article and spoke to a resident about the
security cameras. He agrees with Vice Mayor Rosenof in that 1) the City needs more police
officers in a fourth zone which is extremely expensive and 2) he mentioned two incidents
where cars came right off the Expressway into Parkland on a chase from FHP. He feels this
activity is a warning to us that we are not on an island unto ourselves and that crime can flow
into Parkland.
He gave a scenario that if he was going to add just one police officer or this security camera
system, he feels the camera system could cover more ground than one officer. He feels this is
intelligence and he looks at it differently than red light cameras. He agrees with the ACLU that
it will deter crime in Parkland and crime will go elsewhere. He felt that the only way this
system would work is to be a 50/50 partnership between BSO and the City. This is something
he would like to talk to the Sheriff about.
Comments by Commissioner Weissman:
Commissioner Weissman stated that if the economy was different he would vote “yes”
immediately. The initial cost of the system is not what would deter him, but the cost for
someone to sit and watch a monitor for 24 hours a day — this is not just one person, but a
number of people. Also his concern is in addition to the initial cost, there would be an annual
cost to the budget. He does not see implementing this system unless there is help from BSO.
Comments by Mayor Udine:

Mayor said he leans in favor of supporting the security camera system, but he is only interested
in the proposal for ingress and egress of the City due to the way the City of Parkland is laid out.
He feels there will be resistance in getting help from BSO. He pointed out that the City does

the City Attorney to see what are our options i.e., personnel, cost benefit analysis, legalities,
etc.
The Mayor moved out of the normal order of the agenda to hear Item #3: Dog Park Design
3. DISCUSSION: DOG PARK DESIGN

City Manager, Caryn Gardner-Young gave a brief explanation of the item being presented. She
explained that Keith & Associates was the least expensive consultant for design and they
provided the attached design. She then turned it over to Brian Archer who gave a brief
presentation of the Dog Park Design.
City Manager, Caryn Gardner-Young pointed out some important components of the Dog Park
plan.

e The enhanced landscaping (screens the dog park from Holmberg Road)

o Large dog and small dog components separated by fencing

e Water fountain and washing station that both small and large dogs can use.

e Additional parking for the dog park users as well as Liberty Park users
Commissioner Rosenof asked what was the surface of the Dog Park? Staff replied “St.
Augustine” grass. Commissioner Rosenof was concerned for the health of the dogs with the
use of pesticides on grass. Staff stated there are safe pesticides that can be used on the grass.

2. DISCUSSION: PARK LIGHTING

City Manager, Caryn Garden-Young turned it over to Scott Davidoff, Parks and Recreation
Director. Mr. Davidoff explained that as part of the Strategic Plan, the City Commission
ranked park lighting as a management agenda top priority. He then gave a brief PowerPoint
presentation on the park lighting issues.
Mayor Udine asked if the Parks & Recreation Board recommended maintaining the status quo.
Staff replied, “That is correct.” He also commented that his concern is when there is inclement
weather that the lights should be turned off.
