III PUBLIC INPUT
Stuart Krantz, 6348 N.W. 62n Terrace, commented about biking to City Hall and thanked the
Commission for the Holmberg Road bike lanes, the road repaving, and the fire hydrants. He was
concerned, however, about safety, believing that the City had cut corners with the sidewalks and
violated its own landscape ordinance. He also spoke regarding Parkside Drive and the possibility
of moving the sidewalk.
Mike Squillace of the Ranches thanked the Commission for the birthday celebration with the
Historical Society and the time capsule. He thanked the Vice Mayor for his help on resolving his
bridge issue, the City Attorney for his good counsel to the Commission about Ranches issues, and
the Mayor for taking his phone calls about violations written in the Ranches.
Marie Gosser of Pinetree Estates spoke in support of Mr. Squillace and her witness of the court
order. She felt that Pinetree was receiving inadequate attention as to illegal dumping in the area.
She asked that the City buy a vacant lot at the end of the street for a park.
Dr. Leo Gosser of Pinetree Estates spoke regarding the Broward Beekeepers' Association, which
was growing. They hoped to educate the public and promote backyard beekeeping. He asked
that the City consider a Beekeepers' Day or some recognition, and he spoke of Association
activities and offered to act as liaison. Dr. Gosser also complimented the City on the Holmberg
Road project and, as an addendum to his wife's comments, he suggested that the nine acres at the
end of N.W. 62n Terrace, 63'' Way, and 65 Terrace would make a nice preservation area.
Jeff Schwartz, new president of the Historical Society, asked regarding an old fire truck owned by
the City. If the truck was still available, he had a personal interest in buying and restoring the
truck, which might then be donated back to the City.
Mayor Udine suggested that Mr. Schwartz meet with the City Manager.
Christopher Spute gave a statement of character for Mr. Squillace, whom he had known and
worked with in volunteer projects for many years.
Former Mayor Harold Bockhold spoke about litigation. He believed the parties should sit down,
negotiate, and reach a conclusion to it.
Kevin Bolling of the Ranches believed the Vice Mayor has been an outstanding representative for
the Ranches. He asked whether beekeeping was permitted within the Ranches. He also spoke in
support of Mr. Squillace.
There were no further comments or questions, and Mayor Udine closed to the public.

Comments made by Commissioner Rosenof:
Welcomed his family, and especially his sister from San Francisco
Attended Representative Kiar's town hall meeting, which was also attended by Vice
Mayor Ritter and Commissioner Lieberman. The League of Women Voters had
published a paper about the amendments on the November ballot, and he urged residents
to obtain it.
Comments made by Commissioner Smith:
Thankful that the relationship between the City and Mr. Squillace and Mr. Bolling had
improved. He was sorry that they were both cited for something they should not have
been and noted that the citations were rescinded. He apologized on behalf of the City.
Comments made by Vice Mayor Moskowitz:
National Recycling Day would be celebrated November 15 The City would organize
with the schools a plastic bag recycling effort for which awards would be given to
students or groups of students. He promised that more information would be
disseminated to the community as it became available.
The new website would include the ability to calculate a household's carbon footprint.
Agreed that Holmberg Road was progressing nicely.
Heard that Dr. Gosser had excellent honey.
As to the Ranches, he admitted that he had been just as frustrated as residents about the
oil issue, which had gone on since June, with respect to clean -up efforts and locating its
source. He noted that the City had now spent upwards of $25,000 on the issue.
Reports were anticipated within the next week to 10 days as to the quality of the water
and whether more significant testing about the damage was needed. He promised that
residents would be kept up -to -date and noted that further information from them may be
needed with respect to action against the contractor.
Also regarding the Ranches, he commented that something had finally been done with
Trotter's Lane. Additionally, asphalt would be laid in some areas where the rock
surface had not held and in cross -lanes in the coming year. Almost $400,000 had been
allocated in the budget to start repaving some of the roads there. He anticipated a three
or four -year plan to repave all the roads.
Acknowledged the disagreement from Ranches residents regarding the ordinances to
"clean up" the businesses in the quasi residential area, and he was happy that relations
had improved. The City was trying to resolve the litigation, and there had been some
progress, but he noted that the insurance company was "in the driver's seat." He
believed that, no matter what may have happened in the past, the parties must move
forward. He would continue to make himself accessible to Ranches residents.
Comments made by Commissioner Weissman:
With regards to Pinetree, he wished the City had the ability to purchase land. He had
been a proponent for green space since the expansion of Pine Trails Park. He noted
that fire hydrants had been installed along Holmberg Road in the Pinetree area, which

 WHO MUST FILE FORM 8B
 This form is for use by any person serving at the county, city, or other local level of government on an appointed or elected board, council,
 commission, authority, or committee. It applies equally to members of advisory and non-advisory bodies who are presented with a voting
 conflict of interest under Section 112.3143, Florida Statutes.
 Your responsibilities under the law when faced with voting on a measure in which you have a conflict of interest will vary greatly depending
 on whether you hold an elective or appointive position. For this reason, please pay close attention to the instructions on this form before
 completing the reverse side and filing the form.
 INSTRUCTIONS FOR COMPLIANCE WITH SECTION 112.3143, FLORIDA STATUTES
 _ A person holding elective or appointive county, municipal, or other local public office MUST ABSTAIN from voting on a measure which
 inures to his or her special private gain or loss. Each elected or appointed local officer also is prohibited from knowingly voting on a mea-
 sure which inures to the special gain or loss of a principal (other than a government agency)by whom he or she is retained (including the
 parent organization or subsidiary of a corporate principal by which he or she is retained);to the special private gain or loss of a relative; or
 to the special private gain or loss of a business associate. Commissioners of community redevelopment agencies under Sec. 163.356 or
 163.357, F.S., and officers of independent special tax districts elected on a one-acre, one-vote basis are not prohibited from voting in that
 capacity.
 For purposes of this law, a "relative" includes only the officer's father, mother, son, daughter, husband, wife, brother, sister, father-in-law,
 mother-in-law, son-in-law, and daughter-in-law. A"business associate" means any person or entity engaged in or carrying on a business
 enterprise with the officer as a partner,joint venturer, coowner of property, or corporate shareholder(where the shares of the corporation
 are not listed on any national or regional stock exchange).
