3. DISCUSSION: TRIPLE H & DEBUYS
PRE- ANNEXATION AGREEMENT
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis said there is an existing annexation agreement with Triple H
and DeBuys and as part of the land use amendment process, it should be understood that
although it says pre - annexation agreement, DeBuys and Triple H are in the City. He stated that
they are at the land use plan stage and are one step further along than the others that will be dealt
with at this meeting.
City Attorney Maurodis said as part of the process, there was a need to make some changes to the
pre - annexation agreement. He then reviewed the proposed amendment to the annexation
agreement and his comments included the following:
• There is a reduction in the amount of commercial acreage.
• The applicant wishes to leave the dedication of roadway requirement for the county
platting process. The County is rigorous in requiring road dedication, will require safe
and adequate access, and has transportation fees.
• Originally there was a provision for a specific dedication for County Line Road and
University Drive; the applicant is proposing to take care of this at the platting stage.
• There is a specific commitment from the owner to pay their fair share of the
reimbursement for the guardrail.
• The dedication of a school site is being moved up from the rezoning stage to the land use
stage.
City Attorney Maurodis explained that he and Dennis Mele, on behalf of the applicant, are
looking for an agreement in concept as to the business points, and then will finalize the
agreement and bring it back to the Commission in January before it goes to the county for a
county transmittal of the land use plan amendment (to DCA). He noted that there will also be an
agreement to provide for the above in a restrictive covenant.

Dennis Mele, on behalf of the applicant, spoke about the particulars of the school site dedication
and his comments included the following:
• The Commission backup includes two alternates for Section 3; the applicant's version is
in green print and City Attorney Maurodis' is in purple. What is being proposed is a
combination of the two.
• At the time of rezoning, the applicant will identify the 15 -20 acre site, will make sure the
City agrees with its location, will go to the school board and ask them to agree with its
location, and then will set aside that property through the type of deed and title review as
described in language similar to that used by City Attorney Maurodis.
• The clock will not start to run until the applicant gets the first building permit for a
residential building. From then there will be a seven year period in which the school
board gets the first five years to determine if they want the site. If at the end of that five-
year period the school board says it does not want it, there is an additional two -year
period for the City to try to find a charter school or some other school that could come
into that site. At the end of the seven years, if there is no school with the school board
and no school with the City, then the property goes back to the applicant.
• Language was added on how to incorporate the restrictive covenant.
• All of the school site decisions agreed upon by the applicant and City are subject to the
school board's final approval.
Mayor Udine recognized staff's work on this issue and added that the EAB discussed it a few
weeks ago; they were in agreement with the Commission in wanting to ensure a school site is
carved out. He spoke about the school board and reiterated the merits of extending the time
period.
Commissioner Weissman said he is happy with this deal and noted it needs to be recognized that
there are a total of approximately 2,300 acres in this area, somewhere in the neighborhood of
4,000 homes will be built, generating about 8,000 K through 12 students. He added that any of
the other developers who did not come to the plate and front this whole annexation can not be
put in the same position. Commissioner Weissman stated that the land will need to be put aside
with no ifs, ands, or buts and with no time constraints on it because once the land is gone, this
Commission or future Commissions will not have options with regards to schools.
Commissioner Rosenof said he agrees with the compromise, but maybe it should be taken one
step further in that other acreages may need to be dedicated for specific uses within the whole
property, such as land for parks and roads. He added that it might be a good idea to figure out
how many acres are needed for each of these functions and maybe even asking each of the
individual landowners to proportionally share in either the cost or the dedication of land for each
of those functions.
Commissioner Moskowitz agreed with Commissioner Weissman's and Commissioner Rosenof s
comments, but noted that there have been some people who have been partners with the City
through this whole process, while others are now trying to ride on their coattails. He agreed with
Commissioner Weissman insofar as those people should not get the same treatment.

5. DISCUSSION: HENDRIX & BRUSCHI
PRE - ANNEXATION AGREEMENTS
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis said John Milledge is present on behalf of the Bruschi property
and then the Hendrix property will be discussed.
City Attorney Maurodis explained there is a similar proposal to the Bruschi family and said they
are in a much different situation as they own much less property. He noted that with regard to
the school provision, the Bruschi family is willing to dedicate eight acres to the school district if
the school district wants it, which will be done at rezoning. City Attorney Maurodis said if the
school district does not accept it, the Bruschi family would dedicate three acres to the City for
any public purpose that the City deems fit.
Commissioner Weissman said he would rather have the eight acres come directly to the City, as
an eight acre site is not going to be sufficient for the City or School Board to build a school. He
stated that he does not want the acreage to be lost and a deed restriction can be put on it that it
can only be used for K through 12 facilities or swapped for land that can be used for this purpose.
Commissioner Weissman noted that with the land coming to the City, the City can then go to the
school board, bringing land to the table, and request they build a school; if the school board is not
given the funds from Tallahassee to accomplish that, then the City may wind up going into the
charter school business or contracting out. He added that provision for the eight acres should be
in perpetuity as the City is going to need the schools to accommodate 8,000 more children.
Mayor Udine said he thinks land swap is a good concept, understanding that an eight acre site is
never going to be used for a school.
City Attorney Maurodis commented it may be better to take three or four acres for whatever
purpose the City deems fit.
There was discussion regarding the potential number, grade, and location of schools needed to
service this area and the deeding of land to the City.
