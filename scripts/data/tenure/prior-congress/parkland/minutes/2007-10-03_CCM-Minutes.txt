2. CONSIDERATION: SPECIAL EVENTS PERMIT -
MSDHS MARCHING BAND
Planning Director Roberta Moore made a brief presentation about this Special Events Permit. for
outdoor sales of pumpkins and Christmas trees. She noted that the material provided to the
Commission included sparklers, and the petitioner had since indicated that sparklers were not
part of this permit. It would be located in a commercially-zoned district. Staff had worked with
the petitioner over the past few weeks and only one issue remained outstanding: removal of the
tent following the pumpkin .sale and its replacement prior to the Christmas tree sale. The
petitioner, as well as ABC Promotions, had indicated that they would prefer the tent to stay up
because of the timeframe and associated costs, but staff recommended its removal. A special
event permit will be issued once the Commission's decision is rendered.
Petitioner Rich Hall, president of the Douglas High School marching band organization, spoke
regarding the considerable time and effort to fund this organization, with almost constant
fundraising events necessary. This sale was a terrific opportunity to raise $10,000 by simply
representing a vendor, and he asked the Commission's favorable consideration of their request.
Mayor Udine noted that this would be .held in the location used for Christmas tree sales by
another company for the past several years, with which Ms. Gardner-Young agreed. While he
was in favor, his only issue of concern was the effect on the Women's Club Pumpkin Patch,
which had been held for years. The Mayor stated that he would vote in favor, but with
reservations; he did not want to be placed in a position to judge the importance of the charities,
nor become involved in a battle between two important service organizations. He asked whether
the pumpkin sale can be reconciled with that of the Women's Club.
Mr. Hall stated he had spoken with Linda Douglas of the Women's Club, and he gave his word
that the sales would take place on State Road 7 only. Their band and the school would not solicit
sales, and they would not interfere with the sale by the Women's Club - a supporter of the band.
Linda Douglas, President of the. Women's Club, confirmed that she had a pleasant conversation
with Mr. Hall; she believed they had an understanding. Her concern had been their promotion
within the school by the school or band parents. She admitted that, at questions posed by the
press at this meeting, perhaps it was a larger issue than they had previously thought. The event
was their major fundraiser and various elements of Douglas had been recipients; she could not
foresee the effects of the sale. She applauded them on raising the $10,000, but admitted her
mixed feelings about the competition in sales.
At the Mayor's .question, Ms. Douglas indicated that two truckloads of pumpkins would be
delivered. Mr. Grad had assured them three months ago that students would be there to help
them unload.
Commissioner Weissman asked confirmation that the band would be guaranteed $10,000 no
matter what the actual sales numbers were, with which Mr. Hall agreed. The Commissioner

3. ORDINANCE NO. 2007-20: REDISTRICTING
COMMENTS: FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND,
FLORIDA, REDISTRICTING THE CITY INTO FOUR
CITY COMMISSION DISTRICTS, PURSUANT TO THE
CITY CHARTER SECTION 3.02, PROVIDING FOR THE
REDISTRICTING OF THE CITY INTO FOUR
COMMISSION DISTRICTS AS NEARLY AS
PRACTICABLE ON AN EQUAL POPULATION BASIS
BY CONTIGUOUS BOUNDARIES; CONTAINING A
SEVERABILITY CLAUSE; CONTAINING AN
EFFECTIVE DATE.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2007-20. She
recalled that this matter had been discussed at workshop. The consultant had also been asked to
investigate other additional alternatives, which were determined would not meet the necessary
criteria. Plan 1 had been attached to the Ordinance, but the Commission would still have the
opportunity to make changes. Ms. Gardner-Young noted that both Ms. Tartaglione and Mr.
Richard were present to answer questions.
Mayor Udine explained that Plan 1 had seemed to have the consensus. While the Commission
felt that Plan 2 would be in the future, it made sense to keep it closest to .that which currently
exists with the census numbers in use.
Commissioner Weissman believed that both of the plans met legal requirements; he suggested
that it was more a question of whether the City should be "ahead of the curve" this time around
for redistricting. He believed that his district would be affected the most of the sitting
commissioners and, for that reason, he leaned instead toward Plan 2. The Commissioner felt that
the City should move toward the future.
Mayor Udine opened to the public.
Natalie Bigio of Pinetree Estates commented that she did not have an issue with either plan. She
noted, however, that the Vice Mayor had mentioned at the workshop that it would be good to
draw the districts so that communities with unique situations, and specifically those non-gated,
would deal with one commissioner. She suggested that this sounded very much like single-
member districts, which she believed the City should have. As this would require a Charter
change, she questioned how it might be brought about.
Mayor Udine offered that this issue had been brought to the Charter Review Board, which had
recommended against single-member districts. He noted that each commissioner represented
every resident; while the commissioners must live within their respective districts, they were

<<PG>>

elected City-wide. He stated that the Commission had tried to operate under the idea that, with
something specific to a commissioner's district, the Commission would defer to that
commissioner. The Mayor noted that, in the past, five separate inquiries on one subject could
have been addressed with all five commissioners; it had led to inconsistencies and. a lack of
productivity.
Ms. Bigio indicated her agreement with that, but she believed that the real issue was that
residents of the western areas of the City were voting on who should represent the people in the
eastern part of the City, where there were totally different issues. She believed that voting blocks
of other areas often shutout knowledgeable candidates, which she considered an inequity.
Mayor Udine acknowledged the different sides to single-member versus City-wide voting. He
thanked Ms. Bigio for her comments.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2007-101
Parks and Recreation Director Scott Davidoff made a presentation and noted that the contract had
undergone a number of different changes in the terms over the past months. Staff, the City
Attorney, and the YMCA staff and its attorney had worked diligently to come to agreement, and
he believed that everyone was now comfortable with the terms. He offered that YMCA
representatives were present to answer any questions.
Ms. Gardner-Young acknowledged the YMCA board members present, who came to the podium
to introduce themselves: Robert McGrath, Chairman of the North Branch; Randall Wood, board
member of the North Branch and Vice Chair of the YMCA county association; Steven Kelton, a
board member of the North Branch and Parkland resident; and Sheryl Woods, President and
Chief Executive Officer of the YMCA of Broward County and Parkland resident. Ms. Woods
acknowledged the time, effort, and energy that all of the parties had put into the agreement, and
she thanked them.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked regarding equipment replacement within four years, with which
Ms. Gardner-Young agreed, as well as confirmation of the contract's term. Ms. Gardner-Young
stated that the initial contract would be for five years, with the right to terminate 180 days before
expiration; there would be three three-year renewals with the right to terminate 180 days before
each renewal.
Mayor Udine questioned the operations of the Young-At-Art program. Mr. Davidoff offered
both Young-At-Art personnel and the YMCA staff were excited by the possibilities. Young-At-
Art was a known commodity, and he assured that the contract would work exactly the same as it
would have with the City.
As to the signage, Mayor Udine commented that everyone was in agreement that the sign would
state "Parkland YMCA Family Center." He believed it important that the City have recognition
on the building.
Mayor Udine felt that the facility would be a "home run." He stated that some e-mail inquiries
had been received, and the City had tried to address every one in the agreement. To inform the
public, he advised that the agreement included the conditions that Parkland residents would pay a
lower membership fee than non-residents and would be given first priority in program sign-ups;
Non-residents would not, however, be precluded from using the facility. The Mayor commented
that he liked the idea of the subcommittee for resident input on programming. He was excited
that the City's residents would receive the expertise of the YMCA while saving the City's
taxpayers hundred of thousands of dollars for the facility operation. As stewards of the City's tax
dollars, the Cormnission could not overlook this opportunity, and he was thankful for the
YMCA's partnership.
Mayor Udine opened to the public.
