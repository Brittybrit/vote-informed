a brief presentation, which included the following comments:

o Utilization of a temporary debris management site (TDMS) would be necessary in the
event of a category #3-5 hurricane, and perhaps for a level 2, depending upon the severity.

o A debris site is used for the storage of disaster related debris; it can be used for the
purpose of reducing debris in size or for temporarily holding it until there is a place to
dispose of it, or both.

e Broward County provided estimates of the quantity of debris anticipated for the different
categories of storms.

e A category 4 is the point where the quantity of construction and demolition debris
becomes greater than the quantity of vegetative debris.

e Capacity of the site is determined by the acreage that is available, height the debris is
stacked, diversity of the debris, space for administrative operations, timing and rate of
reduction operations, timing and rate of fallout operations, and whether operation is on a
24/7 basis or only during daylight hours.

e Any site that is selected will be out of use for two years until all the remediation can be
finished. FEMA pays for the remediation at 75%.

e Potential sites in the City include Liberty Park, Pine Trails Park, Terramar Park, and the
Lox Road/Parkside Drive parcel.

e Broward County is basically saying the cities are on their own for finding sites to handle
their debris if they are hit by a category 3-5 storm.

eo TDMS’s require permitting in advance.

Commissioner Moskowitz suggested using the tract of land where the Farmers Market is held
and possibly getting an agreement with the Beattys for their parcel; the two together would
provide a large tract of land, centrally located, that would not disrupt the City’s regular business.
He also said there are private landowners with vacant parcels that an agreement could be reached
with.

Mayor Udine commented that he would rather not use one of the City parks because he does not
want to see the parks closed for so long.

Vice Mayor Smith agreed that the Farmers Market parcel would be a good location.
Commissioner Weissman asked if the City would have to prepare egress/ingress onto the
property and Public Works Director Berkman responded in the affirmative. Commissioner
Weissman said he wants to ensure that it would not encroach onto the Equestrian Center.
Commissioner Rosenof questioned whether these sites can be used by anybody else other that the
City.

Public Works Director Berkman explained that the sites are heavily controlled because
everything has to be accounted for and other people’s debris can not be comingled with what the
City collects.

2. DISCUSSION: PARK LIGHTING
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young introduced Parks & Recreation Director Scott Davidoff,
who gave a presentation, which included the following comments:

e City staff has received requests from residents to revisit the policy on City parks.

o The Parks & Recreation Board voted in 2007 and 2010 to keep the lights on for the
duration of the park hours, largely to avoid sending the message that the parks are
only for the sports leagues.

e Pine Trails, Quigley and Terramar parks are open until 10:00 p.m. and the sports
leagues are one of the big users of these parks in the evening hours.

e Additional park users include pick-up games, regular play, walkers, joggers, bicyclists
and Frisbee players.

e The parks are very dark when the lights are not on which presents some real safety
concerns.

o There is an existing rain-out policy where all the lights are turned off.

o Staff tried to do a cost study.

Mayor Udine stated that if the leagues are in the parks most nights of the week until 10:00 p.m.,
then he agrees the lights should be on. He said his bigger concern is that the lights should be off
on Sunday nights when it is raining. He noted that the rain-out rules should be followed, but
otherwise he wants the lights on until 10:00 p.m. and suggested looking at it in the summer time
and if nothing is going on, then the lights should be turned off.
Commissioner Moskowitz said that he feels the issue is better control and the park rangers
should have some flexibility to use their professional judgment. He stated that perhaps the lights
could be left on for the field closest to the parking rather than lighting the entire park.
Commissioner Weissman commented about people complaining about the lights when a park
was already established before they moved next door. He said that the parks have a lot of people
in them even when it does not look that way, and if it is not a cost factor, then it makes more
sense to keep the lights on.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young added that if the field lights are turned off, the sidewalks for
people walking or jogging become too dark and it becomes a safety issue.

what makes a good dog park and what issues might arise. He then reviewed the following
matters concerning a dog park:

Landscape Architect Planner Archer noted that staff estimates the maintenance to cost $20,000
annually and said a detailed cost estimate for a dog park is in the Commission’s backup.
Commissioner Moskowitz said that there is a need for a dog park in the City because dogs are
not allowed in the regular parks. He stated that of all the parks in the three cities near Parkland,
their dog parks are the most successful and added that dog parks are not for the dogs but for the
people who own dogs. Commissioner Moskowitz said that he feels this is something the City
should consider and could be creative in finding the funding for this park, possibly through
monies left over at the end of the year, raising money from development, and looking for
potential sponsorships. He commented that he is concerned with the potential parking problem;
this is something that will have to be addressed. Commissioner Moskowitz thanked ACAI and
Keith and Associates for their work on this matter.
Commissioner Weissman stated that he is not opposed to a dog park and likes the idea of getting
private sponsorships and contributions; however, there is no money in the park impact fees
account. He said that the park impact fee will be generating half of what it previously generated
due to the values and there will be competing interests for the available dollars to meet the need
for more developed park land. He added that the costs to develop that land are higher than the
costs to develop any of the fields so far, while at the same time the money being generated is
going to be lower than in the past. Commissioner Weissman said he is in favor of the dog park,
but does not want the dollars to come from development that is going on now. He suggested
perhaps a fundraising drive similar to that undertaken for Liberty Park could be used to fund
development of the dog park.
Commissioner Rosenof questioned the use of artificial turf and Parks & Recreation Director
Davidoff responded that the maintenance cost would be less, but there are health issues with
waste on the synthetics.
Mayor Udine commented that no one wants to spend dollars that are being competed for on so
many different levels. He asked about putting together a focus group to determine what the real
interest is, and said before details are hashed out, he would like to see buy-in from the
community.
Commissioner Moskowitz stated that he does not think the park should be turned into a
community project because it could take too long and he does not suggest taking money from
reserves to do this; instead, development should pay for development. He added that he would
like to see this park accomplished within the next year and said because there may be
development starting again in Parkland Golf & Country Club and Heron Bay, perhaps the
developers could donate money to offer this park as an amenity to the City.
