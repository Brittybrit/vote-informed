1. DISCUSSION: HOLMBERG ROAD DESIGN & FUNDING
Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young made brief introductory remarks. She recalled that
staff had in the past worked on a level of service issue for Holmberg Road, and she
acknowledged that traffic backed up at certain times during the day. She believed that a second
issue of aesthetics should be addressed, and four options have been suggested.
Planning Director Roberta Moore offered information about the four options and their cost
estimates: resurfacing Holmberg Road; adding bike lanes, thereby increasing the road width a
total. of eight feet (four feet on each side); resurfacing and the addition. of the four-foot bike lanes;
and widening the road to a two-lane divided roadway, with medians, turn lanes, and bike lanes.
She noted that options 2, 3, and 4 would require the relocation of the existing trees. Ms. Moore
indicated that staff was seeking Commission direction, and she offered that she and Mr. Berkman
could answer any questions.
Ms. Gardner-Young commented that this would be a phased project; it would not be done in one
year. She offered that she did not want to spend funds to resurface should the Commission want
to go in another direction.
Commissioner Weissman noted that bike lanes were proposed to the west of N.W. 62nd Terrace,
and he questioned adding bike lanes to the east. Mr. Berkman believed that the estimate included
the bike lanes from 441 west to University Drive, and he would double-check those figures. Ms.
Gardner-Young noted that a June Sty' memo included the bike lanes from 441 to University Drive.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz agreed that the bike lanes should be constructed to University Drive. He
asked confirmation that only the four feet of asphalt would be added to either side of Holmberg
Road from Riverside Drive to University Drive, as the road itself did not need additional work.
Mr. Berkman suggested that an overlay would be needed at some point in time.
The Vice Mayor suggested that not all of the trees on either side of Holmberg Road from
Riverside to University would need to be relocated, since they varied in distance from the
roadway. Environmental Services Director Brian Archer indicated that adding the bike lanes to
any portion of Holmberg Road would require the relocation of the trees because they would be

too close to the traffic lanes. There would be two options: relocate the trees to elsewhere in the

Vice Mayor Moskowitz agreed that a plan for Holmberg Road should be established and in place

Vice Mayor Moskowitz indicated that he would like to leave Holmberg Road as a two lane road

riding bikes to and from school or to the parks used the sidewalks; they were not using the bike
lanes. While he would like to do it, he would not hold up redoing Holmberg Road if the City did
not have the money for the bike lanes. The Vice Mayor suggested including the bike lanes in the
proposal, and it could be deleted should the funds not be available.
The Vice Mayor also suggested that, in addition to the bike lanes, a few feet of curbing should be
added to every Pinetree street entrance off Holmberg Road. When there was no curbing, turning
vehicles threw rock into the street, which he became road hazards.
As to the last option, Vice Mayor Moskowitz indicated he would not favor a median because it
would change the character of the road. He suggested, however, that areas in need of additional
left turn lanes should be analyzed; he felt this need should not be overlooked.
Mayor Udine commented that he liked Option 3. With the Mayor's questions, staff clarified that:
the overall width of the roadway would be increased by a total of eight feet; Option 1 would
resurface the road as it is and Option 2 added only the bikes lanes; and the City owned sufficient
right-of--way for the widening. Mayor Udine reiterated that he would prefer Option 3, and he
agreed with the additional enhancements mentioned by the Vice Mayor. He would like to see it
brought back as a plan.
Ms. Gardner-Young offered that the intention would be to start setting funds aside, working
toward a segmented plan without incurring additional costs. She believed the options mentioned
by the Vice Mayor could be easily placed within the plan.
Vice Mayor hoped, notwithstanding tax implications, that the project could be completed by the
end of 2009. Ms. Gardner-Young indicated that feasibility would be examined.
Commissioner Weissman stated that he would like to know that the costs would not increase
substantially by segmenting the project. He hoped that several options would be offered, and the

2. DISCUSSION: COMMUNITY CENTER
Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young indicated that Leisure Services Director Scott
Davidoff would give a presentation regarding the Community Center and the amphitheater.
Recalling previous Commission questions, she indicated that Mr. Davidoff would provide
information about staffing possibilities, programming, and furniture and equipment. She asked
Commission feedback for staff direction.
Leisure Services Director Scott Davidoff gave a PowerPoint presentation, copies of which were
included in the agenda packet. He indicated the. anticipated opening would occur in late
December or early January.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz questioned the potential revenue of similar venues. Mr. Davidoff stated
that this would be investigated. He had not been able to identify other municipalities with an
outdoor venue of similar size and without fixed seating. The City's facility would be unique, and
it would be difficult to compare different usages. The Vice Mayor commented that, while the
negatives of having the amphitheater open to pre-approved uses were understood, it would be
interesting to see if it might be a significant revenue source.

Mayor Udine agreed, and added that he would be interested to know about abreak-even point.
He explained: should the facility be leased and become a profit generator, that would be fine;
however, if the City were to limit use of the facility to a few smaller events, he questioned what
that break-even number would be so that it did not cost the taxpayers additional dollars.
Commissioner Weissman commented that the biggest issue of concern would be potential
damages. He suggested this should be provided in the costs and charges to promoters.
The issue of naming rights was discussed. Mayor Udine believed it an important question that
had been repeatedly brought up and that the Commission should direct staff as to what might be
considered. With a significant revenue source, the Mayor indicated that he would be willing to
entertain the idea, and he suggested that there should be a consensus position.
Commissioner Weissman asked that staff investigate any existing contracts in other
municipalities so that the City might structure it to a lump sum up front as well as an annual fee.
He indicated that he would entertain a proposal for. aseven-figure number.
Mayor Udine offered that he was aware that the County was considering naming rights for parks
and other facilities, and he suggested there may be a way to piggyback on their information. Ms.
Gardner-Young indicated that she had already obtained the County ordinance.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz stated that he would be interested in giving the naming rights for a
significant revenue source.
Addressing the Community Center, Mr. Davidoff indicated that the three operational options
were the largest issues under consideration. He explained Option 1, which would result in an
approximately net cost to the City of $731,000.
Mayor Udine believed it important to note for the record that the annual operating costs during
the first discussions and approval of the Community Center project in March 2006 were
estimated between $200,000 and $250,000. Citing the Minutes of the March 1, 2006, meeting,
he recalled that he had questioned those numbers and had made a motion to table, which failed.
The Mayor indicated that he would have liked to have seen these numbers when the approval was
given. He admitted that he was very nervous with this option and the costs involved. Noting the
recent events in Tallahassee and using the Library as a comparison, it would be very difficult to
open this facility at a $731,000 annual recurring cost and then take any part of it away. While
these were the things that made a community great, the Mayor believed it a different and
changing time. He stated that he would not approve that kind of annual net cost to residents.
Commissioner Weissman asked confirmation that Option 1 and Option 2 contained similar
programming operations, and Ms. Gardner-Young responded in the affirmative. He questioned
that a private enterprise could run the programs profitably and pay the City almost a quarter
million dollars while, with the City running the same programming, the gross receipts would be
only $145,000.

Ms. Gardner-Young suggested that the cost to the residents under Option 2 would be greater than
that run by the City. It would be turnkey operation; the YMCA would have complete control of
the building and what they charge.
Commissioner Weissman believed that the YMCA could generate $250,000 of revenue to the
City and make it profitable for themselves at a cost to residents. The residents would use the
Community Center, a public building in a public park, and pay substantially more for the
programs.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that the reason why the YMCA would make more money is
because they would allow non-residents. Mr. Davidoff clarified that the YMCA and fitness clubs
made their money through memberships. These figures were based upon what had been
presented at the January workshop. The rates for the YMCA were double, and he confirmed that
they would allow non-residents.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested that the City would have charged its residents a reasonable
fee, and they would charge a private corporate fee. He asked if the City might do the same.
Commissioner Weissman questioned that the City's residents, who have paid for the building,
would have any benefit as City residents. Mr. Davidoff responded in the affirmative, and he
confirmed that the City could specify that a reduced fee be charged. The YMCA had proposed,
for example, that a resident youth membership would be $20 per month and anon-resident would
be charged $30 per month, a $120 difference per year; a family membership would be nearly a
$200 difference. Mr. Davidoff noted that the YMCA also had grant opportunities.
Commissioner Weissman commented that he was not opposed to turning it over and avoiding the
potential shortfall. He did not believe that City government could run the facility as efficiently.
Mayor Udine offered that both the YMCA and the JCC were non-profit organizations and
subsidized by their agencies. The Commissioner's point was well taken. He did not believe it
possible at this time for the City to open the building, incurring an expense of $700,000 a year
and subsidy by the taxpayers.
Commissioner Weissman assumed that the expenses of the equipment would be borne by .the
lessee. Mr. Davidoff stated that the YMCA looked to have all of the fitness equipment
purchased by the City and ready for use; it would be maintained by the YMCA. The
responsibility for repairs would be negotiated in the agreement. Ms. Gardner-Young added that
it would be similar to that recently negotiated for tennis, and she believed they must have some
accountability for the equipment.
Commissioner Weissman questioned the upfront costs. Mr. Davidoff indicated that the total for
equipment and furniture would be approximately $400,000. The Commissioner then asked if the
City might charge a lease fee for the equipment. Mr. Davidoff replied that would be an option,
and he offered explanation concerning the calculation of the $10 per square foot. The YMCA
had today submitted a proposal so that the City might have real numbers to examine; they would

pay the City $100,000 for the first year, which calculates to $5 per square foot, with potential
increases based upon their revenue over the course of the agreement. Commissioner Weissman
suggested that a percentage of gross over and above a certain base could be built into the
agreement, and Mr. Davidoff indicated that could be done. The Commissioner noted that $10
per square foot was approximately one-third of current commercial leases.
Ms. Gardner-Young stated that, regardless of the option selected, there would still be costs to the
City. At the Vice Mayor's further question, Mr. Davidoff estimated that, based on their figures
and with everything equal in all three proposals, the City would still pay $11,000 per year.
Mayor Udine noted that the City's costs would drop significantly -- from over $700,000 yearly to
$11,000 -and the Community Center would be there for the residents' use. He commented that
it was not a difficult choice. He would not vote to incur another $750,000 a year operating cost.
His position would be that he would prefer to keep the building closed an extra year.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked when the proposals might be brought to the Commission, and Ms.
Gardner-Young stated that she had first wanted Commission direction. The Vice Mayor agreed
with Commissioner Weissman in that the YMCA proposal would require some "tweaking," and
Ms. Gardner-Young confirmed this would be a starting point. Mr. Davidoff offered that the
YMCA had asked for afive-year agreement.
Ms. Gardner-Young questioned the Mayor's suggestion to keep the building closed. Mayor
Udine offered that his statement had been half-jest. The scheduled opening in December or
January could easily become May or June with a hurricane, which would be right at the
beginning of summer when there would be less use. Ms. Gardner-Young noted that the grant
required completion of the amphitheater by January 2008, and Mr. Davidoff offered that the
grant also included the ball fields, the fishing pier, pavilions, and restroom. None of the grants
were tied to the Community Center building.
At the Mayor's reiteration that he would not want to incur another $750,000 per year in operating
costs, Commissioner Weissman noted that it would also require the expenditure of $400,000 for
the furniture and equipment, whether budgeted or not, in order to open the doors. Vice Mayor
Moskowitz commented that some of that $400,000 cost could be offset with additional
negotiation. He did not know the YMCA's policies and past experiences, but he suggested that
the numbers could be improved dramatically based upon knowledge of prior negotiations with
other entities.
Mr. Davidoff noted a third "hybrid" option: the City might contract out to the YMCA or other
wellness company to operate and manage all fitness programming, which would have a net cost
of $557,000. He suggested the City could raise the rates to that proposed by the YMCA.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz compared the cost to the City of the three options, and he believed it an
easy decision.

Mayor Udine agreed. He spoke of the Weston facility operated by the YMCA, and he recalled
that the YMCA had looked to open in this area in commercial space.
Commissioner Weissman suggested that one Commission member be designated to sit in on the
negotiations. He believed it would be easier than sitting in a "void" and speculating what the
YMCA might do for the City and what the City might get from them. The Commissioner
volunteered to do so.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz wanted to be certain that the City did not negotiate itself out of an
agreement..
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis stated that the Commission would not delegate the authority to
negotiate to any one .Commissioner, but the Commissioner could be present ~~vhen the City
Manager hears presentations and things of that nature.
Ms. Gardner-Young commented that she and Mr. Davidoff now understood what the
Commission would be seeking, and they could provide the current proposal for Commission
comments. She suggested that the YMCA appearance before the Commission would follow the
negotiation by staff, if the Commission were comfortable with that.
Mayor Udine believed this would make the process easier.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz suggested that the agreement specifically state that a meeting with the
YMCA would take place in 90 or 120 days into the agreement for an opportunity to make
suggestions or give feedback as to how the YMCA operation was working in Parkland.
Mayor Udine agreed, and he also believed that the City should contractually have the ability to
use some of the meeting rooms as the owner of the building. He reiterated his belief that there
would be no way the City could open the facility on its own.
Commissioner Smith commented regarding dance classes that may be offered by the YMCA and
two successful area dance studios owned by in-laws. He did not believe the Commission would
vote for anything that would hurt area businesses, and he wanted it on record that he would not
hurt his own family.
Mayor Udine believed that the YMCA, in order to make the venture successful, must charge a
decent amount. He noted that residents could not expect to build a community center, have
inexpensive programming, and have their taxes cut, too; someone must pay, and this is the first
example. He asked staff to investigate the YMCA proposal and other outside organizations.
Mr. Davidoff commented that the YMCA or the JCC were the only two organizations that do
everything -- wellness and the community portion. Mayor Udine noted that, as evidenced at the
previous meeting, the City could not diminish services without the expectation of a big fight
from residents.

Finance and Administrative Services Director Barbara Hastings asked, in comparing the costs for
the three options, if staff might also run the numbers to compare "apples to apples": the entity
that the City may potentially contract with and the City running the, facility charging similar fees.
Staff felt that, with a hybrid option such as Option. 3, the City may be able to do the same or
better should the Commission permit similar fees to outside vendors. A presentation on both
options would then be made.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz felt that it could not be "apples to apples"; he felt there would be no way
that the City could cover its costs, particularly if the numbers were to jump exponentially. He
believed it would be a huge risk to the City, and he would not vote in favor.
Mayor Udine noted that the City did not know how the YMCA would operate, but suggested that
people would not be willing to pay the same amount of money for a program at a municipal
facility. However, he believed that the numbers should be examined first.
Commissioner Weissman stated that he would also like to see what the contract would read
because the YMCA may also have an "out" that the City may riot anticipate at this point.
Commissioner Smith related his experiences while on the Board of Directors of the Soref Jewish
Community Center in Plantation for many years. He noted that, in the rebuilding of their facility
and the decision whether or not to build a fitness facility, other JCCs around the country had
overwhelming debt because the costs to run the gym exceeded collections; naming opportunities
were utilized as supplemental revenue. He doubted the venture would be profitable.
Mayor Udine believed that all of the comments were valid. With the Community Center nearing
completion, something must be done. He admitted that the City's decision would be based upon
Tallahassee's direction as the City would not have the growth dollars coming in with taxes. He
believed that the City must take the best of the not-so-great options.
With further discussion, Mr. Davidoff cautioned the Commission that the City's facility should
not be compared to that of Weston's. He noted that, not only was it larger by 20,000 feet, but the
City of Weston had given the land to the YMCA at a dollar per year fora 100-year lease, and
they had raised the money for the building.
Ms. Gardner-Young stated that staff understood the Commission's wishes and further details
would be brought back.
