III PUBLIC INPUT

Randy Correll, 5977 N.W. 73rd Court, spoke regarding Commissioner Weissman's residency and
the recent election of Vice Mayor. He questioned how the Commission's commitment to a one-
year term in August could have changed in three months. He also criticized the use of "VIP" at
events.

Natalie Bigio of Pinetree Estates commented that she had attended the first "Movies in the Park"
on Friday night. It had been well-attended and a great success. She had been glad to see the
future scheduling on the City's website.

Laura Laramee, 6750 N.W. 815t Terrace, first extended holiday greetings and thanked the
Commission for their service and leadership. She spoke regarding the efforts of "Parkland for
Tennis" for support toward a short-term solution for an adequate tennis facility and clay courts.
"Parkland for Tennis" requests that the Commission consider allocating surplus budget funds for
clay conversion of a minimum of Courts 5, 6, and 7 at Terramar Park after the safety issues there
are resolved. The group hoped the City would pursue avenues of funding through UST A grants,
Communities for a Lifetime, or capital improvements with tennis at the forefront and that the
master plan consultant would give tennis players a new facility at Pine Trails or in the "Wedge."

Brenda Selner, 8899 N.W. 66th Lane, also spoke regarding tennis. While she represented an
older population, she assured the Commission that most tennis players were their peers. The
current facilities were not adequate or acceptable to any group. She hoped for Commission
consensus to support tennis in the City.

Mayor Udine indicated that the Commissioners would respond during Commission Comments.

Lisa Ramos, 6504 N.W. 78th Place, also spoke regarding junior tennis at Terramar Park. She
noted that the program had grown considerably since May; the teens had excelled and were
playing in clay-court tournaments. It was felt that the teens would seek other clubs to improve
their play on clay courts, and she believed that two of the seven hard courts should be resurfaced.

Former Mayor Harold Bockhold applauded the Commission for their efforts to improve and
beautify the City, and wished them happy holidays.

PRESENTATION:

WINNER'S CIRCLE

City Manager Mark Lauzier briefly reviewed the background related to the devastation of the
buffer. The Pine Tree Water Control District recommended solutions for both Winner's Circle
and Country Acres would be presented.

John Petty, Pine Tree Water Control District Manager, spoke regarding Winner's Circle and
remedial repairs for damages caused by their contractor in their removal of trees affected by
Hurricane Wilma. He described a three-tiered buffer comprjsed of 32 live oaks, 100 Dahoon
holly, and 900 three-gallon firebush plants, and they had worked with staff to give a complete
vegetative buffer and block all visual alleys. While not the height of Australian pines, he
believed the buffer would be better than before.

Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer noted that plant materials were native. The live
oaks would be strategically located to block particular views across the canal from Country
Place, and the mid-story Dahoon holly would be staggered. He noted that the firebush would
grow to the bottom of the Dahoon holly. Approximately 930 linear feet had been damaged.

Mr. Lauzier commented regarding the concerns of the equestrian community for the maintenance
or installation of an equestrian trail next to the asphalt path. As the plantings were projected to
be within two to three feet of the embankment, he believed that both invasive material and other
prior plantings, such as the Hong Kong orchids, must be removed in order to restore a path area.

Mr. Archer explained the configuration of the area. With the Winner's Circle buffer on the east
side of the asphalt path, the 17-foot City trailway easement was comprised of the asphalt path

for a longer period of time, which would bring the plantings closer to rainy season.

Commissioner Weissman stated that he supported Commissioner McGilvray's remarks regarding
the horse trail.

Vice Mayor Moskowitz noted that the damaged property did not belong to Pinetree Water
Control District, but to Broward County. With Mr. Lauzier's comment regarding the formation
of the water control districts in the late 1960's and early 1970's, Mr. Petty added considerable
explanation regarding their maintenance of the flow way for Broward County. The Vice Mayor
questioned documentation, stating that his position was that, without authorization, the District
had no right to clear the trees. Mr. Petty stated that, of the damage to Winner's Circle, there was
no argument; they had no right to clear and they were making every effort to restore the area.

$8,000 for the tOO-foot area to the south. Discussion ensued. At the Vice Mayor's concern that
the City may not be satisfied with the end result, Mr. Petty commented that the District would
look for the City to relieve it of any future liability over this issue. They were not looking for
release of liability upon acceptance of the plan, but rather with acceptance of the contract and
following the planting.

Vice Mayor Moskowitz summarized that the options were: spend $100,000 to remove exotic
vegetation; do nothing; or spend $8,000 for the area to the south. He was emphatic that the
District should not be relieved of any liability until the project was completed and signed off by
the City. He believed the horse trail and other clearing issues should be put aside until this phase
was completed.
