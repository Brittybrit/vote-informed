1. LEGISLATIVE UPDATE: RUSS KLENET
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young introduced City lobbyist Russ Klenet, who gave a legislative
session update.
Mr. Klenet stated that the City of Parkland had non-stop issues and then commented on the
following topics:

e Seminole Gaming Compact — the City will be entitled to a percentage of the revenue, which
will increase each year going forward.

e House Bill 965 — the only Chinese drywall remediation bill to pass; it pertains to real
property assessments.

e Texting while driving bill — three students from Stoneman Douglas testified before the
Senate Transportation Committee and the bill passed the Senate 34 to 4. The House did not
get an opportunity to consider the bill, but it will come back next year.

e Municipal police pension plans — Senate bill 2106 passed four committees and because of
this, next year there is a better chance. Senator Ring pushed this bill and Rep. Kiar helped
get it through a committee in the House.

e The annexation bill — the bill was well-crafted and everything was done the right way, but
for whatever reason, they could not get the bill heard by the Community Affairs
Committee. He said he could not explain what happened or why the delegation never
brought the bill in front of the committee and noted that Rep. Kiar and Sen. Ring tried
everything they could.

Commissioner Moskowitz commented on the gaming compact and the texting bill, and spoke
about the frustration with the annexation bill. He said that he feels the problem was with the
Broward delegation and he would like to have the chair of the delegation in front of the
Commission to explain his strategy to ensure this moves forward in a special session.
Mayor Udine stated that there were a lot of Chinese drywall bills that did not pass that would have
been detrimental to homeowners with Chinese drywall. Regarding the casino, he said that in the
beginning, the City’s cut of the revenue was zero so it was worth having representation at the table.

6. CONSIDERATION: PINE ISLAND MEDIAN MAINTENANCE

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young introduced Planning & Zoning Director Roberta Moore, who
gave a brief presentation and explained that during discussions between Parkland Reserve and
Broward County regarding a median maintenance agreement for Pine Island Road, the County
indicated that the City had to be a party to a tri-party agreement.
Planning & Zoning Director Moore said that staff reviewed the tri-party agreement that was
prepared by both Parkland Reserve and Broward County; ultimately, it is the County that is making
some of these changes. She stated that staff has some concerns about the proposed tri-party
agreement; in particular, if the Parkland Reserve HOA went defunct, all responsibility would fall
back on the City, which would typically not be budgeted for.
Commissioner Weissman said that he believes the City does not have an option and has to execute
the tri-party agreement because this is a County road. He asked if the City could have a separate
agreement with the developer, which would then be made a mandatory part of the homeowner
association documents and would be an obligation that the City could enforce against the
homeowners.
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis commented that a secondary agreement would simplify the City’s
rights directly against the HOA.

5. RESOLUTION NO. 2010-47: ~~ PARK IMPACT FEES
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, SPECIFICALLY
SETTING FORTH THE VALUE OF LAND FOR THE
PURPOSES OF CALCULATING PARK IMPACT FEES.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2010-47, Park Impact Fees,
and explained that the City collects park impact fees based upon the value of the property. She
stated that the last time an analysis on the value of the property was done was in 2006 and it was
determined that the appropriate value was approximately $423,000 per acre. She said since then,
the housing bust occurred and land values have decreased.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young stated that two separate appraisals were obtained, indicating
that the proper value is $220,000 per acre.
Commissioner Rosenof asked how often this is done and City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young
responded that there is not a set time; the Commission, in conjunction with staff, determines the
appropriate time to do another analysis.
Commissioner Rosenof questioned whether it would be more appropriate to do it at a regular
interval so that it is not capricious.
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis commented that the key is whether the impact fee reflects the cost
of the land.
Discussion was held about the pros and cons of having appraisals done at regular intervals.
City Attorney Maurodis explained that the reason this appraisal was done was because a significant
period of time had passed since the last one and it was clear the value was way out of line. He
stated that appraisals are done at the City’s discretion, he does not perceive a problem, and he
would not be concerned about a legal challenge for not having a specific interval. He noted that
the biggest legal challenge would be if the City is not in line with the price.
MOTION made by Commissioner Moskowitz to approve Resolution No. 2010-47, Park Impact
Fees. Seconded by Commissioner Weissman.
