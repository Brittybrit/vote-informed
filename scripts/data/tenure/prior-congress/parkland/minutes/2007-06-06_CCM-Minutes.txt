III PUBLIC INPUT
Mayor Udine acknowledged the people present to voice their concerns regarding the future of the
City's Library, which he believed was based upon conjecture and speculation. Anticipating that
Senator Ring may shed more light on the situation, he offered that the Legislature was set to meet
in Special Session beginning the following week to discuss the property tax issue. He suggested
that there may be atwo-pronged approach: a January 29th ballot regarding the Save Our Homes
portability and homestead exemption, which changes would take effect with the 2009 tax year,
and aroll-back of government spending.
The Mayor explained that a comment had been made during the Commission's Strategic
Planning session that every City program and services must be examined in order to save money
with respect to the budget reductions anticipated from Tallahassee. He stated that there had been
no plan made to close anything or to take any action, and he spoke of possible actions under
consideration by other cities.
Sid Wise, Aston Gardens, spoke in favor of the Library and its programs. He offered his support
through contact of Tallahassee
Jacob Brodzki, Aston Gardens, also spoke in favor of the Library and its programs. He stated
that he utilizes the Library for regular research for a program he conducts at Aston Gardens. He
urged that the funds be found to keep this valuable resource open.
Perry Eisenberg, Pine Tree Estates, questioned the "trickle-down effect" and the process to
decide the elimination of programs. .Mayor Udine explained the anticipated roll-back of taxes
and the necessity to prioritize expenditures. Mr. Eisenberg asked whether citizens had the ability

1. LEGISLATIVE UPDATE BY SENATOR JEREMY RING
Senator Jeremy Ring commented that he was also a Parkland resident, and he cared deeply about
the City.
Senator Ring offered that the reality of Tallahassee was that they truly did not know what would
happen with the property tax issue. There was a huge philosophical divide about how great the
cuts would be; the Senate would like to pass approximately 15 percent, while Speaker Rubio's
plan called for approximately 50 percent. He anticipated the release of new numbers within days,
and the Special Session would be dependent upon the numbers growing closer. The Legislature
was unified in the recognition of inequity related to property taxes, and he viewed it a significant
issue needing action.
Senator Ring believed, however, that insurance remained the State's largest issue. He spoke
regarding the insurance crisis: what premiums are paid and the risk to taxpayers and the State's
economy should a big storm hit. As to insurance costs to property .owners, the Legislature had

put forth a tremendous number of new policies: eliminating Florida-only subsidiaries to new
companies; substantiating requests for rate increases by providing the profit figures earned by the
parent company; prohibiting cherry-picking; and expanding Citizens to make it more
competitive. A $35 billion catastrophic fund had been created with the idea of selling re-
insurance. The Senator was not sure that these actions were enough; while the bleeding had been
stopped, people were still losing their policies. It was a major crisis, and he believed the fix must
come from the Federal government via a national catastrophic fund.
Senator Ring indicated that he had sponsored legislation regarding election reforms, which
included voting with a paper trail. However, the larger part of the bill was moving the
presidential primary to January 29t~'. He believed that, as the first state in the country, the vote
will not be disenfranchised and,- more importantly, any candidate campaigning in the State must
campaign on its issues. He noted that major candidates had endorsed the national catastrophic
fund, which had happened since the bill had been signed. He believed it would be a key part of
the upcoming presidential election and that candidates would campaign on that issue.
Regarding the risk to taxpayers should the State be hit by a big storm, it had the potential to be
crippling -with increased sales taxes, huge bond issuances, and much larger premiums. Senator
Ring believed that the risk should be transferred from the taxpayers into the open financial
markets. He suggested there was a significant demand for different hurricane financial
instruments, such as catastrophic bonds, wind, barometric futures, etc. He believed Florida
should be the leader in buying, selling, .and trading any sort of financial instruments related to
catastrophic events, and he had given this his priority while not in session.
Senator Ring spoke regarding the Coconut Creek casino and its potential components: a 28-story
hotel, 1,500 hotel rooms, 160,000 square feet of gambling, and anine-story parking garage. To
place it in perspective, the entire Hard Rock casino project in Hollywood could fit into its
parking lot. He believed it would compromise the quality of life in northwest Broward County.
There are currently five acres of sovereign land; the Tribe had acquired another 44 acres and had
requested to add that land to the Trust. Government would lose all control, and the potential
exists that the single largest casino in the world could be built. Senator Ring stated that it was
not about gambling, but he had not anticipated living in "Las Vegas." He lauded Coconut
Creek's letter of opposition to the Bureau of Indian Affairs. The Senator acknowledged the
City's participation in the regional impact study, which had been presented to the Governor for
his use in compact negotiations with the Seminoles. He recommended that each affected city
send a letter to the Bureau of Indian Affairs to oppose the 44 acres going into the Trust.
Senator Ring imagined traffic on 441 on a Friday night. He suggested that the issue of traffic
might be addressed in other ways, through extension of the Sawgrass, extension of Wiles Road to
the Turnpike, and another lane on 441, but he felt that all of these efforts would not offset the
impacts of the casino.
As to the extension of University Drive, Senator Ring stated he was in an awkward position
because he represented both Parkland and Coral Springs -one in opposition and one in favor.
The decision must be made by the Department of Community Affairs, and he imagined that they

IV COMMENTS BY MAYOR AND COMMISSION
Commissioner Weissman noted that he was the only commissioner who actually sat on the dais
when the vote was taken to build the Library, and he stated that he had an emotional attachment
to the Library.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz acknowledged residents' comments about the Library, recognizing the
interests of Aston Gardens residents and the dedication of the Friends of the Library. He stated
that no one wants to cut the Library. The reality was that residents could object to any item
considered, and he expected that the City would easily receive 500 signatures on petitions of
objection.. He described the situation as a win/win for State government, but lose/lose for local
levels. He acknowledged that people needed tax relief, but local government would be the bearer
of the bad news through cuts in services to their residents. The Vice Mayor explained that, with
a 15 percent tax cut, the City would lose $3 million. Should that happen, he believed there no
question that the City would adopt an electric franchise fee, worth $1 million to the City but
costing the taxpayers an additional 10 percent in their electric bills. He spoke of vacant positions
in City staff which would not be filled, but he believed the most difficult would be possible lay-
off of personnel. All discretionary spending must be examined. He described it as a serious
situation and reiterated that, while tax relief is needed, taxes provide services.
The Vice Mayor noted that the operational expenses of the Community Center had been
estimated by former City Manager Mark Lauzier at $250,000 per year; it would actually cost
$850,000 per year, which would be $600,000 more than budgeted. He believed it possible that
the City would not operate the facility. This would not be a policy decision; it was mathematics.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz spoke of the Wedge, the development of which would bring revenues to
the City -much of which could used to improve the infrastructure of the- eastern, older parts of
the City and expand the programs that the City may need to cut now.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz assured that Commission members would make themselves available to
residents to discuss the cuts and, once the City had numbers, every organization would be
consulted. He urged residents to contact their elected State representatives to make known their
feelings regarding the potential cuts in services.
Mayor Udine referred to a memo the Commission had received regarding the Pumpkin Patch.
He directed that the City do what it could to help the Woman's Club, including the reservation of
Liberty Park during that time.
The Mayor offered that City departments were being asked to cut their budgets. He noted that a
letter had been sent out to all programs that the City helps fund, alerting them to the potential
cuts. There was no reason to hold individual meetings with groups because the details are not yet
known.

Interim City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the second reading of Ordinance No. 2007-14.
She offered that Environmental Resources Director Brian Archer was present to answer any
questions. She believed that there had been no changes made since first reading. The City
Attorney had reviewed the Ordinance and was in agreement with the proposed language.
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis commented that these were largely technical changes that makes
the Ordinances stronger, ties it more directly into the South Florida Water Management District,
and staff recommended the Commission's approval.
Mayor Udine noted that, even though it had been recently raining, significant rainfall was needed
to raise the water levels and recover from the drought. This an important tool for BSO's
enforcement of the mandatory Phase 3 restrictions. He believed adherence to the restrictions was
improving.
Mr. Maurodis commented, for the record, that Section 7 would be completed to refer to the
Resolution passed at the previous meeting.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked that all homeowners' associations be contacted to advise them of
the City's actions and to reaffirm their community efforts to curtail water use.
Ms. Gardner-Young advised that a letter had been sent to all homeowners' associations when
Phase 3 was implemented. There had also been e-mail blasts. Additionally, she and Chief
O'Connell attend community meetings, and the communities had been verbally advised.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz questioned whether adherence was being monitored. Ms. Gardner-
Young responded that it was being monitored; Chief O'Connell provided information weekly,
and she would provide that information to the Commission.
Mayor Udine opened to the public
Bob Mayersohn, 6742 N.W. 80t~' Manor, commented that the restrictions had been discussed at
their homeowners' association. He asked whether residents should call BSO to report violations
in the event a resident failed to heed a "friendly" comment from their neighbor. The Mayor and
Ms. Gardner-Young responded affirmatively.
There were no further comments or questions, and Mayor Udine closed to the public.
MOTION made by Vice Mayor Moskowitz to approve the second reading of Ordinance No.
2007-14, Amending Code of Ordinances on Water Restrictions. Seconded by Commissioner
Weissman.
