1. DISCUSSION: 2012-2013 CITY OF PARKLAND BUDGET
Mayor Udine explained that four resident budget workshops were held which were
advertised in the newspaper and via email blast. He pointed out that there were no
residents at any of the four budget meetings. He also explained that staff requested
comments from residents on the budget and did not receive any comments at all.

Ms. Nancy Morando, Finance Director gave a recap of the budget documents that were
prepared in July. She reiterated that the City of Parkland still has a balanced budget.
Although minor changes took place between July to date, none were significant.
She explained that the millage rate was decreased to 3.999 which reduced the total value
received from ad valorem by $56,000.00. She explained that the current year gross
taxable value is $3 billion dollars. Prior year’s gross taxable value was $2.9 billion
which is related to $42 million new construction and a total increase in taxable value of
$103 million.
She explained that the current roll back rate is $3.9372 and there was no change to the
fire assessment rate, however the fire rescue contract went up by 3.7% which equates to
$190,000.00. In accordance with the Solid Waste Franchise Agreement, solid waste
increased by the rate of the CPI which was $10.08 per dwelling unit annually or 3%.
Ms. Morando pointed out that 20% of the total tax bill goes to Parkland with the
Broward School Board receiving 38% and Broward County receiving 28%. She
explained that since July 2012, staffing levels changed slightly by one position. She
showed a big picture overview of $27 million as the proposed budget with three funds
that make up the proposed budget - $22.9 million (General Fund); $4.3 million (Capital
Projects Fund); and $146,000 (Capital Replacement Fund). She then went through each
of the three funds highlighting important projects that will take place in the next fiscal
year.
Mayor Udine commented that the residents are receiving a high quality budget, the City
Commission is lowering the millage rate, and property values are rising.
Commissioner Rosenof clarified that the fleet and facility replacement fund were two
separate funds and wanted clarification that these funds would be used to replace the a/c
unit if it should break. He also asked if a calculation was done of what the millage rate
reduction and other fees would do to an average homeowner. He read in an article that
Parkland’s millage rate is going down, but other fees are going up.
Ms. Morando explained that the article is referring to the increase in the solid waste
assessment which is merely a pass through, so the headline in the article is a bit
misleading.
Commissioner Rosenof questioned if there would be more than onc Sergeant on duty in
any shift.
Chief Stoner replied that there would be one Sergeant per shift, however onc shift is
currently being run by an OIC (Officer in Charge).

Ms. Morando shared with Commissioner Moskowitz that $250,000 was placed in the
budget for beautification and she offered to share with him a list of the budgeted
projects.
Commissioner Moskowitz asked staff to come up with a tree replacement policy to
cover replacement of tree canopy should a natural disaster take place.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young replied that under the cxisting Code, the
homeowner has the obligation to replace.
Mr. Brian Archer, Public Works Director stated that based on the Environmental Work
Plan, there are requirements in the Code for trees to be replaced.
Mayor Udine commented that he would like to take $25,000 from the $120,000 that is
earmarked for the 50" Anniversary event and place it in the YMCA fund just as a place
holder.
Mayor Udine commented that he would like the waiver put in again this year for the
Horseman's Association, although there is a memo in front of them. Ile stated that he
wants to ensure their horse shows take place which he feels adds to the City’s Farmer's
Market. Mayor Udine also would like Scott Davidoff, Parks Director to inform the
Horsemen's Association that it will be done this year, but it is not a guarantee.
Commissioner Willis asked about the increase in the elections budget.
Ms. Couzzo, City Clerk, stated that dollars were budgeted in anticipation of there being
two elections, but if the Charter Amendment passes, those dollars for a special election
may not be expended.
Mayor Udine opened to the public. There were none present and he closed to the public.
