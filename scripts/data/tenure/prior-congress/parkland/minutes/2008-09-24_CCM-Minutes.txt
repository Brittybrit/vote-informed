1. RUSS KLENET: UPDATE ON AMENDMENT 5 COMPACT
Russ Klenet, the City's lobbyist, presented an update on events of the past year, particularly with
respect to the economy and Florida's budget. He noted that Amendment 5 would not be on the
ballot, which would have resulted in $9.5 billion taken from public schools. He also spoke
regarding the status of the Indian gaming compact and the impacts of the Coconut Creek casino
on the City. Mr. Klenet assured that they would continue to work on transportation solutions in
the area, as well as with staff on prioritized capital projects over the next several years for State
and Federal funding. He spoke regarding the politics of Tallahassee and future reapportionment.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz thanked Mr. Klenet for his remarks and for the work he had
accomplished over the past year and a half. As to the casino, he recalled the Coconut Creek City
Manager's presentation, and he asked confirmation of the timeline. Mr. Klenet indicated that
debate continued within the Seminole administration as to the proposed trust acreage, and he
briefly reviewed the history of the Coconut Creek gaming land. He believed that once the
Legislature approves a compact, which he felt likely, the Seminoles would move quickly forward.
Mayor Udine expressed his thanks to Mr. Klenet for his help in Tallahassee with some important
issues. Mr. Klenet promised to keep the Commission posted.

5. RESOLUTION NO. 2008-100: SECOND AMENDMENT TO THE
ADMINISTRATIVE AND USER FEES
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA TO APPROVE THE
SECOND AMENDMENT TO THE ADOPTION OF
ADMINISTRATIVE AND USER FEE AND CHARGE
SCHEDULES FOR SERVICES, ACTIVITIES AND USE
OF FACILITIES PROVIDED BY THE CITY OF
PARKLAND; PROVIDING FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE.
City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the title of Resolution No. 2008 -100. She explained
that this amendment to the City's fee schedule was with respect to building fees. The fees would
cover the costs incurred by the City to provide the services, and she noted that the schedule had
also been made more user friendly. The fees were in line with those of other jurisdictions, and
staff recommended approval.
Commissioner Rosenof questioned whether any other city in the County based their building fees
on anything other than construction value. He gave as an example a gold plated fixture versus
one made of stainless steel, while the cost of inspection should be the same.
Building Official Margaret Bertolami indicated that she tended to agree. She acknowledged that
some municipalities did not use the cost of construction, but it did seem to be the most equitable
way. They have been using a weighted average for the past several years, equalizing the burden.
Commissioner Rosenof asked that this method be studied for any future revision considered.
MOTION made by Commissioner Smith to approve Resolution No. 2008 -100, Second
Amendment to the Administrative and User Fees. Seconded by Vice Mayor Moskowitz.

to the public.

City Manager Caryn Gardner -Young read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2008 -16.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz stated that he may motion to table unless the City Attorney could provide
some answers. He was concerned that, with laying out funds to resolve health and safety issues,
the City would not get its money back when a long list of creditors existed. He asked whether the
City might have some protection.
City Attorney Andrew Maurodis acknowledged there were problems with lien priorities and that
it was a difficult concept.
The Vice Mayor proposed tabling the Ordinance, pending a memo from the City Attorney
outlining the City's options. He would rather have the information before tackling the problem.
Commissioner Weissman recommended that the Ordinance be passed on first reading, as long as
the City Attorney would assure the City's ability to make changes; alternatively, the Ordinance
may not pass second reading. He was concerned that time may be of the essence in the current
economic situation.
Commissioner Rosenof asked whether there might be a way to bifurcate the Ordinance, as the
registration may be without controversy.
Mr. Maurodis suggested passage of the Ordinance as it stood, and he would contact each
commissioner for their ideas. Either a revised Ordinance for second reading or a new Ordinance
for first reading would be brought back. He suggested that, at the least, the City would be
moving forward.
Commissioner Smith noted the number of homes moving into foreclosure and short sale with both
first and second mortgages. He was concerned that the City would have assumed continuing
responsibility of maintenance with even one grass cutting.

<<PG>>

Mr. Maurodis did not believe so. He explained that the main thrust of the Ordinance was to have
the house registered: putting the burden on the mortgagee to maintain the property and giving the
City theoretical ability to fine them for not doing so.
Ms. Gardner -Young agreed that the City's assumption of maintenance was not the intention of
the Ordinance.
Mayor Udine commented that this had also been his concern. He was amenable to the idea of the
registration provision, and he would also agree that the City should intervene in cases with life,
health, or safety issues. He would not agree, however, with mowing a lawn; it would never end,
and there were not the funds to do so. With just one intervention, there would be demands to do
others. While the Mayor stated that he appreciated the problem, it was a slippery slope.
Mr. Maurodis suggested that, if the Commission liked the idea of registration which would
place the burden on the mortgagee, the Ordinance should be passed. He would make some
changes to make certain that the City was not placed in a position to undertake improvements.
Mayor Udine believed the Ordinance should be very specific that only life, health, and safety
issues would trigger the City's intervention, and he cautioned staff in this regard.
Commissioner Rosenof questioned whether the Unsafe Structures Board could come into play.
Mayor Udine commented that he had briefly served on the Unsafe Structures Board. The Board
can declare something unsafe, but they were in the same position as the City: they also did not
have the funds to take action. The County would declare the structure unsafe.
Mr. Maurodis provided further explanation about the foreclosure process and the responsibility of
maintenance.
Discussion ensued. Mr. Maurodis noted there was nothing in the Ordinance that would obligate
the City to maintain, and he stated that language would be added to affirmatively state the City
did not have that obligation. He agreed that questions of life, health, and safety could be brought
to the attention of the City, and specifically to the City Manager, through a variety of sources and
included authority under Florida Statutes Chapter 162 regarding code enforcement.
Mayor Udine opened to the public. There were no comments or questions, and the Mayor closed
to the public.
Mayor Udine commented that he would vote for passage of the Ordinance, with the
understanding that the City Attorney would bring back what needs to be done between first and
second reading.
MOTION made by Commissioner Weissman to approve the first reading of Ordinance No. 2008-
16, Maintenance of Abandoned Property, with all comments directed to the City Attorney for
revision prior to second reading. Seconded by Commissioner Rosenof.

9. DISCUSSION: JOINT MULTI CULTURAL EVENTS WITH
CORAL SPRINGS AND TAMARAC
Mayor Udine explained that he had received a phone call from Coral Springs Commissioner Ted
Mena, who serves as the chair of their multi cultural board. The northwest area cities, and as far
south as Plantation, had been invited to "piggyback" the multi cultural events held by Coral
Springs so that events would be held jointly, thus saving costs. The Mayor indicated that, while
there was no decision made, it was suggested that staff of the cities could try to work together on
certain events, giving examples of the National Day of Prayer, September 11 and Veterans'
Day.
Commissioner Rosenof agreed that it could be examined on a case -by -case basis.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz believed it a good idea but, until the economy improved and more events
could be held, it was an idea that should wait.
Commissioner Rosenof suggested that the City may wish to participate with Coral Springs for
their holiday parade.
Mayor Udine commented that he did not know whether staff had been in touch, nor whether they
would want a check from the City for its participation as that would become an issue. He would
prefer to leave it with staff, and he asked that they bring suggested joint events back to the
Commission for review.
