2A. DISCUSSION: IMPLEMENTATION OF A CAMERA
SURVEILLANCE SYSTEM

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young explained that this item is a joint review. The first part deals
with the implementation of the camera surveillance system that had previously been discussed. She
explained that when staff budgeted for two additional law enforcement officers this fiscal year,
Commissioner Moskowitz had questions whether two officers needed to be hired at the time since
the possibility of considering the camera surveillance system. The second part deals with amending
the BSO Agreement should the City Commission want to move forward with implementing the
camera surveillance system. As a result, Ms. Gardner-Young asked BSO Chief Stoner to provide a
presentation regarding a similar surveillance system that Lighthouse Point is currently using.

Chief Stoner gave a brief presentation on the license plate recognition technology. He explained
how the cameras would be strategically deployed throughout the City’s perimeter and detailed how
the information would be used once the license plate is read. A more detailed overview of the
camera surveillance system is found in the attached presentation.

Commissioner Moskowitz explained his reasoning for looking into this type of surveillance system.
He asked Police Chief Stoner if such a system would decrease crime or solve crime quicker.

Chief Stoner replied that a bigger deterrent would be to have unmarked vehicles in the area rather
than the surveillance system. He feels more personnel on the street would be better. He explained
that a team of two officers in marked or unmarked vehicles depending on the circumstance would
act accordingly based upon the incident.

Commissioner Moskowitz stated he would vote for the two police officers.

Commissioner Weissman feels that it is a false sense of security and he is not in favor of the
surveillance system. He feels many of Parkland’s crimes are crimes of opportunity created by the
homeowners. He asked what was the percentage of crime from outside of the City of Parkland.
Chief Stoner explained that since his tenure, several of the arrests have come from organized groups
from the south end of the County or Palm Beach County. Commander Brian Cowart concurred with
Chief Stoner’s assessment.

Commissioner Weissman explained that the vast number of homes have surveillance cameras at the
gate entrances. He stated if the cost was more reasonable than $500,000 considering the cconomic
state of the City, he cannot vote in favor of the system, but it can be looked at again in the future.
Vice Mayor Rosenof felt that an officer can be more proactive than 38 cameras can. He questioned
the Tegal limitation of the surveillance tape. He also asked if there could be a vehicle mounting or
mobile units. He asked that as an interim solution, if BSO could get vehicle camera mounting.
Mayor Udine asked how many entrances does Lighthouse Point cover?
