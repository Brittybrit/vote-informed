2, CONSIDERATION: YMCA SUBSIDY REQUEST
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young stated that in a previous meeting there was a request
provided to the City Commission for consideration of a subsidy to the YMCA. At that time, staff
was asked to do additional investigation and ultimately bring this matter back before the City
Commission. Ms. Gardner-Young introduced Scott Davidoff, Parks & Recreation Director and
asked him to bring the City Commission up to date on this issue.
Scott Davidoff stated that on August 18, 2010, the YMCA came to the City Commission
requesting a waiver of their lease payments, which the City Commission granted. The YMCA
also asked for a subsidy to cover their utilities, which the City Commission directed staff to bring
back some options to be discussed at a later date. Mr. Davidoff expressed that representatives
from the YMCA are present to answer any questions.
The City Commission was given a packet of information with figures that the City Commission
asked staff to provide as well as options to have an outside entity operate the community center.
Scott expressed that the Parkland Community Partners, the group who submitted a proposal to
operate the Community Center is present to answer any questions.
City Manager Caryn Gardner Young stated for the benefit of those individuals watching the
Channel 78 television station that there were 5 proposals presented to the City Commission:
Option 1 was to continue the relationship with the YMCA. Ms. Gardner-Young clarified that
the lease agreement with the YMCA goes through 2013. So, the YMCA is not looking to
terminate the lease; they want to work in partnership with the City.
Option 2 was the City could close the Community Center entirely.
Option 3 was for staff to provide the same services that the YMCA is providing.
Option 4 was to go with a private entity to provide the same services that the YMCA is
providing.

Option 5 was for staff to provide services but at a reduced level of what is presently being
provided at this site.

Ms. Gardner-Young expressed that at this time, staff is looking for direction from the City
Commission if they would like to take a look at any of the above options. She reiterated that the
YMCA would like to follow up with the discussion they had at the last meeting and looked to the
Mayor on how he would like to proceed.

Mayor Udine stated that the City tries to work with the YMCA any way it can. The YMCA and
the City have been partners and want to remain partners. The YMCA does a very important job
in our City and the number of people they service is extensive. YMCA’s operate not so much for
the bottom line, but for the community programs it provides. He feels the YMCA is doing a
fantastic job, the residents like the services and that the City should be partnering with them. He
takes the position that he wants to partner with the YMCA and continue with the City service.
Commissioner Weissman said he wanted to restate what he said at the previous meeting. He
stated that he has no objection to a subsidy knowing that it is not going to be an ongoing expense
for the City, however in order to justify it he wants the residents to be able to take advantage of
the subsidy in the form of new memberships for the first year. This way, the residents benefit
from those dollars and at the same time builds the membership of the YMCA and put them in a
stronger position so they will not need the subsidy in future years.

Mayor Udine clarified that Commissioner Weissman’s general comment is that he wants to
continue partnering with the YMCA.

Commissioner Weissman stated “yes” to continue the partnership and reiterated that he is in
favor of the subsidy, but wants to be able to direct the tax dollars that the City is spending to
directly benefit the residents of this City.

Commissioner Rosenof stated that in the short term, the City needs to continue the subsidies and
continue the relationship with the YMCA; however on a long term basis the YMCA needs to
take a hard look at their mission statement and how it relates to the City. He stated he does not
mind subsidizing them every year as long as it is a genuine value for the residents.

Vice Mayor Jay Smith had no comments

Commissioner Moskowitz said there is no question that the YMCA came to the City’s rescue.
Had the YMCA not come to the City’s rescue, the facility would have been closed until a
solution was found. Commissioner Moskowitz thinks the City has been more than fair with the
YMCA letting them out of the agreement two years in a row because of the problems that they
have faced as a result of the economy. In better economic times, what the YMCA is proposing
would be feasible in his mind because in good times he thinks it would be easier to do. He has a
problem taking money out of reserves and giving a subsidy to a private organization.

was fine with Attorney White going through the memo, but that it would be repeated in a couple

Mayor Udine agreed and stated that the question really boiled down to why was the accessory
building allowed to be 35” — that is a question he is asked continuously.
Attorney James White stated that at the last meeting, the City Commission was looking for him
to address three issues:

1. Whether horse shows were considered special events and whether it was permitted on the

premises.

2. The accessory structure height

3. Lighting.
He provided a memo on the three issues above and stated he would briefly summarize:
With regards to the issue of horse shows, he has advised that these horse shows or riding clinics
or other special events which draw participation from horses and riders who are not boarders or
clients of the facility are clearly activities which fall outside the scope of the principal use and
allowable accessory uses on the site and therefore those special events are prohibited.
The second issue is with regard to the applicability of the Right to Farm Act with regard to the
height of the accessory structure. As most of you know, the height in the AE1 zoning district is
35” for principle structures; accessory structures or accessory uses are 25° — in this particular case
the accessory structure which is the riding arena is 31°+. That was based upon initial discussions
and initial administrative site plan determination that City staff made on information that was
supplied to them by the applicant that the height of the structure needed to be higher than 25° for
safety concerns — City staff looked at that and made a determination that the Right to Farm Act
would kick in and would provide for an exemption to that 25° height restriction which allows
them to go higher.
The third issue is with regard to the lighting on the site. We have simply said that at this point,
the applicant is required to submit a lighting plan or lumination plan or photometric plan so that
City staff is able to determine what would be permissible on the site. Parkland has exterior
lighting standards that are set forth in the Code of Ordinance. We have simply said that the
applicant has to comply with those standards. As early as this morning I had been in touch with
Mr. Dvorkin, Pine Hollow’s Attorney on that specific issue. Last week, the City started code
enforcement proceedings against the property owner giving him a timeframe of November 22,
2010 to comply. It is Attorney White’s understanding from Pine Hollow’s Attorney, Mr.
Dvorkin that they are in the process of preparing plans to submit to the Planning and Zoning
Department with regards to lighting so until that happens, they really can’t go into further
discussions about exterior or interior lighting until City staff can analyze the plans.

Mayor Udine stated that he thinks we should wait until there are more people present to address
the specific questions they had. He also asked is there was a memo written on the specific
questions that were asked.

Attorney White stated that some of the questions are clearly asking for individual legal advice
which he is not at liberty to give. Some questions deal with the height and lighting that the City
has already addressed. Some questioned why was it allowed since it is a commercial activity.
Attorney White reiterated that it is not a commercial activity — it is an agricultural activity.
Commissioner Moskowitz stated that the riding arena is the main issue which is the 35° tall
structure and the lighting is a sub-topic of the main issue. Commissioner Moskowitz clarified
that the opinion staff received before which has been reiterated by Attorney White is that it is his
opinion of the interpretation of the Farm Act and the Attorney Generals opinion that the 35° is
allowed because it is part of his use under the Farm Act. Attorney White clarified that the issue
is the exception to the 25” height limitation. Commissioner Moskowitz clarified that this is the
opinion that the City has been given by the attorney. He stated that the Right to Farm Act is
extremely vague and there is not a lot of case law on it, but it is interpreted very liberally. He
expressed that the attorney could be right or wrong. There is no way for him to figure out if 35°
is definitively allowed or not. Commissioner Moskowitz expressed that a judge is only one who
can determine if Attorney White is correct or incorrect.

Mayor Udine opened to the public.

Bill Reicherter, lives in the Ranches — expressed he does not care too much about the facility, but
is concerned with the lighting. He asked if is there is a code for commercial lighting in a
residential neighborhood.

Mayor Udine explained that Parkland’s Code of Ordinance allows for lighting in the Ranches
and he believes it is limited to 10°.

Attorney White explained that the City of Parkland has exterior light standards within the Code
of Ordinance. The exterior lighting standards or rules are applicable in certain zoning districts.
In the AEI or residential zoning districts, the general rule paraphrased is that there is a 10°
limitation on exterior lighting — there is an exception for tennis court lighting and so the question
is whether the lights that are in the structure are considered exterior lighting or interior lighting
and if it is taken a step further, even if you classify it as exterior lighting then does the Right to
Farm Act kick in and provide for an exemption from those lighting requirements? Those are
things that we still can’t answer because staff does not have the plans to review.

Mayor Udine expressed there wasn’t a lot of information provided as to a lighting scheme at Pine
Hollow. When the lighting plan is submitted, then it will have to be determined whether the
lighting plan complies with the City’s Code of Ordinance. Mayor stated that clearly Parkland’s
Code of Ordinance allows for outside lighting in certain areas.
