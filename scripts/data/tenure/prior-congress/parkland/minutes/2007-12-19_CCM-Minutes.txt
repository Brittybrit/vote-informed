SYSTEM -BID AWARD
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, TO RANK THE FOUR

AND VOICE PROCESSING SYSTEM; AND TO
AUTHORIZE THE APPROPRIATE CITY OFFICIAL TO
NEGOTIATE A CONTRACT WITH THE FIRST RANKED
FIRM; PROVIDING FOR SEVERABILITY; AND
PROVIDING FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2007-126. She offered that,
in response to this RFP, the City had received 10 proposals. The staff committee had short-listed
the firms to four, due to a tie for third ranked. She stated that each company would make afive-
minute presentation, and staff would then provide further information.
Brief presentations were made by representatives of Argent Systems, Comphax Communications,
Deltacom, and Milner Voice & Data.

Ms. Gardner-Young continued that staff recommended a lease agreement. She noted that the
Commission had been furnished copies of the proposals, as well as a summary prepared by IT
Manager Chevelle Crusan. She reviewed that, with the City's new facilities, it was time to
consider a telephone system, which was previously approved through the budget process.
Finance and Administrative Services Director Barbara Hastings offered that Ms. Crusan's
spreadsheet had outlined the advantages and disadvantages of the proposals, and she noted the
basic features included in all four systems.
Ms. Crusan gave a brief synopsis of her previously-provided summary.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked clarification regarding the inclusion of lightning and acts of God
in Argent's proposal. Ms. Crusan responded that Argent had updated their original proposal; an
insurance policy would be taken out on the City's behalf.
Mayor Udine asked regarding unified messaging and Outlook capability with Argent. Ms.
Crusan confirmed that it was not included in the proposal price; it would be at additional cost.
Mayor Udine questioned that all of the systems would lose functionality without power. Ms.
Crusan responded that each building would have its own switch and work independently, based
upon the availability of power at each location. Ms. Gardner-Young added that the Milner
proposal was a complete Internet-protocol (IP) system; no digital was involved. Clarification
about the other systems was also provided.
Commissioner Weissman asked the age of the City's current system. Ms. Crusan estimated it to
be about 10 to 12 years old. The Commissioner noted that all of the proposals were for five
years, and he asked the costs beyond those five years, including for support, parts, and labor.
Ms. Gardner-Young noted that this provision had not been included in the RFP, and .she
questioned the firms for the cost for years five through 10. Argent responded that costs would be
based upon per port per month; currently, they were $2.50 per port per month.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz and Commissioner Weissman questioned maintenance costs over the last
five .years. While Ms. Gardner-Young stated that she was unaware of any large expenditures,
Ms. Hastings added that service was provided only on an as-needed basis.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked confirmation that all proposals were IP phones, which Ms. Crusan
confirmed, and he questioned the function without power and redundancy in the system. Ms.
Crusan offered that, outside of the phone system, there would be a metro Ethernet line
configured. That line would facilitate City Hall and the other buildings, which will also have
digital back-up lines so that the phones would not be lost.
The Vice Mayor spoke regarding the summary provided by Ms. Crusan, which offered an easy
comparison of the proposals and their components. He questioned the staff support in each
company. Representatives of each firm indicated: Argent - 25 employees, with eight certified

4. ORDINANCE NO. 2007-29: INCENTIVES FOR ENVIRONMENTALLY
CONSCIOUS PURCHASES
COMMENTS: FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND,
FLORIDA, CREATING A PROGRAM PROVIDING FOR
INCENTIVES FOR ENVIRONMENTALLY CONSCIOUS
PURCHASES; PROVIDING FOR IMPLEMENTATION OF
THE PROGRAM; ADOPTING PROGRAM CRITERIA
AND LIMITATIONS; PROVIDING FOR REGULATIONS;
CONTAINING A PROVISION FOR INCLUSION 1N THE
CITY CODE; CONTAINING A SEVERABILITY CLAUSE;
CONTAINING AN EFFECTIVE DATE.
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2007-29.
Environmental Services Director Brian Archer offered that the Commission had directed the City
Attorney at the November 7t'' meeting to draft an ordinance to create a program to provide
incentives for environmentally-conscious purchases made by Parkland residents and businesses.
The Ordinance incorporates 12 program models, which he listed, and staff recommended
Commission approval.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz thanked Mr: Archer for his hard work. While he acknowledged
discussion concerning the caps, he wanted to eliminate them in the individual categories. He felt
that, since the program would not be funded until the 2008/2009 budget year, the funding for the
program could be the overall cap. The program could not cost the City more money since it
would be naturally capped by its funding; the Ordinance also provided that the program could be
suspended at any time. He reiterated his belief that the individual caps should be removed.
Mayor Udine asked the total amount of the programs, and Mr. Archer confirmed that it was
$50,000. The Mayor recalled that the. caps had been his idea, and he suggested that something be
included in Section 4 of the Ordinance that would limit the overall dollars. He believed there
should be some basic structure as to the program operation and certain time periods for
inspection. The Mayor reiterated that he was in favor of the program, but he believed there
should be an overall cap of dollars, a better outline of the program's implementation, and specific
periods for application and inspection.

<<PG>>

Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that he would prefer not to be specific about the dollar
amount. He explained that he had not proposed a dollar figure because there was no funding for
the program at the present time and the possibility exists that, at budget time, the City may not be
able to fully fund the program. He believed that the funding decision should be made at that
point in time. The Vice Mayor agreed with stating specific times for application, and he gave an
example of an early year application, with funding in the next budget and incentive refund checks
mailed six to eight weeks after budget adoption. He suggested that these specifics should be
included for second reading. Additionally, he believed some plan should be implemented to
track the refunds to eliminate the possibilities of fraud.
Mr. Maurodis agreed, but suggested that the maximum could be set in the Ordinance. He asked
further clarification of the payment timing.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz provided explanation that refunds would be paid from the funds
budgeted for 2008/2009. Ms. Gardner-Young added that the intent was to know how much
money so that the specific funds could be budgeted. The applications would have been filed
earlier in the. year.
Mr. Maurodis expressed concern that, without a set maximum, residents may expect full funding
of all applications filed.
Mayor Udine suggested that Section 4 contain language that the City "...shall set up a separate
account to finance up to a maximum of $50,000..." or less and that there is a mechanism to track
the applications.. He believed that applicants must be made aware about the maximum amount.
Noting that Mr. Hemphill was still present, Mayor Udine asked that he investigate grants for
some of these projects.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that Mr. Archer had examined grants for solar heating and
that cities across the nation were implementing such incentive programs. He noted that the
President had signed a bill on this date that would phase out incandescent light bulbs by 2014.
The Vice Mayor expressed his hope that this would be a living, breathing program -where an
unsuccessful element might be substituted with another.
Mr. Maurodis believed that he could satisfy both the Mayor and Vice Mayor by providing a
maximum amount, but authorizing additional funds through simple resolution.
Commissioner Weissman suggested that the subject of retroactive purchases should be
addressed.
The Vice Mayor asked that ownership of hybrid vehicles be excluded from retroactivity. He
suggested that the owners would provide proof of ownership through current registration.
