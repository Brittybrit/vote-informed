4. ORDINANCE NO. 2008-12: VALET PARKING 
 COMMENTS: FIRST READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY 
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND, 
FLORIDA, RELATING TO VALET PARKING; 
AMENDING CHAPTER 22, ARTICLE VI ENTITLED 
“OFF-STREET PARKING AND LOADING” OF THE 
CODE OF ORDINANCES; PROVIDING A CONFLICTS 
CLAUSE AND A SEVERABILITY CLAUSE; AND 
PROVIDING AN EFFECTIVE DATE, CASE NUMBER 
01-ZTA-08. 
City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the first reading of Ordinance No. 2008-12. 
Planning and Zoning Director Roberta Moore indicated that the purpose of the proposed 
Ordinance was to provide a mechanism for businesses or restaurants that wish to provide valet 
parking services for their patrons. She outlined the Ordinance contents in a brief presentation. 
The Planning and Zoning Board had recommended approval of the Ordinance on a 6-0 vote; 
however, with their recommendation, verbiage had been added to require that the parking spaces 
be designated at the furthest location from the entrances. Ms. Moore acknowledged that staff 
had received comments from one commercial development, and their concerns had been 
addressed. 


<<PG>>

Commissioner Weissman commented that one valet service was already operating in his district, 
and they were using spaces closest to their facility; he hoped that would change. Ms. Moore 
responded that it was for this reason that the Planning and Zoning Board had made their 
recommendation. 
The Commissioner noted that businesses in either direction of the restaurant could be impacted, 
and he asked how to assure that would not be the case. Ms. Moore stated that permits would be 
approved on a case-by-case basis. Staff would examine the individual shopping center and, in 
this case, the parking spaces along 441 would be recommended. Copies of the permit would be 
provided to the Sheriff’s Office, and the shopping center owner would sign the permit 
application acknowledging the spaces designated. 
Commissioner Weissman was concerned that other businesses could be negatively impacted. He 
wanted to ensure that other businesses were also properly noticed and that they would have an 
opportunity to object. Ms. Gardner-Young commented that the City, under the Ordinance, 
would have the right to revoke the license at any point if there were adverse effects. She 
suggested that other business owners could contact staff, who would either resolve the complaint 
through the shopping center or revoke the permit. 
Commissioner Weissman asked that the Ordinance include notice to other tenants when an 
application is filed. Ms. Gardner-Young agreed; however, she believed that the burden should 
be placed on the shopping center owner. City Attorney Andrew Maurodis offered that an 
affidavit could be required. 
Vice Mayor Moskowitz commented that this had turned into a case-specific issue. He noted that 
the Ordinance was to allow valet parking, and he believed that the ultimate decision should be up 
to each shopping center. With upset tenants, he suggested that the issues should be handled by 
the owner of the shopping center and not the City. He favored valet parking in the City – and 
residents wanted it – but not at the expense of taking all spaces close to their business. As to the 
notice, the Vice Mayor agreed that this should be left to the individual plazas. 
There was further discussion. 
Mayor Udine agreed with the City Attorney that an affidavit from the shopping center owner that 
the tenants had been noticed would be appropriate, with which Commissioner Weissman also 
agreed. The Mayor did not believe the City should be placed in the position of hearing 
grievances. He suggested that the Ordinance should state that the designated area for valet 
parking would be at an “appropriate” location, rather than “furthest,” determined at the sole 
discretion of the City. He noted this Ordinance would also affect Parkland Golf & Country 
Club. 
Mayor Udine opened to the public. There were no comments or questions, and the Mayor closed 
to the public.

 This form is for use by any person serving at the county, city,or other local level of government on an appointed or elected board,council,
 commission, authority, or committee. It applies equally to members of advisory and non-advisory bodies who are presented with a voting
 conflict of interest under Section 112.3143, Florida Statutes.
 Your responsibilities under the law when faced with voting on a measure in which you have a conflict of interest will vary greatly depending
 on whether you hold an elective or appointive position. For this reason, please pay close attention to the instructions on this form before
 completing the reverse side and filing the form.
 1
 INSTRUCTIONS FOR COMPLIANCE WITH SECTION 112.3143, FLORIDA STATUTES
 --- A person holding elective or appointive county, municipal, or other local public office MUST ABSTAIN from voting on a measure which
 inures to his or her special private gain or loss. Each elected or appointed local officer also is prohibited from knowingly voting on a mea-
 sure which inures to the special gain or loss of a principal (other than a government agency) by whom he or she is retained (including the
 parent organization or subsidiary of a corporate principal by which he or she is retained); to the special private gain or loss of a relative; or
 to the special private gain or loss of a business associate. Commissioners of community redevelopment agencies under Sec. 163.356 or
 163.357, F.S., and officers of independent special tax districts elected on a one-acre, one-vote basis are not prohibited from voting in that
 capacity.
 For purposes of this law, a "relative" includes only the officer's father, mother, son, daughter, husband, wife, brother, sister, father-in-law,
 mother-in-law, son-in-law, and daughter-in-law. A"business associate" means any person or entity engaged in or carrying on a business
 enterprise with the officer as a partner,joint venturer, coowner of property, or corporate shareholder (where the shares of the corporation
 are not listed on any national or regional stock exchange).
