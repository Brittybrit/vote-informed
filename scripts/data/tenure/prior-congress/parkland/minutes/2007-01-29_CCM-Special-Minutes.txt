II PURPOSE:
1. ACCEPTANCE OF THE RESIGNATION OF MARK LAUZIER
City Manager Mark Lauzier expressed regret to end his tenure with the City, but an opportunity
in the private sector as a management consultant had been presented to him that he felt he could

not refuse. While he would like have to have given the City more transition time, he indicated
that he would leave the City's employ in 30 days.
Mr. Lauzier stated that he was proud of the accomplishments and the level of service -from
"horse-and-buggy" to "industrial revolution" with the goal of the "bullet train" -- achieved in the
past two years. He believed the City had an excellent Commission, and that the City was headed
in a good direction. He felt he would be leaving the City in capable hands with a fantastic staff,
and he had no qualms about any difficulties. He offered that, since he was remaining in the area
and felt he was leaving on good terms, he would be available to assist beyond the 30 days in any
manner that he could.
Mayor Udine thanked Mr. Lauzier for his service to the City, and he opened to Commission
comments.
Commissioner Weissman stated that it had been a pleasure to work with him, and he wished him
well in all future endeavors.
Commissioner Smith also thanked Mr. Lauzier for being so helpful to him as anewly-elected
official, and he wished him the best in his new endeavors.
Commissioner McGilvray stated that she had personally wished Mr. Lauzier well and noted that
he would be sorely missed. She hoped he would continue to consult with the City because she
felt his expertise very valuable. She hoped to see him soon.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz clarified that Parkland was not pursuing the "bullet train."
Mayor Udine offered that Mr. Lauzier had joined the City during a time of transition, and he
noted that he had been the only Commission member who had not voted in favor of Mr.
Lauzier's employment. He stated that Mr. Lauzier had quickly earned his respect and had done
an excellent and professional job for the City. He believed the City moving in the right direction.
Bringing the Commission up-to-date, Mayor Udine offered that he had made courtesy telephone
calls to previous City officials over the weekend, as well as to point people on the Wedge
property, to assure them of a smooth transition. He believed the City on solid footing.
With reluctance, Mayor Udine stated that the Commission accepted Mr. Lauzier's resignation.
2. APPOINTMENT OF AN INTERIM CITY MANAGER
Mayor Udine stated that one of the reasons he had called this meeting was to avoid any type of
leadership vacuum. With two ways to appoint -from staff or through an outside agency for a
"range rider," he believed that the City had built "bench strength" and had the ability to appoint
an Interim City Manager from within. He felt that Caryn Gardner-Young would be a good
candidate, and the Commission agreed.

3. DISCUSSION ON THE PROCEDURE FOR THE HIRING OF A
NEW PERMANENT CITY MANAGER
Mayor Udine recalled the last process, and he noted two ways to proceed: to keep. the search in-
house or to hire a headhunting firm.
Commissioner Smith indicated his preference for the first option: with in-house ads placed in the
appropriate journals and soliciting resumes. He spoke regarding past experiences in the private
sector and opportunities lost because of the associated costs.
Commissioner McGilvray felt the City should do both, noting that larger cities utilized
headhunting firms. While she was not as concerned with the fee, she suggested that qualified
candidates not publicly seeking other positions might be found and that all of the "grunt work,"

such as full background checks, would be done by them. Additionally, a search firm could start
quickly and would compile a better pool of candidates faster than the City would on its own.
Commissioner Weissman stated that he understood Commissioner McGilvray's thinking, but he
viewed the headhunter as the fall-back position -after the City has made it own attempt. He
noted that the City had the benefit of sharing the City Attorney with a larger neighboring city,
which is now in the midst of a city manager search, and that Mr. Maurodis was also receiving the
applications for that position. He did not believe the City would have any difficulty with
applicants if ads were run both locally and in the ICMA journal. He suggested that the
applications should be sent to Mr. Maurodis, establishing an applicant deadline of the second
meeting in March. Commissioner Weissman felt that the employment of the City Manager was a
major decision for the Commission and that the responsibility should not be placed elsewhere.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz believed that the comments were valid, but noted that the City was not
under a time crunch because it had Ms. Gardner-Young. He also believed that the City should
seek its own applicants; should there not be a choice from qualified candidates, then the
headhunters could be used. He agreed that the applications should be sent to the City Attorney.
Mayor Udine stated that based his comments on the City's past experience and what he had
observed of other cities. He reflected on the current and recent searches of other cities. He
believed the City paid very competitively, and he felt the City would be bombarded with
resumes. If the consensus of the Commission was to stay within and wait until March to engage
a headhunter, if needed, the Mayor stated he would be comfortable with that route. Mayor Udine
felt that the background checks should be done when the applicant pool was narrowed to a more
manageable list.
Mr. Maurodis asked direction on the ad. With some discussion, it was agreed that the same ad
from Mr. Lauzier's hiring would be used. Personnel would be directed to place-the ads, with
resumes directed to Mr. Maurodis' attention at City Hall. The resumes would be logged by
Personnel.
MOTION made by Vice Mayor Moskowitz to advertise the City Manager position in the ICMA
journal and elsewhere, with the possible option, if not satisfied with the pool of applicants, to
explore the idea of a headhunter. Seconded by Commissioner Smith.
