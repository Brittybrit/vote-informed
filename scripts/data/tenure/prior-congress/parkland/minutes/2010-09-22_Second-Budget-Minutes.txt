Mayor Udine stated that this is a statutory meeting for the budget and millage rate as provided by
the Florida Statutes. This is different than a normal City Commission meeting. This meeting has
been statutorily noticed in the newspaper. He said he realizes that most residents present this
evening are here for reasons other than the budget. He said based on the statute, the first two
substantive areas need to be the millage and the budget. He said he would open to the public, the
Commission will listen but can not take any action.

Mayor Udine opened to the public.

Kathy Geller O’Dwyer, 7707 NW 82 Terrace, is a long time resident of the Ranches. She said
she was here to ask the Commission to enforce the zoning ordinances for AE-1 . She said that
AE-1 is a residential zoning with limited agricultural use. She also requested further definition
of AE-1 with the guidance of the residents of the Ranches. She questioned why the complex of
Pine Hallow was permitted to develop. She feels that Pine Hallow is not a limited agricultural
use. She asked the City to not allow horse shows at Pine Hallow and not allow lighting.

Anthony Prezzemolo, 7555 NW 82 Terrace, agreed with Kathy O'Dwyer. He also asked about
the motor cross track on 84". He spoke about the roads in the Ranches and said they needed
shoulders on the side so drivers can pass. He asked about how tall a building is allowed to be in
Parkland. He questioned whether lights and shows are allowed in that arena. He questioned the
stability of that arena roof during a hurricane. He also questioned parking lots being allowed in
the Ranches.

Dusty Lashbrook, 8600 NW 72 (Trotters Lane), said he loves the feel of the Ranches and the
residential feel it has. He said they did not buy in an area that was to be commercialized or have
traffic issues. He asked that the codes be enforced.

Barry Speigel, 6808 NW 116 Ave., (Heron Bay) said that a group living in Oakbrook Association
requested a berm on Nob Hill to decrease the noise with the increased traffic on that road. He
said WCI was supposed to put trees and shrubs in that area just north of Parkland Isles. There is
also an issue with the lights from the driving range.

Mary Donnelly, 7250 NW 82 Terrace, said she understood the easement was there for a trail for
horse riders to use. She asked why that property owner is allowed to make a ditch in that
casement. She complained about horse riders riding their horses across the road instead of single
file.

Laura Laramee, 6750 NW 81 Terrace, commented about the Pine Hallow arena. She said her
neighbors are all concerned and upset about this structure and that ordinances are not being
adhered to. She said her neighbors have come up with a list of ordinances that her neighbors feel
need to be addressed and she wants to know what the Commission will do to correct this. She
provided a list of the residents that are concerned.
Mike Squillace, 7250 NW 84™ Ave., spoke about the selective enforcement that continues.

Mr. Harold Bockhold, congratulated those Commissioners that were re-elected unopposed. He
spoke about Pine Hallow and spoke about pushing the limits of what is allowed under the Farm
Act.

Mr. Barry Laramee, 6750 NW 81 Terrace, spoke about his family difficulties this past year. He
said because of Pine Hallow and the activities taking place, there is a dust bowl there and it is
very unhealthy for him.

Kevin Bolling, of the Ranches, commented about the issues that involves Pine Hallow.

Cindy Navarra, 7110 NW 87™ Ave, spoke about the coyote issue. She spoke about the trapper
that was hired and gave her reasons for why he did not trap any coyote. She said she showed the
trapper where the coyote dens were, however the trapper explained that he was not allowed on
private property. She said she has a trappers license and a concealed weapons permit. She has
had neighbors ask her to help them rid the coyotes. She knows she can protect her own property,
but she asked if she was allowed to help them.

City Attorney Andrew Maurodis said that the City does not have regulations on this but the State
and County may regarding certain species or discharging a weapon.

Mayor Udine asked the City Manager to check with the County on this issue.

Karen Decker, also spoke about the coyote issue. She said they were right behind her house on
NW 66" Lane. She said there are at least 10 coyotes.

Mayor Udine asked if there were any other comments from the public. There were no other
comments from the public. Mayor Udine closed to the public.

Commissioner Moskowitz commented on the coyotes and explained the Commission
understands how serious an issue it is and that is why they did hire a trapper even though they
were conflicted on it. He said that they consulted the experts from the Florida Fish and Wildlife
and were told that the coyote will repopulate just as fast as you eliminate them. The FF & W said
that education on the coyotes is the answer. They need to find out what the long term solution is.
Mayor Udine said they decided to hire the trapper because the coyote were getting so brazen, but
there were just as many residents that were against the trapping.

Commissioner Moskowitz asked Ms. Navarra to let staff know exactly where the dens are.

Mayor Udine addressed Mr. Barry Speigel’s comments on the berm. He said that the City does
not and can not budget funds for berms on private property, but the Mayor said he will try to
work with Mr. Speigel and WCI to see if something can be done.

Vice Mayor Smith commented that where he lives in Parkland Isles the homeowners pay

approximately $1200 a year for 15 years to pay for the berm that runs around his development.

He said he will be happy to meet with WCI to see what they could do about this.

Commissioner Moskowitz returned to the Pine Hallow issue and recapped going back to 2006

when some residents of the Ranches wanted more control in the Ranches. He said the residents

wanted to stop the commercialization. He said the Commission adopted Ordinances for parking,

screening etc. so he does not understand why the Commission is getting blamed for trying to

commercialize. He said at that time, there were people in the Ranches that did not agree with the

ordinances and controls and the city was sued on it. The City is damned if they do and damned if

they don’t. The City is sued by someone no matter which stand they take. He said the

Commission are the policy guys and staff takes care of the every day issues. He mentioned

attending the ground breaking, however it was vacant land at that point. He said that staff has the

authority to approve things all the time. On this issue, City staff felt it necessary to hire outside

experts to make the determination, because he feels they are probably afraid to make decisions

for fear of the City getting sued.

Commissioner Moskowitz said he will not go down the path again of putting new ordinances in

place just on this issue.

Mayor Udine asked if there were any residents present to speak on the Millage or Budget? He

asked the City Attorney if that had to be done by a time certain.

It was decided to hear and vote on the Millage and Budget Ordinances and then return to

the Pine Hallow discussion.

4.0198. The adopted millage rate of 4.0198 mils is 8.31% less than the roll back rate of 4.3841 and is
computed according to the guidelines set forth in Section 200.065. So therefore, the roll back rate is
4.3841 the adopted rate is 4.0198, the adopted rate is 8.31% less than the roll back rate.”

Kevin Myers, 9817 NW 64™ Place, had concerns about the solid waste bill being included on the tax bill.
He was also concerned about the continued increase in fire rescue fees. He commented that there were
significant increases in the last two years and if that continues it is a concern. He also commented that
the millage was the same this year but went up last year pretty significantly.
Mayor Udine responded to Mr. Myers” comments addressing the waste management fee being included
on the tax bill first. He said the City analyzed this situation and felt that by doing so it would save the
City significant dollars. He also commented on the fire fees and stated that the cost of fire services
continues to increase, however the Commission continues to keep a close eye on other options.
MOTION made by Commissioner Weissman to approve Ordinance No. 2010-21, Adopting the Millage
at 4.0198 mils. Seconded by Commissioner Moskowitz.

MOTION passed unanimously.

The discussion returned to the subject of Pine Hallow.

Commissioner Moskowitz met with some residents in the Ranches and he told them what his
position was on Pine Hallow. He does not feel there should be any lights, nor any entertainment.
He feels very strongly about these two issues because staff’s position is that these are not allowed
by code.

Commissioner Moskowitz spoke about the motor cross track and that it is clearly not an allowed
use and if it is happening out there, code enforcement will need to get involved with that.
Commissioner Moskowitz questioned the height of the structure and whether the code allows for
the 35 feet or 25 feet. He added that staff and the attorney will have to answer why the swales
were allowed from a safety standpoint. He also commented on the supposed parking lot. He said
that he thinks staff is now on notice that anything on this issue needs to come to the City
Commission. He said he will not be voting on any parking lot. He has tried to do what he thinks
is right and if he is wrong he will admit it but he will not be threatened into doing something. He
recapped that on the three issues, the lighting, the entertainment and the parking lot, they need to
work together to make sure Mr. Dvorkin does not move forward on these issues. If something
was allowed and it should not have been, then it needs to be rectified.

Commissioner Moskowitz told the residents that he is more than happy to meet with any resident
on this issue and answer any questions they may have.

Mayor Udine commented that this issue goes beyond what was built at Pine Hallow. He
understands and feels for the Laramee’s because they are right across the street from this, but
after listening to all the comments this evening, these are the same issues the Commission dealt
with 4 years ago. He said after listening to Mr. Bolling’s comments about the feed truck that
almost ran his sons bus off the road, they were in this same spot 4 years ago and they thought
they came up with reasonable ordinances to limit the amount of commercial trucks, however

Ordinances that the Ranches residents can vote on.

Commissioner Moskowitz commented that he feels like he stands alone on the issues of the
Ranches. He wanted to know where the rest of the Commission stands on this.

Vice Mayor Smith agrees with Commissioner Moskowitz on the lighting issue.

Commissioner Rosenof said that the City approved a set of plans without lighting and Mr.
Dvorkin would have to come back for approval for lighting and he would make his decision at
that time.

City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young stated that the attorney’s on both sides have been in
discussion with each other. The City has taken the position of no lighting and has not backed
down from that. Mr. Dvorkin’s latest communication is that the light are needed for safety
issues.

Mayor Udine asked that staff look at what other properties have lighting and what the parameters
are. He said as far as the special events, the Commission has already said no special events will

Commissioner Weissman said that he would defer to the wishes of the District’s Commissioner.
