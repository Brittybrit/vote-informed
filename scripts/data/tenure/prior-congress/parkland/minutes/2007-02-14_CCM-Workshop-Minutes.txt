City Manager Mark Lauzier noted that both BSO and the Fire Services agreements had mid-year
public safety enhancements budgeted. The time to adjust the contracts for the increase in service
level would be between April 1 and October 1. Additionally, the Commission had requested an
analysis of the contract during the past few years, and he noted the spreadsheets attached to the
agenda summary. He invited Chief Haupt to explain some of the ongoing issues, service levels,
and the experience of the past few years.
Chief Don Haupt gave a PowerPoint presentation on the staffing, technology, and services
provided to the City by the Coral Springs Fire Department. He spoke regarding projected costs
based upon the addition of the engine company at the Western Fire Station in April; revenues;
and an analysis of their calls, response times, and projected configuration of service areas.
Commissioner Weissman asked whether the City Manager had received a proposal for 2007-
200~, recalling that the information had been requested by the Commission last December for the
coming week.. Mr. Lauzier indicated that staff was working on it; however, he noted that the
contract required that the information be submitted by June 30tH
Commissioner Weissman recalled that the request had been for the information at the end of
February because, by June 30t", the City would have no options. Mr. Lauzier stated that he did
not yet have a proposal for next year from Coral Springs, and the Commissioner asked if the
information was expected by the directed date.
Chief Haupt responded that information could be provided, but their numbers were dependent
upon other numbers furnished to them in June and July. Mr. Lauzier suggested that the reliability
of the numbers would be in question as Chief Haupt could furnish only an estimate.
Commissioner Weissman noted that the contract with Coral Springs first began in March and it
had not been dependent upon numbers furnished later in the year. Chief Haupt offered that Coral
Springs Fire could furnish those initial numbers because they were already in a budget year; the
"hard" numbers of salaries and the contract were already known.
Commissioner. Weissman reviewed that, from the date of the original contract and over the past
three years, there had been nearly a 30 percent increase -- close to 10 percent per year on average.
At the same time, the contract with BSO has had a cap of approximately five percent per year.
The City, as well as most cities, would face some severe budget "crunching" this year and, when
that occurs, there would not be a lot of options in July. The Commissioner believed that the
Commission had a fiscal responsibility to its citizens to provide the best service at the best price.
Mayor Udine understood that Coral Springs Fire could provide some numbers, but with the
understanding that they would be subject to change based upon the personnel costs.
Using a PowerPoint slide, Mr. Lauzier noted that the base assumption going into the next year
was $3.7 million -given the full year cost of implementing the additional station, with no cost
increase. He believed the City should start from that point.

Commissioner Weissman calculated that as a 30 percent increase, but Mayor Udine noted the
addition of the Western Fire Station and additional staff. Chief Haupt suggested that it would be
a 22 percent increase; there were currently 21 people and the added staff would bring the total to
34. Discussion followed.
Commissioner Weissman suggested that, with an increase consistent with prior years, the $3.7
million figure could easily grow to $4.1 million. Mr. Lauzier acknowledged that could happen
utilizing the past trends, but he suggested that the Commission could direct staff to a target the
$3.7 million figure.
Commissioner Weissman believed that the City should have the actual figures in time to make a
decision on the contract, and not be presented with the actual numbers on June 30~' when the
City had no other options.
Chief Haupt assured that he would get the numbers as close as humanly possible. He explained
the compilation of costs, 90 percent of which were salary and benefits and largely based upon the
union contract, renegotiated each year; he suggested allowing a five percent increase on
operational costs. Although he was not involved in the negotiations, Chief Haupt anticipated that
the union would seek a 7.75 percent increase.
Mr. Lauzier offered that staff had been examining the agreement, including revenue credits and
actual personnel costs, as it related to the mid-year expansion.
Commissioner Weissman recalled that Coral Springs had come in as the low bidder in the 2004
RFP, and he believed it was a wise business decision. He suggested that the City should
investigate other options through RFP for the next fiscal year.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz agreed with Commissioner Weissman's remarks and wanted assurance
that the City was getting the best service for what is paid. He stated that he would be interested
to know the costs of comparable services on the open market.
Mr. Lauzier indicated that Business Manager Nancy Morando was examining costs in
preparation for the next budget.
The Vice Mayor questioned the 12-month notification requirement and the norm for that length
of time. He felt that Coral Springs had all the leverage and, without competition, no motivation
to give the City the best price.
Mayor Udine noted that the City was not entering into a new contract. The l2-month termination
clause was included to protect both the City and Coral Springs.
Chief Haupt offered that the matrix back-up had evaluated the Coral Springs contract with the
City of Parkland, and it was estimated that $470,000 had been saved on an annual basis using last
year's dollars. It was felt prudent that the data should be provided to the City in June during the
budget process, therefore allowing three months' time to refine numbers on both sides.

The Chief suggested that competitive bid was healthy, and he believed it would validate some of
their numbers.
Mayor Udine commented that Fire Service was expensive and a tough bill for residents to pay in
general. He acknowledged that economies of scale have occurred because of the partnership, but
he believed that the service must truly be a combined Coral Springs/Parkland Fire Department.
The Mayor felt that Coral Springs' negotiations with the firefighters also served this City as their
interests were identical. He believed it a difficult thing to bid because there were no other
providers on the City's border, and he was personally not ready to look for other proposals. He
felt comfortable getting preliminary numbers and then moving forward with final numbers.
Mr. Lauzier suggested that, should the City choose to go out for bid in the future, there would be
information for specific services -- location, who, and how many, as well as a proposed contract,
which should be covered in detail -- in order to compare apples to apples.
Chief Haupt added that, regarding a survey, every patient transported is later surveyed and, as to
economies of scale, Coral Springs' engines were already on order and Parkland's engine had
been piggybacked at the same price. He believed that the services given on a routine basis by
Coral Springs Fire to the citizens of Parkland had been exemplary, and he appreciated the
Commission's support. He noted that the new trucks on order would bear the marking, "Coral
Springs/Parkland" because it was the same family.
Commissioner Smith commented that, during the last fire negotiations, he had been involved
with the local politics of Margate, and he agreed that Coral Springs had the stations in the City's
closest proximity. He understood that the process of competitive bid was phenomenal but, in this
respect, it would be more cost effective to utilize the closest provider so as to not have the
increased expenses of larger stations and more equipment in addition to staffing.
Mayor Udine believed that all of the points made were similar and summarized that the City just
wished to assure that it was paying its fair share.
Commissioner Weissman felt that, if this were a multiple year contract with reasonable caps in
place, this discussion would not take place. With its history in three years of an approximate 10
percent increase each year, he anticipated the contract would double in cost in a little over seven
years. With no expectation that revenue would double, he believed that the City would be
required to make hard choices in the coming years in order to provide the best service.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz asked that the Chief also provide the budget quote for Coral Springs for
the next year in order to compare. Chief Haupt responded in the affirmative, and noted that he
would provide that information to Coral Springs in June as well. He again promised some
preliminary information soon.
Mayor Udine hoped there would be a long-term partnership between Coral Springs and Parkland
to provide the residents of this City the best fire and EMS services for the dollar.

Commissioner McGilvray noted that'Coral Springs had provided EMS for 14 years, and the City
had added fire to an already existing service. She recalled that the 12-month notice was to give

2. DISCUSSION: CONSTRUCTION ACTIVITY ON WEEKENDS
City Manager Mark Lauzier recalled that this subject had been introduced by memorandum and
staff had been. requested to bring it back. Staff had followed up with other municipalities, noting
the chart and memo in the backup. While a change was not recommended, the decision to do so
was with the Commission.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz indicated that he had spoken with the City Manager on several occasions
concerning this subject, as he represented a district with considerable construction activity.
While he did not oppose Sunday construction hours, he felt that 9:30 a.m. to 7:30 p.m. was
excessive. He believed that residents would appreciate some quiet time, and he suggested that
the hours might end earlier.
At the Mayor's invitation, Jon Ulsaker, Director of Operations for WCI -East Coast, stated that
he did not believe the Vice Mayor's outline unreasonable. Mr: Ulsaker stated that they would
like the opportunity to work on the weekends as they represented approximately 27 percent of
their construction year. They were unlike other businesses in that they were affected by variables
of weather. However, they would not seriously oppose fewer hours on Sunday.
Mayor Udine stated that he would like to see the City cut back on Sunday construction hours, and
he suggested 9:30 a.m. to 2:00 p.m.
Mayor Udine summarized that the direction to staff was to prepare an ordinance limiting Sunday
construction hours from 9:30 a.m. to 2:00 p.m. He stated that WCI be notified of the first and
second readings for their comments.

Mayor Udine updated that City staff had met again on the previous day with Wedge landowners,

has been drafted and can be amended.
Commissioner Weissman commented that the City had received a letter from a neighboring city
regarding their interest in this issue, contrary to what would be beneficial to the counties
involved. He asked if the City was monitoring any other opposing efforts.
Mayor Udine explained that Coral Springs had sent a letter to the Department of Community
Affairs. and to the South Florida Planning Council. While they do not object to the deannexation

of the land from Palm Beach, their position was that the plans to extend University Drive into
Palm Beach County should continue.
Commissioner Weissman acknowledged this as a major issue because Palm Beach County had
supported the deannexation - with a slim Commission majority -- with that proviso in place. He

Vice Mayor Moskowitz noted that an annexation agreement from the landowners would be
brought to the Commission within the next few weeks, when strategy could then be discussed.
He noted that the City had opposed the extension of University Drive well before the Wedge
came into play and that the City has been at odds with Coral Springs for quite some time. He
believed the City must stay concerned and vigilant of Coral Springs' efforts.
Vice Mayor Moskowitz believed that Coral Springs wanted University Drive extended to bring
them more business; however, the issue was that it would bring more traffic through this City to
get there. The extension would bring this City zero benefits and dramatic change.
Mayor Udine stated that he would continue to keep the Commission informed.
