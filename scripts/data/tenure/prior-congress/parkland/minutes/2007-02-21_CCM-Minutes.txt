3. CONSIDERATION: EAB RECOMMENDATIONS
Assistant City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young commented that a "Best Practices Showcase" for
EABs had recently been held, which she believed had been initiated by Broward County, and
Board members had brought back ideas which they would like to see implemented. The City
supported the concept of mentoring and would encourage employee participation in this program.
At IVIs. Gardner-Young's mention of "Parkland University," Board Liaison Suzi Hayes provided
explanation of the program initiated by Weston in 2002. Opportunities during an entire school
year were provided to approximately 20 students to learn in-depth workings of government by
shadowing a different department each month. At the end of the school year, students assumed
role-playing for a commission meeting, with assignments as residents, developers, department
heads, and elected officials. It had been reported that at least one student had changed career
plans to become a city engineer; it was felt a successful way to promote public service and
government awareness. Ms. Hayes indicated that the EAB thought the idea should be considered
for Parkland.
Ms. Gardner-Young favored the idea, but believed further research must first take place with
Stoneman Douglas. It would be brought back to the Commission after investigation.
Mayor Udine commented that he had attended the "Best Practices" workshop and was in favor of
the program. He suggested that Weston City Manager John Flint be called for his guidance, and
noted that Weston had concluded a school year with a private company-sponsored trip for the
students to meet with legislators in Tallahassee. The Mayor noted that a reintroduction of the
subject of "Civics" was being pushed at the State level, and he volunteered to teach a day
specifically on that subject. He was very supportive.
Ms. Gardner-Young believed the Commission's direction clear. She offered that the EAB would
do "Teacher Appreciation Week" by providing bagels and juices for staff at each school. They
also recommended support of the School Board's legislative agenda -particularly with regard to
adequate funding and district cost differential, which she distributed.
Ms. Hayes added that the EAB had suggested that the City should focus on at least two of the
items. For the benefit of the public present, she briefly explained the topics of adequate funding
for salaries of teachers, substitutes,. and the mandated class size reductions, as well as a

4. ORDINANCE NO. 2007-03: AMENDING ORDINANCE 2006-20
RELATING TO THE EAB
COMMENTS: SECOND READING OF AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY
COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF PARKLAND,

Assistant City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the second reading of Ordinance No. 2007-
03. She noted the changes requested at first reading with regards to the safety committee had
been incorporated. '
Mayor Udine opened to the public.
Sharon Cutler, 7421 W. Upper Ridge Drive, spoke on behalf of the EAB. She indicated that the
Board had acknowledged support of the safety committee at their last meeting and had noted that
safety was a significant part of their strategic plan. The Board proposed slight modifications:
that all EAB members, rather than just four, should also be members of the safety committee, and
that two special meetings would be held per year for discussions on safety, which would include
all school principals, both public and private, and safety experts. It was hoped to be proactive,
rather than reactive, with meetings taking place before the start of the school year and at mid-
year. Ms. Cutler felt it important that the EAB continue to act as liaison with the schools because
of their existing relationships. She noted that many ideas had been a result of EAB attendance at
"Best Practices Showcases."
Mayor Udine commented that he agreed with the suggestions made, and he recalled that similar
ideas had been expressed at initial discussions about the committee. He believed it very
important to include the school principals and suggested that miscommunication had caused the
inclusion of only four EAB members on the safety subcommittee. The Mayor indicated his
preference that the Ordinance should indicate: a minimum of two meetings per year; inclusion of
the entire EAB membership; and appointment by the Commission of the three outside members
to the. safety subcommittee, which would report directly to the Commission.

COLLECTING NON-AD VALOREM
SPECIAL ASSESSMENT FOR
SOLID WASTE
COMMENTS: A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
# CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, ELECTING TO USE
THE UNIFORM METHOD OF COLLECTING NON-AD

THE INCORPORATED AREA OF THE CITY; STATING
A NEED FOR SUCH. LEVY; PROVIDING FOR THE

FOR AN EFFECTIVE DATE.
Assistant City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young read the title of Resolution No. 2007-28. She
noted this Resolution went hand-in-hand with the next item.
City Manager Mark Lauzier explained that the City would have the opportunity to collect the
garbage collection fees for solid waste and recycling services on the tax bill, rather than the
quarterly billing by Waste Management. He noted that Waste Management had experienced bad
debt amounting to approximately $100,000. When these fees were placed on the tax bill, they
could be liened on the property with the force of law, similar to ad valorem taxes. Referring to
his memo, the City would receive some benefit, but Waste Management, which would benefit the
most, had agreed to pay the $30,000 cost to implement.
In light of this, Mr. Lauzier felt it was an opportunity to discuss some service enhancements with
Waste Management. Despite two meetings with Waste Management, he noted that, to date,
Waste Management had not .indicated a willingness to change any services to benefit City
residents or even adjust or lower rates. A representative from Waste Management, John Albert,

<<PG>>

was present to answer any questions. He noted that the existing franchise would expire April 1,
2008, and that the City's leveraging opportunities should be kept in mind.
Commissioner Weissman commented that he had been present at the meetings with Tony
Spadacia. He believed there was a huge benefit to Waste Management to be able to do bulk
billing to the City, rather than processing 8,000 to 9,000 residential bills four times a year with
payments via credit cards and checks, as well as the follow-up to approximately $100,000 in bad
debt. He believed it was a big negative from the City's perspective to put these fees on the real
estate tax bill. He was reluctant to do this unless there was some enhancement of services and
other benefits that could be publicized to residents.
At the Commissioner's questions of cost-of-living adjustments, John Albert of Waste
Management indicated that there had been a disposal increase and a CPI on the collection
portion. Commissioner Weissman noted that only a portion of the fees were adjusted annually.
Commissioner Weissman stated that he expected a good response from Waste Management
concerning an enhancement of services and some reduction in rates. He suggested that the
Commission might pass these Resolutions at this meeting, but it did not obligate the City to
follow through; it could be discussed at some future date before the existing contract would be
renegotiated. He hoped that Waste Management would come back to the City with something
that made sense, and he would then be supportive of the fees on the tax bill. Until that time, the
Commissioner stated that he would oppose it.
Commissioner Smith agreed with Commissioner Weissman, but noted that this implementation
would keep Waste Management's uncollectibles down and would keep homeowners from paying
the bills of other residents through eventual cost increases to cover losses. He noted that tax bills
paid in November had the advantage of a four percent discount. He believed, that in some way,
other consumers would pay.
Mr. Lauzier added explanation that, as of now, the revenue to Waste Management was lost and,
by contract, there were rates and an escalator clause. He did not believe that Waste Management
could, because of the "deadbeats," charge customers a surcharge to cover those losses. He
commented that approximately 10 percent in franchise fees would be lost to the City, but he
believed that, with the money upfront in the fiscal year, there would be a better return in
investment. He agreed that Waste Management would make up the money in some way.
Mayor Udine felt this was the wrong year to add to tax bills. With further discussion,
Commissioner Weissman believed that if it could be advertised that this action would result in a
reduction of fees, the people would understand. The Mayor stated that he understood the
economic costs, but he believed it would be palatable to taxpayers with an increase in services.
Until that could be said, he would be more in favor of tabling the item.
Commissioner Weissman believed the City had a certain timeframe to do this but the City would
not be obligated to implement it.
