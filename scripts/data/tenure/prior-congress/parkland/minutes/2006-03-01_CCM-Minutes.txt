III PUBLIC INPUT

Sara Goodman Lapidus of Country Place spoke regarding plans for Riverside Trail, which had
been included in the master multi-purpose trails plan two weeks ago. She understood that the
City had earmarked approximately $500,000 for the first phase from Trotters Lane south to
Holmberg Road and that an additional $600,000 was planned for the next phase. After speaking
about the existence of Brazilian pepper in that area, she felt that Riverside Trail should be
discussed as an agenda item before money was spent on landscaping or engineering. Ms.
Lapidus believed that the underlying documentation was potentially subject to challenge, and that
it would be a mistake to spend City money making this improvement until the Commission and
City Attorney examine: the CypressHead file with Broward County, platted in 1979 by the
developer, Narco Realty; a copy of the Riverside Coalition vs. the City of Parkland settlement
agreement dated May 17, 2002; a copy of Broward County and Parkland's vacating of the
Riverside right-of-way; a copy of the CypressHead Club's quit claim deed which deeded to the
City all of the land lying east of Tract L and V, dated July 21, 2004; and a copy of the original
Declaration of Protective Covenants and Restrictions for CypressHead at Parkland Lakes by the
developer, Narco Realty, which was joined by the CypressHead Homeowners' Association, aka
CypressHead Club, dated December 27, 1979.

Following Mayor Marks' recollection of the settlement, Mr. Maurodis offered that the settlement
had been undertaken with approval by the court, and quit claim deeds had been obtained from
every abutting property owner. He indicated he would be happy to examine any third party
challenges. It was a complicated situation, and he believed it had been implemented to the best
of the City's ability.

Jay Friedman, a resident of Ternbridge, addressed the Commission about the possibilities of fire
in their neighborhood due to the extremely dry conditions in Ternbridge Preserve and the debris
remaining there. He explained that he and his neighbors had been, and would continue to be,
vocal because they believed the Preserve was a disaster waiting to happen.

Mr. Lauzier noted that the Ternbridge Preserve would be addressed at the March 22nd regular
meeting.

RESOLUTION NO. 2006-13:

SITE PLAN AMENDMENT FOR
PARKING LOT LIGHTING OF
SHOPPES AT PARKLAND
(Quasi-Judicial Hearing)

COMMENTS:

A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, APPROVING THE
SITE PLAN AMENDMENT AND COMMUNITY
APPEARANCE BOARD APPLICATIONS TO REMOVE
NON-CONFORMING PARKING LOT LIGHTING
STRUCTURES AND INSTALL NEW PARKING LOT
LIGHT POLES AND FIXTURES IN COMPLIANCE WITH
THE CODE OF ORDINANCES FOR THE SHOPPES AT
PARKLAND, LOCATED AT 5901 HILLSBORO BLVD.

City Manager Mark Lauzier read the title of Resolution No. 2006-13.

Mayor Marks noted this was a quasi-judicial item.

Assistant City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young explained the petition, which pertained to the
entire shopping center. She noted that the original pole designs were not in compliance with the
City's Code and, after the hurricanes of the past year, over 50 percent of the fixtures and poles
were destroyed. Due to new Code requirements adopted last year, the petitioner was required to
reduce the light that may emanate from the fixtures and adhere to pole heights. Since the
petitioner could not obtain the same poles, a new design was required. They would increase the
number of poles due to the reduction in the height to under 25 feet. Ten existing poles at the rear

<<PG>>

MARCH 1, 2006
PAGE 7
of the building and closest to the residential development would remain at 20 feet or less. In
addition, there would be changes in the pole fixtures, which would be similar to those existing in
outparcels.

Ms. Gardner- Young indicated that staff had reviewed the site plan amendment and the
Community Appearance Board criteria and recommended approval with the conditions: that it
be pursuant to the photometric plan; that the documents and all submittals to the Building
Department must conform to Section 4-453 of the Code; and that all previous conditions not
modified remained applicable. The Planning and Zoning Board additionally recommended that:
the height of remaining light poles be confirmed at 20 feet and under; the City be furnished a
certified cost estimate with a letter of credit; and other items within the shopping center should
be brought into compliance - no later than six (6) months for items requiring Commission
approval and no more than 90 days for those items able to be approved by staff. She believed the
petitioner had made efforts to bring the shopping center into compliance, but they had not yet
fully complied. Staff concurred with the Board recommendations, which is reflected within the
Resolution.

The applicant's representative, Cary Fronstein, waived quasi-judicial.

Mayor Marks commented that he would also like to assure that light spillage into the residential
properties be rectified within 60 days. Ms. Gardner-Young noted that language could be added
to item 2 of Section 1 of the Resolution to state, ".. . and shall be corrected to the City's
satisfaction within 60 days."

Commissioner Udine questioned why it had taken so long to determine non-conformance and
why it had taken a hurricane to get the complaints of residents addressed. He believed staff
should have been more proactive. Ms. Gardner- Young responded that the original light poles
were specifically approved by the then-sitting Commission and were in compliance with an
adopted Resolution at that time. It was not until the hurricane hit that more than 50 percent of
the lighting became non-compliant, and the City could require their conformance to Code. Staff
had been working with the petitioner and felt that the six month time constraint would be
sufficient.

Commissioner Udine asked about other compliance violations and if the petitioner would also be
given six months to comply. Ms. Gardner-Young indicated the petitioner must meet the
requirements for Code compliance; the deadlines imposed were only for additional approvals,
and she offered examples.

Resident Sara Goodman Lapidus asked that the City and applicant work together to make sure
that: the fixtures approved are the least obtrusive lighting and height that would legally meet the
needs, the fixtures block light to the sides, the landscape buffers are augmented and
supplemented, and the lights are turned off after the close of business as the Code requires.

Commissioner McGilvray recalled a safety or security issue, and Commissioner Ribotsky noted
there were two types of lighting and that security lighting could stay on.

RESOLUTION NO. 2006-14:

SITE PLAN AMENDMENT TO
RELOCATE REAR LIGHTING AT
THE WATERWAYS SHOPPES
(Quasi-Judicial Hearing)

COMMENTS:

A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA, APPROVING THE
SITE PLAN AMENDMENT AND COMMUNITY
APPEARANCE BOARD APPLICATIONS TO RELOCATE
REAR LIGHTING FROM THE SEAWALL TO THE BACK
OF BUILDINGS BAND CAT THE WATER WAYS
SHOPPES, LOCATED AT 7499 STATE ROAD 7.

City Manager Lauzier read the title of Resolution No. 2006-14.

Assistant City Manager Caryn Gardner-Young explained this request pertained to the lake area
between the building of Waterways Shoppes and the residential development to the west. The
approved building permits and site plan documentation showed lighting within each column on
the retaining wall along the lake to shine onto the path used as a public area. During
development, the electrical conduits were filled in and the wiring for the lighting could not be
installed within the columns. The petitioner proposed using the same light fixtures, but changing
the location.

Commissioner Ribotsky questioned the circumstances in the filling in of the conduits, and Ms.
Gardner- Young believed the petitioner would be in a better position to respond to her question.

<<PG>>

As to site plan amendment requirements, Ms. Gardner-Young indicated that traffic circulation
and concurrency were unaffected by the petition; from the staff perspective as to compatibility,
the light fixtures were those previously approved and the consideration had been about the
impact upon the residences to the west. Based upon photometrics received, the lighting would
not be any greater than what the residences would have had with the lights placed upon the
seawall.

Ms. Gardner-Young stated that the Community Appearance Board requirements, pedestrian
amenities, and exterior lighting requirements were considered met. Photos of the lighting fixture

Staff recommended approval with conditions: compliance with the photometrics plan provided
at this meeting, and that all previous conditions of Waterways Shoppes not amended are
applicable. She noted that the Planning and Zoning Board unanimously recommended approval.

quasi-judicial proceding.

intention to build the seawall according to plan. During the course of construction, conduits
were installed, but an inspection of electrical was not called for and, as a result, the conduits were
filled in and the seawall capped. To correct the error, it would require ripping up the entire
seawall. Ms. Chakas reiterated that the same light fixture would be bracket-mounted on the
building and, as a result, the lights would be further from the residences. In their opinion, it was
a better situation for the residential neighborhood. She noted that the same light fixtures were
also used on the front of the buildings, which were approved on the site plan.

its vision.

2 of Pine Trails was one of the largest and most important projects facing the City for many years
to come and represented implementation of the Capital Improvement Program and budget
process. Bids were opened on February 3rd with three very well qualified respondents. After
reviewing the bids, qualifications, and references, staff and the City's consultant recommended
award of the project to Padula & Wadsworth Construction, Inc.

Mr. Lauzier introduced Scott LaMont and Eric Propes of EDSA, master plan consultants for Pine
Trails Park.

Mayor Marks asked regarding the two add-alternates and the budget. Mr. Lauzier indicated he
was currently in the process of evaluating the CIP, and was not recommending the award of the
add-alternates at this meeting. He noted that furniture, fixtures, equipment, and interior issues

suggested it critical that the City have at least one reference aside from the School Board. As to
the $250 a day liquidated damages in relation to $11 million, she believed it meaningless.

Commissioner Ribotsky also commented that she loved the buffering, but was concerned about
EMS access to some of the fields, such as the great lawn. Mr. LaMont responded that one of the
provisions in the park was that the sidewalks would be a minimum of eight feet and were

not examined the plans since the beginning of the project, and Commissioner Ribotsky asked that

Phase 2 of the park, but felt that a "ballpark" of additional costs, such as construction
management, should be taken into account.

suggested that the City Attorney and the consultants must review this matter. Mr. LaMont noted
this was compatible with other agreements and came from the City's own documentation.

Mr. LaMont commented that the contractor had satisfied the requirements of the bid with the
references provided.
