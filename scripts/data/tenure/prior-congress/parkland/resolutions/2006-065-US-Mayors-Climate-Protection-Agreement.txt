 1 RESOLUTION NO. 2006- 65
 3 A RESOLUTION OF THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE CITY OF
 4 PARKLAND, FLORIDA TO ENDORSE THE UNITED STATES
 5 MAYORS' CLIMATE PROTECTION AGREEMENT
 6
 7
 8 WHEREAS, the U.S. Conference of Mayors has previously adopted strong policy
 9 resolutions calling for cities, communities and the federal government to take actions to reduce
 10 global warming pollution; and
 11
 12 WHEREAS, the Inter-Governmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC), the international
 13 community's most respected assemblage of scientists, has found that climate disruption is a
 14 reality and that human activities are largely responsible for increasing concentrations of global
 15 warming pollution; and
 16
 17 WHEREAS, recent, well-documented impacts of climate disruption include average
 18 global sea level increases of four to eight inches during the 20th century; a forty percent
 19 decline in Arctic sea-ice thickness; and nine of the ten hottest years on record occurring in the
 20 past decade; and
 21
 22 WHEREAS, climate disruption of the magnitude now predicted by the scientific
 23 community will cause extremely costly disruption of human and natural systems throughout the
 24 world including: increased risk of floods or droughts; sea-level rises that interact with coastal
 25 storms to erode beaches, inundate land, and damage structures; more frequent and extreme
 26 heat waves; more frequent and greater concentrations of smog; and
 27
 28 WHEREAS, on February 16, 2005, the Kyoto Protocol, an international agreement to
 29 address climate disruption, went into effect in the one hundred forty-one countries that have
 30 ratified it to date; thirty-eight of those countries are now legally required to reduce greenhouse
 31 gas emissions on average 5.2 percent below 1990 levels by 2012; and
 32
 33 WHEREAS, the United States of America, with less than five percent of the world's
 34 population, is responsible for producing approximately twenty-five percent of the world's global
 35 warming pollutants; and
 36
 37 WHEREAS, the Kyoto Protocol emissions reduction target for the U.S. would have been
 38 seven percent below 1990 levels by 2012; and
 39
 40 WHEREAS, many leading US companies that have adopted greenhouse gas reduction
 41 programs to demonstrate corporate social responsibility have also publicly expressed
 42 preference for the US to adopt precise and mandatory emissions targets and timetables as a
 43 means by which to remain competitive in the international marketplace, to mitigate financial
 44 risk and to promote sound investment decisions; and
 45
 46 WHEREAS, state and local governments throughout the United States are adopting
 47 emission reduction targets and programs and that this leadership is bipartisan, coming from
 48 Republican and Democratic governors and mayors alike; and
 49

 2 WHEREAS, many cities throughout the nation, both large and small, are reducing
 3 global warming pollutants through programs that provide economic and quality of life benefits
 4 such as reduced energy bills, green space preservation, air quality improvements, reduced
 5 traffic congestion, improved transportation choices, and economic development and job
 6 creation through energy conservation and new energy technologies; and
 7
 8 WHEREAS, mayors from around the nation have signed the U.S. Mayors Climate
 9 Protection Agreement which, as amended at the 73rd Annual U.S. Conference of Mayors
 10 meeting, reads:
 11
 12 The U.S. Mayors Climate Protection Agreement
 13
 14 A. We urge the federal government and state governments to enact policies and programs to
 15 meet or beat the target of reducing global warming pollution levels to 7 percent below 1990
 16 levels by 2012, including efforts to: reduce the United States' dependence on fossil fuels and
 17 accelerate the development of clean, economical energy resources and fuel-efficient
 18 technologies such as conservation, methane recovery for energy generation, waste to energy,
 19 wind and solar energy, fuel cells, efficient motor vehicles, and biofuels;
 20
 21 B. We urge the U.S. Congress to pass bipartisan greenhouse gas reduction legislation that
 22 includes 1) clear timetables and emissions limits and 2) a flexible, market-based system of
 23 tradable allowances among emitting industries; and
 24
 25 C. We will strive to meet or exceed Kyoto Protocol targets for reducing global warming
 26 pollution by taking actions in our own operations and communities such as:
 27 1. Inventory global warming emissions in City operations and in the community, set reduction
 28 targets and create an action plan.
 29 2. Adopt and enforce land-use policies that reduce sprawl, preserve open space, and create
 30 compact, walkable urban communities;
 31 3. Promote transportation options such as bicycle trails, commute trip reduction programs,
 32 incentives for car pooling and public transit;
 33 4. Increase the use of clean, alternative energy by, for example, investing in "green tags",
 34 advocating for the development of renewable energy resources, recovering landfill methane
 35 for energy production, and supporting the use of waste to energy technology;
 36 5. Make energy efficiency a priority through building code improvements, retrofitting city
 37 facilities with energy efficient lighting and urging employees to conserve energy and save
 38 money;
 39 6. Purchase only Energy Star equipment and appliances for City use;
 40 7. Practice and promote sustainable building practices using the U.S. Green Building
 41 Council's LEED program or a similar system;
 42 8. Increase the average fuel efficiency of municipal fleet vehicles; reduce the number of
 43 vehicles; launch an employee education program including anti-idling messages; convert
 44 diesel vehicles to bio-diesel;
 45 9. Evaluate opportunities to increase pump efficiency in water and wastewater systems;
 46 recover wastewater treatment methane for energy production;
 47 10. Increase recycling rates in City operations and in the community;
 48 11. Maintain healthy urban forests; promote tree planting to increase shading and to absorb
 49 CO2; and

 4 WHEREAS, The United States Conference of Mayors endorses the United States
 5 Mayors Climate Protection Agreement as amended by the 73rd annual United States
 6 Conference of Mayors meeting and urges mayors from around the nation to join this effort.

 11 Section 1. The City Commission of the City of Parkland hereby endorses the United States
 12 Mayors' Climate Protection Agreement and authorizes the Mayor to execute the appropriate
 13 documents for the City to support the Agreement.
 14
 15 Section 2. That this Resolution shall become effective immediately upon adoption.
