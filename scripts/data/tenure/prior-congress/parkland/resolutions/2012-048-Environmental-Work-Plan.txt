 Memorandum 
TO: BRIAN ARCHER, PUBLICS WORK DIRECTOR 
From: WILLIAM MOHLER, ENVIRONMENTAL SUPERINTEDENT 
Date: MAY 11, 2012 
Re: ENVIRONMENTAL WORK PLAN UPDATE 
A 2012 Environmental Work Plan (EWP) has been created. The structure of the previous EWP has been 
retained. The following memorandum depicts updates to the current sections, as well as, lists the addition of 
2 sections; Section 9: 2012 Comprehensive Plan and Section 10: 2012-2013 Goals and Objectives. 
 
SECTION I Passive Parks and Nature Preserves: 
 
• Doris Davis Forman Wilderness Preserve 
Update- Invasive Exotic control is now under the responsibility of the city. The 2012 invasive 
exotic removal and maintenance Request for Proposal (RFP 2012-08) will enable Public Works 
to contract out the removal of exotic invasive species. A “Partners in Preservation” grant 
application has been submitted to Broward County for funds to aid in the removal of invasive 
exotic species, as well as, funding for native planting. The project amount requested through 
“The Partners in Preservation” grant is $50,000. $35,000 of this sum will go towards the 
removal of invasive exotic species and $15,000 will be put towards replanting native species of 
trees and shrubs in the preserve. Community outreach programs will be initiated with 
Riverglades Elementary School and parkland residents starting in April of 2012. 
 
• Parkside Drive Trailhead 
Update- The invasive exotic removal RFP 2012-08 will enable Public Works to contract out 
the removal of exotic invasive species on this site. This site will be placed on an annual exotic 
species removal plan for treatment of invasive exotic species not removed during the initial 
treatment. A nature preserve maintenance plan is being finalized and sent to The Florida Dept. of 
Environmental Protection, Marsha Connell, Regional Coordinator Office of Greenways and 
Trail.

SECTION III Streets and Road Rights of Way: 
 
Update- A collection of digital data in the form of GIS and CAD usable information is currently 
being collected and sorted. This data will be layered on DOQQ imagery of Parkland. Current 
features include streets, water bodies, parks, and landscape plans for recent projects. A GIS 
based geodatabase has been ordered through Geographic Technologies Group (GTG) that will 
contain locations of all storm water easements and drainage areas. This data will be utilized in 
the implantation of National Pollution Discharge elimination system permits (NPDES). 
 
Update- Emphasis is being placed on the use of native plant material for new installations and 
replacements of plant material in city right of ways. 
 
 
SECTION IV Promote Environmental Awareness and Education: 
 
• National Arbor Day Foundation Tree City USA Certification- Tree City USA is an ongoing 
community improvements program that is a good source for information and promotional tools 
to provide awareness to the residents. 
Update- All Tree City USA Awards are current since 1991 and as of April 2011 Parkland is still 
a certified Tree City USA award winner. 
 
• Community Wildlife Habitat Certification- This certification is a team effort including 
neighborhood associations, schools, garden clubs, chamber of commerce, local nurseries, and 
places of worship. This program will reach the majority of the community and provide extensive 
education and awareness about the environment and the proper use of plant materials. 
Update- the Community Wildlife Habitat Certification was accomplished and future 
maintenance of the certification will be achieved by incorporation into annual environmental 
events. 
 
• “The Right Tree in the Right Place” 
Update- The “The Right Tree in the Right Place” ordinance was adopted in 2006 and is 
presently being enforced. 
 
• It is recommended to have a city employee become member of Environmental Education 
Council of Broward County. There motto is to ,”Provide a communications, networking support 
structure, and county wide vehicle for facilitating the integration of environmental education and

SECTION V Tree Replacement Program: 
 
It is recommended to collect GPS locations of large specimen trees and start creating a 
geodatabase for future needs. This tree geodatabase can be used in conjunction with areal 
imagery of tree canopy to determine tree canopy percent coverage. 
 
SECTION VI Hurricane and Tropical Storm Tree Assessment, Evaluation and Risk: 
 
Update- Prevention strategies for existing trees and new plantings have been put into place. For 
example; staff specifically inspects all new plantings prior to installation for damaged and 
improper root balls on trees to alleviate future hazardous trees. Staff also, each year, provides 
awareness and education for tree care in preparation of the upcoming storm seasons. Possible 
tree canopy analysis and tree GPS location data will facilitate proper reimbursements through 
FEMA for the destruction caused by hurricanes. 
 
SECTION VII Recycling Action Plan: 
 
Update- Methodologies to increase recycling throughout the city. Education in the form of e-
mail blasts, web site articles and articles in local publications to inform residents and business 
owners about their recycling responsibilities. Increase placement of additional recycling 
receptacles around city. Possible locations to include entrance to dog park and/or other parks. 
Establish communication with waste management to increase pick up locations.
