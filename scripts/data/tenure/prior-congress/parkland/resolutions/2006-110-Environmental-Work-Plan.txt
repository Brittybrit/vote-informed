 Passive Parks and Nature Preserves:

 control within the City of Parkland. The challenges
 facing successful resource management of these
 sites are both ecological and social. Not only must ;
 these natural areas be restored and maintained for
 their ecological function but each must also provide
 education and awareness by way of public use.
 The Environmental Work Plan is a result of the
 City's relationship with State and County agencies,
 through binding or other funding mechanisms, for the sole purpose of preservation of these
 valuable natural resources. The City, through various contracts has agreed to maintain and
 operate these properties for public education and enjoyment. Under these contracts the City's

 complete. Six sites, which are listed below, have been selected to be part of the Capital

 CIP Funding Responsibilities
 The CIP proposes $107,600 for fiscal year 2006-07, (please refer to attachment 'A',
 Environmental Work Plan Chart) outlining the expenditures for these years. Fiscal year 2006-07
 CIP proposes funding for the Holmberg Road Natural Area, Tract C and C 1 (Ternbridge
 Preserve) and Terramar Park Preserve. The Holmberg Road Natural Area south ten acres has
 received initial and follow-up exotic plant treatments per mitigation requirements from Broward
 County. The ten acres would require quarterly maintenance and monitoring in fiscal year 2006-
 07 and in subsequent years reducing to twice a year in fiscal year 2007-08. Tract C and C 1 are
 presently being maintained and require further exotic maintenance; the removal of additional
 fallen Cypress Trees has been completed with plans to replant 145 new native trees. A quarterly
 exotic maintenance fee of$850 has been proposed to make available this obligatory preservation
 and removal of newly invasive plant species. Quarterly maintenance would follow in subsequent
 years reducing to twice a year in fiscal year 2007-08. Fiscal year 2006-07 Environmental Work
 Plan proposed spending totaling, approximately $107,600 with the remaining years presently
 unfunded.
 Fiscal year 2006-07 would bring an increase in CIP spending through reoccurring maintenance at
 the Holmberg Road Natural Area (10 acres), Tract C and Cl (Ternbridge Preserve) and Terramar
 Park Preserve and the addition of initial exotic plant specie treatment at Site 68A & 68B (Emmer
 Property), follow-up treatment on the 28 acres of the Holmberg Road Natural Area and exotic
 clean up at 6 Acre Wood. The procurement of the Parkland's committed maintenance at the
 Doris Davis Forman Wilderness Preserve and additional exotic maintenance and monitoring of
 the Holmberg Road Natural Area will need to be addressed in fiscal year 2007-08.
 After the initial phase of this segment of the Environmental Work Plan is complete through fiscal
 year 2007-08, and the proposed $107,600 is exhausted, the planned CIP Projects, the Pine Trails
 Wetland Mitigation and the Margate Blount Property will be in a condition of continuous
 maintenance. Exotic/invasive plant species maintenance will be at a level necessary to be
 performed two times annually. With increases in the cost of doing business, the following
 amounts could increase by 5-10% per year. The following is a breakdown of perpetual
 maintenance costs for each project based on proposals received by qualified vendors:

 ecological integrity of the sites, while providing opportunities for quality human enjoyment.

 evaluation of each resource and the scoping process. The scoping process (public involvement)
 is particularly important because it ascertains the expectations of the public for conservation and
 use of the natural resources of a site. The scoping process is also an opportunity to communicate
 to the public the unique, valuable and/or threatened resources of each site. Public participation
 and cooperation is important, if not essential, for successful management of the proposed natural
 areas.

 SECTION II
 Parks and Municipal Property:
 Future Environmental Needs
 Public Parks and Municipal property both upcoming and existing will necessitate attention in the
 future as land acquisitions and enhancements are completed. Imminent additions to the
 Environmental Work Plan such as the Beaty Property, the Four Acres on Parkside Drive and Old
 City Hall Site presently have no funding source for exotic removal but the Margate Blount
 Property has a Broward County reimbursement funding source in place when the sale of the land
 is complete. Mitigation and grant opportunities are possible for monies to aid with maintenance
 and enrichment of these properties.
 ➢ The Margate Blount Property will require an initial exotic removal costing approximately
 $30,000. This improvement and all other enhancements will be funded through the
 Broward County reimbursement for the purchase of the property.
 ➢ The Beaty property will require minimal exotic removal when improvements are
 implemented along the Holmberg Road Green Belt. Completion of Master Planning and
 the accomplishment of an ecological design would require this location to be placed on
 the Environmental Work Plan. The execution of a lake/wetland in conjunction with the
 creation of a native plant and animal habitat would require specific exotic plant
 monitoring and maintenance.
 ➢ Four Acre property at Loxahatchee Road and Parkside Drive is overgrown with exotic
 invasive species and it is recommended that upon site improvements the exotics plant
 materials be addressed for removal .
 ➢ Old City Hall site at the corner of Parkside Drive and Holmberg Road contains several
 invasive exotic plants and it is recommended that upon site improvements and/or
 development the exotics be addressed for removal
