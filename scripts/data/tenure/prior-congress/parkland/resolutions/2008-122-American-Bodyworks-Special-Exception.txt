 10 WHEREAS, the City Commission, as the governing body of the City of Parkland,
 11 Florida, pursuant to the authority in Chapter 163 and Chapter 166, Florida Statutes, and
 12 the Code of Ordinances, is authorized and empowered to consider petitions related to
 13 zoning and land development orders; and
 14
 15 WHEREAS, the notice and hearing requirements as provided in the Code of
 16 Ordinances, as adopted by the City of Parkland, have been satisfied; and
 17
 18 WHEREAS, the subject site has been approved on the City of Parkland's Future
 19 Land Use Map as Commercial and possesses a zoning designation of Business 2 (B-2);
 20 and
 21
 22 WHEREAS, the proposed Special Exception use was reviewed by the City of
 23 Parkland's Planning and Zoning Board at a public hearing conducted on October 16,
 24 2008 and it recommended approval of the petition by a 6-0 vote; and
 25
 26 WHEREAS, the City Commission has considered the evidence and testimony
 27 presented by the Applicant and other interested parties and the recommendations of the
 28 various City of Parkland review agencies and staff; and
 29
 30 WHEREAS, the City Commission has made the following findings of fact:
 31
 32 1. Surrounding Use Considerations: The proposed 5,441 square foot
 33 gymnasium requires a Special Exception approval within a B-2 zoning district. The
 34 customers' ability to use the facility 24 hours a day will adversely affect the surrounding
 35 uses due to the increase in crime. The proposed request for the gymnasium is to be a 24
 36 hour facility which can become a nuisance to the adjacent single family neighborhood
 37 during the early morning hours (between 12:00 a.m. and 5:00 a.m.). The gymnasium
 38 will be located inside and buffered by landscaping and the physical building.
 39
 40 2. Access, traffic generation and road capacity: The proposed 5,441 square
 41 foot gymnasium will not result in the adjacent roadways being over capacity and the
 42 traffic generation is similar in characteristics of other uses permitted in the same zoning
 43 district.
 44
 45 3. Utilities: The proposed 5,441 square foot gymnasium will not affect the
 46 services that will be provided to the site by such entities as Coconut Creek Utilities
 47 Department, Florida Power and Light, and Waste Management. No upgrades to the
 48 existing utilities are planned based upon the proposed use.

 2 Services provided by Broward County Sheriff's Office and Coral Springs Fire Department
 3 may be affected depending on the amount of calls received from this establishment.
 4 Broward Sheriff's Office ("BSO") has indicated that their biggest concern is with crime
 5 and the exposure to the increased opportunities to commit crime by a 24 hour facility.
 6 Per BSO, violent crime opportunity will present itself for clients walking from the car to
 7 the gymnasium or from the gymnasium to the car. Property crime opportunity (i.e. car
 8 theft or burglary) will present itself while the clients are inside the gymnasium.
 9
 10 4. Comprehensive Plan Considerations: Policy 1.3.14 of the Land Use
 11 Element states the Land Development Codes and Regulations of local government
 12 entities shall protect existing and planned residential areas, including single family
 13 neighborhoods, from disruptive land uses and nuisances. Based on the request for the
 14 gymnasium to be a 24 hour facility, the proposed use can become a nuisance to the
 15 adjacent single family neighborhood during the early morning hours (between 12:00 a.m.
 16 and 5:00 a.m.). Policy 1.1.1.h of the Land Use Element states land development
 17 regulations will, at a minimum:... provide that development orders and permits shall not
 18 be issued which results in a reduction of the level of services for the affected public
 19 facilities below the level of service standards adopted in this Comprehensive Plan. BSO
 20 has indicated that the proposed gymnasium will result in more law enforcement service
 21 needs therefore reducing the present level of service.
 22
 23 The proposed 5,441 square foot gymnasium generally conforms to and generally
 24 complies with the stated goals, objectives, policies and land uses established by the
 25 City's Comprehensive Plan however the hours of operation do not conform to and
 26 comply with the Comprehensive Plan.
 27
 28 NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE CITY COMMISSION OF THE
 29 CITY OF PARKLAND, FLORIDA that:
 30
 31 SECTION 1. The Special Exception Case No. 02-SE-08, the petition of Hope
 32 Calhoun, Esq., Ruden McClosky, Smith, Schuster & Russell, P.A. requesting a Special
 33 Exception to allow a proposed 5,441 square foot gymnasium at 7525 N. State Road 7
 34 within the Waterways Shoppes is hereby approved subject to the conditions of approval
 35 contained herein, which are in addition to the general requirements otherwise provided by
 36 ordinance:
 37
 38 1. This approval is based on a Site Plan entitled "Waterways Shoppes" prepared by
 39 Barranco Gonzalez dated May 9, 2007 and stamp-dated received by the Planning and
 40 Zoning Department on August 14, 2008.
 41
 42 2. This approval is limited to a 5,441 square foot gymnasium with the following uses:
 43 o A check out area;
 44 o An area for the display of books, videos, and software;
 45 o A product demo area;
 46 o A nutrition workshop;
 47 o A fitness area;

 7 3. The hours of operation shall be limited to 5:00 am to 12 midnight Monday through
 8 Sunday. If in the future the applicant would like to operate the gymnasium 24 hours a
 9 day, a Special Exception application is required to be resubmitted and processed through
 10 the public hearing process with the expectation that an armed security guard will be
 11 required to be on staff during the hours of at least 12 midnight until 5:00 am. Applicant
 12 shall execute a binding agreement, in a form acceptable to the City Attorney, prior to the
 13 issuance of any Certificate of Occupancy or Business Tax Receipt.

 15 4. Should the petitioner and/or property owner desire any other type of use, a new Special
 16 Exception application must be reviewed and approved by the City Commission.
 17
 18 5. In the event of the operation of this establishment creates any burdensome and
 19 negative impacts on neighboring residences, such as noise, parking, traffic and/or
 20 other nuisances, the applicant will make all improvements required to mitigate these
 21 nuisances so as not to negatively impact adjacent commercial/residential areas.
 22 6. Failure to comply with the conditions set forth herein shall constitute a violation of the
 23 City Code and shall cause this grant of a Special Exception to be void.
 24 7. The applicant shall obtain proper approvals such as Certificate of Occupancy and
 25 Business Tax Receipt from the City prior to operations.
