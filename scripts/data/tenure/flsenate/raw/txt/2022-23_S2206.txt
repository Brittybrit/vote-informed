1. Project Title           Nurse Educator and Nursing Program Enhancement

2. Senate Sponsor         Ana Maria Rodriguez

3. Date of Request        01/13/2022

4. Project/Program Description
    This initiative will support the critical shortage of diverse nurses and nurse educators in the State of Florida by 1) providing
   additional faculty positions for admission of additional diverse students in FIU undergraduate nursing programs, and 2)
   increasing the number of diverse Nurse Educators qualified to teach in nursing programs in the State of Florida. Florida is
   projected to have a shortage of 60,000 nurses by 2035, and providers are experiencing significant shortages due to the
   pandemic due to early retirements, transition to travel nursing at higher salaries, and attrition. The nursing shortage is
   compounded by and rooted in the shortage of nursing faculty, limiting the number of students admitted by FIU and
   accredited Florida nursing schools. This initiative will educate and train new diverse registered nurses and Nurse Educators
   to serve as clinical educators in health care institutions and faculty in nursing schools.

12. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Nursing Schools in the State of Florida are limited in the number of nursing students that can be admitted to Associate
    Degree and Bachelor of Science in Nursing programs due to the limited number of RNs prepared to teach nursing. This
    severely limits the number of students that can be prepared as registered nurses (RNs) and is a major driving force
    behind the nursing shortage. The Initiative will support 1) A Nurse Educator Program that will admit and train Nurse
    Educators to teach in Nursing schools in Florida and serve as clinical supervisors in hospitals; and 2) will provide
    additional faculty to support the increase in number of admitted students in Bachelor of Science in Nursing program at
    FIU and thereby the number of registered nurses in the region to support the hospital shortages.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     NWCNHS will hire diverse faculty for the Undergraduate Nursing Program and Nurse Educator Program who will teach
    and train diverse nursing students who mirror the South Florida population of medically under-served patients. Funds will
    provide faculty salaries, funds for recruitment of diverse students for both programs, recruitment of clinical preceptors,
    scholarships to enhance recruitment, success, retention and successful graduation and employment of diverse working
    registered nurses and advanced practice nurses who desire to teach, and students who desire to become registered
    nurses to provide competent bedside care. The College focuses on developing healthcare providers that provide high
    quality, culturally-competent care in partnership with the diverse society they serve—a society with ethnically, racially,
    socially, and culturally diverse individuals, families, communities, and populations.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Diverse graduates of the undergraduate nursing program will be employed by hospitals and other providers to deliver
    care to medically under-served patients which will aid in reducing the critical nursing shortages in the region. Graduates
    of the Nurse Educator program will be prepared to serve as faculty in nursing schools across the State of Florida for a
    minimum of five years and/or clinical nurse educators in health care institutions who will supervise and train new nurses
    to insure the highest quality of care is provided at the bedside.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Students enrolled in NWCNHS represent the ethnically diverse population of patients in the South Florida region and
    U.S., with 84% minorities of whom many are bilingual. New faculty recruited to teach in the College will match the existing
    highly diverse faculty with 60% from minority backgrounds. The College will partner with Miami-Dade College and
    additional diverse community colleges with working registered nurses who desire to earn their baccalaureate and
    master's degrees in nursing and become nursing faculty who will teach in nursing schools throughout the State of Florida.
    Ultimately, thousands of diverse and medically under-served patients in hospitals across the South Florida region will be
    served through additional highly skilled culturally competent nurses who will provide care at the bedside.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Given the State of Florida is projecting a shortage of 60,000 nurses by 2035, this initiative will reduce the nursing
    shortage by 1) increasing the number of nurse educators who will teach in state nursing schools and 2) increasing the
    number of diverse students directly admitted to baccalaureate nursing programs at FIU. Additional benefits include
    providing educational advancement and economic opportunity for diverse working registered nurses to become nurse
    educators with minimal student loan debt; advancing employment and educational opportunity for community college
    nursing graduates to become master's prepared nurse educators; supplying the region's health care institutions with new
    nurses while reducing recruitment cost, and improving quality of patient care and outcomes with nurse educator
    supervision of new nurses.

    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?
