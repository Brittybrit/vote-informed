4. Project/Program Description
    Matching funds for the Miami-Dade County Peace & Prosperity Fit2Lead after-school and summer paid internship program
   and to expand the number of post-secondary educational and vocational scholarships, specifically targeting at-risk and
   justice-involved youth. In Spring and Summer of 2021, Miami-Dade County saw a sharp increase of community violence,
   particularly gun violence, permeate throughout its communities, mirroring similar disturbing trends in major cities and
   counties throughout the country. In response to this epidemic, Miami-Dade County immediately implemented a multi-
   faceted response including the development of the Peace & Prosperity Plan and the deployment of Operation Community
   Shield, and the establishment of the Community Violence Intervention Initiative.

14. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Miami-Dade County, through its Police Department and its Office of Neighborhood Safety, has continuously focused on
    implementing innovative and sustainable solutions to reduce community violence, including gun violence. In 2021, a
    collective strategy was developed and employed to address this crisis locally, including the Peace & Prosperity Plan and
    Operation Community Shield. Funds will be used to achieve the following goals:
    . Expand youth paid after-school and summer internship opportunities (serve 350 additional at-risk and justice involved
    youth)
    . Double the number of scholarships for college and vocational training-eligible youth/young adults (150 $2,500
    scholarships).
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     This program brings together coordinated expertise from the County's parks, police, social services, and juvenile justice
    departments to provide paid internships and comprehensive wraparound services to at-risk youth and members in their
    household. These funds will be utilized to provide youth employment (wages), enrichment activities such as fishing and
    financial literacy, experiential learning, character development activities, uniforms, and background screening. During the
    school year students will work 12 hours per week and in the summer, hours will increase to 25 or more per week. Funds
    will also be used to provide 150 $2,500 post-secondary education scholarships via an application process.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     The funding will allow for:
    . The creation of additional paid internship opportunities for at-risk and justice-involved youth during the summer and
    school year. Internship host sites include various County departments, schools, and non-profit organizations. Youth will
    also engage in diverse activities to build social emotional learning awareness, professional relationships, environmental
    consciousness and financial responsibility.
    . An increase in the number of college and vocational training scholarships provided to at-risk and justice involved
    youth/young adults directly and indirectly impacted by gun violence.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Miami-Dade County youth, with a keen focused on at-risk and justice-involved youth.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Increased number of at-risk and justice involved youth participating in paid internships and professional mentorship;
    enhance youth employability skills; reduction in justice-involved youth re-offending; post-secondary education enrollment;
    justice-involved youth successfully completing diversion program and/or successfully closing their case with the Juvenile
    Services Department. Pre and post evidenced-based assessments, arrest/rearrest data, case reviews (aggregate), and
    participant surveys.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Funds could be provided on a reimbursement basis after services are performed.
