4. Project/Program Description
    With the increased awareness of climate change and beneficial use of sustainable energy sources, government agencies
   are revising their current "green" initiatives, specifically those relating to the use of petroleum and other combustible fuels,
   to further adhere to legislative ecological standards and regulations. For Miami-Dade County, this intiative is referred to as
   "Climate Action Strategy" and all department's within the county are expected to adhere to it. As such, Miami-Dade Fire
   Rescue (MDFR) will install (8) plugin electric vehicle charging stations at (2) MDFR fire stations to promote the continual
   transition from "tailpipe", fossil fuel combustion to an electrically-charged fleet. This, in turn, will continue supporting the
   move towards more sustainable energy sources in MDFR and give first responders the benefit of charging light and heavy
   fleet vehicles at fire stations.

     Miami-Dade Fire Rescue (MDFR) will install (8) plugin electric vehicle charging stations at (2) MDFR fire stations to
    promote the continual transition from "tailpipe", fossil fuel combustion to an electrically-charged fleet. This will continue
    supporting the move towards more sustainable energy sources in MDFR and give first responders the benefit of charging
    their county vehicles at fire stations in Miami-Dade County.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     This project will install (8) electric vehicle charging ports at (2) MDFR fire stations for the use of MDFR county and fleet
    vehicles, assisting in the transition towards more sustainable and environmentally-friendly energy sources. This gives first
    responders the benefit of charging their county and emergency response vehicles at fire stations across Miami-Dade
    County.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Installing (8) charging ports at (2) MDFR fire stations will produce less fossil fuel emission from county vehicles, as they
    will be utilizing alternative fueling sources that do not require the use of combustible energy. Citizens of Miami-Dade
    County can then benefit from a cleaner and sustainable environment, which can assist with lowering fuel costs, promote
    better air quality, and increase ecological preservation and protection within the county.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Miami-Dade County, specifically MDFR, which is about 2.7 million residents.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Allowing MDFR first responders, as well as other personnel, to charge their county and emergency response electric
    vehicles at fire stations will offer staff more locations to power their county electric vehicle, while continuing the promotion
    of sustainable energy and resources regarding transportation. This will ease the burden that fossil fuel energy sources
    cause to the environment and wildlife, giving Miami-Dade County citizens the protection and support from MDFR
    regarding climate change and use of sustainable energy sources.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Funds could be provided on a reimbursement basis after services are performed.
