2. Senate Sponsor         Ileana Garcia

3. Date of Request        10/27/2023

4. Project/Program Description
    This project aims to improve safety for children and families walking to school. The Little Havana area has been identified
   as having the highest instances of pedestrian crashes and fatalities based on crashes between 2012-2016 (5-YEAR
   TREND) - 293 Crashes and 12 fatalities. The data was developed from the Department of Highway Safety Motor Vehicles
   (DHSMV), crash data provided by FDOT, confirmed by the State of Florida, and the Miami-Dade County Department of
   Transportation and Public Works (DTPW). Results found Children and families walking to Citrus Grove Elementary and
   Middle School face danger when crossing the street, thus requiring improvements that prioritize pedestrian safety by
   creating safe routes to school. The project includes a pedestrian signal upgrade, high-emphasis crosswalk markings, curb
   extensions, s-shaped Mid-block crossing with rectangular rapid flashing beacons (RRFB), concrete islands, other median
   improvements, and adding a new traffic light.

14. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     This project aims to improve roadway conditions on 22nd Ave, where Citrus Grove Elementary and Middle are located, to
    have zero fatalities. This zone has been identified as having high pedestrian crashes and fatalities. Children and families
    walking to Citrus Grove Elementary and Middle School face danger when crossing, thus requiring improvements that
    prioritize pedestrian safety between 2012-2016 (5-YEAR TREND) - 293 Crashes and 12 fatalities. The data was
    developed from the Department of Highway Safety Motor Vehicles (DHSMV), crash data provided by FDOT confirmed by
    the State of Florida, and the Miami-Dade County Department of Transportation and Public Works (DTPW).
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     The project includes design, reconstruction, construction, and CEI services on SW 22th Avenue from NW 3rd to NW 6th.
    Six curb extensions and replacement, including crosswalk markings on side streets, installation of a new mid-block
    crossing controlled by an RRFB between NW 5th and 6th, concrete islands where existing chevron markings are, and
    upgrading existing span wire to pedestrian signal with a high-emphasis crosswalk.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     The benefits of this project include safer roadway and sidewalk conditions for youth and their families walking to school,
    pedestrians at large, and motorists in an area with high instances of pedestrian crashes and fatalities.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The target population served by this project includes area residents, businesses, and visitors walking or driving through
    this major roadway. The estimated number of individuals served is over 7,000 daily (pedestrians: including children and
    their parents, school staff/administration, the general community, as well as motorists driving through this area).
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     The primary benefit of this project is to provide safer conditions for pedestrian mobility, motorists, and residents. The
    success of this project will be measured in decreased traffic accidents.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Failure to meet deliverables without notification will result in financial penalties as described in contract.
