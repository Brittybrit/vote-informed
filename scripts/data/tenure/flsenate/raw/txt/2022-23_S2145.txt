4. Project/Program Description
    This project will support the planning and design phase of two Community Academic Health Centers for Primary Care
   Residencies based in Federally Qualified Health Centers (FQHCs). The State of Florida requires greater capacity to
   provide residencies in primary care in order to retain high-quality medical providers upon completion of their residency
   programs. Funding for these efforts will support all efforts associated with ideation, planning, and design including
   engagement of medical schools, hospitals, community-based organizations, and experts in Academic Health Centers. As a
   result of this funding, the Florida Association of Community Health Centers (FACHC) will develop a definitive 'playbook' for
   opening, operating, and sustaining a Community Academic Health Center in Florida, with specific planning for the opening
   of two centers and a template for future centers to come.

12. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The establishment of "grow your own" primary care residents to fill needed shortages at primary care provider slots such
    as FQHCs. Federal data, from the Health Resources & Services Administration (HRSA), indicates that providers that
    complete residencies in the primary care setting tend to stay in primary care once their residency is completed. This
    "grow your own" tool is an excellent recruitment tool to help Community Health Centers further provide healthcare for
    Florida's most vulnerable populations. Accordingly, this funding will help with the planning and design for building world-
    class Community Academic Health Centers that provide residency programs for primary care providers.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     With funding, FACHC will create a first-of-its-kind 'playbook' that describes all aspects related to the launch, operations,
    and sustainability of a Community Academic Health Center within FQHCs, with the specific focus on primary care in order
    to retain providers that are trained in Florida for long-term careers. Funding is necessary to support planning using
    evidence, leveraging best practices, and creating programming for a Community Academic Health Center that is relevant
    to the needs of the state and provides benefit to providers. The planning will include support for the accreditation process,
    building sustainable financial models, integrating recruitment strategies, and defining partnerships leading to the launch of
    two centers.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Funding for this project is necessary to ensure proper planning and design work associated with providing healthcare for
    the state's most vulnerable populations, including the uninsured and under-insured (which is at the core of FQHCs'
    mission). As a result of this funding and the completion of this project, and with secured funding, the launch of two
    Community Academic Health Centers can commence which will positively impact patient populations.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The planning portion of this work will include a targeted population of medical schools, hospitals, and community-based
    partners that collaboratively engage with FQHCs to create two Community Academic Health Centers.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Florida lacks in our ability to provide primary care residency programs. It is an area of great concern as provider
    shortages ramp up in geometric proportion. While we provide medical school training in 10 schools, most have nowhere
    to go for primary care residencies in state. FQHCs provide a setting where residents do tend to remain in primary care
    within the state once they complete their residency training. This project will be measured on the ROI of number of
    patients seen by these providers and, where data are available, any cost savings to the State. Expectations for long-term
    impacts is up to 25 residency placements within the centers, supporting up to 500 patients per year per resident based on
    standard workload calculations.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Penalty for not completing deliverables is the inability to launch the two Community Academic Health Centers referenced
    in this proposal.
