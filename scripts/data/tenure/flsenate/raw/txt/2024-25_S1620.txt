1. Project Title         Foster Forward: Developing Self-Sufficient Aged-Out Foster Youth

2. Senate Sponsor        Alexis Calatayud

3. Date of Request       11/27/2023

4. Project/Program Description
    Foster Forward helps some of Florida's most vulnerable residents, foster youth, to become self-sufficient, productive
   citizens by providing them with the support, resources, and the family environment needed to maintain housing, avoid
   incarceration, and achieve good mental and physical health through personalized coaching, year-round housing, engaging
   workshops, community connections, and college and employment readiness programming. We also work closely with
   schools to ensure students overcome learning loss, read at their grade level, and graduate. As a result of our programming,
   our students have low housing turnover rates, high graduation and college enrollment rates, and maintain checking and
   savings accounts, and obtain employment at Genesis Hopeful Haven (GHH) and community partners.

14. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Genesis Hopeful Haven (GHH) prepares youth to successfully transition from the foster care system to a self-sufficient,
    purpose-driven adulthood. The requested funds will provide them with a path to graduation, employment, and life skills.
    Over 300 foster youth will receive the support, resources, and family environment needed to maintain housing, avoid
    incarceration, become self-sufficient, and achieve good mental and physical health.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     GHH will help foster youth successfully transition out of care through comprehensive evidence-based solutions, including
    personalized coaching, stable year-round housing, engaging workshops and field trips, and community connections. We
    will also provide our successful college and employment readiness programs, including vocational training and an
    internship program.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     These funds will provide foster youth with essential life skills, enrichment, educational, and career classes led by
    knowledgeable staff and community partners. We will offer personalized tutoring, mental health support, behavioral
    assessments, one-on-one coaching, mentoring, job opportunities, housing, and accountability. We will also provide door-
    to-door transportation to ensure access to all services and programming.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Our primary target audiences include students who are foster youth ages 10-14, youth transitioning out of foster care
    ages 15-17, and former foster youth ages 18-25. GHH will serve 300+ youth annually. Citrus Family Network reported
    that 40% of foster youth in Miami-Dade will experience homelessness by age 19, 25% will be in the justice system by age
    20, and only 3% will earn a college degree. The Annie E. Casey Foundation notes that once they age out, they are more
    likely to have health and housing issues, employment and academic difficulties, incarceration, and other lifelong issues.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     The expected outcomes of this project include but are not limited to, improved physical and mental health, quality of
    education, life skills, and housing access, as well as the creation of immediate job opportunities, enhanced economic self-
    sufficiency, and diversion from the criminal/ juvenile justice system. We will measure these outcomes through surveys,
    one-on-one meetings, behavioral charts, grades, progress reports, financial savings, housing status, and employment
    history. We will also consistently solicit feedback from foster parents, GHH staff, teachers, counselors, and school
    administrators. To ensure this, we have performance benchmarks for GHH staff and conduct internal audits for our
    programs.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Discontinue funding would be the most effective penalty. However, GHH will meet deliverables as we always have with
    our community partners and grantors. Failure to meet these goals negatively affects our foster youth the most, something
    we refuse to do.
