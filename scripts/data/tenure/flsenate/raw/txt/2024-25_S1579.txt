4. Project/Program Description
    Scouting-LFL collaborates with specific school districts to deliver Florida mandated " Character Education " programs per
   statute 1003.42 (s) and provides a classroom based character education for students in schools with focus on at-risk, low
   performing, and Title 1 schools and to provide students with learning opportunities, mentorship, and life skills. Based on
   current funding, Miami-Dade and Lee Counties are the selected school districts. Lessons support schools with topics on
   respect, citizenship, responsibility, good decision making, kindness, and honesty. The Scouting-Learning for Life program is
   delivered to students through enhanced instruction, mentoring activities, and leadership training, and added value activities
   that offer additional character building opportunities. Students will advance through their grade specific program and earn
   achievements and awards.

    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Scouting-LFL will collaborate with specific school districts to deliver Florida mandated " Character Education " programs
    per statute 1003.42 (s) and to be verified by the DOE quarterly program delivery report to meet or exceed requirements to
    a set number of students. The project provides a classroom based character education for students in schools with focus
    on at-risk, low performing, and Title 1 schools and to provide students with learning opportunities, mentorship, and life
    skills.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     The Scouting-Learning for Life program is delivered to students through enhanced instruction, mentoring activities, and
    leadership training, and added value activities that offer additional character building opportunities. Students will advance
    through their grade specific program and earn achievements and awards.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Scouting- Learning for Life provides 8-10 character education lessons for every student participating in the program in
    each school district. Participating schools include the state's lowest performing schools, Title 1 and at-risk student
    populations. Lessons support schools with topics on respect, citizenship, responsibility, good decision making, kindness,
    and honesty.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     At-risk youth, youth in homeless programs, and grade school students in Title 1 schools are the target population. At the
    current rate of state and local funding, over 25,000 youth benefit from the Scouting-Learning for Life program.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     The direct benefit is the improvement of the quality of education in the schools through the development of the desired
    character traits of the lessons. The methodology includes performance data tracked in quarterly implementation reports,
    end of year evaluation reports, project deliverable forms, quarterly program activity report forms, verification of staff and
    teacher training, and pre- and post- surveys. In each school district, the Scouting-Learning for Life program collects data
    on students understanding of eight core character traits utilizing a pre-survey and post survey.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Under the Scouting-Learning for Life program, DOE may assess performance penalties of $2 per student if the program
    does not deliver the specific number of lessons in a school year. This has been the agreed upon strategy over the past
    years. We will continue to exceed the desired deliverables and measures as outlined.
