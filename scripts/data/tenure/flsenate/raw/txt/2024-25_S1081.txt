4. Project/Program Description
    Continued growth in density and school traffic (cars & kids) have increased conflicts on nearby roads where a park and
   children's learning academy are located (SW 4th AVe and SW 15th). The crash data reviewed and analyzed (2014–2018 )
   provided by the City of Miami identified safety concerns for pedestrians, bicycles, and vehicles. The analysis revealed 59
   crashes along SW 15th Road between SW 11th Street and SW 3rd Avenue, including documented crashes that resulted in
   injuries and property damages. This project aims to improve safety conditions, especially for students arriving at school as
   pedestrians from Brickell, The Roads, and along the Underline. This complicated intersection has numerous conflict points
   with cars and pedestrians. Improvements include a five-leg traffic circle, flashing beacons, high-emphasis crosswalks,
   improved lighting, and repaving. Additionally, this intersection is prone to flooding and draining improvements are part of
   the scope.

14. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     This project aims to improve safety conditions on SW 15th Ave and SW 4th, especially for students arriving at school as
    pedestrians from Brickell, The Roads, and along the Underline. This complicated intersection has numerous conflict
    points with cars and pedestrians. Improvements include a roundabout traffic circle, flashing beacons, high-emphasis
    crosswalks, improved lighting, and repaving. Additionally, this intersection is prone to flooding, and drainage
    improvements will be done.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Design, reconstruction, construction, and CEI services on SW 15th Avenue between and SW 4th): This complicated
    intersection has numerous conflict points with cars and pedestrians. Improvements include a roundabout traffic circle,
    flashing beacons, high-emphasis crosswalks, improved lighting, curb extensions, sidewalk replacement, sidewalk
    construction, crosswalk markings on side streets, new signage, and major drainage improvements.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     The benefits of this project include safer roadway and sidewalk conditions for youth and their families walking to school,
    pedestrians at large, and motorists in an area with high instances of pedestrian crashes, reported injuries and property
    damage. Improvements include a roundabout traffic circle, flashing beacons, high-emphasis crosswalks, improved
    lighting, curb extensions, sidewalk replacement, sidewalk construction, crosswalk markings on side streets, new signage,
    and major drainage improvements.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The target population served by this project includes area residents, businesses, and visitors walking or driving through
    this major roadway. The estimated number of individuals served is over 4,000 daily (pedestrians: including children and
    their parents, school staff/administration, the general community, as well as motorists driving through this area).
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     The primary benefit of this project is to provide safer conditions for pedestrian mobility, motorists, and residents. The
    success of this project will be measured in decreased traffic accidents and no flooding.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Failure to meet deliverables without notification will result in financial penalties as described in contract.
