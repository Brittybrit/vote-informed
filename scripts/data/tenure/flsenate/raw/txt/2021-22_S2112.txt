4. Project/Program Description
    We have a growing concern that schools, as currently structured, are not meeting the educational needs of our youth. A
   large number of children are simply slipping between the cracks at school, failing to consolidate basic skills, developing
   negative self-perceptions of their competence as students, and eventually withdrawing from academic challenges. The
   Wayne Barton Study Center believes that low-income children deserve the same opportunity as their more advantaged
   peers to explore expressive arts, sports, and other developmentally enriching activities. School reform efforts and the push
   toward National and State standards have brought these trends directly into the public view. In fact, recent studies reveal
   that many policy makers are now looking to after-school Academic Enrichment programs to correct educational failures.
   This project will provide Afterschool Academic Enrichment Services to approximately 2049 students (6) Days per week (5)
   hours per day.

12. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Prior to the Covid-19 Pandemic a large number of students were simply slipping between the cracks at school, failing to
    consolidate basic skills, developing negative self-perceptions of their competence as students, and eventually
    withdrawing from academic challenges. In light of the Covid-19 Pandemic and schools functioning through
    Distance/Virtual Learning the number of students falling behind has drastically increased. The goal of the proposed
    project is to provide children with the opportunity to improve academically, in order to meet or exceed State standards in
    core academic subjects, develop positive interactions with others, develop lifelong pro-social skills, avoid destructive
    behaviors in a safe and nurturing environment and help at-risk children successfully obtain a high school diploma as well
    as further their education.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     The methods used to achieve these goals are to (1) provide a friendly, yet academic environment for children and teens
    to complete homework, study for tests, and improve general academic skills, (2) provide professionally trained teachers
    for a range of academic subjects, (3) provide an ongoing assessment of needs, based on student performance, (4) and
    provide structured activities that stress education and personal growth. Certified Teachers, at a ratio of 15:1, will provide
    academic instruction, homework assistance, tutoring and enrichment activities; (6) Days per week, (5) Hours per day to
    approximately 2049 students. Services provided include: Transportation to and from various schools throughout Dade,
    Broward and Palm Beach to the facility, academic evaluation and tutoring, SAT Prep and Facilitation, college assistance
    and readiness, health and nutrition, recreation, educational field trips, hot meals and counseling/supportive services.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The target population of students we will be providing services to are classified as severely impoverished, Academically
    Low- Functioning (many of whom have learning deficits due to drug exposure while in-vetro). 90% of the students we
    provide services to will be 1st Generation College Students.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     The benefit to the State of Florida will be the long-term outcome for future generations. The centers for Disease Control
    (2018) stated that “Good education predicts good health and disparities in education and educational achievement are
    closely linked. Despite these connections, public health professionals rarely make reducing the number of students who
    drop out of school a priority, although nearly one-third of all students in the United States and one-half of Black, Latino,
    and Native American students do not graduate from high school on time. Furthermore, the CDC contends that “Education
    is one of the strongest predictors of health. The more schooling people have the better their health is likely to be.
    Although education is highly correlated with income and occupation, evidence suggests that education exerts the
    strongest influence on health.
