4. Project/Program Description
    Over 300,000 children in the state of Florida have a parent incarcerated; over half of these children are young children as
   their parents are early in their criminal career. Research states that these children are acutely at-risk for anti-social
   behaviors, mental illness and cognitive delays stemming from exposure to Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACE). As a
   silent population, these children are often overlooked by social service systems because no system exist for identifying
   them and offering services. Program will provide supplemental support services for children ages 0-5 with an incarcerated
   parent, including home safety visits, Ages and Stages Questionnaire Assessment and ongoing caregiver community
   supports program.

14. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     When a parent is incarcerated, infants and young children suffer from insecure attachment and bonds to their parent and
    this insecurity undermines children's ability to cope and to grow in a healthy manner. These broken bonds have been
    linked to diminished cognitive abilities and inabilities to establish positive relationships. Over 25% of children with
    incarcerated parents are under 5 years of age. COI works with the caregivers to provide safety, developmental support
    and trauma informed wrap-around care. The program also provides critical training on brain development and parenting
    within the correctional facilities to guide the incarcerated parent on how to support their child in overcoming the
    challenges and achieving a healthy developmental track. This training is particularly important for female incarcerated
    parents who are working to reunite with their children and required to learning parenting skills, especially to satisfy for
    court requirements.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Babies 'N Brains provides home visits to provide wellness check ins, child safety assessments and Ages and Stages
    Questionnaire (ASQ) for children 0-5 to screen developmental delays. The program is proposing to enroll a minimum of
    300 children. The program also provides inmate education on infant brain development and child wellness. Over the past
    eight years, five infants/young children of COI clients have passed away due to untimely accidental deaths. As a result,
    COI staff will work to conduct home visits with 85 percent of households with children 5 years of age and under to
    conduct home safety checks, to provide safety equipment, assist with questions of caregivers about the child's
    development and provide trauma-informed wrap-around care coordination. Care coordinators will continue to provide
    intensive emotional support to small children who are having difficulties grappling with the absence of their incarcerated
    parent.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Inmate education on infant brain development and child wellness, child safety assessments and Ages and Stages
    Questionnaire (ASQ) for a minimum of 300 children (ages 0-5) to assess development delays, and trauma informed wrap-
    around care coordination. Babies 'N Brains will collaborate with community service providers for referrals and to assist
    with developmental delays. Home safety assessments and home safety materials will be delivered to 85 percent of
    households with children age 5 years old and under. Advocacy services will be provided to enhance public understanding
    of the plight of children of incarcerated parents.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The target population served by this project are children, ages 0-5 year of age with incarcerated parents and their
    caregivers. We serve currently and formerly incarcerated parents, assisting them to establish and maintain positive
    relationships with their children and caregivers. We anticipate serving 300 children with these funds. This equates to
    approximately 200 families or incarcerated individuals. Research from Cambridge University documents that children with
    incarcerated parents are at twice the risk of mental illness and three times the risk of antisocial behaviors. Children with
    incarcerated parents are exposed to higher levels of community and direct violence. Many are economically
    disadvantaged and often face homelessness. Further, many within the COI population struggle with developmental
    disabilities and/or physical disabilities.

    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     The expected benefits are to interrupt intergeneration incarceration by identifying and working with children with
    incarcerated parent early in their development. Activities will reduce Adverse Childhood Experiences by teaching brain
    development and child safety precautions. Family visits will create strong bond between incarcerated parent, children and
    caregivers to promote healthy families. Improving parenting skills will be the focus during individual sessions, support
    groups and learning experiences for incarcerated parents, caregivers and children. Measures include pre and post
    surveys, observational data using proven research tools and techniques, such as the Ages and Stages Assessment and
    other evidence based programs.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     $1,000 financial consequence per child under proposed 300 children to receive home visit and ASQ assessment. $500
    for every percentage under 85% of households with children five years old and under who do not receive a home safety
    assessment and supporting supplies.
