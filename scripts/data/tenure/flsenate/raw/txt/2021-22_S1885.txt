4. Project/Program Description
    Funds will support the Inclusion Theatre Project,
   a one-of-a-kind theatre training program for students with developmental and intellectual disabilities founded in 2014 in
   Coral Gables, FL by the Area Stage Company. ITP currently serves over 300 youth, at no cost to them, through on site
   classes at ASC and coordinated outreach classes by ITP instructors at local partner schools. Yearly, students present two
   public access musical theatre productions, as well as multiple outreach performances throughout the community. Though
   all ASC Conservatory classes have always been fully inclusive, ITP is focused solely on its students' progress, allowing
   instructors to tailor their approach based upon unique student needs and abilities. The program originated from ASC's
   belief that training in the performing arts teaches vital tools for life.

12. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The specific goal and purpose of the funds requested is to continue to serve the population of 300+ developmentally and
    intellectually disabled students that the Inclusion Theatre Project currently serves. This funding will make theatrical
    education possible for these students, helping to improve their social-emotional intelligence, communication and
    leadership skills, cognition, coordination, and emotional wellbeing, as well as providing a sense of community for them.
    ITP's primary goal has always been high-quality performing arts education for each and every student. The program also
    aims to counter the devastating effects of judgments often leveled at students with development and intellectual
    disabilities in today's society. Unfortunately, the shortcomings of our students are often emphasized instead of their
    unique strengths and abilities. ITP focuses on helping students manifest what they are capable of achieving.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Activities provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds include theatrical classes in acting, dance (ballet, jazz,
    tap, musical theatre, flamenco), music, and singing as well as technical theatre including stagecraft, costuming, makeup,
    lighting design, and stage management. Classes are taught by ITP trained instructors who are veterans in their various
    artistic fields as well as experienced working with individuals with developmental disabilities. Students also participate in
    two to three fully produced musical theatre stage productions each year which they rehearse and perform in addition to
    classes. Students and instructors also participate in multiple community outreach activities yearly.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Direct services include artistic education, as well as improvement in the following areas: social-emotional intelligence,
    communication and leadership skills, cognition, coordination, and emotional wellbeing. To the greater community, ITP
    provides education about and exposure to the disabled community, allowing camaraderie, acceptance, and inclusion to
    become the society norm.

    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     ITP serves approximately 320 Miami Dade county students, ages 6 and up, with developmental, intellectual, and
    physical disabilities. Students currently come from ITP partner schools: Crystal Academy, Great Heights Academy, the
    Marian Center School and Services, Miami Learning Experience, and Our Pride Academy, as well as from Miami Dade
    county public schools.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Onsite students at ASC, and their parents are given pre and post assessments and surveys in order to measure
    outcomes and satisfaction. Additionally, partner school classes are evaluated once per year to ensure satisfactory
    student progress. Student attendance is tracked carefully for all groups, and perhaps most effectively and importantly, all
    students participate in multiple theatre performances onstage each year. This is where progress and development are
    most evident. Success is defined as follows: 100% of all students will have improved in all parameters (cognition, fine
    motor skills, verbal communication, cooperation, self-confidence, etc.) as evidenced by pre and post assessments, 100%
    of students will have completed all rehearsals and performances throughout the session, and 100% of students and
    parents will be satisfied with the program.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     As per the current contracting agency of these funds- The Florida State Agency for Persons with Disabilities, according
    to contract #ACX61, page 12, section 38 of the existing contract, instances of noncompliance will be penalized through
    financial consequences.
