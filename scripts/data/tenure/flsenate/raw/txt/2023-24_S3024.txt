1. Project Title          Florida Nonhuman Primates (NHP) Career Opportunities

2. Senate Sponsor        Ana Maria Rodriguez

3. Date of Request       03/13/2023

4. Project/Program Description
    This program will provide career and job skills experiences to high school and college students interested in biomedicine
   and conservation. Veterinarians and scientists will supervise students in a service-learning setting emphasizing animal care
   and conservation medicine working with nonhuman primates (NHP). Demand for NHP is surging in biomedicine and from
   competition from China, increasing the need for a skilled and compassionate workforce in the lab, zoos and native habitats
   to provide care and conserve species. Students will be prepared to compete for jobs in this field of high demand and
   importance to the biomedicine industry and to conservation.

    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Career and job skills experiences for high school and college students interested in biomedicine and conservation.
    Veterinarians and scientists will supervise students in a service-learning setting emphasizing animal care and
    conservation medicine working with nonhuman primates (NHP). Demand for NHP is surging in biomedicine and from
    competition from China, increasing the need for a skilled and compassionate workforce in the lab, zoos and native
    habitats to provide care and conserve species.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Under the supervision of and collaboration with veterinarians and faculty, work plans and instructional and training
    materials will be developed; students will be recruited and work directly with the animals and receive instruction on their
    care, welfare, and conservation; and students will receive exposure to career opportunities.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     This is a hands-on, service-learning opportunity, working alongside experienced professionals, in the high demand fields
    of animal care and medicine and the related field of conservation focused on nonhuman primates. The services shall be
    utilized to attract the maximum amount of at-risk youth participants from the community possible.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     High school and college students as well as at risk youth. The number of individuals to be served is expected to range
    between 51 and 100.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     This project will improve the ability of Florida students to compete for jobs in the biomedical, zoo and conservation fields
    through training experiences focused on the care of nonhuman primates. The outcome will be measured by the roster of
    students who successfully complete service learning programs and of those who go on to related jobs in industry, zoos,
    etc., or to higher level education.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     All deliverables and performance measures shall be met. Monkey Jungle has never entered into a contract and not met
    the deliverables and performance measures.
