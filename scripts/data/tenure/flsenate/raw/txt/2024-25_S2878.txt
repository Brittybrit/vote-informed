4. Project/Program Description
    We aim to ease the suffering of children in foster care, the loneliness of senior citizens and the economically disadvantage.
   Our Community Garden Initiative is a comprehensive effort to establish edible and sustainable community gardens in
   several communities in Miami-Dade and surrounding Counties. A community garden is a carefully planned, perennial
   landscape that simulates the structure and function of a natural forest ecosystem while providing an abundant, diverse, and
   year-round supply of edible plants, produce and herbs. This project aims to transform underutilized parcels of land into
   thriving food gardens that will serve as an invaluable resource for our community. The components include: Site
   transformation, educational programs, community engagement, nutritional support, and environmental sustainability. The
   results include enhanced community nutrition, environmental stewardship, educational opportunities, community cohesion
   and economic resilience.

     The specific goal of installing a food forest is to create a sustainable and resilient community resource that addresses
    various societal challenges. Through the establishment of food forests, we aim to achieve the following objectives:
    improved food security, community nutrition, educational opportunities, economic empowerment, and promotion of
    sustainable practices.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Community engagement, educational outreach, harvest and distribution, nutritional support, advocacy and outreach,
    collaboration with local organizations, and evaluation and improvement.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Education of nutritional sustainability, healthy food security, community engagement, reduced or free food costs.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Elderly, persons with poor mental and physical health, economically disadvantaged persons, at-risk youth, grade school
    students, high-school students, and developmentally disabled. 2000-3000 thousand individuals are expected to be
    served.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Improve physical health, improve mental health, enrich cultural experience, improve agricultural education and
    production, improve quality of nutritional education, increase or improve economic activity, enhance individual's economic
    self-sufficiency. We will measure the outcome with a combination of financial data, skill development assessments, and
    feedback from individuals, students, teachers, administration and seniors.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Return the funds.
