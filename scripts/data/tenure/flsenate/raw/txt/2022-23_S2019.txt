4. Project/Program Description
    The City’s existing system is comprised largely of 4”-8” cast-iron mains constructed over 50 years ago with 2” galvanized
   service lines that serve customers primarily through rear service easements. In addition to being undersized to serve the
   existing and projected population of the City, the system suffers from documented persistent leakage, as well as
   intermittent failures and ruptures causing disruptions in service, damage to infrastructure, and potential contamination. In
   2015, The City of West Miami (City) was awarded an FDEP grant to perform an analysis of the citywide potable water
   system and prepare a master-plan to identify deficiencies and a capital improvements plan. The Master Plan was
   completed in 2016 and a capital plan was identified with prioritized areas for implementation. In 2019 the City was awarded
   an appropriation from the State of Florida to begin Phase I. This Grant will target improvements in the Phase II priority
   basin of the City's Master Plan.

12. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The major goal of the funds will be 100% Completion of Phase II of the Potable Water Replacement Program to include
    new water mains and service meter installations that will significantly reduce leaks caused by the aging system and
    reduce the wasted potable water going into the aquifer. This will provide overall water quality improvements to the region
    by reducing the amount of wasted potable water consumption which has been estimated to be approximately 150M
    gallons per year.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

    The City will undertake a procurement process to hire a qualified contractor that will subsequently construct the designed
    Water Main improvements. The funds will be used for the Construction activities of this phase of the project.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Citizens will be provided improved potable water pressure and much reduced service interruptions as a result of the
    upgraded water mains and new service connections.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     This phase of the project will serve approximately 1/3 of the City's population or nearly 3000 citizens.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     The expected benefits of this project are clearly outlined in the City's Potable Water Master Plan completed in 2016. The
    Master Plan identified up to 150M per year of lost Potable Water through the leaks and breaks that have constantly
    plagued the City's aging and deteriorated pipes. In addition, the existing undersized system does not provide sufficient
    pressure in more hydraulically distant parts of the City and inferior fire flows throughout. With Phases I and II of the
    Master Plan completed, the Improvements will affect approximately 70% of the City's population and the outcomes will be
    measurable through a tangible reduction in leaks and main breaks yielding reductions in recorded water consumption,
    along with improved pressures measured through the City.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     If we fail to meet our deliverables or performance measures, we should be given notice and the opportunity to come into
    compliance. Should we fail to address the concerns, funding should be withheld until a time that the project complies with
    the requirements. Failure to meet deliverables would cancel the contract and forfeiture of the remaining balance of
    unspent Appropriation.
