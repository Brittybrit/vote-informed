4. Project/Program Description

12. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     LEAP will continue to provide its in-prison trauma-focused education program at Homestead Correctional Institution.
    LEAP will expand its supportive housing from six residents to twelve residents. LEAP’s programming provides tools and
    education for women as they support themselves and their children. LEAP programming is proven to be effective in
    achieving lower rates of recidivism among its gradutes, as compared to women in the state of Florida overall. Stable,
    suitable and safe housing is key to ensuring that lower rate of recidivism. LEAP’s proven rate of recidivism is less than
    6%. We have learned that true rehabiltation and successful reentry only happen through comprehensive pre and post
    release programs, which is why we focus on providing a continuum of care to women involved with the criminal justice
    system.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     LEAP will provide in-prison education, entrepreneurial training, workforce readiness, housing, mentorship, and reentry
    support services including case management, substance abuse programming, rent subsidies, healthcare stipend, paid
    internship, and other.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Curently incarcerated women screened by the Department of Corrections will recieve in-prison, trauma-focused
    education. Upon graduation, and based upon availability, supportive housing will be provided for up to twelve citizens at a
    time. Residents of the supportive housing will be provided with a paid internship. Other reentry support services including
    case management will also be provided.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     LEAP’s target population is women currently incarcerated within the state of Florida. Nearly all of LEAP graduates are
    indigent with limited options for housing and employment after their release from prison. Over 80% of incarcerated
    women suffer from the untreated effects of childhood sexual trauma, domestic violence and addiction as well as trauma
    experienced while incarcerated. The majority of formerly incarcerated women are mothers of children under the age of 18
    and have sole custody. These women are most often the primary caregivers for their children and will resume care of
    their children upon release. LEAP provides its program participants with the tools necessary to be self-sustaining.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     LEAP will measure the rates of recidivism of its program graduates. Project success will be measured by continuing to
    achieve a lower rate of recidivism than the state of Florida’s overall rate. LEAP Case Manager will track rates of
    recidivism. Furthermore, although it is more difficult to measure, we expect to see increased rates of employment and
    decreased rates of homelessness amongst our program graduates.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Failure to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract will result in repayment to the
    administering agency.
