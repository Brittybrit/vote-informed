4. Project/Program Description
    Incarceration of parents/caregivers impacts 300,000 FL children. Research demonstrates that these children engage in
   anti-social behaviors and suffer mental illness at higher levels, putting them on the path toward incarceration themselves.
   COI, Inc. is an innovative approach to interrupt that pattern by collaborating with FDC to rebuild families with wellness,
   literacy, civic and STEM learning experiences that support both children and their incarcerated parent's improvements as a
   family unit. The program started in South Florida and has expanded to most of the metropolitan areas, serving over 2,600
   families with over 6,000 children to date. COI, Inc. worked with a warden to design special child-focused visitation focused
   on reassuring the children about their parent’s safety, improving parenting and addressing behavioral issues. Visits are
   quarterly and to date, COI, Inc. has coordinated over 585 child-focused visits to 22 Florida correctional facilities.

14. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     When a parent is incarcerated, children are uncertain about the safety of their parent and this insecurity undermines
    children's ability to cope and to grow in a healthy manner. The result for young children is insecure attachments and
    bonds. These broken bonds have been linked to poorer peer relationships and diminished cognitive abilities. Over half,
    58%, of children with incarcerated parents are under 10 years of age. School-aged children and teenagers face problems
    such as poor academic performance, peer relationship issues and classroom behavior problems, often resulting in
    suspension and high drop-out rates. The Children of Inmates, Inc. Family Strengthening and Reuniting program
    addresses these challenges by reconnecting children with their incarcerated parent and working to sustain that
    connection. Follow-up and support services are offered to help the child overcome the challenges and achieve a positive
    developmental track.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     In an effort to continue to support families impacted by parental incarceration, COI is working to resume bonding visits to
    support family strengthening in FL correctional facilities with this funding. In the summer of 2022, COI conducted 17
    bonding visits, resuming the process of connecting over 100 families across the state. In FY 2023, COI was only able to
    conduct 4 bonding visits. Prior to the pandemic, in 2019, by leveraging State and local funding, COI conducted 74 visits
    involving over 846 incarcerated parent/caregivers,1686 children with 848 caregivers. COI will also continue to work with
    incarcerated individuals and FDC to identify new families.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Direct services to be provided will include transportation, trauma informed support and activities to support families
    during the bonding visits. For participating “fragile families” wrap-around care coordination, including facilitated referrals to
    appropriate service providers to meet basic needs including addressing health, homelessness, disabilities, substance
    abuse and educational needs, child development assessments, child therapy and child development activities and child
    safety protection gear will be provided. COI also facilitate pro-social community and hobby events for the families.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The target population served by this project are children with incarcerated parents and their caregivers. COI serves
    children, ages 0-21 throughout Florida. We also serve currently and formerly incarcerated parents, assisting them to
    rebuild relationships with their children and caregivers. We anticipate serving 1,000 children with these funds. This
    equates to approximately 600 families or incarcerated individuals. Research from Cambridge University documents that
    children with incarcerated parents are at twice the risk of mental illness and three times the risk of antisocial behaviors.
    Many are economically disadvantaged and often face homelessness. Further, many within the COI population struggle
    with developmental disabilities and/or physical disabilities. Children with incarcerated parents are exposed to higher
    levels of community and direct violence.

    be measured?

     The expected benefits are to interrupt intergeneration incarceration by identifying, working with and supporting children
    with incarcerated parent. Working with "fragile families", activities will focus on reducing physical, verbal aggression and
    anti-social behaviors in children with incarcerated parents who exhibit these behaviors. Family visits create strong bond
    between incarcerated parent, children and caregivers and promote family support for the incarcerated parent upon
    returning to community. Activities focus on increasing knowledge of civic responsibilities as a platform to connect the
    children to their parent/caregiver. COI also encourages one-on-one direct time between the child and incarcerated parent
    to bond without interruptions from caregivers. During this bonding time, COI staff monitor the interactions for
    appropriateness but do not intrude into the bonding experience directly. One-on-one and family contact allows the child to
    experience parental engagement.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Financial Consequences of $1,250.00 per occurrence if at least eighteen (18) bonding programs are not completed-
    annually. Financial Consequences in the amount of $800.00 for each child less than five hundred (500) unique children.
    Financial Consequences of $1000 if at least 75 unique children of "fragile families" are not provided enhanced services.
