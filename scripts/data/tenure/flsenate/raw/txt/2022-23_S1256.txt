4. Project/Program Description
    The Firefighter Cancer Initiative (FCI) is a unique program led by the University of Miami Sylvester Comprehensive Cancer
   Center, which was awarded designation from the National Cancer Institute (NCI). This program aims to understand and
   address why firefighters are at increased risk of developing and dying of cancer relative to the populations that they serve.
   FCI supports novel translational research, wide scale education and prevention initiatives, and collaboration with firefighters
   to develop and implement practices that can reduce noxious exposures associated with disease risk.

12. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     A study published in a journal of the American Society of Clinical Oncology found that due to COVID-19 cancer
    screening rates are dramatically down, which means increases in cancer morbidity and mortality will likely be elevated for
    years to come. Because of the unique demands of first responders, this decline in screening may be worse. We will
    continue to expand firefighters access to cancer screening through our mobile clinic, which provides screenings on-site at
    fire stations; expand access to education on best practices for cancer risk reduction including on-line access to curriculum
    and developing guidelines for sub groups such as Urban Search and Rescue teams responding to catastrophic events
    such as Surfside Collapse; facilitate prevention and earlier detection of disease; identify exposures that account for
    increased cancer risk; field test new technology and methods that measure exposure in the field; and evaluate the impact
    of best practices on cancer risk reduction.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Expand research on characterizing firefighter cancer, for which we have recruited an expert whose work with World
    Trade Center-exposed firefighters has been pivotal in characterizing an increased incidence of multiple myeloma; expand
    access to cancer screening and earlier detection of disease through a mobile clinic; expand access for firefighters across
    the state to education on best practices for cancer risk reduction and prevention strategies, including virtual curriculum
    and education modules, and tailored guidelines for sub groups; identify exposures that account for increased cancer risk
    and field test new technology and methods that measure exposure in the field through wearable and mobile devices;
    evaluate the impact of best practices on cancer risk reduction; and work with IARC and WHO internationally to highlight
    the need for continued research on cancer in the fire service and collaborate with NIOSH at the Federal level in building
    the National Firefighter Cancer Registry.

     Funds are invested in increasing participation in cancer screening and prevention for cancers overrepresented within the
    fire service, including access to these services through a unique mobile clinical infrastructure. Additionally, funds will
    support continued evidence-based education of Florida fire departments and sub groups within the fire service including
    Urban Search and Research and Wild-land firefighters on cancer risk related to occupational exposures as well as best
    practices for risk reduction derived from our scientific progress to date. Funds will also provide for scientific inquiry and
    evaluation of cancer in the fire service and ways to track and evaluate exposures.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The Firefighter Cancer Initiative serves the Florida fire service, including career and volunteer firefighters and inspectors.
    FCI has engaged 8,000 of such individuals including 150 first responders who responded to the Surfside Collapse as well
    as over 7,500 first responders in COVID related projects. We anticipate increasing this number by 1,000 in the coming
    year as well as continuing in our efforts nationally, as FCI leads the national conversation on cancer in the fire service.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     FCI aims to increase the life expectancy of firefighters and their families by better understanding cancer risks related to
    occupational exposures. FCI research findings will result in developing new safety standards and best practices to reduce
    firefighters’ increased risk of developing and dying of cancer. Outcomes will be measured in various ways, including
    number of firefighters engaged; number of firefighters screened for cancer; successful new technologies to monitor
    exposure; number of tumor specimens submitted to the tumor bank to identify biomarkers and therapeutics; continued
    analysis of the dataset linking the Florida Cancer Data System with State firefighter data. We plan to increase uptake of
    best practices, including cancer screenings, as well as increase the amount of federal dollars brought to Florida for this
    initiative.
