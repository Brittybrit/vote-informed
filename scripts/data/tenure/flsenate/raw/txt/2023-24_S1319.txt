4. Project/Program Description
    The Village of Key Biscayne is requesting $450,000 to rebuild the Village’s oceanside shoreline after the loss of a
   significant portion of the beach due to Hurricanes Ian and Nicole in the fall of 2022. Hurricanes Ian and Nicole eroded and
   damaged the beach structure built during last renourishment. Adjacent properties have little protection against future
   storms. With assistance, the Village can prevent further damage and reduce risk to life and property in upcoming hurricane
   seasons. Estimated cost to renourish the beach is $2,953,777. FDEP is considering offering $1,350,762 through its Beach
   Management Funding Assistance Program. Because this was an unplanned direct storm damage loss, Village did not
   budget for the remaining expense. The Village requests $450,000 in assistance to help cover the difference between
   FDEP’s offer and what the Village requires for support. Without assistance, Village cannot restore its oceanside beach, its
   first line of defense against future storm surge

    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The purpose of this funding is to complete a beach renourishment which is critical to the Village's oceanside shoreline
    protection. A healthy shoreline protects residents from the threat from storm surge and prevents impacts like those
    experienced on Sanibel Island from Hurricane Ian. A strong shoreline supports a beach ecosystem, particularly a healthy
    sea turtle nesting habitat, a severely threatened species by sea level rise and storm surge. The beach and dune protect
    about $5B in adjacent real estate and the viability of hundreds of local businesses from storm surge.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Renourishment of the beach will be the activity/service provided with these funds and will directly achieve the purpose of
    these funds - strengthening the Village's oceanside shoreline protection.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     No direct services will be provided to citizens with the use of these funds. The benefits citizens will experience as a result
    of a successful beach restoration is a more enjoyable and quality beach environment, improved shoreline protection from
    storm surge, and healthier beach and marine ecosystem.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     All residents (15,000), thousands of annual visitors, and hundreds of local businesses in the Village are served by this
    project.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     The expected outcome of this project is the restoration of the beach's structure and therefore its function as a protective
    barrier for the Village against storm surge. This will result in protection for property, citizens, and businesses. The impact
    of the restored beach can be measurement by the value of storm surge related damages in future storm events,
    professional survey of beach location and composition, and resident feedback on the condition of the beach.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Failure to meet deliverables will result in the forfeiture of funds or financial penalties as described in the contract.
