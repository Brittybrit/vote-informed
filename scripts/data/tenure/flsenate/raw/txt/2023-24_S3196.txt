1. Project Title         Urban Search and Rescue Heavy Trucks

2. Senate Sponsor        Ileana Garcia

3. Date of Request       03/09/2023

4. Project/Program Description
    The funds will be used to assist in purchasing heavy trucks and equipment required to provide Palm Beach County
   residents Urban Search and Rescue Team assets and capabilities. The purchase will support a newly formed Urban
   Search & Rescue Team for Palm Beach County. The formation of the team will provide services to nearly 1 million
   residents and visitors locally while state-wide assets are requested, assembled and deployed. PBCFR will remain in a state
   of readiness to provide search and rescue services county-wide (additional 500,000 residents), regionally, and assist in
   rescue operations in disaster areas around the state for natural or man-made disasters.

    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The goal is to increase the readiness and ability to respond to man made and/or natural disasters within Palm Beach
    County's response area, and to neighboring counties as requested. Additionally, Palm Beach County Fire Rescue Urban
    Search & Rescue team will remain ready to assist in disaster-declared areas throughout the state. The two heavy trucks
    and equipment are necessary to deploy equipment through-out the 1769 squared miles served and the 19 municipalities
    relying on PBCFR to provide service. The team will provide service to nearly 1.5 million residents and visitors within
    greater Palm Beach County while adhering to and recognizing the requirements of a Type IV team.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Palm Beach County Fire Rescue has allocated funds to build a training prop necessary to train for building collapse.
    Members have been trained and obtained certifications for expedited deployment to the community they serve. Ongoing
    training will be provided to create readily deployable manpower required to fully staff the search and rescue team.
    Additionally drills will be conducted regularly to test deployment readiness and standing plans will be adjusted as
    necessary to meet any response requirements.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

    Urban Search and Rescue response to man made and natural disasters thought the county. The county is prone to
    Hurricanes, Storms and susceptible to man made disasters.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Over 1.5 million residents will be served by the team's state of readiness and local deployment. Palm Beach County's
    service area includes a diverse community with 4 major hospitals, over 250 Assisted Living Facilities and Nursing Homes,
    9 correctional facilities, 2 major power plants, 5 wastewater facilities, 17 potable water treatment plants, 5 airports, 2
    major highways systems, 4 major sugar mills/refinery, over 15 major Distribution Centers, over 350 schools, 2 colleges
    with numerous campuses, rail systems, hazardous substance chemical plants, sports stadiums, raceway, wildlife areas,
    Lake Okeechobee, Herbert Hoover Dike-levee, 45 miles of Atlantic Ocean coastline and beaches and 20 drawbridges
    across the Intracoastal or other waterway. Palm Beach County is a hot spot for tourism and seasonal retirees.
    Additionally, PBCFR has automatic and mutual aid agreements with all of the Fire Departments within the County and 3
    Fire Departments in surrounding counties.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Expected outcome is a fully operational Urban Search and Rescue Team to serve the community and surrounding
    region. Palm Beach County Fire Rescue has faithfully assisted with response and recovery efforts throughout the state
    over the years. Palm Beach County Fire Rescue's Urban Search and Rescue Team state of readiness with further
    expand the County's ability to assist those in need if and when disasters occur.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     The contracting agency may consider liquidated damages based on a monthly audit after a written warning outlining the
    items that fail to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract has been discussed. If after 1
    month a financial deduction would occur in the amount of $1,000 per month until remediation has been achieved.
