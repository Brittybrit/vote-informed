4. Project/Program Description
    Funding will provide professional staff to help eliminate a 25-year cataloging backlog and to provide funds to digitize and
   conserve important documents. Current exhibits highlight boom and bust times in this historically turbulent region.
   Cataloging, preserving, and digitizing materials will improve the museum's ability to interpret this complex story to school
   children, college classes, local groups, individuals, and tourists and to make materials more accessible to the general
   public.

14. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Hire professional staff to assist the director and help eliminate a 25-year processing and cataloging backlog and fund
    digitization and conservation of significant documents. Processing, cataloging, preserving, and digitizing materials will
    improve the museum's ability to interpret the complex story of this region to school children, college classes, local groups,
    individuals, and tourists and will make materials more accessible.

    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     By expanding access to the museum's collections, conducting research, and increasing the number of digital objects; the
    ability to interpret local and regional history to visitors will be enhanced. This will allow the museum to present a wider
    range of perspectives in its tours and exhibits. The project will increase the number of materials that can be made
    available to students and researchers.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     The project will facilitate more comprehensive tours and exhibits enabling visitors to gain a deeper understanding of the
    history of south Miami-Dade County and the Keys. Educational opportunities (pre-school through graduate students) will
    be enhanced since more collections will be available for research. Collections will be made more accessible and be better
    preserved for future generations.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The museum would target the general public but with emphasis on students. It is anticipated over 800 individuals will be
    served.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Museum collections and exhibits connect visitors with the rich and diverse history, literature, arts, music, dance, and
    traditions of the region. The museum enhances visitors' knowledge of these various cultures and their contributions to the
    area. Visitor numbers increase when museums have more varied and diverse content. The museum provides information
    about the history and culture of the area and supports a wide range of educational tours. In the past, tours have been
    given to classes ranging from preschoolers to graduate students, mentally challenged adults, and the elderly. The
    museum has also worked with student volunteers and paid and unpaid interns. The museum helps to draw tourists to
    downtown Homestead. Visitors from the local area, the state, the nation, and the world regularly visit the museum. One
    position will be created for an Assistant Director/Archivist. Results to be measured by an increase in the number of
    visitors and classes and the hiring of an Assistant Director.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     If deliverables or performance measures are not met, then funding will cease to be distributed.
