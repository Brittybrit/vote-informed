4. Project/Program Description
    The Town of Cutler Bay (the “Town”) is requesting $400,000.00 for Capital Improvements that will improve the stormwater
   management system and treat runoff pollutants in the Saga Bay neighborhood. The project site (Saga Bay Sub-Basin 1.5)
   consists of approximately 48.4 acres of single-family homes and approximately 8,700 linear feet of roadway. The
   stormwater drainage system outfalls into Saga Bay Lake and would benefit from improved water quality pre-treatment. This
   residential community regularly experiences heavy flooding due to inadequate drainage infrastructure and there are eight
   (8) repetitive loss properties in the neighborhood. Existing catch basins will be modified or reconstructed as required to
   provide sediment traps (sumps), baffles to protect the French drains and weir structures shall be installed prior to each
   outfall to restrict the discharge of pollutants to Saga Bay Lake and ultimately Biscayne Bay.

12. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

    The goal of this project is to improve drainage in the flood prone area.
    The project location is north of Franjo road (SW 207th Street), south of SW 199th Street, east of Old Cutler Road, and
    west of SW 82nd Court and is part of the DA-4-4 Basin. Secondly this project will improve water quality in Miami-Dade
    County by treating stormwater runoff. All drainage improvement projects conducted by the Town include best
    management practices that will ultimately reduce pollutant loads to Biscayne Bay by 89% or greater.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Activities and services include; (1) Design and permit the drainage system; (2) adding supplementary catch basins;
    replacing inadequate catch basins; installing an approximate 390 LF of 18” Pipe; and installing approximate 1140 LF of
    French Drain. Water quality and quantity improvements will be provided through the use of sediment traps (sumps) and
    pollution retardant baffles to protect the french drains in accordance with the final construction design documents; (3)
    Conduct community education promoting the project and best practices for preserving Florida's natural resources.
