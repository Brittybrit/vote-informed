12. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Through the comprehensive collection, analysis, and management of stroke center data, and the continued impact on
    the landscape and organization of stroke system of care, the FSR aims to 1) improve the quality of stroke care throughout
    Florida, 2) decrease and address identified disparities and gaps in stroke care, 3) enhance and support synergy across
    all stroke systems of care.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     The Florida Stroke Registry (FSR) produces evidence-based results, which impact the quality of stroke care for
    Floridians through: 1) Organizing data collection across acute stroke centers; 2) Application of interoperability of Fl. state
    agency databases with the stroke registry; 3) Production of annual reports (for over 126 stroke centers) to track,
    measure, and benchmark stroke performance and outcomes; 4) Production of region-specific reports to help organize
    stroke systems of care and to improve the provision of treatment.; 5) Developing, Demonstrating, and Disseminating
    interventions to address disparities and gaps in quality of care; 6) Expansion to 100% participation of all FL stroke
    centers, (currently at 76% of the total 165 stroke centers) ; 7) Outreach promoting data-driven best practices applied at all
    phases of stroke care (pre-hospital, in-hospital, and post-hospital).
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     The FSR serves Florida citizens by monitoring the quality of stroke care they or loved ones receive at stroke centers.
    FSR data-driven feedback provides hospitals benchmarks to increase the number of patients receiving life-saving stroke
    treatment; improve overall stroke care quality; provide interventions; and inform policy to facilitate Floridians receipt of the
    highest quality of stroke care.

    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Target populations include hospital systems of care, health professionals (clinicians, pharmacists, paramedics,
    administrators, stroke coordinators, case managers, policy advocates, academics), stroke patients and caregivers, and
    stroke advocacy groups. Direct collaboration will occur with over 126 currently participating Florida hospitals, and
    (currently) multiple Emergency Medical Service Departments.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Expected benefits include improvement of hospital stroke performance measures, increasing the numbers of stroke
    patients who will receive life-saving treatments, and reducing disability from stroke. These are all benefits that could result
    in a reduction of healthcare costs associated with the disease of stroke. Outcomes will be measured through data-driven
    results provided through relevant FSR analyses and visualized for hospitals in their Annual and Regional reports. The
    Annual reports are developed for each of the participating hospitals to track, measure, and benchmark their quality of
    care performance. Regional reports are developed to meet the needs to track and measure endovascular therapy, which
    since 2017 has become the new standard in stroke care. Regional reports will also provide data driven insight on gaps
    and improvements at a finer geographic and local (countywide) focus.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Subject to penalties put forth by the Department of Health
