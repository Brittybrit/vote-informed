4. Project/Program Description
    Mid Florida Community Services, Inc. Children's Advocacy Center of Hernando County (CAC) coordinates the
   multidisciplinary investigative process of child abuse cases with DCF, law enforcement and the child protection team. It is a
   child-friendly facility that responds to children who are child victims of sexual abuse, physical abuse, threatened harm,
   drug-endangerment, mental injury, neglect, and witnesses of domestic violence. Additionally, the CAC has a trauma
   treatment program for child victims of sexual abuse. Last year the CAC served more than 867 children and families, of
   which 301 were children in trauma therapy. Over the last two years, the CAC had to rent additional satellite office space
   causing services to be provided at multiple locations because the 45 year old main building is no longer big enough to meet
   the needs of the child victims that we serve. It is best practice to provide all the services under one building, in one location.

14. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Funds will be used to construct a minimum 7000 square foot building to provide a child friendly safe environment where
    children who are victims of child abuse can disclose their abuse and begin healing from the trauma they endured.
    Currently therapists are housed at different locations and sometimes if there are several children involved, the families
    have to drive to different locations to receive services for each child. Housing the entire staff at the Children's Advocacy
    Center is best for the children and their families.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     On average, the CAC provides services to over 800 children who are victims of child abuse and their family members.
    Services provided at the CAC include: forensic interviews, specialized interviews, social assessments, forensic medical
    exams (sexual and physical abuse cases), multidisciplinary team staffing, advocacy, deposition and court
    accompaniment, and case management, as well as individual and family therapy for child victims and their non-offending
    family members. The CAC also provides therapeutic treatment for adults who were sexually abused as children. Law
    enforcement and DCF observe the forensic interviews that take place at the CAC.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Services provided at the CAC include: forensic interviews, specialized interviews, social assessments, forensic medical
    exams, multidisciplinary team staffing, advocacy, deposition and court accompaniment, case management, as well as
    individual and family therapy for child victims and their non-offending family members. The CAC also provides therapeutic
    treatment for adults who were sexually abused as children. Law enforcement and DCF observe the forensic interviews
    that take place at the CAC.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Children ages 0-17 who are victims of physical abuse, sexual abuse, neglect, human trafficking, or mental injury, are
    drug endangered, who witness domestic violence, or are survivors of homicide as well as adults who were sexually
    abused as children are eligible for services. Non-offending family members such as siblings, parents, or grandparents are
    also eligible for services. The CAC expects to serve over 825 children and family members this current fiscal year and
    expect that figure to grow.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     The expected benefit is to enhance child abuse investigations and increase prosecution rates with child abuse cases;
    protect children from harm; improve their physical and mental health, reduce substance abuse; and reduce recidivism.
    The CAC provides a multidiscipline-coordinated investigative response with DCF, law enforcement, child protection teams
    and the state attorney where the outcomes of the interview and medical exam are discussed, referrals for therapy are
    made, and there is discussion of what needs the family has. Pre- and post- assessments are completed with therapy and
    evidence-based trauma therapy is utilized. 90% of children served will see a reduction in trauma. Cases are tracked to
    see if additional abuse reports have been called in. Criminal cases are tracked. Victims of sexual abuse and human
    trafficking often turn to substances and the CAC tracks this information during the pre- and post- assessments and
    provides information on substance misuse.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Failure to meet deliverables will result in a return of funds to administering agency.
