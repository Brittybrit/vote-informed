4. Project/Program Description
    Funding will support Breakthrough Miami to continue to advance a strategic growth plan to expand reach and impact in
   Florida, growing our newest site in Palm Beach County from 60 to 120 students served, and preparing for an additional new
   site to open in 2024-25, which will grow to capacity to serve 175 new 5th-8th grade students. These expansion efforts will
   support continued growth towards a total capacity of nearly 2,000 students each year, thereby increasing Florida's talent
   equity and fostering the next generation of leaders.

14. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Funding will support recruiting the new class for 2024 and prepare for the 2024-25 expansion; delivering an evidence-
    based year-round tuition-free academic and social enrichment program; providing school options advising (uniquely
    supporting families in navigating school choice); expanding services to meet evolving needs of students (Social
    Emotional Learning, mental health, social services); providing transportation to ensure all Scholars are able to access
    programing at no cost; continue documentation of program procedures and resources for future expansion.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Funding will support Breakthrough to continue to advance a strategic growth plan to expand reach and impact Florida
    towards a total capacity of nearly 2,000 students each year, thereby increasing Florida's talent equity and fostering the
    next generation of leaders. Funding will support recruiting the new class for year 2024 and prepare for the 2024-25
    expansion; delivering an evidence-based year-round tuition-free academic and social enrichment program; providing
    school options advising (uniquely supporting families in navigating school choice); expanding services to meet evolving
    needs of students (Social Emotional Learning, mental health, social services); providing transportation.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Funding will support Breakthrough Miami's tuition-free out-of-school academic and social enrichment programming
    founded upon a students-teaching-students model and provide unique paid summer work experience through rigorous
    service leadership opportunities to high school and college-age students serving as Teaching Fellows, Teaching
    Assistants and volunteers. A top-ten ranked summer internship by Princeton Review, the 8-week Teaching Fellowship
    supports emerging young leaders to teach academic classes, electives and provide near-peer mentorship.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Breakthrough is positioned as an opportunity generator in South Florida, serving 1500 motivated 5th-12th graders from
    under-resourced communities, 142 Teaching Fellows (17-24 year olds), 40 Teaching Assistants, 24 Success Coaches
    and 400 Volunteers. Acceptance is based on a competitive application and interview process, in which we identify
    students who are academically motivated and meet at least two of five risk factors associated with failure to
    enter/complete college: Ethnic/racial minorities (96% of our students); Family income qualifies for free/reduced lunch
    (85%); First generation in their families to attend college in the U.S. (52%); Single-parent household (47%); Primary
    language other than English (52%).
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Grow and foster Breakthrough's newest site in Palm Beach County to increase enrollment of students to 175. Prepare for
    an additional new Miami Dade County site to open in 2024-25, with capacity to serve 175 new students, leading to
    increased total capacity to nearly 2,000 5th-12th grade students. Increase student enrollment in advanced courses (70%),
    participants reaching GPA of 3.0 or higher (60%), graduating high school (100%) and enrolling in college (95%). Serve at
    least 1500 5-12th grade students from under-resourced communities, 142 Teaching Fellows (17-24 year olds), 24
    Success Coaches and 400 Volunteers. Provide early paid summer work experience for 142 (17-24 year-olds) to serve as
    Teaching Fellows. Supervised by certified teachers, Fellows provide life-skills training, mentoring, and academic
    instruction. Expand social emotional wellness curriculum, training and programming, and increase access to mental
    health services for students and families.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     The suggested penalties if the contract were to be defaulted due to deficient performance measures would be the loss of
    funding, return of funds or lost opportunity for future funding.
