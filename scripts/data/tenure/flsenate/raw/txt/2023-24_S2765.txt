4. Project/Program Description
    Development of simulation center for training of nurses & other frontline personnel. There are direct benefits of simulation
   training including but not limited to decrease in length of stay, increase of patient safety, reduction of errors, increase
   quality of care, competence attainment and improve readiness in clinical settings. Nicklaus is the largest pediatric teaching
   program in the southeastern United States and there is benefit of teaching learners rotating through Nicklaus such as FIU.

     Purchase of various sized patient simulator mannequins and other simulation-based equipment and technologies,
    education and training needs for simulation training, development of simulation practice environment and space. Various
    patient simulators are necessary given we are solely pediatrics and have a range of premature babies to adult sized
    adolescents. There is extensive research demonstrating correlation with simulation training for healthcare professionals
    and patient safety and benefits of simulation training for clinical settings and effectiveness in team performance.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Purchase of various sized patient simulator mannequins and other simulation-based equipment and technologies,
    education and training needs for simulation training, development of simulation practice environment and space. Nicklaus
    is the largest pediatric teaching program in the southeastern United States and there is benefit of teaching learners
    rotating through Nicklaus such as FIU.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Nicklaus Children’s Hospital is South Florida’s only licensed specialty hospital exclusively for children. Direct value
    includes the benefits of simulation in training nurses, medical students, fellows, residents and healthcare professionals.
    Social value includes the benefits to society/patient population serve by Nicklaus including quality of care and fewer
    complications.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Neonates and babies, preschool students, grade school students and high school students given the healthcare
    professionals treating these populations will be trained with simulation.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     improve physical health: Social value includes the benefits to society/patient population serve by Nicklaus including
    quality of care and fewer complications. Utilization of an evidence-based program evaluation tools to assess the
    sustainability and success. Also, appropriate clinical interventions implemented as determined by our Quality and Safety
    team. Enrich cultural experience: patient safety and sustainability measured by competence attainment and validation,
    reduction in alternative training and procedures performed. Improve quality of education: Benefits of simulation training in
    users- nurses and healthcare professionals and improved readiness for clinical practice, enhanced critical thinking and
    reasoning as well as standardization of clinical experiences and education.
    Nicklaus is the largest pediatric teaching program in the southeastern United States and there is benefit of teaching
    learners rotating through Nicklaus such as FIU.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Failure to utilize the funds in the specified manner shall result in funds being returned to the State.
