12. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     This Program provides sewage pump out service to vessels anchored in waters of the Florida Keys to assist boaters in
    complying with State and Federal No Discharge laws to protect water quality for public health and the economically and
    ecologically important marine ecosystem of the Florida Keys. Since the Program's inception, over 3,200 boaters have
    been served diverting over 2.5 million gallons of vessel sewage from Keys' waters. This funding will ensure continued
    water quality protection.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     The Program provides a mobile pump out service to vessels anchored throughout the Florida Keys to encourage boaters
    compliance with No Discharge laws to protect water quality for public health and our sensitive marine environment. The
    service is provided free to boaters to incentivize participation and reduce the potential for illegal discharges.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     As described above
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The target population is all recreational live-aboard anchored vessel owners (both transient and local) throughout waters
    of the Florida Keys. The number of individuals expected to be served is 200-300 anchored boaters who require weekly
    pump out service as well as additional transient/seasonal boaters
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     This program collects thousands of gallons of vessel sewage annually from waters within the Florida Keys National
    Marine Sanctuary. This preserves water quality for public health and the marine environment by eliminating the potential
    for illegal discharges of this highly concentrated wastewater pollutant. These water quality protections also support a
    multi-million dollar commercial and recreational fishing industry as well a a robust tourism industry that provides
    recreational fishing, diving and boating opportunities to 4 million tourists annually and contributes to a $4 billion dollar
    annual economy in the Keys.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
