4. Project/Program Description
    The Miami River Commission was created by the Florida Legislature for the purpose of improving economic development
   as well as protecting the natural areas of the Miami River. The Miami River Commission is mandated by the Florida
   legislature in s. 163.06, F.S., to perform certain functions and make annual reports to the legislature and Governor. While
   ongoing funding is partially received from Miami-Dade County, City of Miami, and private entities, the costs associated with
   state mandated programs, functions, and compliance of state reporting exceeds what is contributed locally.

12. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Improving the Miami River District's economy and natural environment. Assist job-generating business in the Miami River
    District. Improve the environment via removing illegal dumping and invasive plant species, providing new plantings, water
    decontamination, etc. Host public meetings, website, etc. Market and promote the multi-use, multi-cultural, multi-income
    Miami River District, which includes Miami downtown, Little Havana, and Overtown areas.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Assisting new private sector businesses to open along the Miami River. Removing illegal dumping materials and invasive
    plant species clean-up, including state-owned submerged lands. Scavenger Water Decontamination Vessel services,
    collect floating debris while decontaminating 600,000 gallons of river water per hour, and injecting 150,000 litters of
    oxygen into the Miami River every hour, significantly improving water quality. Plantings, landscaping, painting over graffiti
    along the public riverwalk.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Scavenger Water Decontamination Vessel services improving water quality. New plantings, landscaping, and painting
    over graffiti along the public riverwalk. Assist businesses with due diligence and the processes to open. Assist citizens
    with any issue related to the Miami River. Host and provide supplies for volunteer Miami River clean up events. Maintain
    the community vegetable and fruit garden. Frequent updates of the informative website, www.miamirivercommission.org
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     South Florida which uses the public riverwalk and its 9 major parks. The Miami River District has the most densely
    populated neighborhoods in the State of Florida, including downtown Miami, Brickell, Little Havana, Overtown, Allapattah,
    Durham Park, Spring Garden, Grove Park, and Melrose. Miami River District attracts new businesses, which create new
    jobs.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Significantly improving the Miami River District's economy by assisting businesses and improving its natural environment
    by removing garbage and providing water decontamination, new plantings, etc. The outcome will be measured by the
    number of new businesses, quantity of cubic yards of collected and disposed garbage and invasive plant species,
    number of gallons of decontaminated Miami River water, and number of assisted job generating businesses.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Utilize the standard reimbursement contract in which the state only makes payments after the Miami River Commission
    proves it met the deliverables and performance measures provided for in the contract.
