4. Project/Program Description
    Bottlenose dolphins in the Indian River Lagoon (IRL) are facing unprecedented threats, and urgent action is needed to
   safeguard these iconic creatures and the fragile ecosystem they depend on. More than 90% of northern IRL dolphins are
   currently underweight or emaciated, including 97% of female dolphins with dependent calves (Durden et al. 2023). Dolphin
   interactions with fishermen and entanglement in fishing line are also increasing, stressing overburdened marine mammal
   response teams. To address this unprecedented situation, Hubbs-SeaWorld Research Institute (HSWRI) is requesting
   support to enhance marine mammal first responder capacity, accelerate water quality and habitat restoration, and conduct
   targeted public outreach to increase support for restoration initiatives. A related Community Project Funding request
   supported by Congressman Posey was included in the FY24 U.S. House Appropriations Bill, which is pending review by
   the House of Representatives.

14. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Goal 1: Strengthen marine mammal response and assessment, including for mass strandings, Unusual Mortality Events,
    and entanglements in an area with elevated rates of human interaction. Goal 2: Complete required repairs to the
    Institute's boat ramp, dock, and seawall, and install electrical and potable water service to allow partner-led production
    and out planting of the nursery-grown seagrass plugs and shellfish seed stock needed for IRL habitat restoration. Goal 3:
    Conduct targeted outreach emphasizing the urgent need for restoration of IRL habitats and the connection between
    healthy dolphin populations and a vibrant fishing community.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Operational support will increase marine mammal first-responder workforce capacity and improve outcomes for
    entangled and stranded dolphins. Understanding dolphin health trends and
     threats will also aid Florida Fish and Wildlife agency stock management and other obligations under the Marine Mammal
    Protection Act (MMPA). HSWRI will also work with Florida-based commercial aquaculture producers Sea & Shoreline and
    SEAMRI LLC to build high-capacity seagrass and shellfish nurseries to provide essential seed stock for restoration of
    habitats critical to dolphins, manatees, and regional fisheries.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Direct services to citizens include outreach emphasizing the urgent need for restoration of IRL habitats and the
    connection between healthy marine mammal populations and a vibrant sportfishing community. We will engage with the
    sportfishing community through workshops, presentations, and informational placards at prominent fishing locales to
    increase support for marine conservation efforts, including responsible disposal of entangling fishing line. We will also
    work directly with Florida-based commercial aquaculture producers Sea & Shoreline and SEAMRI to build and operate
    high-capacity seagrass and shellfish nurseries to support ongoing IRL restoration efforts.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The IRL is a regional economic driver, generating an inflation adjusted $3.6 billion in lagoon related recreation and
    commercial fishing. Addressing loss of water quality, degrading habitats, and reoccurring marine mammal mortality
    events will help to improve and maintain economic conditions on which most residents, businesses, and visitors directly
    and indirectly depend.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?
