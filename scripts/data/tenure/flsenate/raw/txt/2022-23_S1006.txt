1. Project Title          Nutrition Education for School Health and Wellness

2. Senate Sponsor        Ana Maria Rodriguez

3. Date of Request       10/07/2021

4. Project/Program Description
    Common Threads (CT) seeks to grow our evidence-based nutrition education for school health and wellness programs
   throughout the State of Florida. This project is projected to reach 5,521 Floridians (approximately 3,500 students and 1,800
   teachers and parents) with nutrition and healthy cooking education. Our programs include: Educator Professional
   Developments (18 live classes; 6 virtual) Small Bites (720 live classes; 320 virtual), Cooking Skills and World Cuisine (300
   live classes), Family Cooking Classes (20 live classes), Parent Workshops (12 live classes), Grocery Store Tours (5 live
   classes), Virtual Cooking Classes (25 virtual), On-Demand Programming (unlimited), Teacher Resources (estimated 500
   Teachers).

12. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Our goal will be to improve the health and wellness of under-served communities. Our output goals for the 2022-2023
    program year are: 1) to serve at least 5,521 students, parents/guardians, and teachers with nutrition and healthy cooking
    education and provide resources and; 2) for participants to receive up to 45,953 hours of programming and to cook and
    consume up to 34,702 healthy meals and snacks.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Common Threads' approach to nutrition education is unique, as it is intended to improve multiple elements of community
    health and wellness by engaging children, families, teachers, and other adult role models in our hands-on cooking and
    nutrition education classes. This comprehensive approach ensures children and families are supported in making healthy
    choices across environments-where they live, learn, work, play and shop-thus bolstering and helping to sustain its
    positive impact.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     We will provide Educator Professional Development (18 live classes, 6 virtual) to help educators learn concepts to
    integrate nutrition into core content areas. Our Small Bites program (720 live classes, 40 virtual) will provide nutrition
    education reinforcing core academic concepts through grade level aligned lessons and healthy snack recipes. Cooking
    Skills and World Cuisine (300 live classes) and Virtual Cooking Classes (25 virtual) teaches students how to cook
    culturally diverse, affordable, and healthy dinners. Our Family Cooking Classes (20 live classes) teach families how to
    cook together and incorporate healthy habits at home while maintaining their existing cultural traditions. Parent
    Workshops (12 live classes) and Grocery Store Tours (5 live classes) teach caregivers how to shop for and prepare
    healthy meals on a budget. Lastly On-Demand and Teacher Resources are available State-wide at no charge for
    teachers to access educational materials.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     We provide programs in schools and with community-based organizations in communities where at least 80% of children
    participate in the free- or reduced-priced school lunch program. Our youth programming focuses on grades preK-8th. We
    also provide programming to parents/caregivers and educators of these students. In total, we expect to serve
    approximately 3,500 students and 1,800 adults.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     The expected benefits or outcomes are: participants will increase their liking for vegetables; participants will improve their
    self-efficacy for food preparation; participants will increase their nutrition knowledge; participants will self-report increased
    consumption of vegetables and whole grains; and participants will increase the frequency of helping to cook at home and
    communication with their family about healthy eating. We measure these outcomes in a variety of ways through our
    dedicated research and development team: 1) pre- and post-surveys; 2) anecdotal or self-reported communication; and
    3) class observations.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

    We acknowledge the standard contract penalties in the Department of Education process. The Department of Education
    may withhold all or a portion of the funds if the contract deliverables are deemed unsatisfactory.
