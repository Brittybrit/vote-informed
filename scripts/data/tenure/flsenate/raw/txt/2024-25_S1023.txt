4. Project/Program Description
    The main objective of the State of Florida funding initiative is to support and enhance quality HIV/AIDS research through a
   pilot grant program leading to innovative cure research for HIV/AIDS and vaccine-developments in response to the health
   needs of Florida's citizens. One of our State Pilots is close to a breakthrough in terms of a cure for HIV infection. Further
   pilot awardees have gone on to secure competitive federal funding that has impacted our understanding of HIV/AIDS and
   new and emerging infectious diseases based on research supported by the State of Florida funding initiative. These pilot
   awards represent a significant return on investment for every dollar of State pilot funding, awardees have secured twelve
   dollars in federal funding. In addition, State funding supports a clinical research unit at Jackson Memorial Hospital which
   allows people living with HIV to participate in clinical studies to develop novel approaches to combating HIV infection.

     The main objective of the State of Florida funding initiative is to support and enhance quality HIV/AIDS research through
    a pilot grant program leading to innovative cure research for HIV/AIDS and vaccine-developments in response to the
    health needs of Florida's citizens. Two of our State Pilot Awardees published with Dr. Ronald C. Desrosiers; one
    publication on a strategy that leads to an AIDS cure and another publication on significant advances in developing a
    potential HIV vaccine. These scientific accomplishments were supported with State of Florida funding and are an
    important step towards ending the AIDS epidemic. Further pilot awardees have gone on to secure competitive federal
    funding that has impacted our understanding of HIV/AIDS and new and emerging infectious diseases based on research
    supported by the State of Florida funding initiative. Through the HIV/AIDS research conducted we are one step closer to
    reaching the Ending the Epidemic goals set on state and federal levels
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Support innovative research leading to a cure for HIV/AIDS and AIDS vaccine developments. State funds will be spent
    on pilot grant projects to allow researchers to develop fruitful lines of research and be more competitive for federal grant
    funding. The funds also support the research unit at Jackson Memorial Hospital, which ensures underrepresented and
    minority populations are represented in clinical research. This research unit supports over 30 state and federal grants by
    supplying samples from these important populations.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Funding supports a research Unit at Jackson Memorial Hospital which facilitates inclusion of underrepresented
    individuals in clinical research studies that provide access to cutting edge therapies.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

    The larger HIV/AIDS infected population in Florida are served with a direct impact on over 800 individuals in the Miami-
    Dade area.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Improved health care outcomes, access to novel medications, and increased support for patients enrolled in clinical
    studies. Increased participation of minorities in clinical research studies and increase access to cutting edge treatments.
    An increase in federal grant funding as pilot grants allow researchers to develop innovative lines of research and be more
    competitive for Federal grants. Currently, for every dollar in State Pilot funding allocated, we are receiving 13 dollars in
    Federal funding. This is a significant return on investment. State funds also drive innovative and high quality HIV/AIDS
    research projects aimed at developing a cure for HIV/AIDS as well as an AIDS vaccine-development in response to the
    health needs of Florida's citizens.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Subject to the penalties put forth by the Florida Department of Health.
