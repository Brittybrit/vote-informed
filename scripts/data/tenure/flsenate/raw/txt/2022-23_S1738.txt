4. Project/Program Description
    Incarceration of parents/caregivers impacts 300,000 FL children. Research demonstrates that these children engage in
   anti-social behaviors and suffer mental illness at higher levels, putting them on the path toward incarceration themselves.
   COI, Inc. is an innovative approach to interrupt that pattern by collaborating with FDC to rebuild families with wellness,
   literacy, civic and STEM learning experiences that support both children and their incarcerated parent's improvements as a
   family unit. The program started in South Florida and has expanded to most of the metropolitan areas, serving over 1,700
   families with over 3,000 children to date. COI, Inc. worked with a warden to design special child-focused visitation focused
   on reassuring the children about their parent’s safety, improving parenting and addressing behavioral issues. Visits are
   quarterly and to date, COI, Inc. has coordinated over 500 child-focused visits to 22 Florida correctional facilities.

 When a parent is incarcerated, children are uncertain about the safety of their parent and this insecurity undermines
children's ability to cope and to grow in a healthy manner. The result for young children is insecure attachments and
bonds. These broken bonds have been linked to poorer peer relationships and diminished cognitive abilities. Over half,
58%, of children with incarcerated parents are under 10 years of age. School-aged children and teenagers face problems
such as poor academic performance, peer relationship issues and classroom behavior problems, often resulting in
suspension and high drop-out rates. The Children of Inmates, Inc. Family Strengthening and Reuniting program
addresses these challenges by reconnecting children with their incarcerated parent and working to sustain that
connection. Follow-up and support services are offered to help the child overcome the challenges and achieve a positive
developmental track, with the support of caring, responsible adults.
b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

 COI will conduct a minimum 60 bonding visits to support family strengthening in FL correctional facilities. The Covid-19
pandemic halted visitations and severely interrupted bonding for the children and their parents; further, it left children and
caregivers with a heighten concern about their loved ones, creating additional trauma. During this period, COI continued
to operate, providing door-to-door outreach to bring food, personal protective equipment, cleaning supplies, educational
supports and most importantly, child development activities to over 400 families. We focused on providing civic and
STEM education activities to foster enhanced literacy rates and community connectedness. With the relaunch of bonding
visitations, COI is engaging incarcerated individuals and FDC to proactively serve "fragile families." We have found
children from “fragile families” consistently have more difficulties with behavior, health and education. We will create care

c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

 Direct services to be provided will be transportation and social services to support families during the bonding visits. For
participating “fragile families” wrap-around care coordination, including facilitated referrals to appropriate service providers
to meet basic needs including addressing health, homelessness, disabilities, substance abuse and educational needs,
child development assessments, child therapy and child development activities and child safety protection gear will be
provided. Tutoring and evidenced based engagement activities and supplies will be provided through the STEM and
literacy activities. Advocacy services will be provided to enhance public understanding of the plight of children of
incarcerated parents.
d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

 The target population served by this project are children with incarcerated parents and their caregivers. COI serves
children, ages 0-21 throughout Florida. We also serve currently and formerly incarcerated parents, assisting them to
rebuild relationships with their children and caregivers. We anticipate serving 1,500 children with these funds. This
equates to approximately 600 families or incarcerated individuals. Research from Cambridge University documents that
children with incarcerated parents are at twice the risk of mental illness and three times the risk of antisocial behaviors.
Many are economically disadvantaged and often face homelessness. Further, many within the COI population struggle
with developmental disabilities and/or physical disabilities. Children with incarcerated parents are exposed to higher
levels of community and direct violence.
e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
be measured?

 The expected benefits are to interrupt intergeneration incarceration by identifying, working with and supporting children
with incarcerated parent. Working with "fragile families", activities will focus on reducing physical, verbal aggression and
anti-social behaviors in children with incarcerated parents who exhibit these behaviors. Over 85% of COI children report
reduced anti-social behaviors. Family visits create strong bond between incarcerated parent, children and caregivers and
promote family support for the incarcerated parent upon returning to community. Using FDC data, COI found that over
89% of COI participants successfully re-enter and rebuild their lives over a three year period. In short, family matters for
reentry.
