4. Project/Program Description
    Pace Center for Girls is a nationally recognized proven early intervention model that prevents girls from dropping out of
   school, becoming victims of human trafficking, or entering the juvenile justice system. Through a network of 21 Pace
   Centers and 16 Pace Reach programs across Florida, Pace works with more than 3,000 middle- and high-school aged girls
   annually. The Pace model provides comprehensive wrap around social services ,academic services with daily academic
   instruction, school-to-work readiness skills development and a year of after-care. Despite the COVID pandemic, Pace has
   continued to achieve strong outcomes for the girls we serve. To continue to achieve these outcomes in today's competitive
   environment, Pace is requesting a cost of living increase in the daily rate per slot to align with the cost of services. Pace is
   requesting $1,469,000 for the Pace Center model, and an additional $131,000 for the Pace Reach Counseling Services
   model.

12. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Funds will be used to increase the daily rate per slot for Pace Centers to $67.86, and the daily rate per slot for Pace
    Reach Counseling Services to $49.94. This rate increase reflects a 3% cost of living increase over the past three years.
    The DJJ daily rate per slot for Pace Centers and Pace Reach covers all social service costs, including mental health
    counseling, therapeutic services, life-skills development, and case management for at-risk girls and their families.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     No new services will be provided with the rate increase however, providing this cost of living adjustment will allow Pace
    to continue to provide existing services and continue achieving strong outcomes in today's competitive environment. All
    girls attending Pace currently receive comprehensive wrap-around social services including individual assessments, case
    management, individual trauma informed cognitive behavioral counseling and therapy, group counseling and therapy,
    Spritied Girls! Life skills curriculum, and family case management. Once a girl transitions from Pace she receives a
    minimum of one-year of follow-up services and after care.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Comprehensive wrap around social services and the full academic school day are provided for girls at risk of delinquency
    through Pace Center for Girls. Case management for the girls family is also provided to ensure a stable home
    environment. Funding will provide a cost of living adjustment allowing us to attract and retain highly trained staff.
    Prevention and early intervention services for girls results in better community outcomes, a decrease in systems
    involvement and ultimately a cost savings to tax payers.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Over 3,000 females ages 11-17 statewide who are at risk of juvenile justice system involvement and dropping out of
    school. The girls' families benefit from Pace both indirectly and through family engagement, home visits and counseling.
    The general public benefits through increased public safety.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Short term outcomes for girls include 1) maintaining attendance and being enrolled in school; 2) advancing grade levels;
    3) demonstrated increase in self-efficacy and pro-social coping skills; 3) reduction in risk behaviors. Long term outcomes
    for girls include 1) high school graduation and enrollment in post-secondary education or the workforce; 2) obtaining
    employment; 3) no involvement in the criminal justice system; 4: decreased economic dependency. Pace Centers utilize a
    combination of output data (e.g., number of girls served, dosage of services) and outcome data (data on the effectiveness
    or quality of services provided), to measure outcomes. Outcome data includes the percent of girls that increase their
    academic performance as measured through credits earned, report card, grade advancement, graduation or obtaining a
    GED.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Our current contract with DJJ outlines penalties for failing to meet deliverables. DJJ may reduce the amount of monthly
    payment if sufficient evidence shows that deliverables were not met. Outcomes for FY 2019-20 include the following: 89%
    of girls statewide are in school or employed one year after completing Pace, and 94% had no further involvement with the
    juvenile justice system one year after completing Pace.
