2. Senate Sponsor         Alexis Calatayud

4. Project/Program Description
    This project will create curriculum for the Department of Education and exhibitions that tell the story of the search for
   freedom, the plight of communism, and engaging future generations in the historical and cultural significance of the building
   as well as the impact of communism. We will honor victims of communism, educating about the experiences of individuals
   affected by communist regimes -- this will include educational components, curriculum and videos that can be disseminated
   as a resource across the state and school districts. This project will preserve historical memory, promote understanding,
   and safeguard against the recurrence of oppressive ideologies at the Freedom Tower. To launch exhibition programming,
   MDC will partner with the nonprofit Colonial Florida Cultural Heritage Center and the historic Corpus Christi Catholic
   Church, whose mission is to acquire, preserve, and make available to the public the cultural and artistic patrimony of
   Florida.

    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The funding requested will enable Miami Dade College to create and launch new permanent exhibit related to Victims of
    Communism and rotating exhibitions in the galleries. As the site and visitor experiences are reimagined, the project is
    vital to connect local and national audiences to the experiences of Cuban and other Diasporas in Miami that arrive in
    search for freedom. This project will also create curriculum for school districts to use as educational materials on Victims
    of Communism.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     This project will create a statewide curriculum and an exhibit on the Victims of Communism. Additionally, have the most
    dynamic repository of Miami’s story and stories of exile. Best-in-class experience design will guide visitors through the
    dozen-year history of refugee assistance provided in the Tower, known as the “Ellis Island of the South,” and reflect upon
    the waves of exiles that transformed our city. Leveraging the nimbleness of museum digital technology, Miami’s freedom
    stories will be gathered, presented, and celebrated - reinterpreting the Tower as a 21st century beacon of hope, courage,
    and opportunity.

    This funding will also develop curriculum and educational materials to educate students in Florida on Victims of
    Communism.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

    A comprehensive education experience on the history of the Freedom Tower and the history and impact of communism.
    This will also include history and partnership with the Colonial Florida Cultural Heritage Center.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The target population of 2.7 million residents in Miami Dade County in addition all students throughout the State of
    Florida. The museum's exhibitions, programs, and initiatives aim to enrich the cultural experiences of a diverse
    demographic, including national and international visitors.

    For the Victims of Communism curriculum to strive to serve school age students across Florida.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Greater understanding of the importance of freedom. Understanding of the history of communism and the impact it has
    had. The story and the importance of the Freedom Tower. Curriculum, videos, instructional guides on the victims of
    communism.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Mitigation of risk is built into the project’s timetable; smaller launch events can be presented prior to launch of full
    operations; exhibitions have a scalable interpretive model where expansions and updates of museum artifacts,
    ephemera, and collections can be made quarterly.
