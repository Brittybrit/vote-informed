4. Project/Program Description
    The Camillus House Phoenix Human Trafficking Recovery Program is designed to provide low-demand transitional
   housing and supportive services for adult-aged women who are victims of human trafficking. These individuals are
   identified and referred by Miami-Dade State Attorney Katherine Fernandez Rundle’s Office (SAO), Homeland Security,
   police departments and other providers for clients who meet the criteria as victims of domestic and commercial sexual
   exploitation, labor and other forms of human trafficking. Supportive services assist victims through several phases
   including: 1) crisis intervention and assessment; 2) case management; and 3) social reintegration. The funding requested
   for this project will support ongoing operational expenses and staffing for the program. The program has a professional
   case management and clinical team deploying the latest evidenced based strategies to guide participants through the
   process of trauma recovery and social reintegration.

14. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The Camillus House Phoenix Human Trafficking Recovery Program is designed to provide low-demand transitional
    housing and evidence-based supportive services for adult-aged victims of human trafficking, with the goal of providing a
    safe and supportive environment to allow victims to stabilize and prepare them to transition to a healthy, independent life
    that includes employment and stable housing. For referrals from the Miami-Dade State Attorney’s Office (SAO) and other
    SAOs, US Dept of Homeland Security and other law enforcement agencies, the supportive environment and behavioral
    health services provided prepare these survivors to serve as Victim-Witnesses, assisting in successful prosecutions to
    end the cycle of trafficking. Improved health, successful treatment for mental health and addiction issues, sustainable,
    legal employment and housing also reduce financial impacts and reliance on public systems (hospitals, crisis, detox,
    welfare/public benefits, etc.).
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     The Camillus House Phoenix Human Trafficking Recovery Program provides Low-Demand housing & supportive
    services designed to assist victims through several phases, including: 1) crisis intervention & assessment; 2)
    comprehensive case management and stabilization; and 3) social reintegration, including sustainable employment and
    housing stability. The funding requested for this project supports ongoing operational expenses and staffing for the
    program. Camillus has dedicated case management and clinical staff (masters level clinician) that deploy the latest
    evidenced based practices to guide participants through the process of trauma recovery. Funding is also provided for
    access to educational and vocational training programs and job placement assistance, and for housing navigation
    services to assist in identifying permanent housing in the community. Residential support staff assists the clients in re-
    integration activities and ensure the clients' safety.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     The Camillus House Phoenix Human Trafficking Recovery Program is the only dedicated residential program serving
    adult-aged victims of human trafficking in South Florida. The low-demand supportive services provided help prevent
    chronic homelessness and re-victimization by: 1) Stabilizing survivors of human trafficking by providing a safe place to
    receive care and services (and treatment, if needed) to enable them to address their trauma and begin recovery in a safe
    setting that responds to their needs; 2) Enhancing self-esteem using evidence-based therapeutic models to make victims
    of human trafficking less vulnerable to any form of exploitation; 3) Assisting victims of human trafficking to obtain
    training/employment and/or benefits to facilitate financial independence. 4) Providing housing navigation services to
    return victims of human trafficking safely to the community. Services are supported by a Case Manager, master's Level
    Clinician, Resident assistants and Housing Navigator.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     This program serves victims of human trafficking over the age of 18, as identified and referred by the Miami-Dade State
    Attorney’s Office (SAO), other SAOs, US Dept of Homeland Security and other law enforcement. By the nature of their
    situation these individuals are homeless or at risk of homelessness and in need of a safe place to live to receive care,
    treatment and services while also preparing to serve as victim-witnesses in the trials of the human traffickers. The
    program serves approximately 40 persons a year. This program serves as a statewide resource, available to receive
    referrals from other counties to maximize benefit to victims of human trafficking throughout the State.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Stabilizing these individuals benefits the State of Florida by ensuring that these persons do not become chronically
    homeless and are not re-victimized, while also allowing the SAO to successfully prepare witnesses for trial against
    traffickers. This program will also provide enhanced self-esteem and additional vocational skills so that these women are
    less vulnerable to any form of exploitation. Performance and outcomes include: 1) A minimum of 3 adult victims will
    receive services each month. 2) 100% of clients in the Human Trafficking Recovery Program will have a service plan that
    is updated every 30 days. 3) At least of 50% of residential adult victims of human trafficking participating in the Human
    Trafficking Recovery Program will successfully complete the program. 4) 70% of victims successfully completing
    treatment in the Human Trafficking will be stably housed.
