4. Project/Program Description
    The problem of year-round seaweed accumulations on Key Biscayne's beach is a costly public health concern. The
   seaweed causes bacteria growth resulting in swim advisories. Currently, the Village pays to collect the seaweed from the
   beach every morning and transport it to a landfill. However, there is a more sustainable and beneficial means of removing
   seaweed from the beach. The Village, in collaboration with Miami-Dade County, is initiating a seaweed composting
   program. Composted seaweed would be used by the Village and County as a soil supplement in parks and other municipal
   properties. The composting program would be used as a STEM educational opportunity at a local high school. The program
   would demonstrate the viability of composting as an alternative and spur more communities to participate in composting
   seaweed instead of disposing of it in landfills.

14. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The purpose of the funds requested will be to initiate the seaweed composting program and demonstrate to regional
    partners that there the program is a viable alternative to contributing to landfill waste.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Activities and services to meet the intended purpose of these funds include program development with Miami-Dade
    County, selection of a composting site, development of that site to receive seaweed and process it as compost,
    monitoring nutrient levels in the compost created, educational opportunities for local high school students in STEM
    programs, and distribution of
    compost to participating communities to be used in landscape maintenance.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Local high school students will have the opportunity to learn about ocean ecosystems, currents, and hydrodynamics that
    bring seaweed to the shoreline, nutrient composition of seaweed, and the composting process.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     All residents (15,000), visitors, and businesses in the Village are served by this project since it would provide a more
    environmentally sustainable and potentially financially efficient means of processing the seaweed that accumulates on
    the beach. High school students in particular will be served by this project through the educational opportunity offered.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     The expected outcome of the project is the initiation of a successful seaweed composting program for the Miami-Dade
    region. The success of the program can be measured in cubic yards of seaweed removed and resulting volume of
    compost created as well how much of that compost is reused. The number of communities participating is another
    measure of success. Another outcome is the educational opportunity for high school students which can be measured in
    participation and student reviews.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Failure to meet deliverables will result in the forfeiture of funds or financial penalties as described in the contract.
