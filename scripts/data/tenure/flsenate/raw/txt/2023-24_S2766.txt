1. Project Title          The Commodore Trail Heritage Education Program - Coconut Grove

2. Senate Sponsor        Alexis Calatayud

3. Date of Request       02/17/2023

4. Project/Program Description
    The Commodore Trail through Miami's Coconut Grove neighborhood is the most heavily used five-mile-long urban trail in
   Miami-Dade County. This request proposes to curate and implement a self-guided historical tour along the trail focusing on
   the cultural and historic focal points of Coconut Grove. The tour will include 36 interactive points of interest, accessible
   through a mobile application; supportive signage; classroom materials for local schools; and public promotional materials.

    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The five-mile Commodore Trail in Miami-Dade County has been the core of Miami since its founding. Today it's the
    county's center for family-friendly outdoor health and fitness. This request proposes to create a self-guided historical tour
    along the trail focused on the culture and history of Coconut Grove. The tour will include 56 interactive points of interest
    accessible through a mobile application, supportive signage, classroom materials for local schools, and public
    promotional materials.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Expand and implement a comprehensive community heritage education program accessible to Miami students (14
    schools), residents, and visitors. Classroom education and field-trip materials, heritage and environmental signage,
    mobile application and content (digitally and in print), and self-guided and docent-led tours included. Impact over 560,000
    Florida residents annually.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Direct impact to residents and visitors (all ages and abilities) through a 56-stop walking/cycling tour along the
    Commodore Trail. Students from Coconut Grove’s historic Bahamian community and twelve other schools will participate
    via classroom materials and interactive presentations while local youth groups, families, and visitors will engage through
    geocaching features and field trips.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Elderly persons, persons with poor mental health, persons with poor physical health, economically disadvantaged
    persons, at-risk youth, developmentally disabled, physically disabled, preschool students, grade school students, high
    school students, university/college students, visitors, and users of the eleven parks joined together by the Commodore
    Trail.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     The cultural and historical nature of the tour will draw visitors to the Commodore Trail and will encourage multi-modal
    methods of exercise for participants. For every $1 spent on walking trails, $3 is saved in medical expenses (American
    Heart Assn. 2016). Exercise has been proven to improve mental health, as has access to the humanities (American
    Journal of Psychiatry 2017). Each of the 56 stops will engage visitors in a new aspect of Coconut Grove’s unique culture
    and history. The improved Commodore Trail’s cultural history tour will become an important destination for visitors.
    560,000 visitors came to the Commodore Trail corridor in 2020. 300,000 additional visited the northern end of the trail at
    Vizcaya.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     If the Friends of the Commodore Trail fails to meet the contractual deliverables, the financial consequences would be
    that the agency recovers its actual administrative costs related to the failure or delay.
