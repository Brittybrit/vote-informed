    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     In 2022, the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) evaluated the carcinogenicity of occupational exposure
    as a firefighter. After thoroughly reviewing the available scientific literature, IARC's Working Group classified occupational
    exposure as a firefighter as carcinogenic to humans (Group 1), on the basis of sufficient evidence for cancer in humans.
    With this new classification, FCI will continue to expand firefighters access to cancer screening through its mobile clinic,
    which provides screenings on-site at fire stations; expand access to education on best practices for cancer risk reduction
    including on-line access to curriculum and developing guidelines for subgroups; facilitate prevention and earlier detection
    of disease; identify exposures that account for increased cancer risk including PFAS and PFOA. FCI will also field test
    new technology and methods that measure exposure in the field; and evaluate the impact of best practices on cancer risk
    reduction.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Expand research on characterizing firefighter cancer as well as expand access to cancer screening and earlier detection
    of disease through two mobile clinics; expand access for firefighters across the state to education on best practices for
    cancer risk reduction and prevention strategies, including virtual curriculum and education modules for firefighters and
    firefighter cancer survivors; and tailored guidelines for sub groups; identify exposures that account for increased cancer
    risk and field test new technology and methods that measure exposure in the field through wearable and mobile devices;
    evaluate the impact of best practices on cancer risk reduction; and work with IARC and WHO internationally to highlight
    the need for continued research on cancer in the fire service and collaborate with NIOSH at the Federal level in building
    the National Firefighter Cancer Registry.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Funds are invested in increasing participation in cancer screening and prevention for cancers overrepresented within the
    fire service, including access to these services through a unique mobile clinical infrastructure. Additionally, funds will
    support continued evidence-based education of Florida fire departments and sub groups within the fire service including
    Urban Search and Research and Wildland Urban Interface (WUI) firefighters on cancer risk related to occupational
    exposures as well as best practices for risk reduction derived from our scientific progress to date. Funds will also provide
    for scientific inquiry and evaluation of cancer in the fire service and ways to track and evaluate exposures.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The Firefighter Cancer Initiative serves the Florida fire service, including career and volunteer firefighters and inspectors.
    FCI has engaged over 7,500 in research projects, over 500 firefighters have been screened by the FCI clinical team and
    ongoing COVID projects have included 6,500 first responders. FCI anticipates increasing this number by 1,000 in the
    coming year as well as continuing efforts nationally, as FCI leads the national conversation on cancer in the fire service.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     FCI aims to increase the life expectancy of firefighters and their families by better understanding cancer risks related to
    occupational exposures. FCI research findings will result in developing new safety standards and best practices to reduce
    firefighters’ increased risk of developing and dying of cancer. Outcomes will be measured in various ways, including
    number of firefighters engaged; number of firefighters screened for cancer; successful new technologies to monitor
    exposure; number of tumor specimens submitted to the tumor bank to identify biomarkers and therapeutics; continued
    analysis of the dataset linking the Florida Cancer Data System with State firefighter data. FCI plans to increase uptake of
    best practices, including cancer screenings, as well as increase the amount of federal dollars brought to Florida for this
    initiative.
