2. Senate Sponsor        Ana Maria Rodriguez

4. Project/Program Description
    The Women of Tomorrow Mentoring & Scholarship Program partners with 200+ professional mentors and 115 public-
   schools in Miami-Dade,Broward & Palm Beach counties to create pathways out of poverty for 3000 at-risk girls each year
   (17,000 since inception in SFL), prevent high school dropouts (96% graduation rate), increase access to technical/higher
   education (83% enrollment), and equip at-risk girls with tools needed for academic resilience and success in today's
   workforce. Technology and database enhancements would support and expand engagement with mentors, school
   coordinators, staff, and Alumnae Network; in-person mentoring and career-focused fieldtrips, vocational college campus
   visits; & ensure sustained engagement among alumnae as a bridge for at-risk girls as they develop in their academic,
   vocational and professional careers. This request will also ensure school coordinators experience strategic training
   ensuring long term engagement and re-engage a number of inactive schools.

14. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     State funding this year would leverage the existing resources and tools to provide enhanced in-person group mentoring
    sessions, and career discovery focused field trips, and college campus visits. State funding would also accelerate the
    ongoing work of the Alumnae Network, including and not limited to people search services for re-engagement, including
    career and aptitude tests for students, WOT App and portal enhancements networking and job opportunities/resources
    for the program’s network of 3,000 active mentees and over 17,000 SFL student alumnae. Job boards and tangible
    career resources would be introduced to senior students in the program in addition to training videos and resources to the
    Alumnae Network, which would include a job shadowing program. Job shadowing would be organized in collaboration
    with our business partners, matching student requests. Funding from this grant would activate a number of inactive
    schools.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     In times of economic uncertainty and inflation, our program will further enhance employment prospects for at-risk women
    and girls, WOT will provide aptitude assessment tests and self discovery tools allowing students to assess their skills for
    targeted job markets in an economy with increased inflation. A number of inactive schools will also be activated -
    encouraging new mentees to experience the program. The new program resources will complement and underpin the
    development of mentee life plans to equip them with the tools they need to sustain their academic and professional
    career paths upon graduation. Senior graduates will be integrated into the Alumnae Network, thus providing them with
    broader scope of opportunities either through higher education and/or workforce placement and job shadowing
    experiences (in collaboration with our business partners.)
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Mentoring sessions, career focused field trips, and college campus visits for at risk women and girls; training videos and
    mentee video profiles to enhance networking and job opportunities for the program's network of 3,000 active mentees
    and over 17,000 SFL alumnae mentee graduates; aptitude and skills assessment tools to correlate skills to targeted job
    markets ensuring their financial independence and self-sustainability. Mentoring program resources for a number of
    inactive schools and the at-risk girls in those communities. Through our group mentoring program, our students also
    make a positive impact influencing their family and communities as they transform their lives.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

    3000+ at-risk girls in 115 public high schools, 200+ professional mentors, and 17,000 program alumnae in Miami-Dade,
    Broward and Palm Beach counties.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     The program inspires and empowers at-risk girls to envision and achieve greater goals for themselves, through academic
    resilience achieving their full potential, helps them avoid risky behaviors, prevents high school dropouts and increases
    access to technical/higher education. Their success will be measured through metrics reporting - monthly - quarterly and
    annually. Methodology: measured through graduation rates, online digital platforms, in-person school visits and summit
    feedback surveys.

    Currently 96 percent of students in the program graduate from high school; 83 percent have firm plans to enroll in higher
    education; Over nine out of ten mentees have pursued higher education supported by more than $6.3 million in
    scholarships awarded to graduating seniors; 90 percent of mentees report the program has had a significant impact on
    their lives.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Women of Tomorrow would return unspent funds to the state.
