4. Project/Program Description
    Goodwill South Florida offers vocational education & training programs where participants are taught job skills and gain
   work experience in positions within one of Goodwill’s business divisions, in the areas of sewing, customer service/retail
   education and custodial services. Participants who successfully complete training are placed in paid positions within
   Goodwill’s Apparel Manufacturing Division; one of its 35 Goodwill retail stores for customer service; and
   governmnent/commercial businesses for custodial services. These programs focus on those individuals who have the
   capabilities and interests to perform in a job, but are still in need of individualized supervision and supportive counseling
   due to a specific condition or conditions which impair their productivity and earning capacity, or precludes them from
   obtaining or maintaining a job in the community. The appropriation will provide a stipend to support individuals who are
   enrolled in the sewing program.

12. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Nonprofit organizations are among those who have suffered the most. Goodwill will be able to put those funds back into
    the programs that serve people with disabilities. We will re-invest the funds in enhancing the job skills and life situations,
    not only to those we serve but the families that depend on them. It will specifically help us put over 450 people through
    our skills & job training programs that will result in a job upon successful completion.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Goodwill South Florida is the 17th largest private employer in South Florida, employing 3,100. Our workforce includes
    1,200 people with federally defined disabilities, making us the largest employer of people with disabilities in all of Florida.
    We are very proud of the fact that we devote more than 95% of our expenses to Program Costs, and less than 5% of our
    expenses on management or other support services. The appropriation will allow us to invest in programs that help
    transition someone seeking our services from dependency to independence. Rather than using tax dollars to receive
    state services, our successful job placements can help convert tax users to become tax payers by now being able to find
    a job and contribute.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

    The appropriation will provide a stipend to students enrolled in the Sewing Training Program so that they are able to
    maintain an income while learning a new, employable skill.

    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Last year, we provided certified vocational training to 154 individuals while 2,528 people received job training and wrap
    around services while employed at Goodwill. An additional 671 were provided with placement services for community
    jobs. Overall, our essential job programs served 7,060 people. Of those served 50% had one or more disability and the
    remaining 50% had one or more barrier to work. Our program participants are reflective of the diverse South Florida
    community: 59% identify as Hispanic or Latinx; 19% as African American or Black; 9% Haitian; 3% as White; and 10% as
    Other.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Goodwill is one of the most cost-effective nonprofits in the U.S. with 96% of our expenses going toward funding direct
    programs. As part of an accreditation program, there are many outcomes and measurable units of service that are put
    into place. One of the best ways to measure outcomes is by the number of participants enrolled in programs, how many
    participants complete and successfully graduate from the program and how many people we provide services for each
    year and how many job placements we fill.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Cessation or recovery of funding if the stipends are not provided to enrolled students.
