4. Project/Program Description
    The Town of Cutler Bay requests $520,000 to support the planning, design, and engineering phase of its Marlin Roadway
   Improvement Project. The proposed project aims to apply complete streets principles to Marlin Road, one of Cutler Bay's
   major collector roadways. Public input from residents reveal concerns about speeding along Marlin Road, particularly near
   Belle Aire Elementary School, which is along the project site. There have also been multiple bicycle and pedestrian
   accidents based on crash data from 2014-2018. Planned improvements along the corridor include: installation of 10’' wide
   high-visibility crosswalks; pedestrian refuge in the median; installation of 5’' wide sidewalks and ADA ramps; installation of
   bike lanes or share-the-road signage; installation of pedestrian lighting, as feasible; operational analysis and signal
   progression improvements; and traffic calming techniques (i.e., speed tables, roundabout), as feasible.

12. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The goal of the project is to improve the safety of Marlin Road, one of Cutler Bay's major collector roadways, by
    implementing complete street principles that support all modes of transportation and all users regardless of age or ability.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     The proposed appropriation request will fund the planning, design, and engineering phase of the project.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     The proposed project aims to apply complete streets principles to Marlin Road, one of Cutler Bay's major collector
    roadways. Public input from residents reveal concerns about speeding along Marlin Road, particularly near Belle Aire
    Elementary School, which is along the project site. There have also been multiple bicycle and pedestrian accidents based
    on crash data from 2014-2018. Improvements made to Marlin Road will benefit all area residents, school children, and
    travelers along the road by providing for safe travel and improved roadway.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Marlin Road is a public roadway in Miami-Dade County and one of Cutler Bay’s major collector roadways.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Based on data collected, Marlin Road's service level is "D" between Old Cutler Road and Caribbean Boulevard and "F"
    between Caribbean Boulevard and north of US-1. Flow along Marlin builds as it progresses north, which is consistent with
    the fact that most residents in south Miami-Dade County must travel north for employment. The proposed improvements
    will increase the level of service along Marlin Road to a minimum "B" and improve connection to US-1, the South Dade
    Transit-way (bus rapid transit corridor), and the Florida Turnpike - creating a regional benefit.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     If the town fails to meet the deliverables or performance measures, it should be given notice and the opportunity to come
    into compliance. Should it fail to address the concerns, funding should be withheld until a time that the project complies
    with the requirements. Failure to meet deliverables would cancel the contract and cause forfeiture of the remaining
    balance of unspent appropriation.

13. The owners of the facility to receive, directly or indirectly, any fixed capital outlay funding. Include the
    relationship between the owners of the facility and the entity.

     The roadway is owned and maintained by Miami-Dade County. Like the town's other complete streets projects that are
    owned by the county, the town completes the engineering and construction of the project and supplements the county's
    maintenance operations plan. This includes landscaping, street sweeping, sidewalk inspections and repairs, and general
    upkeep.
