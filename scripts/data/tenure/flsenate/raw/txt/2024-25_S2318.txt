4. Project/Program Description
    The City of Wildwood is the fastest growing community in the US according to various outlets/publications. Raw
   wastewater flows have been increasing 30%/year for the last 3 years, and the population is expected to increase 24%/year
   through 2030 and then 10.7%/year for the following decade. The City is requesting State funding to assist in the
   construction of a new AWT wastewater treatment facility to replace the old and obsolescent infrastructure (existing capacity
   - 3 MGD; required 2030 capacity - 6 MGD). The new WRF will provide the required capacity, generate a high-quality
   product, reclaimed water, that is low in nutrients and will meet AWT standards. These funds will allow the City to: (1)
   enhance, preserve and improve the surface, groundwater and drinking water quality in the local area as well as within the
   Rainbow/Silver Springs BMAP; (2) protect the general public from environmental harm; (3) increase/improve economic
   activity and (4) create job opportunities.

14. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     e funds will allow the City to: (1) meet current and future wastewater demands; (2) reduce nutrient loadings to the
    environment and springs; (3) enhance, preserve and improve the water quality of groundwaters, surface waters, springs,
    and wetlands; (4) protect the public from environmental harm; (5) reduce system overflows; (6) increase economic
    activity/opportunity; (7) increase tourism by keeping critically important water resources clean, usable and resource; and
    (8) create job opportunities.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     The requested funds are for construction of a new AWT treatment facility to replace the failing and aging facility
    infrastructure (~50 years old), and to increase capacity to keep pace with the rapid population growth within the City. The
    City provides a regional solution to wastewater treatment and effluent disposal by treating wastewater from the Coleman
    Prison (~0.8 MGD) as well as the wastewater generated within the City's service area. The reclaimed water produced is
    used throughout City's reclaimed water service area and The Villages/North Sumter Utilities (use ~98% of the reclaimed
    water generated).
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     The improvements will provide the needed wastewater treatment capacity and infrastructure required within the City's
    service area due to the rapid population growth being experienced as well as the following: protect critical water
    resources in the local area (surface waters, wetlands, groundwater, and the springs (Rainbow/Silver); protect local
    residences and property; protect the general public from harm; and enhance, preserve and improve the environment,
    wildlife quality and resources used by the local residents and tourists by reducing sanitary sewer and sludge
    management system overflows.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Residents of the City of Wildwood (~28,000) and those within the City’s service area. The population has quadrupled
    since the 2010 census and is estimated to increase to 131,000 by 2050. The rapid population increase requires
    substantial utility system improvements and increased capacity. Financial assistance from the State is requested due to
    the need to protect the local environment and springs; the need to provide a long-term, sustainable solution to
    wastewater treatment and effluent disposal in the service area; and to increase both economic activity and job
    opportunities within the local area.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     These funds will provide the following benefits/outcome: (1) enhance, preserve and improve the environment and wildlife
    quality within the local area; (2) protect the general public from environmental harm and the degradation of local water
    resources; (3) protection of public and private property (yards, streets, structures, etc.); (4) increase tourism; (5) increase
    and improve local economic activity; (5) improve groundwater, surface water, and drinking water quality. Outcomes will be
    measured by the decrease nutrient concentrations, and the reduced number of system overflows.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Fines and clean-up costs associated with sanitary sewer overflows.
