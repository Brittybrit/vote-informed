4. Project/Program Description
    The Firefighter Cancer Initiative (FCI) is a unique program led by the University of Miami Sylvester Comprehensive Cancer
   Center, which was awarded designation from the National Cancer Institute (NCI). This program aims to understand and
   address why Firefighters are at increased risk of developing and dying of cancer relative to the populations that they serve.
   FCI supports novel translational research, wide scale education and prevention initiatives, and collaboration with
   Firefighters to develop and implement practices that can reduce noxious exposures associated with disease.

    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) has classified the Working group of occupational exposure as
    a firefighter as carcinogenic to humans (Group 1), on the basis of sufficient evidence for cancer in humans. With this new
    classification, FCI will continue to expand firefighters access to cancer screening through our mobile clinic, which
    provides screenings on-site at fire stations locally; expand access to clinical screenings in remote and rural areas of the
    state of Florida via telemedicine; develop a satellite resource center in St.Lucie/Martin/Indian River counties to create a
    physical presence in this area as well as navigate firefighters and their families to care at Sylvester, provide support
    services to survivors, and enroll firefighters in FCI studies; expand access to education on best practices for cancer risk
    reduction including on-line access to curriculum and developing guidelines for sub groups; identify exposures that
    account for increased cancer risk.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Expand research on characterizing firefighter cancer as well as expand access to cancer screening and earlier detection
    of disease through 2 mobile clinics and telemedicine; expand access for firefighters across the state to education on best
    practices for cancer risk reduction and prevention strategies, including virtual curriculum and education modules for
    firefighters and firefighter cancer survivors; and tailored guidelines for sub groups; identify exposures that account for
    increased cancer risk and field test new technology and methods that measure exposure in the field through wearable
    and mobile devices; evaluate the impact of best practices on cancer risk reduction; and work with IARC and WHO
    internationally to highlight the need for continued research on cancer in the fire service and collaborate with NIOSH at the
    Federal level in building the National Firefighter Cancer Registry.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Funds are invested in increasing participation in cancer screening and prevention for cancers overrepresented among
    active and retired firefighters, including access to these services through a unique mobile clinical infrastructure.
    Additionally, funds will support the expansion of clinical services to firefighters in remote and rural areas via telemedicine.
    FCI will develop a resource center at a satellite location to interact and navigate firefighters to services at Sylvester and
    provide support services to cancer survivors and their families. Funds will allow for continued evidence-based education
    of Florida firefighters, fire sub groups and local healthcare providers on the cancer risks from occupational exposures as
    well as best practices for risk reduction derived from our scientific progress to date. Funds will also provide for scientific
    inquiry and evaluation of cancer in the fire service and ways to track and evaluate exposures.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The Firefighter Cancer Initiative serves the Florida fire service, both career and volunteer firefighters including fire
    subgroups (investigators, wildland, trainers and urban search and rescue (USAR)) . FCI has engaged over 8,000 in
    research projects, over 800 firefighters have been screened by the FCI clinical team. We anticipate increasing this
    number by 1,000 in the coming year as well as continuing in our efforts nationally, as FCI leads the national conversation
    on cancer in the fire service.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     FCI aims to increase the life expectancy of firefighters and their families by better understanding cancer risks related to
    occupational exposures. FCI research findings will result in developing new safety standards and best practices to reduce
    firefighters’ increased risk of developing and dying of cancer. Outcomes will be measured in various ways, including
    number of firefighters engaged; number of firefighters screened for cancer; successful new technologies to monitor
    exposure; number of tumor specimens submitted to the tumor bank to identify biomarkers and therapeutics; continued
    analysis of the dataset linking the Florida Cancer Data System with State firefighter data. We plan to increase uptake of
    best practices, including cancer screenings, as well as increase the amount of federal dollars brought to Florida for this
    initiative.
