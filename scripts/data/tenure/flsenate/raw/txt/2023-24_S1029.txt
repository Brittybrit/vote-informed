4. Project/Program Description
    The Camillus House Phoenix Human Trafficking Recovery Program is designed to provide low-demand transitional
   housing and supportive services for adult-aged women who are victims of human trafficking. These individuals are
   identified and referred by Miami-Dade State Attorney Katherine Fernandez Rundle’s Office (SAO), Homeland Security,
   police departments and other providers for clients who meet the criteria as victims of domestic and commercial sexual
   exploitation, labor and other forms of human trafficking. Supportive services assist victims through several phases
   including: 1) crisis intervention and assessment; 2) case management; and 3) social reintegration. The funding requested
   for this project will support ongoing operational expenses and staffing for the program. The program has a professional
   case management and clinical team deploying the latest evidenced based strategies to guide participants through the
   process of trauma recovery and social reintegration.

a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

 The Camillus House Phoenix Program is designed to provide low-demand transitional housing and supportive services
for adult-aged women who are victims of human trafficking. These individuals are identified and referred by Miami-Dade
State Attorney Katherine Fernandez Rundle’s Office (SAO), Homeland Security, police departments and other providers
for clients who meet the criteria as victims of domestic and commercial sexual exploitation, labor and other forms of
human trafficking. The program seeks to meet survivors where they are at and deliver services based on individual needs
including case management and treatment if requested.
b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

 The Camillus House Phoenix Human Trafficking Recovery Program is the only program of its kind serving adult-aged
women who are victims of human trafficking in South Florida. Low-demand supportive services assist victims through
several phases including: 1) crisis intervention and assessment; 2) comprehensive assessment and case management;
and 3) social reintegration. The funding requested for this project will support ongoing operational expenses and staffing
for the program. Camillus has dedicated case management and clinical staff including a master level clinicians, case
manager; and resident assistants deploying the latest evidenced based practices to guide participants through the
process of trauma recovery, education assistance, obtaining employment, and securing permanent housing. This
program is not restricted to persons in Miami-Dade. Camillus is working with State DCF officials and State Attorney
Katherine Rundle.
c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

 Ghe goal of the Camillus House Human Trafficking Recovery Program is to support victims of human trafficking in order
to prevent chronic homelessness and re-victimization by: 1) Stabilizing survivors of human trafficking by providing a safe
place to receive care and services to enable them to address their trauma and begin recovery in a low demand setting
that responds to their needs. 2) Enhancing self-esteem and vocational skills to make victims of human trafficking less
vulnerable to any form of exploitation. 3) Assisting victims of human trafficking to obtain employment and/or benefits to
facilitate independent living. 4) Providing housing navigation to return victims of human trafficking safely to the
community.
d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

 This program will benefit female victims of human trafficking over the age of 18, as identified and referred by the Miami-
Dade State Attorney’s Office and other law enforcement. By the nature of their situation these individuals are homeless or
at risk of homelessness and in need of a safe place to live to receive care, treatment and services while also preparing to
serve as witnesses in the trials of the human traffickers. The program serves approximately 40 persons a year. This
program is not restricted to persons in Miami-Dade, Camillus is working with State DCF officials and State Attorney
Katherine Rundle’s Office to maximize benefit to victims of human trafficking from throughout the State of Florida.
e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
be measured?

 Stabilizing these individuals benefits the State of Florida by ensuring that these persons do not become chronically
homeless and are not re-victimized, while also allowing the SAO to successfully prepare witnesses for trial against
traffickers. This program will also provide enhanced self-esteem and additional vocational skills so that these women are
less vulnerable to any form of exploitation. As per contract, performance and outcomes include: 1) A minimum of 3 adult
victims will receive services each month; 2) 100% of clients in the Human Trafficking Recovery Program will have a
service plan that is updated every 30 days; 3) At least of 50% of residential adult victims of human trafficking participating
in the Human Trafficking Recovery Program will successfully complete the program; and 4) 70% of victims successfully
completing treatment in the Human Trafficking will be stably housed.
f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

 As per contract, corrective action plans may be required for noncompliance, nonperformance, or unacceptable
performance under this Contract. Penalties may be imposed for failures to implement or to make acceptable progress on
such corrective action plans. The increments of penalty imposition that shall apply, unless the Department determines
that extenuating circumstances exist, shall be based upon the severity of the noncompliance that generated the need for
corrective action plan.
