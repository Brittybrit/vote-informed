Public Hearing: “Adoption of District-Wide Instructional Materials”
Ms. Maria Teresa Rojas, School Board Chair, called the Public Hearing to
order at 1:00 p.m., and Dr. Gallon, III led those in attendance in The
Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag of the United States of America. The
Board then held a moment of silent meditation.
The Chair invited anyone from the audience who had not already signed
up and wished to address Agenda Item SP-1 to proceed to the lobby
counter outside the auditorium for assistance from staff.
Mr. Walter Harvey, School Board General Counsel was recognized to
explain the procedures to be followed during today’s public hearing. Mr.
Harvey explained this was the School Board’s open notice public hearing
to receive public comment on instructional materials being recommended
to the Board for adoption, pursuant to Board Policy 2510, Instructional
Materials and Resources, in agreement with Chapter 1006 of the Florida
Education Code. This hearing was established as part of the process for
the district’s review, evaluation, and selection of non-state-adopted
instructional materials. He further explained that the public notice and
access had been provided for the recommended materials specifically
listing the materials and how they could be accessed at least 20 days
before the hearing as required by school board policy in Florida statutes.
Mr. Harvey reviewed Board Policy 0169.1, Public Participation at Board
Meetings, and indicated that the public would be allowed to comment
regarding the proposed adoption of the listed materials. After hearing the
speakers and answering any questions, the Board should consider
Agenda Item SP-1, memorializing and confirming that the Board has
received public comment regarding the proposed instructional materials
to be adopted in accordance with Florida law and Board policy. After the
vote, the public hearing of instructional materials may be adjourned

instructional materials discussed today were adopted with the importance

and teachers deserve access to high-quality content that is rigorous and
aligned with state standards. Today’s public hearing was an essential part
of our commitment to ensuring a quality education for our community and
a transparent decision-making process regarding instructional materials
in our District. He thanked Ms. Lourdes Diaz, Ms. Lisette Alves, Mr. Daniel
Mateo, and Ms. Diaz’s staff who worked so hard to make sure the
textbooks were in the hands of our students, teachers, and the entire
infrastructure related to the delivery, security, and allocations for
textbooks and digital materials in our school district. He turned it over to
Ms. Lourdes Diaz, Chief Academic Officer, who started us off with the
presentation.
Ms. Diaz stated the instructional materials presented today were selected
by committees of M-DCPS classroom teachers who were certified and
taught in the subject areas considered for adoption and by parents of
students who would be using these materials as required by State Statute.
Teachers were selected from a pool of volunteers while parents of district
students were selected at random from responses to a district-wide call
for parent reviewers. The review of materials being recommended today
followed the review and adoption procedures established by Florida
statute 1006.283(2)(B)8 which covers the adoption of instructional
materials by each district's School Board.
The district’s instructional materials review committee for K-12 science
was composed of subcommittees representing various grade levels or
subjects for the material being reviewed. Subcommittees reviewed
multiple titles per subject area or grade level. The recommended titles
presented today for Core Science are all State adopted receive the
highest scores by the district's review process and reflect each
subcommittee's top choice. The adoption of instructional materials will
provide students with new content for the following subject areas:
Grades K-5 Science
Grades 6-8 Comprehensive Science
Grades 9-12 Anatomy Physiology, Biology, Chemistry,
Environmental Science, Forensic Science,
Marine Science, Physical Science, Physics, AP
Biology, AP Chemistry, AP Environmental Science,
and AP Physics.

instructional resources that are aligned with current state standards. As
mentioned earlier, Florida Statute 1006.283 established the process by

their boards and students have devices which makes more sense. We
have seen this trend in colleges and universities. As we prepare students,
we incorporate more and more of the digital elements. However, our

activities to engage different students at different levels. We scaffold as
you know, we prepare some materials that are more accessible to English

the primary source and the major tool to ensure that standards-aligned
instruction was taking place. We supplement with other things digital
software, materials from iPads, and so on.
