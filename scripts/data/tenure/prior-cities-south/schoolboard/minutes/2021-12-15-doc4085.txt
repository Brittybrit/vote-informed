He has served as Lead Steward for the United Teachers of Dade, the Florida Association of Science Teachers (FAST) member and past president in 2005, Dade County Science Teachers Association (DCSTA) president from 2006 to the present, and the Chemistry Alliance Society in Miami-Dade County President in 2004.

Dr. Hueck holds a Bachelor of Science degree from the University of Southern Mississippi, a Master of Science in Education Biology from the University of Miami, and a Doctor of Education Degree in Educational Leadership from Nova Southeastern University.

The School Board of Miami-Dade County, Florida, is honored to recognize such an outstanding and dedicated teacher who has not only served Miami Senior High School, but the entire Miami-Dade County Public Schools community.

B-6 – Good Cause
Ms. Lubby Navarro, School Board Member; Ms. Perla Tabares Hantman, Chair; Dr. Steve Gallon III, Vice Chair; Dr. Dorothy Bendross-Mindingall, Ms. Lucia Baez-Geller, and Ms. Luisa Santos, School Board members; proposed that The School Board of Miami-Dade County, Florida, approve Resolution No.21-056 of The School Board of Miami-Dade County, Florida, recognizing Ms. Denise Stemen for her unwavering leadership and commitment to ensuring the safety of South Florida and beyond. [A copy of Resolution No. 21-056 is on file with the official records of this meeting.]

Deputy Special Agent in Charge (“DSAC”) Denise Stemen has been a special agent with the Federal Bureau of Investigation for more than 22 years, primarily responsible for managing criminal, cyber, counterterrorism, Weapons of Mass Destruction, counterintelligence investigations, and intelligence matters. Additionally, she has overseen the FBI Miami Division’s administrative and security operations. After an over two-decade long career in law enforcement, DSAC Stemen will be retiring on December 18th, 2021.

Ms. Stemen first became a Special Agent in 1999 as the Cyber Division Program Manager for the Greater Detroit Area. In 2007, she was moved to the Washington D.C. Metro Area to provide guidance and support to seven Domestic FBI field offices and 6 International Legal Attaché offices concerning criminal and terrorist matters. In 2009, Ms. Stemen came to Miami to serve as the Supervisor and Program Coordinator for Complex Financial Crimes and Investigation, responsible for investigating mortgage fraud in the wake of the Great Recession upending real estate in Florida. In 2012, her focus shifted to supervising Domestic Terrorism, Weapons of Mass Destruction and Threat Response Programs. 

In 2018, she led all Complex Financial Crimes Investigations within the FBI Miami Division’s Criminal Branch, which included pursuing Money Laundering, Health Care Fraud, Civil Rights violations, and Public Corruption both domestic and international. Ms. Stemen became the DSAC overseeing all investigative and internal operations of the FBI Miami Division. As one of the nation’s four offices with extraterritorial responsibility, the Miami Division’s mission to protect the American people extends overseas, specifically in Latin and Central America. DSAC Stemen is a Certified Private Pilot and has supported criminal and terrorism investigations by providing surveillance from the air.

Prior to becoming a special agent, Ms. Stemen taught at the high school level in Broward County Public Schools. She also coached girls’ volleyball for ten years, boys’ volleyball for five years, and girl’s golf for three years. In 1989, Ms. Stemen attended Ohio University on a full scholarship and graduated with a Bachelor of Science Dual Major in Special Education and Art Education. After her career change, she went on to earn a master’s degree in Computer and Information Systems Security from the University of Maryland in 2017.

SUPERINTENDENT’S INFORMATIONAL REPORTS TO THE BOARD ON SELECTED TOPIC

The level and quality of education in this community

Superintendent Carvalho said that just about a week ago the district was notified that the U.S. Chamber of Commerce alongside Fordham Institute; an institute that has been reasonably skeptical about the efficacy and success education across America. It is a very conservative-leaning entity, but this institute joined forces with the U.S. Chambers of Commerce to conduct an important study across America that detailed which cities/urban center in America were doing work in the area of education that was leading to results that were outliers, considering the national landscape of performance. Considering all the news everyone has been receiving recently, he could not think of a better way of ending this year than by touting and showcasing the results of that report which clearly includes that Metro-Miami; the South Florida communities including Broward and Palm Beach is a superstar city when it comes to education. This is on the basis of education attainment adjusted for the social factors known to impact a child’s ability to learn. Whether it is English language limitation, poverty, visibility, and the like. However, it does not take into account the educational level of the family or the annual earned income of the family. 

This is a critical report: 1) because of its source and they admit upfront, “we are usually very doubtful about the ability of urban education in America to actually succeed. But then they follow with a recognition of the entities in the country with Metro/Miami leading the pack nationally. They actually defined the odds, breaking through the ceiling and achieving; at unprecedented level in the country. The report also states that even though Metro/Miami refers to an area that expands beyond Miami-Dade County that the two counties that are elevating their status national are in fact; Miami-Dade and Palm Beach. They look at education attainment as the primary factor that is measured, graduation rates, but then a significant concern for this school system over the years, the ability of these districts to actually outperform the national average as far as desegregated data on the basis of poverty, English language limitation, visibility, but also racial and ethnic minority groups. Simply Miami/Metro is not only outperforming America, but the ability also to narrow the gap compared to the rest of America is significantly better. A lot of discussion regarding this topic was held where all got mired by a simplistic examination of the here and now static, analysis of data without taking into account the national context or the contextual variable that they know influences student performance.

Therefore, he took this opportunity to thank the individuals that are most responsible for this latest recognition. Just a few months ago the Council of Great City Schools published what he believes is probably the most important analysis and study of a district’s performance across America. Looking specifically at the 76 largest school systems in America and determining what they deferred to as a statistical district defect that is zero positive or negative on the basis of the measurable impact that that district has had on the A-1 (Cont’d.)
