11:00 A.M. - PRESENTATION OF RESOLUTIONS
The Chair called the meeting to order at 11:00 a.m. and Mr. Alonso led those
in attendance in The Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag of the United States of
America. The Chair asked everyone to continue standing and pause for a
Moment of Silent Meditation.
The Chair informed the public that according to Board policies, the proposed
Board Member resolutions would be presented at 11:00 a.m. The Board would
then reconvene its regular public meeting, including other hearings, in
accordance with the Policies of the Board at 1:00 p.m. or soon thereafter.
The Chair extended an invitation to anyone from the audience who wanted to
address the resolutions, endorsements, or proclamations, they must visit the
lobby counter outside the auditorium for assistance from staff with signing up.
The Chair noted that the Board must approve additional speakers by a majority
vote. If a quorum is not present, then no votes will be taken. The vote on the
Supplemental Speakers List will be reserved once there is a quorum of five
members present to vote.
Upon a motion being made by Ms. Colucci and seconded by Ms. Blanco, the
Board voted 5-0 to approve the Supplemental List of Speakers for the
resolutions, endorsements, and proclamations at this time. [It is noted for the
record that Dr. Bendross-Mindingall, Mr. Espino, and Dr. Gallon, III had not yet
arrived at the meeting.] [It is noted for the record that Ms. Baez-Geller was
absent from today’s meeting.]
The Chair announced that there were four Board members’ resolutions to be
presented during this session.
Upon a motion being made by Ms. Colucci and seconded by Ms. Blanco, the
Board voted 5-0 to move agenda items B-3, B-4, B-5, and B-6 to the floor for
presentation. [It is noted for the record that Dr. Bendross-Mindingall, Mr.
Espino, and Dr. Gallon, III had not yet arrived at the meeting.] [It is noted for
the record that Ms. Baez-Geller was absent from today’s meeting.]
Upon the vote being made by Ms. Colucci and seconded by Mr. Alonso, the
Board voted 5-0 to approve these resolutions. [It is noted for the record that
Dr. Bendross-Mindingall, Mr. Espino, and Dr. Gallon, III had not yet arrived at
the meeting.] [It is noted for the record that Ms. Baez-Geller was absent from
today’s meeting.]
The Chair went on to state that pursuant to Board Policy 0165, whenever
practicable as determined by the Board Chair, presentations that include
students should occur prior to any other presentations during this session of
today’s meeting.
The Chair announced the first presentation on the agenda is B-3, but Board
Member Bendross-Mindingall had not yet arrived at the meeting. The Chair
then announced Agenda Item B-4 to be presented.

Dr. Dorothy Bendross-Mindingall, Board Member, and Ms. Mari Tere Rojas,
Chair, Mr. Roberto Alonso, Ms. Mary Blanco, Mr. Daniel Espino, and Dr. Steve
Gallon, III, School Board Members, proposed that The School Board of Miami-
Dade County, Florida, approve Resolution 23-055 of The School Board of
Miami-Dade County, Florida, recognizing Shirley “Coach Lamb” Presley. [A
copy of Resolution No. 23-055 is on file with the official records of this
meeting.]
The Vice Chair introduced this item.
Dr. Bendross-Mindingall appeared before the Board to present her Agenda
Item B-3.
Dr. Bendross-Mindingall asked Ms. Shirley “Coach Lamb” Presley to join her
on the floor as she spoke of all Ms. Presley’s accolades by mentioning that
she exemplified a lifetime of dedicated service and passion for the field of
education. Ms. Shirley Presley, a retired educator, committed an astounding
42 years of her life to shaping the minds of countless students, especially in
the area of exceptional education. Most of her esteemed career, spanning 39
years, was spent at Edison Park K-8 Center, where she displayed an
unwavering commitment and unswerving devotion to teaching, particularly to
students with an extensive range of abilities and a variety of needs.
Throughout her tenure at Edison Park, Ms. Presley’s commitment to
excellence extended beyond the classroom, serving for over 20 years as the
Head Delegate for Special Olympics, Girl Scout/Boy Scout leader for Special
Education (SPED) students, and United Way Student/Faculty Ambassador.
She also contributed significantly to various committees and events, including
Black History, Theodore Gibson Oratorical Contest, Science Fair, Spirit Week,
and the initiation of the Black History Jeopardy game show at Edison Park.
Moreover, Ms. Shirley Presley’s invaluable contributions to her school
community included the sponsorship of the Halloween Parade and Costume
Contests, provision of costumes and props for SPED, Christmas, and musical
programs, in addition to bringing characters to life during the Literacy Parade.
Ms. Shirley Presley’s devotion to service reached beyond her school
community as well. She played a key role as the Founder of the Super Peewee
Cheer for the Miami-Dade and Broward Super Peewee Football and Cheer
League. She was also the Scholastic Coordinator for the Liberty City Optimist
Club of Florida, where she served as Head Scholastic Coordinator for Greater
Miami South Florida Pop Warner. Dr. Bendross-Mindingall also mentioned that
Ms. Presley will always have a special place in her heart as she continues her
journey because, during her 33 years with Liberty City Optimist Club, she wore
many hats, including Lifetime Board Member, Fundraiser, T-Ball Coach, Super
Peewee Cheer Coach, Soccer Coach, Head Cheer Commissioner, and after-
school tutor. Always an educator at heart, her most rewarding experiences
stemmed from her dedication to tutoring children of all ages, attending parent
and teacher conferences, visiting classrooms, and sponsoring book events for
all.
Dr. Bendross-Mindingall stated Ms. Shirley Presley’s dedication to education
and the community started early in her life. Her high school journey was
marked by her role as a member of her school’s color guard and her
enthusiastic participation in the school band during school assemblies, and
pre-game and half-time shows during football games. She was also managing

The Chair introduced this item.
Mr. Alonso from the dais explained that he would be presenting the approved
resolution to the Mayor in the City of Hialeah Gardens in the coming month
but did want to take a moment today to read into the record the importance
recognition of Yioset de la Cruz an unsung hero. Mr. Alonso mentioned that
Mayor De La Cruz was born in Havana, Cuba, on March 1, 1973. He arrived
in Miami, Florida, in 1983 and moved to Hialeah Gardens the following year.
Mayor De La Cruz’s career in public service began in 1996 as Parks Director
for the City of Sweetwater. On July 18, 2000, he was elected Mayor of the City
of Hialeah Gardens, becoming one of the youngest Mayors in the State of
Florida.
During his tenure as Mayor of the City of Hialeah Gardens, Mayor Yioset De
La Cruz has proven to be a strong believer in education and the role that
education plays in the life of a child and the future of a community. He has
been a staunch supporter of schools in the City of Hialeah Gardens and has
been an outstanding partner of Miami-Dade County Public Schools.
