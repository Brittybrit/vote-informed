Eleventh Judicial Circuit. [A copy of Resolution No. 21-038 is on file
with the official records of this meeting.]
Chair Hantman introduced this item.
Ms. Rojas introduced Judge Correa and invited her family to join her at
the podium. Judge Cristina Rivera Correa has served as a County
Court Judge for the Eleventh Judicial Circuit since the Governor of
Florida appointed her in 2020. She currently presides over
misdemeanor criminal and traffic matters, as well as acts in the
capacity of a Circuit Court Judge in the domestic violence division.
Prior to her appointment to the bench, Judge Correa was an Assistant
School Board Attorney in Miami-Dade County Public Schools (M-
DCPS) for seven years. In this role, Judge Correa litigated civil cases, -
including labor and tort matters- in state, federal, and administrative
court. In her initial jury trial as counsel for the School Board, Judge
Correa secured the first defense verdict in favor of the School Board in
over 20 years. Additionally, Judge Correa served as the training
attorney to Miami-Dade Schools Police Department on legal issues for
Florida Department of Law Enforcement certification. Judge Correa is
the first attorney from the School Board Attorney’s Office to become a
judge.

H-13
Dr. Dorothy Bendross-Mindingall, School Board Member, and Ms.
Perla Tabares Hantman, Chair; Ms. Lucia Baez-Geller, Ms. Christi
Fraga, Dr. Lubby Navarro, Dr. Marta Pérez, Ms. Maria Teresa Rojas,
and Ms. Luisa Santos, School Board Members, proposed that The
School Board of Miami-Dade County, Florida, endorse the month of
October 2021 in Miami-Dade County Public Schools as National
Domestic Violence Awareness Month.
Chair Hantman introduced this item.
Dr. Bendross-Mindingall said that statistically women were usually the
victims of domestic violence, more so than men. In October 1981 the
Day of Unity was created by the National Coalition Against Domestic
Violence to connect domestic abuse advocated from across the
country who were working to end violence against women and their
children. In 1987, the first Domestic Violence Awareness month was
celebrated. Two years later, commemorative legislation was passed by
Congress. Legislation has been passed every year to recognize
October as Domestic Violence Awareness Month.
Continuing, Dr. Bendross-Mindingall said that Domestic Violence
Awareness Month had become an important vehicle to promote
awareness of domestic abuse and also champion the strength and
resilience of battered women and their children. For the last 21 years
she had brought forth this issue to create public awareness of what
occured to women, children, and some men. She noted that they
probably had the best education program of all in teaching children to
keep their hands to themselves.
Dr. Navarro thanked her colleague, Dr. Dorothy Bendross-Mindingall,
for championing this cause; No More Broken Hearts was her legacy.
She identified herself as a domestic violence survivor and noted than
when a woman became the victim of domestic violence, the entire
family, especially the children, suffered. She also served the
community where Transition South—a court protected facility—was
located, behind Caribbean K-8 Center. She had the honor of visiting
with the Superintendent during the pandemic to provide them food and
services. Concluding, she pledged her continued support to this cause
and thanked Dr. Bendross-Mindingall for her work.
Ms. Rojas also expressed gratitude towards the maker of this item for
bringing it forth for consideration. She shared her impressions from
attending a session held at Southwest Senior High School where
victims of domestic violence spoke before students and family
members. She encouraged more sessions like that.
Student Advisor CoriAnna White addressed this item and shared that
she had personal knowledge of students in high school who were
being impacted by domestic violence from their partners. She raised
inquiry about Board sponsored initiatives she could share with her
peers to inform them of the resources available. In response, Dr.
Bendross-Mindingall said she would ensure Ms. White was made part
of the current program in place at schools.
