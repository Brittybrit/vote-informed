Dr. Gallon said it was honor and privilege to stand as a conduit for

Mrs. Valtena G. Brown was a life-long educator of 33-years; the

This was not just an identifier of who she is, but a description of the life
she leads; and the life she was destined for. It is a life dedicated to
community service forged by parents that believed in God, community,
education and family. Throughout her life, she has worked with senior
citizens for their betterment; worked in concert with the school system
collectively and independently for the betterment of children, parents,
and families. In addition to this, Mrs. Brown has also dedicated her
services to her church and sorority; and has sponsored and participated
in events to bring the issues of at-risk families to the forefront while
working collectively to find solutions for adversities that plague the
community and the school district.
Continuing, he said Mrs. Brown, served as the Superintendent/Chief
Operating Officer for Miami-Dade County Public Schools for nearly 9
years. She was responsible for the operational function of all PreK - 12
and Adult/ Vocational schools. Her role also included the supervision of
Transportation, Food and Nutrition, Operations, Athletic/Activities,
Charter Schools Operations and Alternative Education. Prior to that
appointment, Mrs. Brown served as region superintendent, assistant
superintendent, administrative director, principal, assistant principal
and counselor for Miami-Dade County Public Schools. She
implemented programs that directly impacted student achievement,
community awareness and family engagement; creating afterschool
and evening care for students until 8:00 p.m. This allowed working
parents in the southernmost part of Dade County who worked in
Monroe and in the agricultural fields, a safe haven for their children until
they completed their work.
Mrs. Brown created mentorship programs for middle school girls
providing them with opportunities to interact with women and an avenue
for exchange of concerns and ideas; and created opportunities for
cultural, educational and social experiences. Fostering professional
development, she established forums for young educators and aspiring
administrators to exchange ideas and be provided with sound
information to assist with their professional endeavors. In addition,
under her leadership as region superintendent, there was an
insurgence of choice options for students and parents. In the
community Mrs. Brown created adult literacy classes to assist parents
to support and accelerate daily lessons learned by their children. This
initiative provided classroom teachers in community centers, migrant
camps, and churches an opportunity to reteach student lessons to their
parents. Recognized as one of the first Drug Free School of Excellence
Program school districts in the United States, she established an
anonymous reporting and request for assistance hotline. The program
bridged divergent communities through parent workshops and
community-based counseling sessions at close proximity to student's
homes. Equally important, she served on the City of South Miami Parks

abuse. She encouraged everyone to wear a blue ribbon during the
month of April to show their commitment to this cause. She also noted
the possibility of increased child abuse cases as a result of the Covid-

Dr. Gallon noted that this item was not only symbolic of their
commitment to this cause, but it also had substance relative to actions.
He then raised concerns with regards to the District’s current efforts to
address food insecurity in the community.
In response, Superintendent Carvalho said they continued to provide
meals to students; the total to date was 22 million meals served. They
were delivered to parents through Food and Nutrition Department as
well as through partnerships with community-based organizations.
Through World Central Kitchen, thousands of meals were being
distributed to the poorest areas of the community. An outreach line was
available for parents to request assistance with specific needs. They
were working to identify and address the needs of students throughout
the county. He expressed that the biggest challenge was not knowing;
the need was not always immediately known to them. In a virtual
environment it comes difficult to determine the additional need a
student may have. They field calls from teachers, counselor and
sometimes even neighbors. Wellness checks were then done to assist
families. Lastly, he said they had identified thousands of students who
were either disengaged or not progressing academically as they
should. Often, the need was the same; children who faced food and
home insecurities also decline academically. The best place for those
children was the schoolhouse. They were been able to identify and
bring back in excess of seven thousand students.
Continuing, Dr. Gallon inquired about students who were “off the grid”,
as reported by the news.
Superintendent Carvalho indicated that about 10,000 students were
initially unaccounted for. It has been verified that most of them left the
School District; private school, enrolled in another county, left the state,
or left the country. Once all those were excluded, about 1,700 student’s
whereabouts were still unknown; (1) they failed to enroll in the school
they were futured for, or (2) enroll as Kindergartens, or (3) withdrew but
didn’t enroll in the school they intended to. Those were the students
who were truly missing. He then addresses the continuous efforts made
by the District to locate them, including wellness checks, home visits,
neighbors, and parent’s former places of employment. They have been
able to reconnect with about 800 of those students. They have been in
touch with housing authorities where they believe some of these
children may have lived. They were still in pursuit of about a thousand;
some may have left the country due to immigration issues.
Ms. Baez-Geller spoke in support of this item. She noted that many
times the perpetrators were no brough to justice because of the victims’
limitations. The pandemic has only served to expand those limitations.
She encouraged continued awareness and pursuit of justice for abused

mathematics, and computer science.

score of 3 or higher on a 5-point scale on the corresponding AP
exam.

college credit or advanced placement to students who have
performed satisfactorily on the exams.

Dr. Navarro explained that HB 5 and SB 1450, sponsored by
Representative Ardian Zika and Senator Ana Maria Rodriguez
respectively, were proposed acts relating to civic education curriculum
and seek to establish the “Portraits In Patriotism Act.” The identical bills
aim to bolster civics education, in part by directing the Department of
Education to curate a video library sharing first-person accounts of
people who lived under foreign governments and stipulate that these
resources are based on diverse individuals’ personal stories that
illustrate civic-minded qualities. Under these identical proposals the
social studies credit requirement for high school graduation would also
be revised to require that the United States Government course include
a comparative discussion of political ideologies such as communism
and totalitarianism that conflict with the principles of freedom and
democracy in the nation’s founding principles. These bills aim to ensure
that Florida’s public schools do all that they can to help students
understand their roles, rights, and responsibilities as residents of the
state and of the nation. These bills also attempt to foster “a sense of
civic pride and desire to participate regularly with government at the
local, state, and federal levels.” Miami-Dade County Public Schools has
historically supported efforts to educate students on the importance of
civic engagement and about those individuals who have impacted U.S.
culture/society in a positive manner throughout various grade levels in
history courses. As such, this item seeks approval by the Board of an
official position in support of HB 5 and SB 1450 and of the “Portraits in
Patriotism Act,” or other substantially similar legislation.

$130 billion to elementary, middle and high schools to assist with safe

students, was estimated to receive $1.1 billion.

focused on addressing vexing, seemingly intractable issues related to
student learning, especially those students with the greatest needs. In
doing so, it expanded its focus and attention on students whose
learning and lives have been disproportionately impacted by the
COVID-19 pandemic through the adverse consequences of distance
learning and other innovative, nontraditional learning approaches. With
the infusion of these unprecedented funds, the District will enhance its
capacity to mitigate and address the unprecedented adverse impact
that the COVID-19 pandemic has had on its students and their learning
for over a year, with studies anticipating that learning loss will be greater
among low-income, Black, and Hispanic students---a demographic
highly concentrated in Miami-Dade County Public Schools and whose
anticipated academic, social, and emotional needs exacerbated by the
COVID-19 pandemic in large part and generated the unprecedented
amount of federal funds.

accountability, integrity, and transparency in the operation of the school
district, including those that involves expenditures, procurement, and
related financial matters—the ultimate authority and as a result,
accountability rested with the School Board. Thus, the School Board

developing a cohesive, yet comprehensive plan of action for the use of
funds allocated to the District through the American Rescue Plan Act of
2021. In doing so, it would provide leadership, direction, and
engagement to ensure that all resources and federal funds were
targeted and used as intended and include measures that ensure
