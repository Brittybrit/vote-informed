Ms. Blanco presented her agenda item and began by stating that last month
she was visiting South Dade Senior High School and while there she could not
help but notice the bling on the ring on the principal’ s Mr. De Armas finger.
She mentioned she asked what that ring was from? Mr. De Armas mentioned
to her the wonderful accomplishments of the South Dade Senior High School
State Champion Wrestlers. Ms. Blanco mentioned she had to bring them
before the Board to have them recognized. During her visit to the school, it
was clear that the administration of the school was proud of these young men
for their accolades on and off the mat. She continued by stating for years this
team a dominating force on the mat, the Buccaneers, the renowned wrestling
team at South Dade Senior High School, have completed another remarkable
season, becoming once again State Champions, FHSAA 3A Dual Meet and
FHSAA 3A IBT. With this victory, highlighted by an undefeated season, the
Buccaneers have been State Champions for ten consecutive years. “The
Dynasty,” as the wrestling team is now known, has proven time and time again
that determination and willpower are the cornerstone to sustained greatness.
The amazing Buccaneers have amassed an extraordinary athletic record of
achievements, becoming State Champions 23 times, Florida High School State
Champions 18 times, Florida High School Dual Meet State Champions 5 times,
and Florida High School State Runner-ups 8 times. In total, the Buccaneers
have been either State Champions or State Runner-ups for 26 of 29 seasons.
Additionally, the team has also enjoyed national recognition such as National
Record for "Most Takedowns in a Single Dual Meet" by Wrestling USA
Magazine (129) and "BEAT THE STREAK," when in 2008, South Dade ended
the longest winning streak in sports history (459) by handing Brandon High
School their first defeat in 34 years.
The Buccaneers team is composed of Bryan Morales, Guillermo Jaramillo, Luis
Acevedo, Nathaniel Williams, Tyree Graham, Elvis Solis, Misha Arbos, Gavin
Balmaceda, Cordell White, Lawrence Rosario, Chris Sanchez, Ansel
Cervantes, Sawyer Bartelt, and Marcelo Rosario. The team is headed by their
very talented coaches Victor Balmaceda and Humberto Reyna, both members
of the Florida Chapter of the National Wrestling Hall of Fame, under the
leadership of South Dade Senior High School’s Principal Mr. Jay De Armas.
Ms. Blanco mentioned that this was a wonderful testament not only to the
young men but to the wrestling program at South Dade Senior High School and
to the coaches that encouraged them to accomplish their international goals
with hard work, commitment, and perseverance of being successful in life.
Ms. Blanco called for the principal Mr. De Armas to share any of his comments
of his wrestling team. Mr. De Armas mentioned this was his 15th year and
seventh year as a principal at South Dade Senior High School, so he knows
the program very well. He thanked his two coaches Mr. Balmaceda and Coach
Reyna and was extremely proud of the young men not just for wrestling but for
being leaders in the school. He was overly thankful to Ms. Blanco for giving
recognition to his students and the wrestling program at his school. He also
thanked the young men for representing the school by winning every
tournament they entered and receiving medals each time.

Ms. Santos asked to have Ms. Diaz, to explain some of the changes we have
made, for example when it comes to reading, we have different programs to
use such as the new FAST testing. Could you explain how we may have shifted
the way we use platforms like I-Ready in conjunction with the FAST testing to
alleviate any duplication that may have existed.
Ms. Diaz explained that the state requires four different types diagnostic
assessments of classroom assessments which are screenings, progress
monitoring, diagnostics, and summative assessments that are outlined in the
plan. To ensure that we are providing these multiple opportunities for our
students we utilize different assessments. The FAST is our primary or our main
progress monitoring assessment which is administered three times per year
but through our different core and supplemental materials, we also have
additional progress monitoring which could be weekly in the lower grades or
short little assessments that they take to show mastery in the earlier grades,
and biweekly in the upper grades. I-Ready is utilized as the primary as a
diagnostic because the FAST assesses grade level content and only reveals
student performance within the grade level band. Through I-Ready we can
detect if there were any deficiencies early on in previous years, not within the
grade band. Ms. Diaz continued by stating that the district also utilizes
assessments for example, students who are in tier two secondary would be
those students utilizing the Read 180 program in a separate course, not in core,
but in their intensive reading class, they take scheduled i-Ready and Read 180
assessments as they go through the program again to provide different types
of data. The reason for the different types of assessments is that they give us
different information following the four categories required by the state.
