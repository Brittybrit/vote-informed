WalkSafe incorporated interactive presentations, hands-on activities, and
engaged materials to teach children about pedestrian safety in an age-
appropriate manner. The program has reached over 62,800 Miami-Dade
County Public Schools (M-DCPS) students and over 1,700 adults this

In addition to education, WalkSafe collaborated with schools, local
organizations, and law enforcement agencies to improve infrastructure and
implement safety measures near our schools. The program included
initiatives such as enhanced crosswalks, traffic calming measures, and
improved signage to increase pedestrian safety.
Furthermore, WalkSafe conducts research to assess the effectiveness of
their interventions and identified areas for improvement. By collecting data
and the evaluated impact of the program, WalkSafe aimed to continuously
enhance pedestrian safety initiatives and promote evidence-based
practices.
The WalkSafe program is currently expanding its efforts through a
comprehensive pedestrian safety initiative aimed at providing virtual
education to middle school students. This project, in its developmental
stages, offers modules tailored specifically to middle school students,
aiming to reach a broader audience and reinforce crucial pedestrian safety
practices for youths and adolescents. Through engaged and interactive
virtual lessons, students will learn about the health and mental benefits of
walking, safe walking behaviors, and pedestrian safety, empowering them
to make informed decisions while navigating their surroundings.

Ms. Luisa Santos asked staff, what must a student do in order to earn the
Seal of Biliteracy?
Ms. Lourdes Diaz responded that the student must take four (4) courses
consecutively in the same language. (i.e. Spanish 1, 2, 3, 4 or French 1, 2,
3, 4). To get the Gold seal the student must have a score of 4 or higher on
the English Language Arts (ELA). Another way to earn the seal is by getting
a qualifying score on a recognized exam (i.e. AP exam, CLEP exam,
International Baccalaureate, the ACE exam and SAT subject test). For each
of those exams there are different scores. Depending on the score the
student can receive either a gold or silver seal in biliteracy. It can be
received by the student taking the courses, testing, or a combination of both.
Ms. Santos asked has this procedure always been in effect?
Ms. Diaz responded she was not sure if it has always been in effect because
it became a statute in 2016.
Ms. Santos asked how are students informed about the Florida Gold and
Silver Seal of Biliteracy Program, and what accounts for the students that
are eligible versus the students that actually get the seal?
Ms. Diaz responded the students are advised through their counselors and
their foreign language teachers. Schools host assemblies to advise the
students of the different scholarship opportunities and the different diploma
designations. What can make a student that was eligible become ineligible
is a drop in their GPA (3.0 is required) or an assessment score.
Ms. Santos followed up asking if a student qualifies for a seal is it given
automatically?
Ms. Diaz responded that there is a report on the Principal’s Portal that
advises the school counselors and registrars or whomever is in charge of
this program at the school, it will advise them who is eligible and who earned
the seal, in turn the staff member in charge would enter the code in the
system so that the student is awarded with the seal.

scores for that school year.
