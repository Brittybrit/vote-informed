Continuing, she said that Winston Elliott Scott, a retired United States (U.S.) Navy Captain, aviator, and astronaut, was born in Miami (Coconut Grove), Florida, and graduated from Coral Gables Senior High School. At Gables High, he was a member of the band and is 51244500B-3 Cont’d.

remembered as one of the school’s best trumpet players. After graduation, he went on to college and received a Bachelor of Arts degree in music from Florida State University (FSU).

After graduating from FSU, he entered the Naval Aviation Officers Candidate School in 1973 completing an extensive flight training and was designated a naval aviator in 1974. Captain Scott later earned his Master of Science degree in aeronautical engineering with avionics from the U. S. Naval Postgraduate School in 1980.

During his Navy career, Captain Scott served as a helicopter pilot with Helicopter Anti- Submarine Warfare Squadron Light Thirty-Three at the Naval Air Station (NAS) North Island, California, flying the SH-2F helicopter. He later served a tour of duty as a fighter pilot with Fighter Squadron Eighty-Four at the NAS Oceana, Virginia, flying the F-14 Tomcat fighter/interceptor. He subsequently served as production test pilot and engineering director at the Naval Aviation Depot at NAS Jacksonville, Florida, as a research development test and evaluation (RDTE) pilot and Deputy Director of the Tactical Aircraft Systems Department in Warminster, Pennsylvania. As a RDTE pilot, Captain Scott experienced the F-14 Tomcat, the F/A-18 Hornet, and the A-7 Corsair aircraft. He has accumulated more than 7,000 hours of flight time in more than 24 different military and civilian aircraft and more than 200 shipboard landings.

Captain Scott was selected to become an astronaut by NASA in 1992. Serving as a mission specialist, he logged over 24 days, 14 hours, and 34 minutes in space including three spacewalks totaling over 19 hours and 26 minutes.

Captain Scott retired from NASA and the U. S. Navy in 1999 to accept a position at his alma matter, Florida State University, as Vice President for Student Affairs. He then served as Director of the Florida Space Authority. His responsibilities included the development of space-related industry and economic initiatives.

He represented the State’s interests in the development of space policies and programs and advised the governor and lieutenant governor on all civil, commercial, and military space matters. Captain Scott urged then Governor Jeb Bush to dissolve and combine the three state space organizations: the Florida Space Authority, the Florida Aerospace Finance Corp, and the Florida Space Research Institute, into a single organization. The combination became what is now Space Florida.

Captain Scott subsequently served as the Dean of the College of Aeronautics and Senior Vice President for External Relations and Economic Development at the Florida Institute of Technology in Melbourne, Florida. Semi-retired, Captain Scott currently serves as Senior Advisor to the President of the Florida Institute of Technology. Captain Scott’s book about his experiences in space, Reflections from Earth Orbit, was published in 2005.

Ms. Lucia Baez-Geller, Board Member; Ms. Perla Tabares Hantman, Chair; Dr. Steve Gallon III, Vice Chair; Dr. Dorothy Bendross-Mindingall, Dr. Marta Pérez, Ms. Christi Fraga, and Ms. Luisa Santos, School Board members; proposed that The School Board of Miami-Dade County, Florida, approve Resolution No. 21-047 of The School Board of Miami-Dade County, Florida, recognizing the Adaptive Beach Days Program. [A copy of Resolution No. 21-047 is on file with the official records of this meeting.]

Ms. Baez-Geller expressed honor to present this resolution. She said that Adaptive Beach Days Program helps people with disabilities visit the beach and enjoy the ocean. Adaptive Beach Days Program, which launched in 2016, has re-started its efforts to host participants on Miami Beach where volunteers transform a section of the beach making it accessible to those who use wheelchairs. The programs will be held every second Sunday of each month.

Activities include art, aqua, dance and music therapy, adaptive water sports, yoga, and meditation classes. Also, a resource library will be available. Sabrina Cohen created the Foundation that organizes the events for the Adaptive Beach Days Program. Cohen gained greater insight into the needs of people with disabilities after she suffered a C-5 spinal cord injury in a 1992 car accident when she was only 14 years old and a sophomore at Miami Beach Senior High School. She has been confined to a wheelchair since the day of that accident.

The Sabrina Cohen Foundation is spearheading the construction of a three-story wellness center that will store equipment for the beach program, and will include a gym, pool and science robotics lab.

The Adaptive Beach Days program is truly making a difference in our community and bringing sunshine to individuals with disabilities who were especially hard hit during the pandemic as volunteers were unable to help them go outdoors due to social distancing requirements. The School Board applauds the Adaptive Beach Days initiative.

The idea to rename the Highway came from Isabella Banos, the sixteen-year-old granddaughter of Mr. Modesto Abety (former CEO/President of The Children’s Trust), who was driving her to a friend’s house one day. Isabella heard the GPS in the car saying turn on South Dixie Highway and wondered why a name that symbolized slavery was still being used? She suggested to her grandfather Mr. Abety to rename it Harriet Tubman Highway.

Mr. Abety then decided to send an email to the 13 Miami-Dade Commissioners, asking them to change Old Dixie Highway to Harriet Tubman Highway. Mr. Abety wrote: “We’ve got to change this” “I hope you agree.” Commissioner Moss responded and took the lead to make sure that Mr. Abety and Isabella’s vision would come to fruition. On September 18, 2021, Old Dixie Highway was renamed Harriet Tubman Highway.

Harriett Tubman was an abolitionist, political activist, and a former slave who risked her life more than thirteen times to lead others to freedom. She was known as the “Moses” of her people, helping others gain freedom as she mobilized the Underground Railroad as the “conductor.” Harriet Tubman was never caught and never lost a passenger. She was the fearless freedom fighter who liberated hundreds of slaves.

H-14
Dr. Steve Gallon III, Vice Chair; Ms. Perla Tabares Hantman, Chair; Dr. Dorothy Bendross-Mindingall, Ms. Christi Fraga, Dr. Lubby Navarro, Ms. Lucia Baez-Geller, Dr. Marta Pérez, Ms. Maria Teresa "Mari Tere" Rojas, and Ms. Luisa Santos, School Board members; proposed that The School Board of Miami-Dade County, Florida, direct the Superintendent to:

review current district curriculum in elementary, middle, and senior high schools that teach financial literacy concepts and skills;
identify and list “standalone” courses that teach financial literacy concepts and skills presently offered in middle and senior high schools;
explore the feasibility of offering financial literacy courses as an elective to students in middle and senior high schools, specifically those schools that do not offer deliberate, intentional instruction in financial literacy for the 2022-2023 school year;
conduct a survey of middle and senior high school students that will provide:

an initial measure of financial literacy proficiency;
an initial measure of interest in specific financial literacy courses;
the establishment of a baseline and benchmarks for future financial literacy courses; and
a description of the levels of financial literacy in terms of key socio-economic groups and explanatory variables that will enable M-DCPS to identify students with the greatest needs and gaps in the provision of financial literacy resources;

explore and engage partnerships with business and organization to provide seminars, sessions, and/or workshops to students on the key concepts and skills of financial literacy including but not limited to personal financial management, budgeting, investing, credit, and debt; and
provide an update to the School Board at the Academics, Innovation, and Technology Committee Meeting.

Chair Hantman introduced this item.

Student Advisor Cori’Anna expressed gratitude to Dr. Gallon for proffering this item, for she felt it was very important; financial literacy just to be provided with the students at this school district. Further, she said she could definitely say that a lot of the traits that students exhibit is reflected the same in adulthood. Therefore, she felt that if they were provided with these resources at a young age, they will be able to utilize them as adults.

Ms. Santos thanked Dr. Gallon for this very important topic and requested to become a co-sponsor of this item. 

Upon motion being made by Dr. Gallon and seconded by Ms. Rojas, the Board voted 6-0 to approve co-sponsorship of this item. [It is noted for the record that Dr. Pérez, Dr. Navarro and Ms. Fraga were away from the dais when this vote was taken.]
