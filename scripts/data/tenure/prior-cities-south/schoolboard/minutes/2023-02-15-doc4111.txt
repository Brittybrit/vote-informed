The Chair presented the item.
Dr. Gallon began his presentation by saying that it is his distinct honor,
pleasure, and privilege to serve as the conduit to present and celebrate
the 2023 Wilson Scholars that represent an outstanding number of young
men from Florida City to County Line Road.
Dr. Gallon mentioned the 5000 Role Models of Excellence Project was
founded in 1993 by then Miami-Dade County School Board Member, Dr.
Frederica S. Wilson, a steadfast supporter of improving public education,
and now a member of the U.S. House of Representatives. A dropout
prevention/mentoring program, the mission of the 5000 Role Models of
Excellence Project is to intervene in the lives of at-risk youth and provide
them with alternatives to dropping out of school and self-destructive
behaviors. The program pairs male students, ages 9-19, with successful
professional adult mentors, who provide them with advice, guidance, and
educational assistance. The 5000 Role Models of Excellence Project
serves more than 8,000 students in 120 chapters in Miami-Dade County.
Chapters are also located throughout Florida - Pinellas County, Duval
County, Broward County and in Detroit, Michigan.
This year, Wilson Scholars includes students from the following senior
high schools: Alonzo & Tracy Mourning, Booker T. Washington, Coral
Gables, Coral Reef, DASH, G. Holmes Braddock, Miami Beach, Miami
Carol City, Miami Coral Park, Miami Lakes Educational Center, Miami
Northwestern, North Miami, North Miami Beach, and William H. Turner
Technical Arts High School. Since the inception of the program, over $50
million in scholarships have been awarded to graduating seniors. This
year we honor 49 graduating seniors. Dr. Gallon mentioned as a former
principal it is important to take note there is a difference between seniors
and graduating seniors and asked the audience to recognize the hard
work of a graduating senior!

Dr. Marvin Dunn is a nationally respected scholar, author, historian,
documentary filmmaker, and community activist. His lifelong work has
been dedicated to bringing an empowering perspective to Black history in
Florida, especially in Miami-Dade, that has been missed, distorted, and
minimized in the past. He is professor emeritus and retired chairperson of
the Department of Psychology at Florida International University. Dr. Dunn
is a renowned expert on race and ethnic issues in America. Most recently,
Dr. Dunn founded the Miami Center for Racial Justice in 2020, where he
now serves as the non-profit’s president. The Miami Center for Racial
Justice's purpose is to tell and preserve the true Black history of Florida,
especially those stories that are difficult to hear.
Ms. Nadege Green is a powerful writer, independent researcher, and
community historian born and raised in Miami. A child of Haitian
immigrants and former farmworkers, she is the founder of Black Miami-
Dade, a history and storytelling platform that resists the erasure of Miami’s
Black past. Her practice and approach to storytelling is deeply rooted in
history and first-person narratives that explore and connect issues around
race, culture, climate justice, and displacement. She is the editor of the
newly released book “More Than What Happened: The Aftermath of Gun
Violence in Miami”—a community anthology of essays, poems, prose and
photo essays by Miami youth and adults who have been impacted by gun
violence and threats of gun violence. She amplifies the voices of the Black
community in Miami-Dade, giving our students the opportunity to see
themselves as part of the narrative.

a bachelor's degree from Howard University and a Juris Doctorate from
the Thurgood Marshall School of Law. First elected to the Florida House
