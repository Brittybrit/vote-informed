Vice Chair Gallon introduced this item.
In her introduction, Dr. Bendross-Mindingall said that as Miami-Dade
County Public Schools continued to transition back to in-person
instruction for some (giving parents a choice between face-to-face
instruction and virtual learning from home), making sure that the health
and safety of all students, teachers, and personnel was paramount.
Around the worlds many school Districts were asking for teachers and
school personnel to be prioritized for the Covid-19 vaccine; some
teachers in other states had already received the vaccine.
Continuing, she expressed concern that the State of Florida had not
prioritized teachers as a category to receive the vaccine. In Section 3
of the Florida COVID-19 Vaccination Plan, it is stated that “(t)he goal of
the Florida COVID-19 Vaccination Plan is to develop and update, as
needed, a plan for vaccinations as guidance is provided by the CDC.
Prioritization of vaccine recipients is not yet determined by the CDC.
Priority groups may vary based on the vaccine that is ultimately
approved, vaccine availability and the groups it is authorized for. Based
upon current federal guidance, it is expected that initial priority groups
will include health care personnel, essential workers, persons with
medical conditions that place them at high-risk for COVID-19
complications, and older adults (≥65 years of age).” Public schools
have been struggling to bring students back into the classroom and, in
many places, school buildings have had to shut down again as Covid-
19 has ravaged our communities, infecting over one million children.
The alliance pointed out how these shutdowns profoundly affect our
families as well as our local economies. If school personnel could be
vaccinated early in the process, school buildings could even serve as
vaccination centers for the general public, as schools are a centerpiece
of almost every community and served important roles during the mass
polio vaccinations. This could help a vaccination program reach its
goals more quickly and effectively. The goal of the Florida Department
of Education and the Board is to return to some type of normalcy and
get all our students and personnel back to brick-and-mortar safely.
Concluding, she read the recommended action of this item.

• Diversity Equity and Excellence Advisory Committee (DEEAC) staff
follow-up from December 18, 2020 on Agenda Item H-6 from
October 2, 2019 outlining DEEAC responsibility. The action directed
the Superintendent to work with DEEAC to prepare a
comprehensive updated report, pursuant to Policy 9142.
Emphasized that all areas delineated in Board action are to be
included in this report, and in a timely fashion.
- Superintendent Carvalho agreed to follow-up with an estimated
date upon checking with staff.
- Dr. Gallon indicated he looks forward to this report as well, as it
is required annually, and DEEAC is now noncompliant for 10-
years.
- Superintendent Carvalho and Mr. Torrens indicated to the Board
that the report was drafted nad pending final approval by
DEEAC. There had been significant difficulty in getting quorum
for that committee to meet. With new Board member appointees,
they hoped to accomplish that in short order.
- Dr. Bendross-Mindingall further inquired, for clarity, as to
whether the report had been received; to which Mr. Torrens
restarted his response. He said the report was drafted; however,
the policy requires the report to be voted on by the Committee.
The report is from the committee to the Board, staff facilitates it.
- Ms. Santos addressed the issues this committee was having
with meeting quorum. She encouraged her colleagues to check
on their appointees to ensure they were still active and able to
join.
- Ms. Rojas commented on her appointee’s involvement and
expressed pride in their participation, for the record.
Superintendent Carvalho recognized Ms. Valtena Brown for her
decades of service to the School System. He said she was a friend, a
trusted advisor, an icon in this community. She has been a stalwart
example of integrity, intelligence, perseverance, powerful decency,
humanity, and understanding. Concluding, he said MDCPS was a
superior school system because of people like Ms. Brown and
individuals she has groomed. She is a great leader, mother,
grandmother, wife, and has contributed so much to this school system.
Chair Hantman expressed her utmost respect for Ms. Brown and
wished her many blessings.; as well as appreciation to the Members of
the Board and staff for a successful meeting.
