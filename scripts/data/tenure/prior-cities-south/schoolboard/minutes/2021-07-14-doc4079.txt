B-3 CONT’DThe Guinness Book of World Records lists “Sabado Gigante” (Giant
Saturday) as the longest televised variety program in history—airing for
52 years. Since 1962, Sabado Gigante’s creator and only host, Mario
Kreutzberger is known as Don Francisco. He became an integral
member of many households of all backgrounds building connections
across generations and even helping some viewers learn Spanish.
Don Francisco delivered joy, music, company, and information while
encouraging family values and fun for audiences.
In 1978, Mr. Kreutzberger wanted to further serve the public. That year
he created the first “Teleton” (Telethon) in South America. For 43 years
he hosts this TV charity show for disabled Chilean children, raising
more than a billion dollars. He then helped create 14 more “Teletons”
(Telethons) and is the Hispanic spokesperson for the US Muscular
Dystrophy Association. For his important social work, the United
Nations Organization for children conferred to him the title of
Ambassador of UNICEF; and since 2012, Mario Kreutzberger has
been Vice-President of the Muscular Dystrophy Association in the
United States.
During his career, Mr. Kreutzberger has received numerous awards
and honors, including a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame, the Papal
Benemerenti Medal awarded by Pope John Paul II, and an Emmy. His
name is in the Hall of Fame of the Academy of Arts and Sciences of
Television in the United States. He recently completed his fourth
bestselling book, Con Ganas de Vivir (With Desire to Live) where he
describes some of his amazing interviews, his family history, his
success, challenges, motivations, and much more.
On behalf of the School Board, Dr. Perez congratulated Mr.
Kreutzberger and presented him with this proclamation. In return, Mr.
Kreutzberger addressed the Board to express appreciation for this
recognition.

B-5
Ms. Lucia Baez-Geller, Board Member, and Ms. Perla Tabares
Hantman, Chair; Dr. Steve Gallon, III, Vice Chair; Ms. Christi Fraga,
Dr. Marta Perez, Ms. Maria Teresa Rojas, and Luisa Santos, School
Board Members, proposed that The School Board of Miami-Dade
County, Florida, approve Resolution No. 21-031 of The School Board
of Miami-Dade County, Florida, recognizing Maika and Maritza Moulite.
[A copy of Resolution No. 21-031 is on file with the official records of
this meeting.]
Ms. Baez-Geller introduced Maika and Maritza Moulite. They were a
sister-writer duo who authored Dear Haiti, Love Alaine and One of the
Good Ones. In the young adult fiction novel Dear Haiti, Love Alaine, a
school presentation winds up going awry and as a result the main
character is sent to Haiti where she writes a memorable report. In the
book One of the Good Ones, the main character is struggling to cope
with the death of her older sibling and embarks on a journey to
commemorate her in this story.
Maika and Maritza are daughters of Haitian immigrants, both sisters
are Miami natives and attended Miami-Dade County Public Schools.
Maika is focused on media portrayals and its impact on marginalized
groups, while Maritza is exploring ways to improve literacy in under-
resourced communities. The Moulite sisters have appeared both
virtually and in-person at Books & Books, the Miami Book Fair, MDC
Live Arts Lab and through other virtual and social media platforms
including Instagram, Zoom and YouTube.
Earlier this year, Maika and Maritza visited North Miami Beach Senior
High School (NMB) where their dream of becoming published authors
began after a visit to NMB from fellow alumnus, and New York Times
best-selling author Brad Meltzer in 2004. That year, when Meltzer
visited and made donations to the school, he inspired the Moulite
sisters who were students sitting in the auditorium to hear him speak.
Currently, both sisters are working towards earning doctoral degrees.
Maika is attending Howard University after earning a bachelor’s in
marketing at Florida State University, and an MBA from the University
of Miami. Maritza is attending the University of Pennsylvania. Prior to U
Penn, she earned a bachelor’s in women’s studies from the University
of Florida and a master’s in journalism from the University of Southern
California. In a Channel 10 interview in early February, Maritza’s
positive message captured the essence of both sisters’ outlook on life
when she said, “you can do whatever you want, just work hard at it,
make a plan, reach out to the people who have inspired you, and there
is a chance that they will help you in your journey to what you want to
do.”
On behalf of the School Board, Ms. Baez-Geller commended Maika
and Maritza and presented them with this proclamation. In return,
Maritza and Maika addressed the Board to express appreciation for
this recognition.
