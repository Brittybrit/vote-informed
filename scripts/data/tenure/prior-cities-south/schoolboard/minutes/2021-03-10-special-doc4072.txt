Public Hearing: “Adoption of District-Wide Instructional Materials”
Ms. Perla Tabares Hantman, School Board Chair, called the Public
Hearing to order at 10:13 a.m., and Ms. Fraga led those in attendance
in The Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag of the United States of America.
The Board then held a moment of silent meditation.
The Chair invited anyone from the audience who had not already signed
up and wished to address Agenda Item SP-1 to proceed to the lobby
counter outside the auditorium for assistance from staff.
School Board Attorney Walter Harvey was recognized to explain the
procedures to be followed during today’s public hearing. Mr. Harvey
explained this was the School Board’s open notice public hearing to
receive public comment on instructional materials being recommended
to the Board for adoption, pursuant to Board Policy 2510, Instructional
Materials and Resources, in agreement with Section 1006 of the Florida
Education Code. This hearing was established as part of the process of
the District’s review, evaluation and selection of non-state adopted
instructional materials.
Mr. Harvey reviewed Board Policy 0169.1, Public Participation at Board
Meetings and indicated that the public would be allowed to comment
regarding the proposed adoption of the listed materials. After hearing
the speakers and answering any questions, the Board should consider
Agenda Item SP-1, memorializing and confirming that the Board has
received public comment regarding the proposed instructional materials
to be adopted in accordance with Florida law and Board policy. After
the vote, the hearing may be adjourned.

Following this meeting, the instructional materials recommended for final
adoption may be presented to the School Board at its March 17, 2021
Regular School Board meeting. These materials would be available for
use during the 2021-2022 school year.
The Chair relinquished the floor to Superintendent Carvalho for opening
remarks.
Superintendent Carvalho said that the instructional materials that would
be discussed today were selected by committees made up of M-DCPS
classroom teachers, who are certified and teach in the subject areas
being considered for adoption. The materials are adopted on a five-year
cycle, per FLDOE. The subject areas covered by this adoption are:
• K-12 English Language Arts (ELA),
• K-12 Spanish for Spanish Speakers, and
• Advanced Placement (AP) courses in English Language, English
Literature, and Spanish Literature
The materials selected received the highest scores and reflect the
committee’s highest choices. The review of materials being considered
today followed the review and adoption procedures established by
Florida Statute 1006.283 (8), which governs the adoption of instructional
materials by each district’s School Board. The steps outlined in the
States adoption procedures are reflected in the Districts instructional
material adoption timeline. They allow for a smooth and seamless
process from the selection of the review committees to the public
hearing to approve materials up for adoption by the Board. Continuing,
he outlined the opportunities for the public to review the materials and
provide input; student editions of the material were made available for
public review; a public hearing is held to receive public comment; and a
public meeting is held to approve the materials. Concluding, expressed
gratitude towards the Chair and members of the Board.
Dr. Sylvia Diaz, Chief Academic Officer, was recognized to explain the
adoption process of the instructional materials and list the titles being
recommended. She noted that petitions from a parent of a public-school
student in M-DCPS or a resident of the county to contest the School

The Chair then called on Members of the Board to discuss Agenda Item
SP-1. [A verbatim recording of this discussion is on file in the Office of
the Board Recording Secretary.]
[Dr. Perez assumed her seat at the dais during this portion of the
meeting.]
Ms. Rojas expressed gratitude towards the Superintendent staff for the
presentation and the information provided to them. She then raised
concerns regarding one book title that was removed and asked Dr. Diaz
to address it.
Dr. Diaz explained that the book in question was ¡Qué chévere! from
Carnegie learning, it was the title for AP Spanish Language. The teacher
committee that selected the title had requested that the publisher make
changes to four pages; the publisher did not make those changes in time
for public hearing, as a result, the book was withdrawn from
consideration at this time. The committee will reconsider the book next
year, assuming the publisher makes the changes requested. The book
was not been replaced at this time.
Ms. Rojas raised questions with regards to:
• Whether Savvas Learning Company was new.
This is the company that purchased Pearson Education. [Diaz]
• Whether the books from Spain had been fully vetted, as were those
from the U.S.
All books are fully vetted and align to the standards. [Diaz]
Ms. Santos thanked Dr. Diaz and Ms. Alves for previously answering her
detailed set of question. She then raised inquires, as follows:
• Basis for deciding which text series was appropriate for K-5 Dual
Language Spanish versus world language Spanish.
Dual Language programs are more rigorous in scope than a World
Language Spanish course; they are double the amount of time; they
promote bilingualism and biliteracy by maintain the native language
while adding another language at the same time. Students receive
academic content instruction; they learn content in both English and
the targeted language. World Language courses do not teach
academic content through the targeted language; they are simply
learning to speak, read, and write that language. [Alves]
• Whether the World Language series prepared students that test out
of their first year and go directly to the second year, in the upper
grade levels.
• Teacher manual for the Dual Language and EFL materials to support
teachers who may not have preparation for Spanish Language
instruction.
All materials come with teacher editions. Additionally, they are
provided with ongoing Professional Development specifically
designed for those new teachers entering the EFL programs. [Alves]

• MJ Spanish for Spanish Speakers (6-8); is it Spain centric?
Yes. The textbook is produced in Spain. [Alves]
Ms. Baez-Geller raised concerns with regards to striking a balance
between physical textbook and digital.
Dr. Diaz explained that these books were selected with the idea that
instruction could be in the physical classrooms or remote. The
committees were careful to select series that could accomplish both.
She acknowledged the need for print material for the younger grade
levels. She also noted that he digital companions for all these were an
improvement from what was available in the past.
Dr. Pérez asked whether any of the books were consumable and how
often they needed to be changed; to which Dr. Diaz responded that
consumables were part of the purchase and that although is was a 5-
year adoption cycle, the books for the subjects being considered today
had been in the district for longer because that because the State
changed the adoption timeline with the Best Standards.
Continuing, Dr. Perez noted that other countries did not change
textbooks so often because the instruction doesn’t change; books were
smaller and were covered in its entirety. She hoped the State would
allow textbooks to be used for longer.
At the request of the Chair, Dr. Diaz further clarified that the titles being
adopted for elementary ELA were the updated version of the current
series in use, as teachers were very fond of it. She noted that the new
edition enhanced and addressed some of the shortcomings teachers
found with the current series and included some of the things teachers
were having to supplement. This was not an option for secondary ELA,
as the current series was discontinued.
Dr. Gallon raised the following inquiries and concerns:
• Blended learning instructional programs/platforms being utilized for
Spanish instruction. Specifically, digitals learning resources for
enrichment and intervention in Dual language and other immersion
type programs. [Alves]
The current textbook series had digital components. [Diaz]
Dual Language courses also use the digital resources of the content
area adoptions. Supplemental apps are also available to students in
through the portal. [Alves]
• Stand-alone instructional technology program to enhance instruction
in Spanish and provide intervention.
Instructional technology resources available are tied to the content
and the curriculum taught in those courses. [Alves]
• Availability of online blended learning programs for teachers to utilize
through differentiated instruction for Spanish language acquisition.
Supplemental programs are available in the portal to teachers and
students for small group instruction, for example, Dual Lingo,
Imagine Learning, and Achieve 3000. They are suggested and linked
through the pacing guides where they can be utilized more
relevantly. [Alves]
