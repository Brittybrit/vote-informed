Ms. Rojas, Chair, began her presentation by thanking the staff and
students for allowing her the opportunity to recognize MAST Academy for
being the first Florida high school to receive the Gold Apple Environmental
Award.
On behalf of the Principal, Dr. Cadian Collman-Perez, the Assistant
Principal, Dr. Michael Gould, faculty members Mr. Alan Miller, Lynn
Paisley and some of the Green Champion students came before the
Board to accept MAST Academy recognition.
Ms. Rojas informed the listening audience that MAST Academy was
founded in 1991 at a building which was once the local Planet Ocean
Museum. MAST Academy school’s mission provides a marine setting and
nurturing environment for students leading to academic success, career
preparation, and appreciation of the sea and environmental awareness.
In 2015, MAST added a 6th through the 8
th
-grade program and a
Cambridge Advanced Program of Studies that tripled the student body
size and doubled the school’s square footage with a large construction
project. Aside from the increased capacity efficiency, improvements
resulted in a reduction in electricity consumption by almost two-thirds. In
July 2019, MAST requested and obtained an energy efficiency audit from
Florida Power and Light. That report served as the baseline for MAST
energy efficiency goals. The Parent Teacher School Association
established the MAST PTSA Sustainability Committee to introduce green
infrastructure and strengthen hands-on sustainability activities such as
organic gardens, school-wide recycling, Bayshore beach cleanups, and
coastal restoration projects. In 2018, the PTSA partnered with the Phillip
and Patricia Frost Museum of Science to create a Green Champions
program that acknowledges MAST students at the end-of-the-year
ceremony for committed community service hours towards sustainability
efforts. The Green Champions have won an environmental award every
year since 2017, including being the recipient of the United States
Department of Education Green Ribbon Schools Award. This past
January of 2023, Mast Academy was recognized by the Florida
Department of Environmental Protection's Florida Green Schools program
with a Gold Destination and the Gold Apple Award. State schools have
the opportunity to achieve different Apple levels throughout a four-tier
designation system. Green, bronze, silver and gold Apple which is the
highest designation. MAST Academy is the first high school in Florida to
receive the Gold Apple Award, which was made possible by the Green
champions. A group of committed students, teachers, parents, and
community partners who have stepped up to do their part to make MAST
Academy a zero-waste and zero-net energy school by 2030. The Florida
Green School designation program is an initiative launched in 2016 that
recognizes the K through 12th-grade schools that make a voluntary
commitment to conserve and protect Florida's natural resources and
demonstrate environmental improvements. The Green champions were
acknowledged for making progress in each of the Gold Apple focus areas,
communications, waste reduction, air quality, alternative transportation,
gardens, and energy efficiency. Congratulations to the principal Dr.
Cadian Collman, MAST faculty, and staff, PTSA, and the amazing
students at MAST Academy for their efforts and continuous engagement
and commitment to conserve and protect their natural resources and
improve the environment. In addition, Ms. Rojas personally thanked Ms.
Michelle Drucker whom she had known since the onset of being a School
Board member in 2016. She expressed that Ms. Drucker is very bright,
committed, tenacious, and will never take no for an answer, but most
importantly, to the children, the future leaders of tomorrow.

Ms. Baez-Geller thanked the prominent men and women who spoke on
the item and this important issue. She expressed that she is very proud
and honored with their presence at this Board meeting. Miami-Dade
County Public Schools system prides itself on being equitable and being
at the forefront of these issues. The ability of not being able to just say
things in policy but to also act upon them. In this case they take pride in
adhering to federal, state, and local laws regarding anti-discrimination
policies. These are laws that they not only follow, but that want businesses
with whom they do business with to follow. They expect to hold the people
with whom they work to the highest standard. Ms. Baez-Geller said that
her concern and the reason for bringing up this item is that that despite
the laws, despite their stances on antidiscrimination, there is still an issue
with equal pay for equal work in Miami-Dade County. It is her hope that
for those entities that do business with the school district will be able to
adhere to the Equal Pay Act. Which is why she would like for the
Superintendent to direct district staff to review and to conduct a feasibility
study regarding equal pay clauses for their vendors. Also, establish that
with anyone with whom they work that they are paying men and women
equally for equal work. Currently, we ask the people with whom we do
business with to adhere to several provisions. She asked them to certify
that they are ADA compliant for people with disabilities, hire locally, hire
people in state instead of people from out of state, comply with the Jessica
Lunsford Act, and make sure that everybody wears their identification
badge, and has been background checked. Sometimes we ask them this
stuff informally, sometimes we ask them to sign an affidavit, but either way
we ask them to comply. So, we're asking for one more thing for them to
be addressed, which is for them to state that they pay men and women
equally for equal work. She thinks it's an important step for their institution
and think it sends out a message to the community that M-DCPS values
and their commitment is tied to the work that they do on the dais as a
School Board. Hearing about the negative impact economic impact that
paying women has on the community. It is her hope that the district
through this feasibility study, sets the tone and the standard to improve
the quality of life for their students by bettering the quality of life of their
mothers.
