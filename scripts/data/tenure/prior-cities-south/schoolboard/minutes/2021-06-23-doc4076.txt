11:00 A.M. - PRESENTATION OF RESOLUTIONS
The Chair called the meeting to order at 11:04 a.m., and Dr. Pérez led
those in attendance in The Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag of the United
States of America. The Board then held a moment of silent meditation,
but before doing so, she said she, alongside the Board, offered heartfelt
sympathy for the sorrow former Board Member Dr. Karp and his family
must be feeling after the loss of their dear mother. She then assured Dr.
Karp that the Board was there for him during this painful time and hoped
that their presences provided him with some comfort and solace to ease
his suffering.
Additionally, she said that at the May 19, 2021 School Board Meeting,
Agenda Item H-6, proffered by Ms. Baez-Geller, was approved. This
item authorized the Superintendent to observe a moment of silence at
today’s meeting in remembrance of the employees and students of
Miami-Dade County Public Schools who have passed away during the
school year. As sponsor of the item, she asked that Ms. Baez-Geller
present the commemoration. The Board then held a moment of silent
meditation for Dr. Karp and family. Following, Ms. Baez-Geller honored
all those in the M-DCPS family; students, faculty and staff, who were
loss during this school year with a moment of silence.
The Chair informed the public that pursuant to Board policies, proposed
Board member resolutions would be presented at the 11:00 a.m.
session; and, upon vote being taken, the Board would recess to present
the approved resolutions. The Board would then reconvene its Regular
public meeting, including other hearings, in accordance with the policies
of the Board, at 1:00 p.m., or soon thereafter.

Dr. Bendross-Mindingall expressed honor and pleasure to present this
proclamation which she felt could not ever do all that she can and all
that she should for this wonderful group. The Silent Victims of Crime
Program was founded by Neil and Paulette “Tinkerbell” Pfeiffer in South
Carolina. They have committed to this program for over 25 years.
The Children of Incarcerated Parents is a 501(c)(3) non-profit
organization that is committed to changing the lives of the children of
inmates and providing them with the support and resources they need
to succeed.
The mission of Silent Victims of Crime (SVC) is to assist children of
inmates by enhancing their ability to improve their economic,
educational, and personal stability while addressing behavioral, social
and mental issues. By helping these at-risk children to deal with life
challenges in a positive, proactive manner empowers them to make wise
choices and contribute positively to society.
Silent Victims of Crime has a “5 Circles of Support” methodology to
accomplish their vision of breaking the intergenerational cycle of
incarceration, created by their founder in 1996 and based on a
collaboration between families and communities who share a common
goal. The five circles are youth empowerment programs, parent
programs, caregiver support, community collaboration, and mentoring
and peer support.
The Board of Silent Victims of Crime consists of seven members, all of
whom are chosen to represent the best interest of SVC. The 2021 Board
of Directors consists of Board Chairman David Massey, Board Treasurer
Neal Pfeiffer, Board Secretary Sandra Joseph, and Board Members Bill
Ghitis, Julian Johnston, Dr. Dorothy Bendross-Mindingall, and Tim Carr.
Silent Victims of Crime and their Board of Directors has made countless
contributions to the community over the course of the organization’s
inception. SVC has empowered millions of children of incarcerated
parents in both South Carolina and Florida through programs of
educational enrichment, social support, and positive mentoring.
Ms. Paulette “Tinkerbell” Pfeiffer and Mr. Neil Pfeiffer appeared before
the Board to address this item.

Every year, the Miami-Dade Council of PTAs/PTSAs awards the Paul
Bell Principal of the Year Award to an outstanding principal who has
demonstrated extraordinary leadership and dedication to the vision,
mission, and the values of the PTA/PTSA at his or her school, and
exhibits outstanding commitment to the parents, students, and staff.
This year’s winner is Mr. Hebert M. Penton, Principal, Ponce de Leon
Middle School.
Mr. Penton began his teaching career as a social studies teacher at
Madison Middle School and then at Brownsville Middle School. Ten
years later, he started his administrative career as an assistant principal
at Lamar Louise Curry Middle School, and then at G. Holmes Braddock
Senior High School and Rockway Middle School. In 2017, Mr. Penton
became principal at Ponce de Leon Middle School and in 2019, Magnet
Schools of America named Ponce de Leon Middle School as one of the
top ten magnet programs in the nation. The school has received a
number of recognitions including the designation as a STEM certified
school, the Green School Designation by the Florida Department of
Agriculture, and under his leadership the school offers dual enrollment
courses to eligible eighth grades.
Mr. Penton earned a bachelor’s degree from Florida International
University and a master’s degree from Nova Southeastern University.
