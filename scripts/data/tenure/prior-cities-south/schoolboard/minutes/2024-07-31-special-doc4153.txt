 The total millage rate to be levied is .097 mills lower than FY 23/24.
Yet the millage is at the maximum allowed by law.
 Fifty-nine (59) percent of the total non-voted millage is the required
local effort which is necessary by law to receive funding from the
state.
 The Board received the Executive Summary on 7/22/24 which
includes projected revenues and appropriations for all funds. The
tentative budget includes:
1. A significant decrease in State FEFP funding is expected compared
to the 4th FEFP calculation for FY 23/24. This reduction, amounting
to $117 million, is primarily due to an increase in the deductions for
Family Empowerment Scholarships. The number of Family
Empowerment Scholarship FTEs is projected to grow by over 15,000
from FY 23/24 to FY 24/25.
2. A higher FEFP contribution coming from State funds as opposed to
coming out of the pockets of our local taxpayers due to the reduced
Required Local Effort (RLE) millage.
3. Fringes are increasing mainly due to an increase in FRS rates
mandated by legislation which increased our average FRS rate from
14.72% to 14.91%.
4. Fringes are also increasing since our actuaries are projecting an
increase in our medical insurance costs of $37M.
5. Increased funding for mental health.
6. Increase in charter-related appropriations due to the projected
increase in their FTE of over 3K and the increased share of Local
Capital Improvement Revenue as well as our Referendum funds.
7. An $87 million Reserve for Tax Roll Yield Shortfall due to lower tax
collections experienced in the past several years.
8. Contingency reserve of over $97M which protects the Board against
unknown decreases in revenue for such things as a decrease in
membership, including a higher than projected shift of students to
scholarship programs or charter schools.

 The largest decrease by far relates to the fact that most of the federal

down, we must ensure the continuity of enhanced renovated
buildings, upgraded technologies, sustainability of schools and the
safety and security of students. By featuring sustainability principles
in facility design we are aiming to create environmentally friendly
spaces that promote energy efficiency, water conservation and
waste reduction. This approach contributes to the District’s
commitment to achieving 100% clean energy use by the end of the
decade per our Infinite Possibilities Strategic Plan.
 As a result of our sound financial management, Moody’s Investors
Services ratings of the District’s GO and COPS programs remain the
same. Moody’s noted in their rating outlook the likelihood that the
District’s financial position over the next few years will remain strong.
 As our COVID Relief funding ends during FY 24/25, this underscores
the need to optimize funds utilization. Every financial decision we
make must be grounded in our commitment to our mission, vision
and core values as an organization. We will continue to prioritize
educational excellence, innovation, and safety as we bolster our
focus on early childhood education, increase proficiency levels,
address the achievement gap, and expand post-secondary
education opportunities. The safety of our students and staff will
remain a top priority to ensure we provide safe and supportive
learning environments across our District.
