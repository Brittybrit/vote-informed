The Chair presented this item:
Mr. Espino recognized St. Jude Children’s Research Hospital and asked
representatives to come forth as he provided a summary of their
accomplishments. St. Jude Children's Research Hospital (St. Jude) is a
world-renowned pediatric treatment and research facility located in
Memphis, Tennessee. Consistent with the vision of its founder Danny
Thomas, St. Jude opened its doors in 1962 with the promise that no child
would be denied treatment based on race, religion or financial resources.
The hospital's mission is to advance cures and means of prevention for
pediatric catastrophic diseases through research and treatment. St. Jude is
known for providing cutting-edge medical care to children with cancer and
other life-threatening illnesses. Most of their funding comes from individual
contributions and donors. Families never receive a bill from St. Jude for
treatment, travel, housing, or food.
St. Jude is designated a National Cancer Institute Comprehensive Cancer
Center, the first and only cancer center solely focused on pediatric cancer
to receive this distinction. Since its founding more than 60 years ago,
treatments developed at St. Jude have helped push the overall childhood
cancer survival rate from 20% to more than 80%.
September is recognized as Childhood Cancer Awareness Month. During
this time of the year, St. Jude Children’s Research Hospital and other
organizations honor children and survivors affected by pediatric cancer and
raise awareness about the need for research and treatment of the disease.
The St. Jude Walk/Run 5K hybrid event is an inspiring, family-friendly event
taking place virtually and nationwide. It unites people behind the lifesaving
mission of St. Jude: Finding cures. Saving children. Their goal is to
fundraise $300,000 for childhood cancer research.

Ms. Baez-Geller thanked all the speakers for taking the time to come out

Mrs. Beverly Heller graduated with a BBA from the City College of New York,
where she met her husband, David. She then went on to obtain a master’s
degree in education from Hunter College of New York City. In the 1970s, Mr.
and Mrs. Heller moved to Miami Beach and had three daughters, all
products of Miami-Dade County Public Schools. Aimee, Mandee, and
Cheree attended Temple Beth Sholom’s Pre-K Program, Biscayne
Elementary, North Beach Elementary, Nautilus Middle School, and Miami
Beach Senior High.
During this time and afterwards, Mrs. Heller initiated and oversaw the
Telecare Program for the Miami Beach Community Center, which helped
many elderly residents to live independently. She also taught Pre-K at
Temple Beth Sholom, as well as various grades at North Beach Elementary
School and Fienberg-Fisher K-8 and the Adult Education Center. She
served as union steward and project manager.
Mrs. Heller was awarded the Miami Beach Elaine Weisburd Teacher of the
Year Award and the North Bay Village Optimist’s Club Teacher of the Year
Award. She was then hired to work for the Office of Magnets and School
Choice, where she created the grade-level annual calendar, and instructed
other early education teachers.
Shortly after, Mrs. Heller was elected as Vice-President for the United
Teachers of Dade (UTD), representing 36,000 union members, while also
working and attending NOVA University for a Doctoral Degree in
Educational Leadership.
Now, Mrs. Heller has five grandchildren, and works part-time in the family
business, International College Counselors. She remains active in the
community and serves on various committees, advocating for students,
teachers, and families. She has served in a leadership capacity for the
Miami Beach Commission for Women, UTD Jewish Education Association,
Jewish Family Services, M-DCPS Gifted Advisory Board, M-DCPS Magnet
Advisory Board, M-DCC PTA/PTSA, Dade-County District Citizen's

Mr. Bell explained to the listening public how the Progressive Firefighters
Association was established. He uttered that the organization was started
by the first African American Fire Chief of Miami-Dade County Dr. Charles
Phillips and Dr. David James who formed the organization. He further
elaborated that the organization has been actively advocating for diversity
and inclusion. He then gave a summary of the swim program, and how
important the program is because of the number of kids lost over the years
to senseless drownings. He explained that Florida has the highest drowning
rates in the nation, which is why they took it upon themselves to start a
water safety program. Mr. Bell made known that they have been running the
program for the last 10 years teaching over 2000 kids water safety and
CPR. He then thanked those who have supported the program which
consists of retired or are still working firefighters who make it their business
to show up. This year, the swim program at Charles Haley had over 200
kids and they ran a four-to-five-week Summer Swim program. Mr. Bell
thanked Mr. Danny Lopez from the City of Miami Parks and Recreation for
allowing them to take the program over to Charles Hadley Park, Mr. Andre
Adams from Children’s Trust for providing funding to the program, and the
additional men and women who take the time out of their busy schedules to
support the program.
Chief Raied “Ray” Jadallah gave kudos to Captain Keith Bell and
recognized the Progressive Firefighters Association. He stated that the
Progressive Firefighter Association has been teaching thousands of
children to swim. Chief Jadallah expressed to Captain Bell that they stand
shoulder to shoulder to ensure that every resident and every child can swim
in Miami-Dade County. The Chief ended by saying that he had a saying in
his office for Miami-Dade Fire Rescue which says, “There's a lot of things
that happen by chance, but leadership doesn't happen by chance,” and
