Ms. Blanco read the proclamation that mentioned the shared desire to
help people in the community who lacked the financial resources to
celebrate the holiday cheer, in December 1997, a pair of friends,
Rosemarie Rey and Andrea Lopez, began collecting toys from close
friends and relatives to deliver gifts to families in need in Miami-Dade
County. They wanted to make the greatest impact, so they decided to go
to places with the greatest need and little assistance. Driving in a
borrowed truck, with toys in tow, the pair of friends headed to the migrant
workers' camps in the south area of the county, and from the back of the
truck, started handing out the toys. The following year, their giving
operation expanded to include visits to Florida City Elementary and Coral
Gables nursing home and donations of much-needed items to The Baby
House, the former name of the Cerebral Palsy home.
Ms. Blanco also mentioned what began as two friends wanting to do just
a little more for people in need, The Christmas Smiles Project has grown
into an expansive giving operation that each year brings happiness and
joy to more than a thousand people in the community and counting. Other
partners have joined the effort such as Lourdes Mestre, coordinator of the
Ministry of Mothers Sharing (MOMS) at Saint Timothy Catholic Parish
Church. Almost a decade ago, upon learning about the need for toys in
The Project, she kindly donated to the effort, marking the beginning of a
beautiful and giving partnership. In addition to helping The Project collect
more toys and bring more joy to many more children, this partnership has
opened more opportunities to reach more people in need. The Project now
collects toys from Saint Timothy Catholic Church and Saint Timothy
Catholic Parish School.
Throughout the years, many people and entities have benefited from the
generosity of The Christmas Smiles Project including La Salle Learning
Center, Campbell Drive Elementary, Florida City Elementary, The Baby
House, Ronald McDonald House, Kristi House, San Juan Bosco School,
Sacred Heart Church, Citrus Elementary, Wayside Baptist Church,
Migrant Worker Public Housing Communities in Homestead, Saint Vincent
De Paul, Bahamas, Notre Dame in Little Haiti, Alex’s Place in Jackson,
Caribbean K-8 Center, Christ the King, San Martin de Porres, Saint
Vincent de Paul, among others. The work of The Project extends beyond
the Christmas season. They hold events to help families in need year-
round, including events during the Thanksgiving season, back-to-school
time, and other occasions such as their recent visit to the Golden Glades
Cerebral Palsy home.
Ms. Blanco recognized The Christmas Smiles Project, its founders,
families, friends, volunteers, and donors whose work and dedication
throughout the years have made the mission of The Project possible. She
celebrated the recognition of their many contributions to the community
and their love for their neighbor.

The Chair presented this item. The Chair announced for Ms. Santos to

Ms. Santos mentioned the celebration and recognition of the outstanding
contributions of Arthur and Polly's Conservatory of the Arts community
leaders. She was grateful for her colleagues' support of Resolution No.
24-011 established by the amazing married couple Arthur and Polly Mays,
education pioneers who were called champions of education. They
started their school Mays Middle School which sits at the heart of the
Gould's community in South Dade and has been integral to the local
redevelopment of the community since the 1920’s. With its award-winning
Magnet status, the school's evolution reflects its significance to our
community. It was established during the era of segregation and of
course, remains a testament to their commitment to providing education
to our local youth.
Ms. Santos intrigued the audience with a few important dates from the
history of our schools and the role they have played. Arthur and Polly
Mays in 1914, took it upon themselves to make sure black students in the
South had access to education through our Public School System at
Mount Pleasant Baptist Church. In 1926, a decade later, they persuaded
the School Board to finally open Gould's Elementary, but students could
not get there, so they purchased an actual bread wagon from the Holsom
Bakery in Homestead to provide free transportation. Then, drove the older
kids to George Washington Carver, which was where Blacks could go to
High School. The younger kids had to go all the way from Homestead to
the end of the Florida Keys. They made sure those students made it to
the schools that were available at the time and even though Arthur and
Polly Mays themselves did not have much formal education, they valued
education deeply. This was how they became very successful. Mr. Mays
ended up being a landowner of much land, so he generously donated land
to the School Board on which the school currently exists.
In 1935, Goulds Senior High School was opened and at a Special Board
Meeting, we were able to see an excerpt of the minutes from May 30,
1951, which the school of course was renamed in their honor.
Today, Arthur and Polly Mays Conservatory of the Arts (APMCOTA) is an
award-winning magnet school located in the Goulds community, part of
the South Dade area of Miami-Dade County, Florida. It offers a fine arts
college preparatory curriculum for 6th to 12th-grade students. The school
is named after Arthur and Polly Mays, a married couple who were
considered champions of education in the Goulds, and who at the time of
segregation started a school for black children in the area. Building on this
legacy of educational commitment and dedication to service, some of the
school’s alumni, faculty, and staff have gone on to play impactful roles in
the community. Ms. Santos acknowledged a few current leaders of this
community who are carrying on that legacy. She asked each leader to join
her on the floor when she called their names, and she said a few words
about them and was joined by the current amazing principal. In 2023, Dr.
Jones Carey was the South Region Principal of the Year.
• Dennis Davis, a 1964 graduate of Mays High School, returned to his
alma mater as a classroom teacher after earning a bachelor’s degree in
mathematics education in 1968. In 1975, he earned a master’s degree in
school administration. For over 38 years, Mr. Davis has served the
community as a classroom teacher, assistant principal, and region
director. In 2000, he was named Principal of the Year.

youth. Part of this work includes the establishment of the Little League
Football clubs in Miami-Dade County.

call and became a licensed and ordained Minister on May 22, 2011. His
ministry helped expand his vision to help the least, lost and left out. He
continued his passion by galvanizing other pastors, churches, and
organizations that shared his vision to make our neighborhoods a safer
place to reside and raise families. With this goal in mind, Pastor Lorenzo
Johnson Sr. founded the Community Youth Against Violence (CYAV) with
a vision to return our South Florida Black neighborhoods back to a place
where children can venture outside and play with their friends, and seniors
can relax on their front porch without the fear of gunshots and senseless
deaths of innocent people. One of the most important strategies
implemented by the group was to change people’s mindsets, especially
the mindset of our youth.
Pastor Johnson founder of The Community Youth Against Violence offers
outreach services to youth and families designed to provide tools and
resources to mend broken families and connect them with support to
address their emotional, physical, and mental needs. Not afraid to
confront issues, Pastor Johnson also brought social awareness to
neighborhoods, from South Miami to the North Dade County line,
organizing various peace walks with youth and other concerned citizens.

homicide that snatches the life of an innocent person, especially a youth.

weekly Zoom Training Workshops. These workshops are attended by
students, parents, educators, county and city officials, and anyone with an
interest in changing behaviors to produce positive outcomes. Currently,

received great reviews.

2Dr. Bendross-Mindingall read the proclamation for the celebration of
Black History on a national level first began in February 1926, in large part
thanks to the efforts of the great American historian, Dr. Carter G.
Woodson. His efforts led to the national observance of Black History
Week. This week became the time to celebrate the many achievements
and contributions of African Americans to this nation. In 1976, as part of
the nation’s bicentennial celebration, Black History Week became Black
History Month.
Dr. Bendross-Mindingall mentioned that every United States President
since 1976 has officially designated the month of February as Black
History Month. The Association for the Study of American Life and History
(ASALH), founded by Dr. Woodson, for many years, has helped lead the
annual National Black History Month celebration. Every year, Black
History Month is given a national theme by the ASALH. The 2024 Black
History Month theme, African Americans and the Arts explores the key
influence African Americans have had in the fields of "visual and
performing arts, literature, fashion, folklore, language, film, music,
architecture, culinary, and other forms of cultural expression."
African American art, deeply rooted in the fusion of African, Caribbean,
and Black American lived experiences, permeates a myriad of cultural
expressions, spanning visual and performing arts, literature, fashion,
folklore, language film, music, architecture, culinary arts, and more.
Despite historical denial and appropriation, a continuous thread of Black
artistic production has persisted from ancient times, shaping global trends.
Enslaved Africans in the Lowcountry pioneered sweetgrass basketry,
laying the foundation for visual artistry in craft. From spirituals born out of
bondage to the blues influencing genres like gospel and soul, Black
contributions to music have been transformative.
