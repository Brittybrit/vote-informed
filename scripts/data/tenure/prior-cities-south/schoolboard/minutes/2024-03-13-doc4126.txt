The Vice Chair returns the gavel to the Chair. The Chair announced Ms.
Colucci to present her item.
Ms. Colucci asked for the audience to watch a video dedicated to The
WOW Center. Upon completion of the video Ms. Colucci invited Ms.
Arlene Peterson and members from the WOW Center. She went on to
read the item. The WOW Center is an Adult Day Training (ADT) Center
whose mission is to provide access to opportunities and resources for
adults with developmental disabilities in the community. Fifty-one years
ago, these programs were not available. Upon high school graduation, the
options for continued education and community involvement for adults
with developmental disabilities were limited. The WOW Center is the
result of the hope and dedication of a small group of parents who
organized, looking to fill the gap and meet the needs of their aging children
with disabilities.
Since its founding, in 1972, The WOW Center has broadened its
programs, services, and activities, amassing a reputation throughout
Florida, as being on the frontlines of disability inclusion, innovative
program development, and generating purposeful opportunities for adults
with developmental disabilities. In Miami-Dade County, The WOW Center
supports and serves nearly two hundred adults with developmental
disabilities, ages 21 to 83. Personalized support plans are offered that
address the adults’ specific needs and goals. The WOW Center
accomplishes this by focusing on gross/fine motor skills, locomotion,
strength, and development. The Wow Center’s goals target mental
alertness, conversational skills, as well as preventing depression, and
social isolation. The WOW Center offers a diverse range of recreational
and social activities, such as art classes, sports, community outings, and
music therapy. These activities promote social interaction, community
engagement, and personal expression. The WOW Center's ADT model
has been called a model of excellence by the Agency for Persons with
Disabilities in Florida. Because of their “above and beyond” approach,
which integrates The WOW Center’s social services team, the Center is
able to offer education, resources, and support services to families,
ensuring they have the tools to advocate for their loved ones and navigate
the challenges associated with developmental disabilities.
The WOW Center’s work empowers adults with developmental
disabilities. Through their multi-faceted community effort, The WOW
Center is dedicated to making Miami more inclusive through their
partnerships with businesses and organizations, by offering training,
community events, and opportunities for employment; making inclusion
visible, tangible, and life-changing for those they serve.
Ms. Arlene Peterson thanked the Board, Chair and Superintendent Dotres
for the recognition.

Ms. Rojas presented her item on Mr. Raul Malo. The soaring, almost
operatic voice of Raul Malo was the signature sound of the Mavericks, the
Miami-based band that scored both critical and commercial success in the
1990s with its slightly Latin-flavored brand of country music. Mr. Malo was
not only the group’s voice but also its creative spark, with musical interests
that extended beyond country to rock and roll and jazz, and the music of
his ancestral homeland of Cuba.
Raul Malo was born in Miami, Florida. His parents separately had fled
Cuba after 1959; his mother left immediately while his father hid out for a
time in the Mexican embassy in Havana. Mr. Malo’s parents met and
married in Miami, and Raul grew up with the music they enjoyed,
American country music and romantic pop like Roy Orbison and Bobby
Darin, as well as Cuban tunes.
Raul started singing early and formed a band at age 12, while attending
Rockway Middle School in Miami-Dade County Public Schools. He also
played bass guitar, and he and his bandmates found engagements at
quinceañeras, the “sweet 15” parties common in many Latin American
cultures.
