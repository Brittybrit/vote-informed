Steve Gallon, III, and Ms. Luisa Santos, School Board Members, proposed that The

The Chair presented this item.
Ms. Monica Colucci went to the floor to present her Agenda Item B-4.
Ms. Colucci asked Mr. Weir to join her on the floor as she presented his proclamation
and shared a short video of his life. She also shared that he turned 99 years old on
June 11, 2024, which he enjoyed with his family. His daily routines were waking up at
6:00 a.m. to take a shower, 7:00 a.m. to be in his car going to the gym for 1 ½ - 2
hours, and listening to his Bible class from 8:30 a.m. – 9:00 a.m. This was how he
stayed young and fit 6 days of the week.
Ms. Colucci went on to state that June 6, 2024, marked the 80
th 
anniversary for the
start of Operation Overlord, which was launched by the unprecedented D-Day
landings on the beaches of Normandy. This operation concentrated the land, sea, and
air forces of twelve allied nations in a high-stake invasion of Nazi-occupied France.
During World War II all American men between the ages of 21 and 45 had to register
for the draft. By 1944, 50 million had registered and ten million had been inducted into
the military. Draft numbers for men in their forties were being called by 1944 as the
military ramped up for the European invasion.
In May 1944, the Western Allies were ready for the long-delayed cross-channel
invasion of northern France, code-named Overlord. General Dwight D. Eisenhower
was the supreme commander of the operation that coordinated the efforts of twelve
nations.
Surprise was an essential element of the Allied invasion plan. If the Germans had
known where and when the Allies were approaching, they would have hurled them
back into the sea with the fifty-five divisions they had in France. The Allies would have
been on the offensive with a 10-to-1 manpower ratio against them.
The Nazis had spent months constructing the Atlantic Wall, a 2,400-mile line of
obstacles. This defensive wall comprised 6.5 million mines, thousands of concrete
bunkers, and pillboxes containing heavy and fast-firing artillery, tens of thousands of
tank ditches, and other formidable beach obstacles.
Mr. James Weir, or as his friends refer to him, Pap-pap, grew up in Mount Pleasant,
Pennsylvania, near Pittsburgh. He enlisted in the United States Navy in August 1943.
He fought on behalf of our nation, in Europe and Asia. On June 6, 1944, Coxswain
Weir crossed the English Channel aboard a Landing Craft Tanks (Amphibious) to
deliver tanks and troops onto the beaches of Normandy. After the battle was won in
Europe, Coxswain Weir fought in the liberation of the Philippines and was deployed
to Japan as part of the occupational forces after the war.
In between those momentous historical events, Coxswain Weir was granted a twenty-
day leave after D-Day. He rushed back to the States to marry his sweetheart, Laverne
Myers. They had been sweethearts since he had sat behind her in sixth grade. They
were married for 73 years. Laverne passed away in 2018.
After the war, Coxswain Weir worked as a master electrician and was a renowned
Corvette racer, leading the national Corvette Club as its President. Coxswain Weir
now lives in Miami, Florida., He has two grandchildren and six great-grandchildren
who love him dearly. He lives with his granddaughter Lisa and her family.

Ms. Sally Alayon thanked the Board and the Superintendent for being supportive of
mental health services. She stated that the mental health assistance allocation plan
is it's the foundation of some of the things they will be doing for the next school year.
This plan follows the MTSS model, which is the Multitiered Systems of Support Model.
This way they can ensure all students receive the support they need. Within the plan
there are a variety of different evidence-based programs that is utilized to support all
students. Tier I – programs are general programs, resiliency, coping skills which are
ways that they teach students how to deal with some stressful situations and some
challenges that may come their way. Tier II – is geared towards the students that are
showing early warning signs and some indicators that they need mental health service
support. There are different types of interventions within the plan that the mental
health coordinators will use with the students. Tier III – have different programs and
interventions for students that need the most intensive support. The plan mentioned
that there are community partners, for example, (Miami Dolphins, Miami Heat, &
Miami Marlins). There are fourteen (14) different agencies that help support this
program.
Ms. Blanco thanked Ms. Alayon for her response. She asked Ms. Alayon to share
information about the designation program that is through the state and the numbers
we have in our District.
Ms. Alayon responded that through the Florida Department of Education schools
throughout the state have an opportunity to become a suicide prevention certified
school. That means that school that goes above and beyond the required training,
take on an extra two-hour training and, and that signifies those mental health
professionals that are within their building. Last school year, they rolled out in the
month of September, Suicide Prevention Awareness Month. M-DCPS had 95 schools
achieve that status. They are in the process now of moving forward a whole another
batch of schools and the goal is to get all of them. Something that they are proud of
is on the Florida Department of Education website under the mental health student
services section, it lists the schools throughout the state that have made this
designation and it's a beautiful thing to see that 95 schools are there representing
Miami-Dade County, which will grow and there’s only one other school from one other
district represented there. That designation lasts for three years, and they will continue
to work to grow the year ones and keep it going.
Ms. Blanco thanked Ms. Alayon and her team for their hard work. She mentioned that
she was happy to see that they are working on lowering the ratio number of students
per social worker, psychologist, and license mental health counselor.

The frequency of screens in our modern lives, amplified by the COVID-19 pandemic,
has led to an unprecedented increase in screen time for both students and teachers.
While technology offers immense educational potential, the escalating rates of
screens raises significant concerns regarding the physical and mental health of
students and employees.
Research has linked excessive screen time to various physical health problems,
particularly in children and adolescents. It has been determined that increased screen
time, especially at close proximity, can strain eyes and contribute to the development
of myopia (nearsightedness). Likewise, the blue light emitted by screens has been
found to suppress melatonin production, a hormone crucial for regulating sleep-wake
cycles. This can lead to sleep deprivation and related health issues. Excessive screen
time frequently causes a displacement of physical activity, leading to a sedentary
lifestyle and increased risk of obesity and associated metabolic disorders. Excessive
screen time has also been associated with a range of behavioral health problems
which include attention deficit and focus issues.
Studies suggest a correlation between high screen time and difficulties with attention,
focus, and hyperactivity, potentially impacting learning and academic performance
while also leading to increased rates of anxiety, depression, and loneliness,
particularly among adolescents. Compounding the issue, the stimulating nature of
screen-based activities have also been found to lead to dopamine dysregulation,
contributing to addictive behaviors and difficulty regulating emotions.

literacy support programs, tutoring services and other valuable support services. This
