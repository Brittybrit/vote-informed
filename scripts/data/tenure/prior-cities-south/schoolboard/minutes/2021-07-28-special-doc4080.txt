The Chair called the meeting to order at 6:04 p.m. and Ms. Rojas led
those in attendance in The Pledge of Allegiance to the Flag of the United
States of America. The Board then held a moment of silent meditation.
The Chair invited anyone from the audience who had not already signed
up and wished to be heard at the public hearing to proceed to the lobby
counter outside the auditorium for assistance from staff.
School Board Attorney Walter Harvey was recognized to explain the
procedures to be followed during today’s public hearing.
Mr. Harvey explained that this was the School Board’s public hearing to
adopt the Tentative 2021-2022 Millage Levy and Annual Budget. During
this hearing, the School Board may amend the tentative budget and
adopt a resolution stating the proposed millage rate to be levied by
separate vote. The first substantive issue before the assembly should
be to determine the percentage, if any, by which the recomputed millage
rate exceeds (or compares to) the rollback rate necessary to fund the
budget, and the specific purposes for which ad valorem tax revenues
were being assessed.
Continuing, he said the general public should be allowed to comment
and ask questions prior to adoption of any measures by the Board.
Pursuant to Board Policy 0169.1, each speaker may be permitted to
speak a total of three (3) minutes regardless of the number of items they
are going to address. After hearing the speakers and answering any

Mr. Ron Steiger, Chief Financial Officer, was recognized next to explain
the budget recommendations. [A verbatim recording of Mr. Steiger’s full
remarks is on file in the Office of the Board Recording Secretary.]
Mr. Steiger said that before them were two items related to the 2021-22
budget: SP-1, which must be approved first, represented the millage
levies for 2021-22 and SP-2, which represented the Superintendent’s
budget recommendations as submitted to the Board in the Executive
Summary and as adjusted for revisions included in Attachment A of the
agenda item. SP-2 also requested approval of the School Allocation
Plan. This item was to be approved following the approval of the millage
levies.
Mr. Steiger further explained that the total millage rate to be levied was
.120 mills lower than 2020-21. The reduction in the millage rate came
from the 2021 Legislature reducing the RLE and a reduction in the Debt
Service. The Board received the Executive Summary on July 13, 2021
which included projected revenues and appropriations for all funds. The
tentative budget included:
• A significant decrease in State FEFP funding from the initial amount
approved by the 2021 Legislature versus the 2nd FEFP calculation
due to the State’s deduction of its initial estimates for McKay
Scholarships as well as Family Empowerment Scholarships.
• A higher FEFP contribution coming from State funds as opposed to
coming out of the pockets of our local taxpayers due to the reduced
Required Local Effort (RLE) millage.
• The calculation of the DCD had yet to be revised as requested and
they took away the allocation which was provided during 2020-2021
legislative session.
• The cost of medical insurance would not be increased for employees.
• A $46 million Reserve for Tax Roll Yield Shortfall due to lower tax
collections experienced in the past several years. This included $36
million for a potential shortfall in 2020-21.
• Contingency reserve was maintained at 3% of revenue. Contingency
protects the Board against unknown decreases in revenue for such
things as a decrease in membership.
• The largest increase by far relates to the federal stimulus funds the
district would be receiving of nearly $1.5B; while nonrecurring, it was
truly a lifeline for the district during this time.
Continuing, Mr. Steiger noted that the overall budget was the highest it
had ever been. That didn’t change the fact that it was a difficult budget
and that these were difficult financial times. They were buoyed by federal
stimulus fund investment, for which they were truly grateful. He
recognized all the budget committees and input received to put together
this budget. He expressed great pride in this budget and the work of his
team in developing it.

Next, the Chair relinquished the floor for remarks from the
Superintendent. [A verbatim recording of his full remarks is on file in the
Office of the Board Recording Secretary.]
The Superintendent thanked Mr. Steiger for his comments and added
the following points:
• This was the largest budget they had ever managed, but it was
reflected of “diabetic financing”; a lot of incoming federal funding that
did not penetrate by law all the elements they would like funding for.
• This funding created short term salvation but did not create a bridge
because of its nonrecurring nature.
• It disproportionately invested in schools, school site staff, and
students; 95.2% was directly earmarked to those three areas.
• It continued to maintain one of the lowest administrative per students
cost ratio in the state.
• It protected the workforce with no layoffs or reductions for the next
fiscal year.
• It maintained health insurance option for employees for free. It did
not increase the copayments or coinsurance for dependent
healthcare.
• It protected the financial stability of the school system now and for
the long haul, maintaining a slightly higher than state required
reserves and projects adding to those reserves because of potential
future tax funding decrease and loss of student enrollment.
• This budget projects taxpayers. Despite the fact this Board had
courageously advocated and passed two different referenda over the
past few years, this budget still reduced the overall millage rate by
.120 mills.
• This budget was reflective of the investments funded through
General Fund but was not reflected of much of what was presented
under ESSER as far as adding counselors, mental health
professional, mental health coordinators, and all of the investments
that they declared to the Board would be made. That was a separate
conservation that was not embedded in this budget.
• Many stakeholder groups had the opportunity to vet this budget and
provide procedural and advice to this process.
• He thanked the community and staff from the Budget Office.
