Ms. Lucia Baez-Geller, Board Member, proposed that The School Board
of Miami-Dade County, Florida, approve Resolution No. 21-013 of The
School Board of Miami-Dade County, Florida, recognizing the Coalition
of South Florida Muslim Organizations (COSMOS) for their contributions
toward the field of education and for their acts of charity during Eid Al-
Fitr on May 12-13, 2021 and throughout the year. [A copy of Resolution
No. 21-013 is on file with the official records of this meeting.]
Ms. Baez-Geller introduced Naveed Anjum, COSMOS Secretary;
Shabbir Motorwala, COSMOS Board member; Tehsin Sidiqqi,
COSMOS Board member, Khalid Mirza, Chairman Muslim Committee
Association of South Florida; and Mohammed Shakir, COSMOS Board
member. The Coalition of South Florida Muslim Organizations
(COSMOS), founded in 2011, aims to foster a better understanding of
Islam, American Muslims, and Muslim cultures through engagement,
education, outreach, and community service to build bridges within the
South Florida community. COSMOS works with interfaith groups and
community partners to advance these objectives.
During Eid Al-Fitr, a Muslim holiday which takes place on May 12-13,
obligatory acts of charity are required for the purpose of helping
individuals and families who are impoverished and in need of spiritual
and financial support. COSMOS has engaged in such acts and
continues to provide needed support in the community. COSMOS’
charitable acts include food distribution drives, assistance with COVID-
19 testing, and aiding the South Florida community through their
coalition partners, which include mosques, a free healthcare clinic, and
a domestic violence shelter. COSMOS also works with MCCJ and the
Greater Miami Jewish Federation to uplift the faith community in South
Florida.

Avenue and 2478 NW 55 Street, Miami, Florida 33142.
Chair Hantman introduced this item.
Ms. Baez-Geller brought up the importance of offering robust adult
community programs. This project with elderly housing provided a
valuable opportunity to engage with prospective stakeholders that may
be willing to work with the district. It also provided an opportunity to
engage the elderly and connect them with the public school system.
Dr. Bendross-Mindingall raised inquiries for the benefit of listening and
viewing audience as to whether there were any plans to have activities
in these senior centers.
Mr. Pérez explained that these projects were currently in their inception,
going through the permitting and review process. They will be making
connections with the developer, putting them in touch with School
Operations and the adult programs to explore what could be offered
once the project is finalized. At this time, however, the project was a
couple of years away from construction of the facility. No children under
18 would be allowed in the facilities.
As a follow-up, Dr. Bendross-Mindingall asked how impact fees were
determined for each project.
In response, Mr. Pérez explained that impact fees were determined by
the number of units on the project and the number of bedrooms in each
unit. The county worked out an algorithm to establish the number of
children the project generates and the affected schools. The district then
verified the capacity at those schools and charged the developer for the
impact to the district. Proportionate share mitigation agreements carry a
payment to the district for educational impact fees from the developer.
This item is a waiver of that.

WLRN is a unique public radio and television station serving the South
Florida community and is an incredible resource and asset for the
District and for our community. WLRN Radio commenced its operations
in 1948 as a non-profit, non-commercial broadcast station licensed to
the School Board; WLRN-TV was established in 1962. WLRN estimates
that it reaches “a combined weekly audience of well over a million people
in South Florida, from Palm Beach to Key West.”
The School Board, as the owner, is responsible for the operation of
WLRN Radio and TV. In this capacity, and pursuant to policy, the School
Board is also responsible for “establishing programming policy and
guidelines that provide the South Florida community with balanced
programming of education services, information, and entertainment
while consistently adhering to all applicable Federal, State, and local
laws.”
In order to ensure that this asset continues to thrive and operates as
efficiently as possible for the benefit of our District and our community,
and in a manner consistent with the District’s 2021-2026 Strategic
Blueprint, this item calls for the Superintendent to schedule a workshop
wherein information will be provided to the Board regarding how the
communication asset represented by WLRN may be leveraged to further
enhance the educational experience for students and the community.

for the modernization and/or relocation of WLRN operations, and its
potential incorporation into the 20-1500 redevelopment plan for the
District offices. She encouraged further conversation that includes
Board input as they continue to develop the 20-1500 plan.

understand, and opine on the future direction of this critical and unique
asset. Consistent with Board policy, this item nor its proposed action is
intended to encroach in any way upon the Editorial Integrity Policy

talk about WLRN.”
