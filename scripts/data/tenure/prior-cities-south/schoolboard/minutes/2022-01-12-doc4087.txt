that going outside to enjoy outdoor activities helped maintain mental and
physical health and connection to others. As a result, the students found
that access to nature is important for everyone. They did research and
learned about biophilic design principles and joined the Biophilic Cities
network. Biophilic design is the idea of using elements of nature and
promoting interaction with nature in all buildings and public places.
Biophilic design positively affects education by improving cognitive skills
and academic performance; supporting development of creativity,
problem solving, and intellectual capacity; and reducing stress. More
generally, biophilic design can create healthier cities by helping reduce
the heat island effect, increasing energy efficiency, inviting exploration
and exercise, improving air and water quality, and reducing food
insecurity.
The students reached out to the Miami-Dade County Resiliency Office,
which taught them more about urban heat islands. In Miami-Dade
County, lower-income communities of color have less tree canopy and
parks. Students and schools in these neighborhoods suffer most from
the urban heat island effect. To address this problem, Tapiztree is
advocating for a Biophilic Campuses program to engage students,
environmental clubs, teachers, and District officials in designing and
implementing biophilic projects. These projects would expand tree
canopy and greenery on school campuses and would educate students,
staff and community members about the health and energy benefits of
reducing heat. With the help of District officials, Tapiztree had its first
webinar with environmental clubs from multiple schools throughout the
district, and the group is moving forward with the goal of promoting
Biophilic Campuses everywhere.

Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity was founded on January 5, 1911 on the
campus of Indiana University in Bloomington, Indiana. The Miami
Alumni Chapter was chartered on November 23, 1946 to open the doors
of college fraternal life for college men everywhere, regardless of color,
religion, or national origin. The Miami Alumni Chapter of Kappa Alpha
Psi charter members are Leo Jenkins, Oscar J. Edwards, Chester
Williams, James E. Hart, Leonard Walker, Frank Jones, Louis Blake,
and Leandus Blackus. As the first male, predominantly African-
American Greek-letter society founded west of the Mid-Atlantic States,
still in existence with an extensive membership, Kappa Alpha Psi
Fraternity and its Miami Alumni Chapter remains dedicated to uniting
men of culture, patriotism and honor, while encouraging honorable
achievement in every field of human endeavor.
Many members of the Miami Alumni Chapter have served as principals,
administrators, and teachers in the Miami-Dade County Public School
system. Member Johnny Jones served as the first African-American
Superintendent. The Miami Alumni Chapter is committed to inspiring
and educating the youth. As a result, the Miami Kappa League program
was started by the Chapter to assist male students finding the best-fit
post high school opportunity be it college, military, or workforce. The
Kappa Leadership and Development League was created to provide an
avenue for young men in middle and high school to develop leadership
skills as well as achieve worthy goals as to make a meaningful and
positive impact in their community.
