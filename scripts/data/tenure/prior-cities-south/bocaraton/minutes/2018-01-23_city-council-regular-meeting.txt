Mayor Haynie advised that the second public hearings public hearings on Items A., B., and C. would be
conducted contemporaneously and separate votes would be taken on each ordinance. Brandon Schaad,
Development Services Director, gave the PowerPoint presentation reviewing the eight issues discussed
at the first public hearing. These included the need for a master plan; the submitted market study; building
heights; the minimum unit size; the geographic allocation of units; an optional PMD overlay versus a new
mandatory district; the perquisite of a train station; and, lastly, infrastructure and options for completion of
same. Upon concluding, Mr. Schaad responded to questions.

The public hearing was opened. Michael Marshall, GrayRobinson, speaking on behalf of several
stakeholders, explained the overall idea behind Midtown as a mixed-use, walkable environment. He then
pointed out elements in the ordinances that his clients believed were detrimental to creating this walkable
environment, including unit allocation, Tri-Rail contingencies, street infrastructure requirement, building
height, non-PMD parking, open space, unit size, setbacks, and shuttle requirements. Upon concluding,
he responded to a number of questions from Council members. Extensive discussion followed. Topics
included funding the infrastructure, the need for a master plan, the Tri-Rail station, and Military Trail
concurrency. Brandon Schaad provided additional information. Angelo Bianco, Crocker Partners,
addressed street infrastructure improvements and concurrency, and also provided further information on
the planning process for their ten-year project. John Donaldson, traffic engineer, explained traffic
concurrency as detailed for the Midtown area. Bonnie Miske!, Dunay Miskel Backman, speaking on
behalf of Simon Properties, noted that her client has an interest in this item while not being part of the
Midtown area. She commented on the ordinances that would create new zoning districts, the transit hub
on Butts Road, and the allocation or units.

Joseph Jaffe, on behalf of Fairfield of Boca, commented on the need for a plan leading to a vision, noting
that one must start somewhere and work together. William Seenyshyn, president of Timbercreek
Homeowners Association, requested that a master plan for the area be completed before moving ahead
and stated his support of the Planning and Zoning Board recommendations. John Watts commented on
the Tri-Rail station, the allowable number of units, traffic on Military Trail, and other topics. Stuart Melsez
offered support of Midtown. Kathryn Sauers expressed her concerns and asked a number of questions
about the availability of information. Adam Beighley, on behalf of Via Verde Homeowners Association,
commented on the stakeholders' interaction with the Association. Lorraine Braile displayed a poster
illustrating the location of the residential units and expressed other concerns. Tim Day expressed his
opposition to the Midtown plan, stating that it will negatively impact his neighborhood and the City as a
whole. Bobbye Miller agreed with the previous speaker and expressed her opposition to the Midtown
plan. JoAnn Landon commented on the lack of mass transit, the increasing number of residential units,
and asked that the Council not approve Midtown. Donna Mincuzzi expressed opposition to the Midtown
project and the number of rental units. John Renne, Florida Atlantic University urban planning professor,
commented on the purpose of the hearing, master planning, traffic congestion in walkable communities,
and other related topics. He offered support of Midtown. Monica Mayotte commented on the need for a
master plan and a market study for Midtown. She asked that no vote be taken. Diane Pingle commented
on traffic on Military Trail and expressed opposition to Midtown. Jason Pellish expressed opposition to
the residential units proposed to be built and the project as a whole. Lenore Wachtel commented on the
need for a master plan and a special assessment as was done for the downtown community
redevelopment area. Steve Utrecht expressed his concern with planned mobility and advocated having
site specific plans before granting vested rights. Brian Stenberg commented on the advent of autonomous
vehicles and the impacts on roadways, and other related topics. Carrie Gibbons expressed her
opposition to the ordinances and any increase in residential units. Pauletta Moore commented on traffic
       on Military Trail and its effect on her neighborhood. Joe Pedalino expressed opposition to Midtown and
       advocated having a master plan and market study. Martina Shokey commented on traffic congestion and
       offered opposition to residential. Michael Marshall responded to a number of the previous comments.
       Seeing no one else come forward to speak, the public hearing was closed.

       Members asked questions of staff. Brandon Schaad and the City Attorney provided information on traffic
       and school concurrency. Angelo Bianco responded to a question regarding the Tri-Rail station
       requirement, other aspects of the Midtown plan and establishment of regulations. He emphasized the
       need to work together. Mayor Haynie enumerated topics that needed to be addressed: information on the
       County's plan for Military Trail, a reduction in the number of units, timing or roadway infrastructure, the
       lack of setbacks and other concerns. She suggested that more time was needed before a decision could
       be made. Members weighed in and asked further questions. Mr. Bianco and Mr. Schaad provided
       information on funding for a shuttle, a transit oriented development, and the Tri-Rail requirement.
       Members made further lengthy comments. Discussion of the timing of a postponement, allowing time to
       work out the differences between the stakeholders and City, and the necessary planning for the area as it
       relates to the land development regulations, ensued.

Motion was made by Deputy Mayor Jeremy Rodgers, seconded by Council Member Andrea Levin O'Rourke, to
extend the meeting beyond 11 pm. Motion carried unanimously on a voice vote.

       Discussion continued. Mr. Schaad addressed the planning process as he sees it going forward, and
       responded to a number of questions. Mr. Bianco expressed his desire to move forward.

               Motion was made by Council Member Andrea Levine O'Rourke, seconded by Council Member
               Robert S. Weinroth, to have a city-driven small area plan for this area.

       Prior to voting, the City Manager summarized the direction he heard during the discussion. The City
       Attorney addressed the planning process and what it is included in same. Discussion ensued. Topics
       included the definition of a small area plan, implementation of land development regulations, timing of a
       plan, the legalities involved, analyzing the roadway network. The City Attorney responded to some of the
       statements made. Members commented further.

               Motion carried on a 4 to 1 vote; Deputy Mayor Jeremy Rodgers, Council Member Andrea Levine
               O'Rourke, Council Member Scott Singer, Council Member Robert S. Weinroth voting yes; Mayor
               Susan Haynie voting no.

               Motion was made by Mayor Susan Haynie, seconded by Deputy Mayor Jeremy Rodgers, to
               postpone Ordinance No. 5433, Ordinance No. 5434 and Ordinance No. 5435 to March 27, 2018.
               Motion failed 2 votes to 3; Mayor Susan Haynie, Deputy Mayor Jeremy Rodgers voting yes,

17.   QUASI-JUDICIAL PUBLIC HEARINGS/VARIANCES & APPEALS:

             Resolution No. 22-2018
             A resolution of the City Council of the City of Boca Raton considering the appeal of the decision
              of the Community Appearance Board approving Rose Paving LLC's request to remove trees
              (Permit No. 17-6127}, with a condition that five such trees remain, at The Atrium of Boca Raton
              located at 1080 NW 15th Street; providing for severability; providing for repealer; providing an
             effective date
                      Resolution No. 22-2018

      The City Attorney reviewed the quasi-judicial procedures that would govern the public hearing. Members
      made their ex parte disclosures. The City Clerk administered the oath to those who wished to speak.

      Nora Fosman, Senior Environmental Officer, gave the PowerPoint presentation.                    The staff
      recommendation to reverse the decision of the Community Appearance Board (CAB) was explained.
      Various pictures illustrated the justification for removal of the trees and that the trees did not mature
      properly due to where they were planted. Upon concluding, she made herself available for questions.

      The public hearing was opened. Mark Jacobsen, CAB chair, explained that the applicant had been
      offered the opportunity to reconfigure the parking lot islands in exchange for allowing the removal of the
      trees. He noted that he is not opposed to removing the trees. Debra Hernandez, on behalf of the Atrium,
      provided a handout to the Council, and stated that the request to remove the trees was due to the fact
      that the roots were causing a public safety issue. She noted that a reconfiguration of the parking lot would
        result in the owner having to submit for a site plan modification rather than simply removing the trees,
        replacing them with properly scaled vegetation, and repaving and restriping the lot. Upon concluding, she
        responded to questions. Brandon Schaad, Development Services Director, addressed the possibility of a
        site plan change as suggested by CAB. Mr. Jacobsen weighed in. Seeing no one else come forward to
        speak, the public hearing was closed.

        At the request of the Council, the City Attorney read language into the record reflecting the approval of
        removing six trees and the requirement to change the configuration of the parking lot islands.

              Motion was made by Council Member Scott Singer, seconded by Council Member Andrea Levine
              O'Rourke, to approve Resolution No. 22-2018 affirming the decision of the Community
              Appearance Board with conditions as stated.
        Members made final comments.
              Motion carried unanimously; Mayor Susan Haynie, Deputy Mayor Jeremy Rodgers, Council
              Member Andrea Levine O'Rourke, Council Member Scott Singer, Council Member Robert S.
              Weinroth voting yes.
