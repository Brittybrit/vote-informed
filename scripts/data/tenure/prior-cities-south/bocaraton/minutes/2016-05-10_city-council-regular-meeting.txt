12.    REGULAR PUBLIC HEARINGS:

        A.     Ordinance No. 5347
               An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton amending Section 3.08, of the Charter of the City of Boca
               Raton to amend the method for filling vacancies on the City Council, including the office of mayor;
               providing that vacancies shall be filled by special election; providing for the submission of the
               proposed Charter amendment to the electors of the City at a special election to be held on August
               30, 2016 (the date of the primary election); amending Section 6-4, Code of Ordinances to delete
               a provision rendered obsolete and establish a minimum qualification period for special elections;
               providing for severability; providing for repealer; providing for revision to the City Charter and for
               codification in the Code of Ordinances; providing an effective date
                        Ordinance No. 5347

Council Member Singer, having requested that the ordinance be drafted, provided the rationale for the
amendments which would provide for elections to fill Council vacancies. Mr. Singer noted the importance of
allowing voters to have their say and then addressed the cost of special elections.

Members weighed in and expressed their support or lack thereof. Council Member Weinroth asked that the City
Attorney share the amendment he requested which addressed only vacancies in the event a Council member
resigns to run for another City office. Discussion of the current code and charter, the ordinance as introduced, and
the proposed amendment ensued.

The public hearing was opened. Glenn Gromann and Alan Feinberg, Jr. expressed their opposition to the
ordinance. Seeing no one else come forward to speak, the public hearing was closed.

        Motion was made by Council Member Scott Singer to approve Ordinance No. 5347. Motion died for lack
        of a second.

Members made final comments. Council Member Singer expressed disappointment and asked again for a second
to his motion. Mayor Haynie echoed the sentiment that this topic has merit and should be addressed and
discussed in the future.
