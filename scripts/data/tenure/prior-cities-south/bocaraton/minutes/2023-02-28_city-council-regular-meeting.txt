 13.     PUBLIC REQUESTS:

 Richard Carlson, representative of the Deerhurst Homeowners Association (HOA) provided a brief history of
 the subdivision and the common area landscaping. He explained to Council Members the difficulty of the
 homeowners to continue to maintain the irrigation, due to the failing of the existing well. He further stated that
 the HOA does not have the right to charge the owners for the maintenance of the well, and that it should not be
 their responsibility to maintain the common area landscaping.

  Phyliss Robinson stated that she was appalled over the loud vehicles racing up and down Palmetto Park Road
  and that it was affecting quality of life at outdoor dining establishments.

  Adam Galicki, President of the Deerhurst Homeowners Association, stated that he was concerned over the
  legal right of the owners to irrigate and maintain the common areas.

Bruce Klarberg, from the Library Commons Homeowners Association, voiced his concern about a safety hazard
on the Brightline Station train platform near the Library Commons property line. He stated that there was a
dangerous opening at the end of the platform, and suggested a fence be installed.

Jonathan Ounjian spoke about the disparity of the zoning for his office building compared to other areas
throughout the City.

In response to the request of the Deerhurst Homeowners Association, Deputy City Manager Andy Lukasik
provided a brief history and overview of the area and noted he had spoken with the members of the Deerhurst
community. Mr. Lukasik explained the City's history of not maintaining common areas and rights-of-way
irrigation. He further stated the City had offered alternative solutions and was willing to help the community by
locating a new well or provide a water meter. Discussion ensued. City Attorney Diana Grubb Frieser provided
additional insight. Regarding the concern about loud and speeding vehicles, Deputy City Manager George
Brown advised that the City had been proactive when dealing with speeding vehicles and described several
actions the City was taking. Deputy Mayor Andrea Levine O'Rourke mentioned that the City had taken steps to
alleviate the noise issue, urged the citizens to continue to be diligent and thanked the public for making the
effort to report their concerns. Mr. Brown then addressed the issue about the train station platform, and advised
that Brightline will erect a fence at the property line and would continue to add landscaping. Council Member
Monica Mayotte mentioned a drainage issue and wanted to know if it was tied to the same issue.

19.     MAYOR AND COUNCIL MEMBER REPORTS:

Mayor Singer thanked the residents of the Deerhurst community for attending the meeting. He stated that
although the City was not responsible for providing irrigation for common areas and did not want to set a
precedent, there were options available, and the City was willing to help. Council Member Yvette Drucker stated
that she wouldn't be comfortable with the City taking over the costs for the Deerhurst irrigation and maintenance
as it could impact the entire City. Ms. Drucker was pleased the City had offered alternative options as a
resolution. Mayor Singer highlighted the upcoming Municipal Election on March 14.

Council Member Monica Mayotte spoke favorably of a concert that she attended in the studio at Mizner Park.

Mayor Singer concluded by mentioning that the State of the City Address was released online and he highlighted
his upcoming Town Hall at the Spanish River Library.
