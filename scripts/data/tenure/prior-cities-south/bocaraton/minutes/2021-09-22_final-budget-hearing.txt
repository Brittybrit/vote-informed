3.     REBUDGETING OF FY 2021-2022 BUDGET APPROPRIATIONS

Sharon McGuire, Office of Management and Budget Director, gave a PowerPoint presentation on the 2021-
2022 budget. Topics included, but were not limited to, a review of the City's mission, vision, and guiding
principles, millage and property tax comparison with 2020-2021, and assessed property valuations over the last
10 years. She then identified the increases or decreases in general operating expenses and reviewed the new
personnel and programs proposed for the coming year. Ms. McGuire then reviewed the tax bill flyer and
emphasized the comparison to other cities, noting that the City's property tax rate continues to be below that of
surrounding municipalities. The slight increase in the City's water rate was noted. Ms. McGuire reviewed
Exhibits 1-3 identifying the re-budgeted items that will be carried over to the 2021-22 fiscal year budget. Upon
concluding, Ms. McGuire and Mr. Ahnell made themselves available for questions.

          G.       City Council shall amend the adopted tentative budget as it sees fit

         Mayor Singer opened the discussion of the Non-Profit Funding requests and provided background of
         the increases to the non-profit items. Council Member Thomson suggested that those line items that
         received a majority of members' support be approved, and that discussion should be reserved for those
         items that did not receive a majority of members' votes. Discussion ensued. Council Member Mayotte
         noted that she transposed two numbers on her list and made the correction. Deputy Mayor O'Rourke
         suggested that the applications should be reviewed more in depth to determine if the funds granted
         were used in our community. Ms. O'Rourke noted that she inadvertently missed the Boca Raton
         Historical Society on her list and agreed to members' suggested amount of $12,000. After discussion,
         there was consensus to agree to the amounts that received at least 4 out of 5 votes for the same
         amounts. Members considered changes to the funding of those remaining non-profits. Working from
         the Mayor's list, changes were agreed to, resulting in an increase of approximately $10,000 to the
         budget.

         Referring to Exhibit 4 regarding requests for funding/changes, Mayor Singer discussed his request
         regarding initiatives resulting from the Harvard Bloomberg Innovation Track discussion. Members
         weighed in. Deputy Mayor O'Rourke gave a PowerPoint presentation in support of additional funds for
         an Art in Public Places program. She reviewed examples of art projects in other cities and request for
         additional funds to hire a consultant, draft a master plan and hire dedicated staff to manage. Mr. Ahnell
         provided additional information. Ms. O'Rourke then discussed her request for additional funds for the
         Boca Raton Museum of Art. Mr. Ahnell noted that the City currently provides an annual $100,000 grant
         to the museum. Discussion ensued and there was consensus to include $25,000 to continue the Art in
         Public Places initiative.

                               Motion was made by Deputy Mayor Andrea Levine O'Rourke, seconded by Mayor Scott
                               Singer, to amend Exhibit 4 to include $25,000 in funding to continue the Art in Public
                               Places initiative. Motion carried unanimously on a voice vote.

                               Motion was made by Deputy Mayor Andrea Levine O'Rourke, seconded by Mayor Scott
                               Singer, to amend the budget to include the FY2022 Non-Profit Organization Funding
                               Requests as amended. Motion carried unanimously on a voice vote.

5.      REGULAR PUBLIC HEARINGS:

         A.        Ordinance No. 5595
                   An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton relating to the General Employees' Pension Plan;
                   providing for a one-time lump sum payment paid for certain retired or disabled general
                   employees; providing for repealer; providing an effective date

         City Manager Leif Ahnell provided a brief explanation of the ordinance. Upon concluding, he made
         himself available for questions.

         The public hearing was opened. Irwin Cineus, organizer for the SEIU public service union, spoke in
         support of the ordinance. Tim Tritle and Steve Griffith expressed their support of the ordinance and
         made additional comments regarding union negotiations and benefits. Mr. Ahnell provided additional
         information on the lack of an annual COLA in the General Employees' Pension Plan. Seeing no one
         else wishing to speak, the public hearing was closed.

                               Motion was made by Council Member Monica Mayotte, seconded by Council Member
                               Yvette Drucker, to approve Ordinance No. 5595. Motion carried unanimously; Mayor
                               Scott Singer, Council Member Yvette Drucker, Council Member Monica Mayotte,
                               Council Member Andy Thomson voting yes.
