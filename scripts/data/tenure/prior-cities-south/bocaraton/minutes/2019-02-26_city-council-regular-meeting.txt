17.     QUASI-JUDICIAL PUBLIC HEARINGSNARIANCES & APPEALS:

                  Resolution No. 22-2019
                  A resolution of the City Council of the City of Boca Raton considering a Coastal Construction
                  Control Line (CCCL) variance, from Section 28-1556(3), Code of Ordinances, which variance is
                   required for the construction of a four-story, approximately 49 feet tall, 14,270 square foot duplex
                   residence seaward of the CCCL (where no structure is permitted seaward of the CCCL without a
                  variance). for a residential (R-3-F) zoned property located at 2600 North Ocean Boulevard;
                   providing for repealer; providing an effective date (SC-16-08)

         The City Attorney reviewed the quasi-judicial procedures that would govern the public hearing. Members
         made their ex parte disclosures. The City Clerk administered the oath to those who wished to speak.

         Brandon Schaad, Development Services Director, gave the PowerPoint presentation. The Code
         provisions related to the CCCL were reviewed. The location of the subject site was provided as were
         renderings and other details of the proposed building.           Mr. Schaad then reviewed the major
         environmental issues associated with this proposed project. Topics included, but were not limited to, sea
         turtle nesting data, dune vegetation impacts, and potential issues from storms and flooding. An evaluation
         of the applicant's response to variance criteria was outlined. Staff recommends denial of the request for
         this variance. It was noted that the EAB recommended denial as well. Mr. Schaad then responded to
         several comments made by the applicant at the EAB meeting.

         The public hearing was opened. Robert Sweetapple, on behalf of the petitioner, offered a number of
         emails and a video to illustrate his claim that certain members should refrain from voting on the variance
         request.   He then asked that three members recuse themselves. Mayor Singer, Council Members
         O'Rourke and Mayotte responded to Mr. Sweetapple's comments and declined to recuse themselves.
         Mr. Sweetapple then made his opening statement. He responded to several of Mr. Schaad's contentions.
         Topics included the FDEP permit, maps of natural lands within the City, response number of the
         statements made in staff's report, the City's beach lighting code, the building size, and Dr. Kirt Rusenko's
         expertise on sea turtles. Mr. Sweetapple asked that Dr. Rusenko be cross examined. The City Attorney
         reviewed the cross-examination procedures. Mr. Sweetapple asked Dr. Rusenko a number of questions
         centering on the lighting at the beach and its effect on turtle hatchlings. Mr. Sweetapple then introduced
         Juan Caycedo, architect of the project. Mr. Caycedo gave a brief PowerPoint, discussing the design of

        the building and its location on the lot. The project engineer, Bill Stoddard, then addressed the work done
        with the FDEP to establish the building footprint and the resulting design restraints. Bonnie Miskel,
        attorney on behalf of the applicant, addressed the requested variance from the CCCL and her contention
        that the criteria for said variance have been met. Historical information was provided. After a brief
        discussion regarding additional time, Ms. Miskel continued her presentation. Tom Tomasello, attorney,
        asserted his familiarity with the CCCL variance process with FDEP and the need for the City to issue a
        letter. He presented into evidence an email from the Florida Fish and Wildlife Commission regarding the
        site's effects on turtles. Emily Gentile, Jan Grennell, Kevin Meaney, David Ofstein, Mark Mannix, Faith
        Goldstein, Robert Denman, Joe Graubart, Tricia Krefetz, Susie Cox, Richard Rose, Ryan Abrams,
        Jessica Gray, Michael Lazio, Stewart Byrne, Dinath Rose, and Sue Merril all spoke in opposition to the
        project and in favor of preserving the beaches. Mr. Sweetapple requested to cross examine Tricia
        Krefetz with regard to her testimony. Mr. Michael Lazio asked to cross examine Mr. Sweetapple about his
        client's knowledge of the CCCL. There was no rebuttal. The public hearing was closed.

         Mayor Singer stated his opinion and moved to adopt the resolution to deny the variance.

         comments and stated their positions.
