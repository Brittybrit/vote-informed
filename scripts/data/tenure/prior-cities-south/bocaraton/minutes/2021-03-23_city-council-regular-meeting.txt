 12.     REGULAR PUBLIC HEARINGS:

          A        Ordinance No. 5551
                   (Second and final public hearing. Four votes required for adoption.)
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton considering amendments to the Infrastructure Element of
                  the City's Comprehensive Plan to update the City's 10-Year Water Supply Facilities Work Plan,
                  incorporate alternative water supply projects identified in the South Florida Water Management
                  District's Regional Water Supply Plan, and revise potable water level of service standards;
                  providing for severability; providing for repealer; providing an effective date (SC-20-03)

         Heather Hansen, Senior Planner, gave the PowerPoint presentation. Noting that this was the second public
         hearing, Ms. Hansen explained that the amendments had been transmitted in November 2020 for review
         by the Florida Department of Economic Opportunity (DEO), the South Florida Water Management District
         (SFWMD), and others. She identified the key elements of the Water Supply Facilities Work Plan that had
         been updated since its last approval in 2016. It was pointed out that comments submitted by Water
         Management District staff had been addressed and revisions had been included in the ordinance before
         Council. The DEO and SFWMD support the amendments and updated work plan, and City staff
         recommends approval. Upon concluding, Ms. Hansen made herself available for members' questions.

         The public hearing was opened and, seeing no one come forward, closed.

                                  Motion was made by Council Member Andy Thomson, seconded by Council
                                  Member Yvette Drucker, to approve Ordinance No. 5551. Motion carried
                                  unanimously; Mayor Scott Singer, Deputy Mayor Andrea Levine O'Rourke, Council
                                  Member Yvette Drucker, Council Member Monica Mayotte, Council Member Andy
                                  Thomson voting yes.

           B.      Ordinance No. 5568
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton amending Chapter 14, "Solid Waste," Section 14-20,
                  "Prohibited Activities," Code of Ordinances, to provide for enhanced minimum civil penalties for
                  various littering offenses, including littering of personal protective equipment used to prevent
                  disease transmission; providing for repealer; providing for codification; providing an effective date

         Council Member Thomson shared a slide presentation and explained the motivation for the ordinance.
         Council Member Drucker inquired if the Governor's mandate against fines for Covid-related offenses would
         impact the enforcement. City Attorney Diana Grub Frieser provided clarification, noting that this ordinance
         created a sub-category in the City's existing code enforcement structure. Council Member Mayotte and
         Deputy Mayor Andrea Levine O'Rourke expressed their support.

 20.    MAYOR AND COUNCIL MEMBER REPORTS:

Council Member Drucker reported on the recent Transportation Planning Agency (TPA) meeting. She noted the
rate of pedestrian/ bicyclist fatalities in the City and asked residents to be aware of cyclists and pedestrians when
driving.

Deputy Mayor O'Rourke congratulated the newly re-elected members of Council. She noted the high level of
vaccines available and reminded residents to not discard of PPE in the street. She also noted the Organizational
Meeting and upcoming Goal Setting sessions.

Council Member Thomson reported on the TPA meeting and the legislative policy visions, specifically the Vision
Zero program, the purpose of which is to eliminate traffic fatalities.

Council Member Mayotte commented on the importance of Home Rule and provided a report on the threat to
municipal government created by current bills coming before the State Senate. Ms. Mayotte thanked Senator Tina
Polsky's staff for their assistance and encouraged residents to reach out to their state representatives regarding
legislation. Among the bills she referenced were those that propose to limit local control of vacation rentals, preempt
local government from adopting design regulations for single- and two-family dwellings, and restrict municipalities
from making decisions in times of emergency. Ms. Mayotte concluded by welcoming new businesses to the City.

Mayor Singer referred to Vision Zero and noted that traffic enforcement in the downtown had doubled. He closed
by noting the retirement of City Clerk Susan Saxton and thanked her for her service.

 21.     ADJOURNMENT:
