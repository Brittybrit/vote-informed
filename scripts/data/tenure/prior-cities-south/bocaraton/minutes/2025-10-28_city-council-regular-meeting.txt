           C.      Resolution No. 160-2025
                   A resolution of the City of Boca Raton providing for submission to the electors of a proposed
                   Charter amendment prohibiting the lease, sale, or other alienation of City-owned land greater
                   than one-half (0.5) acre, or any part thereof, without voter approval by referendum ; providing
                   for s special election to be held on March 10, 2026; providing for severability; providing for
                   repealer; providing an effective date

                    City Attorney Joshua Koehler gave a presentation detailing the merits of the two resolutions
                    relating to the proposed Charter and Ordinance amendments. Upon concluding , he responded
                    to Members' questions.

                    The public hearing was opened. Alex Pollack, Joe Majhess, Mike Liebelson , Jon Pearlman ,
                    Richard Warner, and Jonathan Ounjian , all spoke against proposed Resolutions 159-2025
                    and 160-2025 and encouraged Members to take up the ordinances attached to each
                    resolution. Seeing no one else come forward, the public hearing was closed .

                            Motion was made by Council Member Marc Wigder, seconded by Deputy Mayor Fran
                            Nachlas, to approve Resolution No. 159-2025 as amended to reflect the Special
                            Election date of January 13, 2026.

                   Discussion ensued. Council Member Thomson stated that he approved of the residents
                   getting the opportunity to vote on the proposed amendments to the Charter and Ordinance.
                   Council Member Wigder advised that he did not agree with the proposed language in the
                   Charter and Ordinance amendments and noted that, as written , they could have unintended
                   negative consequences to both the City and its not-for-profit partners. Deputy Mayor Nachlas
                   and Council Member Drucker emphasized that the City Council must represent all residents
