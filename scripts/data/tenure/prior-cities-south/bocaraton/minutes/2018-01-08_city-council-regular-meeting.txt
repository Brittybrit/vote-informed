          Mayor Haynie stated that the public hearings on Ordinances 5433, 5434, and 5435 would be conducted
          contemporaneously. She noted that, as this was the first of two public hearings on the ordinances, no
          vote would be taken. The City Attorney clarified that the ordinances are legislative rather than quasi­
          judicial and the public hearing would be conducted accordingly.

          Brandon Schaad, Development Services Director, gave the PowerPoint presentation, beginning with
          background information on the Southwest Planning Area, commonly known as Midtown. Comprehensive
          Plan policies and objectives, including the Planned Mobility designation, applicable to this area were
          reviewed. It was noted that Land Development Regulations were required to implement the Planned
          Mobility designation. Further background information was provided. Mr. Schaad discussed in detail the


Regular Minutes                                        6                                                January 8, 2018
          changes to the use provisions as the County CG/CHO zoning is incorporated into the City Code. The
          proposed Planned Mobility Development (PMD) regulations were reviewed, noting that the underlying
          assumptions of a built and operational tri-rail station, functioning shuttle service and completed street
          infrastructure. Topics included, but were not limited to, dwelling unit size; street types and designs,
          including landscaping; sidewalks and crosswalks; bicycle lanes; building height and stepback; parking
          (on-street, structures, valet, surface lots); open space requirements; transit provisions other than Tri-rail;
          and the traffic analysis. Mr. Schaad addressed transit oriented development (TOD) and other planning
          considerations. The Planning and Zoning Board recommended approval of the ordinances with
          modifications, which Mr. Schaad reviewed. Staff also recommended approval. Upon concluding, he
          responded to numerous questions from members. Maria Tejara, Traffic Engineer, also responded to
          questions.

          The public hearing was opened. Michael Marshall, representing multiple property owners, generally
          offered support of the vision for the area but pointed out a number of ordinance provisions that are
          untenable. These provisions included the requirement for the tri-rail station, the timing of completion of
          the infrastructure, and building height requirements in the PMD overlay. He also discussed design
          elements, open space, enhanced shuttle service, and other requirements. Mr. Marshall then addressed
          the traffic analysis and the method for calculation of same. Bonnie Miskel, representing property owners
          west of Butts Road including Simon (the Mall), addressed three items in the ordinance: density, parking
          ratio, and allocation of rights. She pointed out that the transportation hub by the mall worked and,
          therefore, complete reliance on a tri-rail station was not necessary. In conclusion, Ms. Miskel asked that
          her client receive a minimum allocation of dwelling units, thanked the staff for their work on this matter
          and made herself available for questions. Kevin Plenzler, Fishkind & Associates, addressed the market
          study for residential development demand done by his firm, which was previously provided to the Council.
          Upon concluding, he responded to Council members' questions. Glenn Gromann, representing several
          property owners, addressed various elements of the ordinances including height, distance from single
          family residential, infrastructure vis-a-vis traffic, and TODs, among other topics. He suggested that
          property owners should be required to maintain the landscaping of medians. Seth Schneider expressed
          his concerns with the potential development that would be allowed should these ordinances be passed.
          He advocated having a master plan for the Midtown area. Gene Folden commented on the airport as it
          related to the Midtown area. He advocated that an avigation easement be a requirement of the Midtown
          development. Seeing no one else come forward to speak, the public hearing was closed.

          Members discussed, asking further questions. Mr. Schaad provided responses. Topics included, but
          were not limited to, unit size, the need for a master plan and a market study, infrastructure, the number of
          units, and traffic. Lengthy discussion ensued. Mayor Haynie announced the date of the second public
          hearing.
