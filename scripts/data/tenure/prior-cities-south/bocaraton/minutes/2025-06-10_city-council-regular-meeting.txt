          D.       Resolution No. 81-2025
                  A resolution of the City of Boca Raton , Florida, rejecting a recommendation issued pursuant to
                  Section 70.51 , Florida Statutes, known as the "Florida Land Use and Environmental Dispute
                  Resolution Act" (FLUEDRA), regarding approximately 4 acres of predominantly submerged
                  real property adjacent to the lntracoastal Waterway , located at 3000 NE 8th Avenue and owned
                  by NE 32nd Street, LLC; providing for a written decision regarding allowable uses; providing
                  for severability; providing for repealer; providing for an effective date

          City Manager George Brown provided an overview of the proposed resolution and history of the
          submerged property at NE 8th Avenue. He advised that staff supported the adoption of the resolution .
          City Attorney Josh Koehler reviewed the legal issues pertaining to the property.

          Hope Calhoun, attorney for the property owner, advised that they did not support the denial of this
          Resolution and asked that the property owner be allowed to fill the submerged land and develop the
          property. William Swaim, owner of the property, also urged Council Members to reject the
          recommendation by staff.

          Mr. Koehler provided legal details and responded to Members' questions. Further discussion ensued .

 10.     RESOLUTIONS AND OTHER BUSINESS:

          A.      Government Campus Redevelopment Partnership Opportunity

                  1.      Reports from Staff and CBRE

                   Deputy City Manager Andy Lukasik provided an update to the interim master plan and advised
                   that a voice vote could be taken to approve the amended changes to the uses, densities, and
                   intensity. Discussion ensued. Council Member Drucker advised that this master plan should
                   include a transit hub. Deputy Mayor Nachlas also supported the addition of a transit hub and
                   enhanced recreation . Council Member Wigder thanked staff and the consultants for their work
                   and advised that this was a checkpoint along a long path . Mayor Singer advised that there
                   would be more public outreach in the future and plans would continue to evolve.

                  2.      Public Comment

                   Judy Morrow spoke in support of keeping tennis in the Downtown area and urged Council to
                   build eight courts in both the Downtown and Meadows Park.

                   Jon Pearlman expressed his opposition to the Government Campus Redevelopment.

                   Evan Bennett spoke against over development, questioned the bifurcation of the Police
                   Station, and the size of City Hall.

                   Patty Dervishi shared concerns about over development of the Downtown .

                   Alexandra Avala spoke against the redevelopment of the Government Campus.

                   Mayor Singer and City Manager George Brown offered additional information and reiterated
                   that plans have been downsized and would continue to develop.

                   Council Member Thomson noted that the City was offering a sensible and responsible
                   development for both the green space , recreation, and government facilities but stated that
                   he was concerned with the amount of commercial development. He advised that he would not
                   support tonight's Interim Plan as presented. Extended discussion ensued . Council Member
                   Drucker proposed a transit hub and reiterated that the scale and scope of the project has been
                   downsized. Council Member Wigder explained that the P3 partnership was necessary for the
                   affordability of the Government Campus. Council Member Thomson advised that the
                   residents' questions needed to be addressed before he would vote in favor of the plan.

                          Motion was made by Mayor Scott Singer, seconded by Council Member Yvette
                          Drucker, to approve staff to move forward on the Interim Master Plan.

                   Additional discussion ensued .

                          Motion passed on a vote of 4 to 1; Mayor Scott Singer, Deputy Mayor Fran Nachlas,
                          Council Member Yvette Drucker, Council Member Marc Wigder voting yes. Council
                          Member Andy Thomson voting no.

The meeting was recessed at 8: 11 PM and resumed at 8:24 PM .

 11.     QUASI-JUDICIAL AND RELATED PUBLIC HEARINGS:

          A.      900 Broken Sound PMD/CIMD

                   1)     Resolution No. 78-2025
                          A resolution of the City of Boca Raton considering an amendment to an approved
                          Phased Planned Mobility Development (PMD) Master Plan (Resolution No. 56-2014,
                          as amended by Resolution No. 90-2016) to (i) amend the phase lines such that an
                          existing approximately 116,316 square foot office building is located in Phase II (rather
                          than in Phase I) (ii) amend the tabular data for Phase I to reflect the as-built
                          development and (iii) authorize the construction of a seven (?)-story, 289-unit multi­
                          family residential building, a parking structure and related site improvements as Phase
                          II of the Master Plan; for the properties generally located at 850 Northwest Broken
                          Sound Parkway (Phase I) and 900 Northwest Broken Sound Parkway (Phase II);
                          providing for repealer; providing an effective date (PMD-13-01 R4/PMD)

                  2)      Resolution No. 79-2025
                          A resolution of the City of Boca Raton considering an amendment to an approved site
                          plan (Resolution No. 57-2014, as amended by Resolution No. 91-2016) for a
                          Commercial Industrial Multifamily Development (CIMO) to authorize construction, in
                          Phase II of a Planned Mobility Development (PMD) Master Plan, a new seven (?)­
                          story, 289-unit multi-family residential building, including 10% affordable and 5%
                          workforce housing units, a parking structure and related site improvements, together
                          with one (1) technical deviation from Section 23-190(d)2 , Code of Ordinances, to
                          reduce the required driveway reservoir distance for the easternmost driveway on
                          Northwest Broken Sound Parkway from 100 feet to 70 feet; for the property generally
                          located at 850 and 900 Northwest Broken Sound Parkway; providing for repealer;
                          providing an effective date (PMD-13-01 R4/SPA/CIMD)

          The Mayor noted that the public hearings would be conducted contemporaneously as the items were
          interrelated. He noted that a separate vote would be taken on each item and should item 11.A.1 . not
          be approved, no vote would be taken on the remaining item. The City Attorney read the quasi-judicial
          procedures which would govern the public hearing. Members made their ex-parte disclosures. The
          City Clerk administered the oath to those who wished to speak.

          Senior Planner Eoin Devlin gave the PowerPoint presentation. He began with an overview of the
          property location, history, proposed zoning amendments, and technical deviations. He advised that
          the Community Advisory Board recommended approval with modifications, and they were
          subsequently incorporated into the plans. He further advised that the Planning and Zoning Board and
          staff recommended approval and , upon concluding, made himself available for questions.
