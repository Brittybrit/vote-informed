10.   QUASI-JUDICIAL AND RELATED PUBLIC HEARINGS:

       A      Resolution No. 104-2024
              A resolution of the City of Boca Raton considering , for the approximately 11 .87-acre property
              generally located at 1800 North Military Trail, an amendment to an approved site plan (Planning
              and Zoning Board Resolution No. 05-29 as amended by Resolution No. 07-21) for a
              Commercial Industrial Multifamily Development (CIMO) to authorize the construction of a new
              six (6)-story, 295-unit multi-family residential building , including 10% affordable and 5%
              workforce housing units, and related site improvements; providing for repealer; providing an
              effective date (SPA-04-25/R4)

       The City Attorney read the quasi-judicial procedures which would govern the public hearing. Members
       made their ex parte disclosures. The City Clerk administered the oath to those who wished to speak.

       Peter Begovich , Senior Planner, gave the presentation . He began with a review of the request, project
       location, and overall site plan of the property. Multimodal features were reviewed, and Mr. Begovich
       highlighted the public benefits of the proposed project. Elevations and renderings were displayed , as
       well as photos of the existing office building . The affordable units and workforce rental information was
       outlined, and staff analysis showed that the project is compatible with adjacent land uses and complies
       with all applicable code requirements. Mr. Begovich noted that the applicant requested modifications
       and both the Planning and Zoning Board and Development Services staff recommended approval.
       Upon concluding , Mr. Begovich made himself available for questions. Brandon Schaad, Development
       Services Director, provided information regarding staff challenges of the first project under the CIMO.
       Discussion ensued on topics including processing applications for affordable housing and verification
       of income.

       The public hearing was opened . Ele Zachariades, attorney for the applicant, gave the presentation .
       Ms. Zachariades began by highlighting the site plan and CIMO regulations. She then reviewed the
       setback and landscape buffers and advised that the project met workforce housing and equivalency
       requirements and exceeded parking requirements. David Abers, on behalf of the applicant, continued
       the presentation with an expanded summary of the location, project renderings , and the restricted
       covenant. Lon Tabachnick expressed his approval of the project but stated that he did not support the
       restricted covenant. Jonathan Ounjian shared his approval. Bonnie Miske! commented on the
       cooperation between City staff and the developers but noted her concern with some details of the
       covenant. Mr. Koehler noted that, should Council wish to move forward , the covenant could be
       amended at a future date. Discussion ensued and Mr. Schaad and Mr. Koehler provided additional
       information. Mr. Schaad proposed alternative language in page 18, as did Ms. Miskel, and Mr. Brown
       suggested additional language on page 7. Discussion continued and there was consensus to move
       forward with additional language in the covenant as discussed. There was no cross examination .
       During the rebuttal, Mr. Abers commented further on the covenant being a work in progress but stated
       that the document was acceptable in its current form. Mr. Brown suggest further additions to the
       language on page 9. Ms. Zachariades suggested the Council move forward with a vote and that the
       revised covenant could be revisited at a future date. Members weighed in, both Mr. Koehler and Mr.
       Brown read the amended language, and the applicant provided their approval of the revisions . The
       public hearing was closed .

                       Motion was made by Council Member Andy Thomson, seconded by Council Member
                       Fran Nachlas, to approve the amended language to Resolution No. 104-2024. Motion
                       carried unanimously on a voice vote.

        B.      Resolution No. 105-2024
               A resolution of the City Council of the City of Boca Raton considering, for a Residential (R-3-
               F) zoned approximately 0.42 acre property generally located at 2600 North Ocean Boulevard,
               a Coastal Construction Control Line (CCCL) variance, from Section 28-1556(3), Code of
               Ordinances, which variance is required for the construction of a four-story, approximately 38-
               foot tall, 6,931 square-foot single-family residence seaward of the CCCL (where no structure
               is permitted seaward of the CCCL without a variance); providing for repealer; providing an
               effective date (SC-16-08)

        The City Attorney read the quasi-judicial procedures which would govern the public hearing. Members
        made their ex parte disclosures. The City Clerk administered the oath to those who wished to speak.

        Chief Planner Ta mash been Rahman gave the PowerPoint presentation . She began with a description
        of the application and advised that the request for variance was being heard as part of a re-hearing
        process. The subject property was described , and a site plan was shown . Placement of the proposed
        building on the property was outlined and Ms. Rahman advised that the project met both zoning and
        dimensional requirements. Renderings of the proposed building were displayed and a chart comparing
        the previous proposal with the current application was presented . She reviewed the applicant's
        updated Environmental Assessment Report and listed conditions of approval including , but not limited
        to, phased construction work, a sea turtle lighting plan , a tree pruning plan , and pre-construction
        requirements. Ms. Rahman advised that the Environmental Advisory Board (EAB) recommended
        denial. Development Services staff recommended approval of the revised application, including the
        conditions of approval, subject to the applicant obtaining approvals from all necessary State agencies.
        Upon concluding, Mr. Brown, Ms. Rahman, and Mr. Schaad responded to questions.

        The public hearing was opened . William Stoddard , representative on behalf of the applicant, gave a
        PowerPoint presentation . Mr. Stoddard began with a review of his professional background and
        highlighted the number of coastal construction permits approved throughout the state of Florida. A
        history of the project and list of professional consultants were shown . Mr. Stoddard noted the
        collaboration between project consultants and City staff to make plan revisions following the court
        ordered rehearing from the 4th District of Appeals. The minimization of impacts, strict adherence to
        building codes, landscaping plans, and comparison of prior and current proposals were discussed.
        Renderings of the proposed building 's elevations were shown , and Mr. Stoddard noted that the
        footprint of the building was landward and not in the location of recorded marine turtle nests. Robert
        Sweetapple, attorney for the applicant, continued the presentation . He noted that the applicant agreed
        to every condition requested by the City, that the property had originally been platted for residential
        development, and that public comments made at the EAB meeting objecting to the project were not
        quasi-jud icially accepted evidence. Mr. Sweetapple stated that the standards to grant the CCCL
        variance had been met and asked Council to recognize his client's rights. Tucker Gibbs, representing
        homeowners of the Yacht and Raquet Club, expressed the concerns of the Club's residents that the
        project had not met the criteria for a variance and requested denial. Stephen Margolis spoke against
        granting a variance for the project citing potential harm to the environment and public safety concerns.
        Jorge Salinger, president of the Yacht and Raquet Club , expressed concerns regarding the sea turtles,
        vegetation , and the dunes. Michael Laszlo stated that he believed the applicant failed to meet the

        of lighting on the sea turtles. Matthew Pintchik, Richard Hutner, Lewis Kurtz, Jaime Akkusu , Linda
