3.     PRESENTATION ON TENTATIVE BUDGET, PROPOSED MILLAGE RATES. AND CAPITAL
       IMPROVEMENTS PROGRAM
             Proposed CIP

City Manager Leif Ahnell gave a PowerPoint presentation reviewing the proposed budget for the coming fiscal
year. The goals and priorities set by Council during the May 2016 Strategic Planning Sessions were listed; details
were provided. The recommended millage rate for the 2016-17 fiscal year is 3.6789, which is a slight decrease
from last year. Mr. Ahnell then reviewed proposed increases and decreases in various operating costs, as well as
detailing new personnel. There followed a number of charts and graphs illustrating the various taxing authorities
on a resident's tax bill; assessed property values in Boca Raton; tax rate history; and tax rates, fire assessment,
library, and water/sewer rates comparing Boca Raton favorably with other neighboring municipalities. He then
turned the presentation over to Sharon McGuire, Office of Management and Budget Director, who reviewed the
2016/17-2021-/22 Capital Improvement Program (CIP). Ms. McGuire noted the sources of revenue that will fund
capital improvements and then reviewed, by department, the projects, vehicles and equipment included in the CIP
for 2016-2017 fiscal year. Upon concluding, Ms. McGuire and Mr. Ahnell responded to questions from Council
members.

          D.      Open public hearing on tentative budget and proposed millage rates.
                         Presentations by non-profit organizations regarding funding requests.

         The public hearing was opened. No member of the public came forward to speak. At this time,
         representatives of 40 non-profit organizations addressed Council and requested funding for the coming
         year.

          E.      City Council "shall amend the tentative budget as it sees fit."

         The Mayor stated that no amendments would be made at this meeting.

         The Mayor announced there was no recomputation of the proposed millage rate at this time.

         G.       Public announcement of percent, if any, by which recomputed proposed millage rates
                  exceed "rolled-back rate", characterized as "percentage decrease in property taxes
                  tentatively adopted."

         No announcement was necessary as there was no recomputation.
