 11.     REGULAR PUBLIC HEARINGS:


          A.      Ordinance No. 5680
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton amending the City's Comprehensive Plan by amending
                  Future Land Use Element Policies LU.1 .1.5 and LU .1.1.6. and adding new housing element
                  policy HO.1. 3.23 to establish a framework for the affordable housing provisions of Subsections
                  166.04151 (6) and (7), Florida Statutes; providing for severability; providing for repealer;
                  providing an effective date (SC-23-14) The public hearings on Items B. and B.1. will be
                  conducted contemporaneously as the items are related. A separate vote will be taken on Items
                  B. and B.1. Should Item B. not be approved, no vote will be taken on item B.1 .

          Mayor Singer noted that this was a transmittal hearing and the first of two public hearings. The second
          hearing would be after the return of the State's review of the Ordinance.

          Development Services Director Brandon Schaad gave the PowerPoint presentation. He began by
          explaining that the ordinance was drafted at the request of Council to address an optional provision
          in subsection six of the Live Local Act of 2023 regarding affordable housing and workforce housing.
          The number of dwelling units and units per acre of the proposed regulations were reviewed, as well
          as mandatory provisions of the Act. Mr. Schaad advised that both the Planning and Zoning Board
          and staff recommended approval and reviewed the future public hearing schedule for the ordinance.
          Upon concluding, he made himself available for Members' questions.

          The public hearing was opened. Jonathan Ounjian spoke against the proposed limits of affordable
          housing units, noting that he did not believe this ordinance to be necessary. Seeing no one else come
          forward, the public hearing was closed.

 13.     PUBLIC REQUESTS:

  Amy Price expressed concerns about large-scale implementation of affordable and workforce housing due to
  the potential impacts on existing infrastructure and suggested that it be initially addressed as a pilot program.

  Steve Reiss spoke on the recent surge of auto crimes and noted the increase in police presence.

  Michael Majhess shared his distress regarding auto crimes in the City and the increasing boldness of
  criminals, especially in the Golden Harbour and Golden Triangle areas. He asked for better communication
  with City staff and additional police assistance.

 Steve Griffith commented on the biennial review of benefits for the General Employee's Pension Plan and
 asked that, in light of the general employee's service to the City, the cost-of-living-adjustment (COLA) for
 general employees be reconsidered.

 Matthew Chapnick highlighted the work of staff of the City's parks and beaches and asked that the City take
 care of the general union employees.

 Joseph Brenner, representative of City employees on behalf of the Service Employees International Union
 (SEIU), spoke about the one-time lump sum given to non-union represented City employees and asked for
 consideration that the same be given to employees represented by the SEIU.

 Roy Ageloff commented about the escalation of auto crimes and expressed concern that the crimes could
 become more serious.

 Gregory Ryan noted the work done by representatives of the general employee's union and shared his
 disappointment that the lump sum given to non-bargaining employees was not given to union members.

 Joe Majhess remarked on the increase in frequency and severity of auto crimes in the City and asked for a
 public education campaign to raise awareness of the issue, as well as a review of current police policies and
 procedures.

 Stacy McLaughlin relayed her concerns about safety in light of the recent auto crimes in the Golden Harbour
 area.

 David Bungarz advised that he was part of the most recent SEIU negotiations and asked that the lump sum
 payment be revisited for general union employees.

 Tom Desernia asked for additional collaboration between the City and residents in those areas most affected
 by the recent increase in crime.

 Mayor Singer responded to concerns regarding public safety and the rise in auto crimes, union negotiations,
 the public request timeclock, and the Live Local Act. At the request of Mayor Singer, Mr. Brown provided
 clarification regarding Florida statutes and the City's pursuit policy. He noted that staff could work on a public
 education campaign and that the issue regarding a lack of communication would be addressed . Mr. Brown
 also advised that patrols had been increased to the areas most affected and that there were other actions
 being taken that were not publicized. Council Members each relayed their commitment to public safety and
 to enhanced communication with residents. Mr. Brown reiterated that staff would work on public education
 and outreach to residents regarding the recent rise in auto thefts. He also expressed his gratitude to the
 general employees and the work that they do to keep the City running and advised that he would look into
 the issues they raised. The Council Members thanked the union members present for relaying their concerns
 and shared their appreciation for the dedication to the City .
