         E.       Resolution No. 135-2021
                 A resolution of the City Council of the City of Boca Raton considering waivers from certain
                 standards and guidelines for marquee signs set forth in the lnterlocal Agreement dated March
                 18, 2009, between the School Board of Palm Beach County and the City, to authorize marquee
                 signs at specified elementary and K-8 schools and waive certain provisions limiting marquee sign
                 width, scrolling text and use of pictures and logos, for the following schools: (i) Verde K-8 School
                 located at 6590 Verde Trail, (ii) Addison Mizner K-8 School located at 199 Southwest 12th
                 Avenue, and (iii) 05-C Boca Area Elementary School located at 1000 Northwest Spanish River
                 Boulevard; providing for severability; providing for repealer; providing an effective date (SC-21-
                 05)

         John Lindgren, Senior Planner, gave the PowerPoint presentation. He briefly reviewed the elements of
         the waivers requested from the Verde K-8 School, the Addison Mizner K-8 School, and the 05-C
         Elementary School. Among the requests were the installations of the marquee signs themselves,
         consent for photos and logos to be on the signs, and the use of scrolling text. Mr. Lindgren outlined
         proposed additional conditions that addressed the allowed brightness of the illuminated signs and hours
         of use. He advised that representatives of the School District had agreed to the conditions. Upon
         concluding, Mr. Lindgren made himself available for questions.

         The public comment period was opened and, seeing no one come forward, closed.

                         Motion was made by Council Member Andy Thomson, seconded by Council Member
                         Monica Mayotte, to approve Resolution No. 135-2021 as amended. Motion carried
                         unanimously; Mayor Scott Singer, Deputy Mayor Andrea Levine O'Rourke, Council
                         Member Yvette Drucker, Council Member Monica Mayotte, Council Member Andy
                         Thomson voting yes.

 11.     QUASI-JUDICIAL AND RELATED PUBLIC HEARINGS

          A.     Ordinance No. 5596
                 An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton considering, for property consisting of approximately 1.6
                 acres, located at 690 and 698 Jeffery Street, and 651 and 699 Kingsbridge Street, a Universal
                 Conditional (UC) rezoning, pursuant to Article VI, Chapter 23, Code of Ordinances, from the R-2
                 and R-3 Multifamily Residential zoning districts to the R-1-G Single-Family Residential zoning


Regular Agenda                                               5                                      September 28, 2021
                 district, subject to conditions, to authorize the future development of ten (10) single-family homes
                 on existing platted lots, and associated improvements; providing for repealer; providing an
                 effective date (UC-21-02/ZC)

         The City Attorney reviewed the quasi-judicial procedures that would govern the public hearing. Council
         Members made their ex parte disclosures. The City Clerk administered the oath to those who wished
         to speak.

         John Lindgren, Senior Planner, gave the PowerPoint presentation. He began by reviewing the
         application and displayed aerial photos of the subject property. The proposed plan and lot detail were
         shown, and sidewalk and pedestrian improvements were outlined. Mr. Lindgren advised that the
         proposed rezoning was compatible with existing future land use designation. The Planning and Zoning
         Board recommended approval subject to modifications to the conditions of approval. Staff recommended
         approval. Upon concluding, Mr. Lindgren made himself available for members' questions.

         The public hearing was opened. Bradley Miller, on behalf of the applicant, made brief comments and
         thanked staff for their presentation. Marie Thomson spoke in support of the ordinance but expressed
         concerns about traffic from new development. Mr. Miller responded to Ms. Thomson's comments. Seeing
         no one else come forward, the public hearing was closed.

                         Motion was made by Mayor Scott Singer, seconded by Council Member Andy Thomson,
                         to approve Ordinance No. 5596.

          Zack Bihr, Municipal Services Director, provided information about traffic calming policies, how
          problem areas are identified and how the traffic study process is implemented.

                         Motion carried unanimously; Mayor Scott Singer, Deputy Mayor Andrea Levine
                         O'Rourke, Council Member Yvette Drucker, Council Member Monica Mayotte, Council
                         Member Andy Thomson voting yes.

 12.    REGULAR PUBLIC HEARINGS:

          A.      Ordinance No. 5597
                 An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton authorizing a Sixteenth Amendment to the Agreement for
                 Purchase and Sale dated November 27, 2017, by and between the City of Boca Raton and G.L.
                 Acquisitions Corporation for the purchase and sale of the City of Boca Raton Municipal Golf
                 Course West, as authorized by Ordinance No. 541 O; authorizing modifications to the agreement
                 to address matters related to finalizing and closing on the sale, as set forth in the Amendment;
                 providing for severability; providing for repealer; providing an effective date

          Deputy City Manager George Brown gave the PowerPoint presentation. An aerial photograph detailing
          the location of the Municipal Golf Course was displayed. Mr. Brown summarized the provisions of the
          ordinance, which included, but were not limited to, changes to the public safety communications tower
          location, verification of the end of the inspection period, confirmation that the City will have no
          obligations for City property within the parcel, and the assurance of access to the communication tower
          site(s). He then reviewed the quit claim deed, easement deeds, and execution of plats associated with
          sale of the property and approved through prior resolutions. Mr. Brown noted that the ordinance was
          being considered as revised, and that staff recommended approval. Upon concluding he made himself
          available for questions.

           The public hearing was opened and, seeing no come forward, closed.

                         Motion was made by Council Member Yvette Drucker, seconded by Council Member
                         Monica Mayotte, to approve Ordinance No. 5597 as revised. Motion carried unanimously;
                         Mayor Scott Singer, Deputy Mayor Andrea Levine O'Rourke, Council Member Yvette
                         Drucker, Council Member Monica Mayotte, Council Member Andy Thomson voting yes.

 14.    PUBLIC REQUESTS:

Christine Lyons, Lanie Gospavic, Brian Clinton, Laura Sweeney, Brendon Brennon, Amy Lang, Sheila Mahon,
Sara Marieno, and Shari Ochrimenko expressed concerns regarding an application for an assisted living facility
(ALF) proposed to be built on NW 12th Avenue. Barbara Caccavale-Hodges also shared her opposition to the
proposed ALF and provided resident petitions regarding the same.

 Jennifer Thompson, the new CEO of Habitat for Humanity for South Palm Beach County, introduced herself to
 the Mayor and Council.

 Denise Sheffler, Roberta Lee, Beverly Sammit, and Gail Borsh off remarked on the City's acquisition of the golf
 course at the Boca Raton Golf and Racquet Club and shared their disappointment that the City would not be
 keeping their Country Club's pool open.

 Mr. Ahnell responded to comments and questions regarding the Boca Raton Golf & Racquet Club. He listed a
 number of projects planned to improve the golf course, tennis courts and associated buildings, and address
 security and traffic concerns. He outlined the cost of keeping the pool running, and explained that if kept open,
 it would need to be available to all City residents. Mr. Ahnell explained that City staff had been communicating
 with the Master Association on dozens of issues and had conducted a community outreach Zoom meeting in
 August. Members weighed in. The City Manager then addressed comments regarding the proposed ALF and
 explained that the project was very early in the application process. Mr. Schaad provided additional information
 and advised that the timeline for a proposed project to go from application to a public hearing can take
 anywhere from several months to years.

 16.     RESOLUTIONS AND OTHER BUSINESS:

          A       Resolution No. 137-2021
                 A resolution of the City of Boca Raton considering the application of Gwendolyn and Lestan
                 Quintanilla for a settlement and reduction of code enforcement liens imposed against property
                 located at 450 NE 37th Street; providing for severability; providing for repealer; providing an
                 effective date

        Brandon Schaad, Development Services Director, reviewed the resolution. He provided the history of the
        property and reviewed Code Enforcement violations that had accrued over approximately one and a half
        years. It was noted that the Special Magistrate had issued an order of Enforcement requiring the applicant
        to fix the violations and comply with City code. Due to cancellation of Special Magistrate meetings
        because of Covid-19, however, the Magistrate had reduced the fines due. Upon concluding, Mr. Schaad
        responded to Members' questions.



Regular Agenda                                               7                                      September 28, 2021
       The public hearing was opened. Spencer Siegal, representing Mr. and Mrs. Quintanilla, gave a brief
       presentation. He noted that two of the Code issues pre-dated his clients' ownership of the property and
       had now been corrected. Mr. Siegal stated that the owners did not live at the property, but that it was
       being used by a relative during the time of the Code infractions. He advised that the Code infractions had
       been corrected and the property had since been sold. Seeing no one else come forward to speak, the
       public hearing was closed.

                        Motion was made by Mayor Scott Singer, seconded by Council Member Andy Thomson,
                        to deny the request for lien reduction.

         Mr. Siegal explained that his clients were under the impression that if the Code Enforcement violations
         were corrected, that the lien would be reduced. Mr. Schaad advised that staff had been clear that staff
         would only be making a recommendation to Council, and that would not bind the Council Members in
         any way. Members weighed in.

                        Motion carried unanimously; Mayor Scott Singer, Deputy Mayor Andrea Levine
                        O'Rourke, Council Member Yvette Drucker, Council Member Monica Mayotte, Council
                        Member Andy Thomson voting yes.
