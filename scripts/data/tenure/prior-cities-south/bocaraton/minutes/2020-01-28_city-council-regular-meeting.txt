         REGULAR PUBLIC HEARINGS:

          A.       Ordinance No. 5512
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton amending Chapter 19, "Building Regulations," by creating
                  Article Ill, Division 1, "Permits," Section 19-100, "Temporary Opening Protection During Hurricane
                  Warning," Code of Ordinances, to authorize installation of temporary wood structural panels for
                  protection against wind-borne debris during a declared hurricane warning on certain existing
                  buildings and structures without obtaining a building permit; providing for severability; providing for
                  repealer; providing for codification; providing an effective date (AM-19-04)

         John Cosmo, Chief Building Official, gave the PowerPoint presentation. The proposed revisions were
         reviewed. Mr. Cosmo noted that the amendments applied not only to storm preparation, but also post-storm
         safety concerns. The amendments are consistent with the City's Comprehensive Plan. Upon concluding,
         Mr. Cosmo responded to members' questions.

         The public comment period was opened. Glenn Gromann expressed his support for the ordinance and
         commented on a property within the City that was potentially in violation of City code. Seeing no one else
         come forward, the public comment period was closed.

         Members made final comments regarding the use of permanent storm shutters.

                  Motion was made by Council Member Andrea Levine O'Rourke, seconded by Council Member
                  Monica Mayotte, to approve Ordinance No. 5512. Motion carried unanimously; Mayor Scott Singer,
                  Deputy Mayor Jeremy Rodgers, Council Member Monica Mayotte, Council Member Andrea Levine
                  O'Rourke, Council Member Andy Thomson voting yes.

           B.     Ordinance No. 5515
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton amending Chapter 19, "Building Regulations," by amending
                  Article Ill, Division 2, "Construction Documents," Section 19-113, "Minimum Plan Review Criteria
                  for Buildings," Code of Ordinances, to exempt certain minor work on one- and two-family
                  residential buildings and townhouses from plans examination by the Building Official (while still
                  requiring applicable permits); providing for severability; providing for repealer; providing for
                  codification; providing an effective date (AM-19-10)

         John Cosmo, Chief Building Official, gave the PowerPoint presentation. Mr. Cosmo began with a review of
         the proposed amendments for plan review exemptions, among them, permits for repairs to NC units, roof
         repairs, and installation of impact shutters for windows and doors. He noted that the proposed amendments
         do not exempt the repairs or renovations from permitting or building inspection requirements. Upon
         concluding, Mr. Cosmo made himself available for questions.

           C.     Ordinance No. 5514
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton amending the Boca Raton Downtown Development of
                  Regional Impact (DORI) Development Order to modify a transportation related condition to
                  eliminate a requirement that the northbound South Federal Highway to eastbound Southeast
                  Mizner Boulevard right turn lane be "free flowing"; providing for severability; providing for repealer;
                  providing an effective date (SC-19-05)

         Susan Lesser, Senior Planner, gave the PowerPoint presentation. Ms. Lesser began by providing the
         background of the request and a summary of the analysis completed by FOOT and the City's consultant.
         Aerial views of the intersection and marked improvements were displayed. The ordinance is consistent with
         the City's Comprehensive Plan. The Planning and Zoning Board recommends approval. Staff recommends
         approval. Ms. Lesser provided additional information in response to comments from the January 27 th CRA
         meeting regarding beautification of the subject area. Upon concluding, Ms. Lesser made herself available
         for questions.

         The public comment period was opened. Paul Borman thanked staff for the change. Glenn Gromann
         commented on the potential traffic impact of the current bus stop location in the free-flow lane. Seeing no
         one else come forward, the public comment period was closed. Development Services Director Brandon
         Schaad addressed the bus stop site. Council Member O'Rourke expressed concerns regarding pedestrian
         considerations.

                  Motion was made by Council Member Andy Thomson, seconded by Council Member Andrea
                  Levine O'Rourke, to approve Ordinance No. 5514. Motion carried unanimously; Mayor Scott
                  Singer, Deputy Mayor Jeremy Rodgers, Council Member Monica Mayotte, Council Member Andrea
                  Levine O'Rourke, Council Member Andy Thomson voting yes.

           D.      Ordinance No. 5513
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton considering amendments to Chapter 28 "Zoning," Code of
                  Ordinances, with regard to pharmacies and medical marijuana treatment center dispensing
                  facilities; to amend the term "neighborhood serving retail" to clarify that pharmacies and medical
                  marijuana treatment center dispensing facilities may be permitted in "Neighborhood Serving Retail"
                  and to define the term "Pharmacies"; to amend the following zoning districts to allow pharmacies
                  and medical marijuana treatment center dispensing facilities as a permitted use: Local Business
                  (B-1) District, Community Business (B-2) District, Central Business (B-3) District, General Business
                  (B-4) District, Commercial (C-1) District, City General Commercial (CG) District, City Commercial
                  High Office (CHO) District, Light Industrial Research Park (LIRP) District, Manufacturing Industrial
                  (M-3) District, Medical Center (MC) District, Motel-Business (R-B-1) District, Village Center (VC)
                  District, Professional, Office and Institutional (POI) District; to amend the following zoning districts
                  to allow pharmacies and medical marijuana treatment center dispensing facilities as a conditional
                  use: Neighborhood Convenience Business (NCBD) District, Warehouse (W-1) District and within
                  commercial nodes of residential planned unit developments; to provide zoning regulations for
                  pharmacies and medical marijuana treatment center dispensing facilities; to amend the prohibited
                  uses in all zoning districts section to remove "medical marijuana treatment center dispensing
                  facilities" as a prohibited use; providing for severability; providing for repealer; providing for
                  codification; providing an effective date (AM-19-08)

         Mayor Singer noted that this was the first of two public hearings and no vote would be taken.

         Tamash Rahman, Chief Planner, gave the PowerPoint presentation. Ms. Rahman began with an overview
         of revisions to the agenda item to reflect the final ordinance. She then provided a brief background of the
         legislation and a map of existing dispensaries in municipalities surrounding the City limits. A list of zoning

         districts within the City that currently allow pharmacies was reviewed. The proposed changes to City
         regulations were discussed. The Planning and Zoning Board vote on the ordinance was 2 to 2. Ms. Rahman
         noted that staff recommends denial of the ordinance due to uncertainty in regard to medical marijuana
         legislation and case law, as well as current state law that inhibits the ability of municipalities to limit or
         manage such facilities. Upon concluding, she made herself available for questions.

         The public comment period was opened. Eric Seville expressed his general support of the ordinance and
         proposed several changes to the language. Robert Eisen, on behalf of Investments Limited, expressed
         concern with the 5,000 square foot size requirement. Charles Felix spoke in support of the ordinance.
         Lauren Niehaus, on behalf of Harvest Health and Recreation, Inc., asked Council to approve the ordinance.
         Daniel Sparks, on behalf of Truelieve, commented in support of the ordinance. Glenn Gromann remarked
         on the current medical marijuana industry and expressed his opposition to the ordinance. Mark Wigder
         discussed potential conflicts with federal and state law regarding marijuana dispensaries and stated that
         he opposed the ordinance. Jason Ibarra spoke in support of the ordinance. Seth Penalver commented in
         support of the ordinance. Cristina Escalante, in support of the ordinance, remarked that although there were
         nearby dispensaries in neighboring cities, the locations were not convenient for those using public
         transportation. Seeing no one else come forward, the public hearing was closed. Mayor Singer noted that
         the second public hearing on this item would be on February 11, 2020.

 20.     MAYOR AND COUNCIL MEMBER REPORTS:

Council Member Mayotte commented on members' trip to represent the City at Palm Beach County days in
Tallahassee, as well as her recent visit to Boca Elementary to read to students.

Deputy Mayor Rodgers echoed Ms. Mayotte's positive remarks on their trip to Tallahassee and asked the City
Attorney about the Ocean Breeze land acquisition. City Attorney Diana Grub Frieser reviewed points of the original
interlocal agreement with the Greater Boca Raton Beach & Park District (District) and provided information about
the City's financial obligation regarding the golf course.

Council Member O'Rourke commented on her visit to Boca Elementary to read to students. She also remarked on
successful meetings with City lobbyists during the members' time in Tallahassee and promoted upcoming City
events.

Council Member Thomson expressed concerns regarding the District's ability to both fund the proposed golf course
and continue to meet other financial obligations with the City.

Mayor Singer reported on his trip to Washington for the Conference of Mayors, noting that topics covered included
innovation, infrastructure, and safety. The Mayor then initiated a conversation among members regarding the
proposed ordinance on medical marijuana. Discussion ensued. Members asked questions on a number of points,
among them, required square footage, potential legal ramifications and how other municipalities had handled the
same issue. Staff members responded. The Mayor concluded his comments by encouraging residents to take part
in the 2020 Census and announcing upcoming City events.
