          A       Ordinance No. 5570
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton amending Chapter 6, "Elections," Code of Ordinances,
                  consistent with recent amendments to the City's Charter; amending Section 6-16, "Proof of

                  providing for severability; providing for repealer; providing for codification; providing an effective
                  date

         Diana Grub Frasier, City Attorney, provided a brief overview of the ordinance. She noted that this was a
         follow-up ordinance which implemented the charter amendments approved by Council and adopted by City

         which changed the word "less" to "fewer".

                                   carried unanimously; Mayor Scott Singer, Deputy Mayor Andrea Levine O'Rourke,

                                   Member Andy Thomson voting yes.

          B.       Ordinance No. 5571
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton amending Chapter 25, "Streets and Sidewalks," Article II,
                  Section 25-39, "Abandonment of Required Rights-of-Way or Required Special Purpose Easements
                  Pursuant to a Site Plan," Code of Ordinances, authorizing the Community Redevelopment Agency
                  to abandon rights-of-way and easements contemporaneously with an individual development
                  approval, and confirming the delegation of authority to act upon an application for an abandonment;
                  providing for severability; providing for repealer; providing for codification; providing an effective
                  date (AM-20-12)

        Brandon Schaad, Development Services Director, gave the PowerPoint presentation. He referred to
        Ordinance No. 5451 which delegated authority for easements and right-of-way abandonments to occur as
        part of the site plan approval but did not specifically grant the CRA the same authority regarding
        abandonments as part of Individual Development Approvals (IDA) in the Downtown. Mr. Schaad noted that
        the amendment also included minor, non-substantive adjustments to language in the current Code. The
        Planning and Zoning Board recommended approval, as did staff. Upon concluding, Mr. Schaad made
        himself available for members' questions.

        The public hearing was opened and, seeing no one indicate their desire to speak, closed.

                                   Motion was made by Council Member Andy Thomson, seconded by Council
                                   Member Monica Mayotte, to approve Ordinance No. 5571. Motion carried
                                   unanimously; Mayor Scott Singer, Deputy Mayor Andrea Levine O'Rourke, Council
                                   Member Yvette Drucker, Council Member Monica Mayotte, Council Member Andy
                                   Thomson voting yes.

          C.      Ordinance No. 5572 (First of two public hearings. No vote will be taken)
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton amending Chapter 28 "Zoning," Article XI, Division 3, "R­
                  B-1 Motel-Business District," Section 28-742, "Permitted Uses," Code of Ordinances, to authorize
                  business, trade, technical, or secretarial schools as permitted uses in the Motel-Business (R-B-1)
                  zoning district; providing for severability; providing for repealer; providing for codification; providing
                  an effective date (AM-20-09)

        Jim Bell, Long Range Project Director, gave the PowerPoint presentation. He noted that the amendment
        had been considered by the Planning and Zoning Board in the past and was once again being brought
        forward in light of the occupancy of a technical school operating within the City on North Federal Highway.
        After analysis, staff determined that "business, trade or technical schools" should be reinstated as permitted
        uses in the R-B-1 zoning district. Mr. Bell referred to historical precedents, citing more than a dozen
        business, trade, and technical schools that had operated under the "educational institutions" provisions.
        The existing Professional, Office and Institutional (POI) restriction which limits enrollment to persons 18
        years of age or older would be removed. He stated that the corrective amendment would reflect the purpose
        and intent of the R-B-1 district by providing appropriate educational uses for community services. Staff
        recommended approval. The Planning and Zoning Board recommended unanimous approval. Upon
        concluding, Mr. Bell made himself available for members' questions.

         The public hearing was opened and, seeing no one indicate their desire to speak, closed. Mayor Singer
         advised that this was the first of two public hearings and no vote would be taken. He noted that the second
         public heating would take place on April 27, 2021.

 20.     MAYOR AND COUNCIL MEMBER REPORTS:

Deputy Mayor O'Rourke commented on a sculpture by artist Hubert Phipps that had been recently installed at the
Boca Raton Innovation Campus. She thanked Mizner Amphitheatre staff for the spray can sculptures signed by the
artist of the Stage Door Mural Project.

Council Member Drucker shared the TPA summer schedule. She advised that she had a scheduling conflict and
invited other members to attend. She then thanked City staff, voters and the Council Members for their support.
Ms. Drucker encouraged all to continue to wear masks and urged residents to drive safely.

Council Member Mayotte provided an update on a pilot recycling program at the beach and waterfront parks being
operated in conjunction with 40cean, and displayed photographs of the garbage and recycling bins being used.
She reported that over half a ton of recyclable materials had been collected within three months and presented
pictures of proposed two-in-one garbage and recycling bins.

Mayor Singer acknowledged all of the City staff for their hard work and highlighted increased patrols and traffic
enforcement measures taken by the Police Department. He concluded by asking residents to keep following
suggested safety measures and encouraged residents to remain positive as the City continues to recover from the
pandemic.
