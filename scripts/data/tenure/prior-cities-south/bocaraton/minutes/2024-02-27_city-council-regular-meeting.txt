11.      REGULAR PUBLIC HEARINGS:

          A.      Ordinance No. 5682
                 An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton amending Chapter 28, "Zoning," Code of Ordinances,
                 to provide for fleet vehicle parking as a main (as opposed to accessory) use; amending Article
                 I, Section 28-2, "Definitions, " to add a definition of "Fleet Vehicle Parking"; amending Article
                 XVI, "Off-Street Parking and Loading," Section 28-1646, "Definitions," to delete the definition
                 "Fleet Vehicles"; amending Article XII , "Industrial Districts," Division 8, "M-3 Manufacturing
                 Industrial District," Section 28-1123, "Permitted Uses," to add "Fleet Vehicle Parking" as a
                 permitted use; amending Article XV, Division 1, "Supplementary District Regulations," by
                 creating Section 28-1317, "Fleet Vehicle Parking," to establish regulations for such use;
                 amending Article XVI, "Off-Street Parking and Loading," Section 28-1655, "Required Off-Street
                 Parking," to clarify the number of required fleet vehicle parking spaces; providing for
                 severability; providing for repealer; providing for codification; providing an effective date (AM-
                 22-05)

        Long Range Project Manager Jim Bell gave the PowerPoint presentation. Mr. Bell provided a summary
        of the history and purpose of the proposed amendment and advised that representatives of affected
        businesses opposed the suggested changes. He presented the results of a survey of peak-time traffic
        from an applicant's property conducted since the last public hearing and noted that the impact of the
        ordinance would apply to the entire M-3 zoned area . Staff recommended approval of the ordinance as
        revised after the Planning and Zoning approval. Upon concluding, Development Services Director
        Brandon Schaad and Mr. Bell answered Members' questions and provided additional information.

        The public hearing period was opened. Attorney Ele Zachariades spoke on behalf of her client and
        expressed their opposition to the proposed restrictions. Traffic Engineer John Donaldson, representing
        the same applicant, shared concerns regarding staffs recommended peak-time limitations. Ms.
        Zachariades and Mr. Donaldson responded to Members' questions. Seeing no one else come forward
        to speak, the public hearing was closed.

         Discussion ensued . Mr. Schaad responded to Members' concerns and noted that the proposed
         regulations were not intended for one particular property, but for an entire zoning district.

  The meeting was recessed at 6:52 PM and resumed at 6:55 PM.

         At the request of Mayor Singer, City Attorney Diana Grub Frieser read into the record proposed
         language on page 5, Section 3, lines 7 - 9,which focused on limiting the number of vehicles trips during
         peak hours , rather than prohibiting pick-ups or drop-offs entirely . Ele Zachariades noted her approval
         of the amended language.

                          Motion was made by Mayor Scott Singer, seconded by Deputy Mayor Monica Mayotte,
                          to approve Ordinance No. 5682 as revised.

         Ms. Frieser read an additional clarification of the proposed amendment into the record.

 19.     MAYOR AND COUNCIL MEMBER REPORTS:

Deputy Mayor Monica Mayotte gave a PowerPoint presentation for items remaining on her to-do list that she
considered inspirational and urged staff to think outside the box concerning City operations. She listed a
number of ideas to help connect with residents and made a number of suggestions, including the modernizing
of Ordinance No. 4035, online renewal of beach stickers, and providing more options for metered parking.
Council Member Yvette Drucker referred to parking options that are available in neighboring cities and
suggested that City staff investigate creative alternatives to the current parking payment structure. Mr. Brown
advised that staff was exploring a number of options regarding the current metered parking. Ms. Nachlas
expressed her support for a number of the innovative parking ideas shared by Ms. Mayotte.
