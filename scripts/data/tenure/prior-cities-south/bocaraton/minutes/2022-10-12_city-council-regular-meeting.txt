          A.        Resolution No. 114-2022
                   A resolution of the City of Boca Raton considering, for the approximately 1.6-acre property
                   generally located at 7940 North Federal Highway, an amended and restated conditional use
                   approval (amending and restating Resolution No. 174-2003) to authorize the expansion of the
                   existing substance abuse treatment facility (Wellness Resource Center) from 5,100 square feet
                   to 10,933 square feet; providing for repealer; providing an effective date (CA-03-17R1)

           The City Attorney reviewed the quasi-judicial procedures that would govern the public hearing.
           Members made their ex-parte disclosures. The City Clerk administered the oath to those who wished
           to speak.

           Heather Hanson, Senior Planner, gave a PowerPoint presentation. She began by describing the
           subject area and current property uses. Ms. Hanson outlined the applicant's request to expand the
           size of the facility, noting that the expansion would involve only internal alterations. The Planning and
           Zoning Board recommended approval with an additional condition, and staff advised against including
           the condition suggested by the Board . A map of the site, including parking details and landscaping,
           was shown. The public notice was reviewed. Staff recommended approval subject to the conditions
           set forth . Upon concluding, Ms. Hanson responded to Council Members' questions and provided
           clarification.

            The public hearing was opened. Jim Frogner, representative on behalf of the owner, thanked staff for
            their presentation and requested approval of the project.

                           Motion was made by Council Member Andy Thomson, seconded by Council Member
                           Monica Mayotte, to approve Resolution No. 114-2022. Motion carried unanimously;
                           Mayor Scott Singer, Deputy Mayor Andrea Levine O'Rourke, Council Member Yvette
                           Drucker, Council Member Monica Mayotte, Council Member Andy Thomson voting yes.

16.     QUASI-JUDICIAL PUBLIC HEARINGSNARIANCES & APPEALS:

         A.        Resolution No. 96-2022
                  A resolution of the City of Boca Raton considering three (3) separate appeals of the decisions
                  of the Planning and Zoning Board in connection with the redevelopment of an approximately 3.5
                  acre property generally located at 2700 North Federal Highway: (1) an appeal of the site plan
                  approval (Resolution No. 2022-010) authorizing the construction of three new one-story
                  buildings totaling 18,366 square feet in three phases; (2) a second appeal (by the applicant)
                  requesting that the site plan approval be modified to remove certain conditions of approval; and
                  (3) an appeal of the decision of the Planning and Zoning Board to grant variances (Resolution
                  No. 2022-011) from Section 28-752, Code of Ordinances, to allow: (i) a six-foot wide
                  landscaped yard abutting a street right-of-way (North Federal Highway), where the Code
                  requires a ten-foot wide landscaped yard abutting a street right-of-way, and (ii) a sidewalk that
                  is parallel to the western property line (adjoining North Federal Highway) where the Code allows
                  sidewalks only generally perpendicular to the plot line within the required landscaped yard
                  abutting a street right-of-way, in the Motel-Business (R-B-1) zoning district; providing for
                  repealer; providing an effective date (SPA-21-04/21-97500004) (VA-21-08/21-97500004)

          The City Attorney reviewed the quasi-judicial procedures that would govern the public hearing.
          Members made their ex-parte disclosures. The City Clerk administered the oath to those who wished
          to speak.

          Tamash Rahman, Chief Planner, gave the PowerPoint presentation. She began with a review of the
          appeals, noting that the two appeals from the HOA had been withdrawn, and proceeded to review the
          appeal from Hotel Properties, L.P. She displayed a drawing of the subject property and described the
          proposed site plan and construction phasing. Ms. Rahman noted the vehicular access points and that
          a condition of approval by the Florida Department of Transportation (FOOT) would require the
          applicant to complete a warrant analysis for the construction of a traffic signal at the middle driveway.
          Another condition of approval would require the applicant to maintain the queueing for the drive­
          through on site. The Planning and Zoning Board (PZB) approved the site plan and two variances. Ms.
          Rahman reviewed the suggested modifications from the applicant and revised conditions of approval
          regarding project phasing and undergrounding of utility lines. Staff recommended a new condition of
          approval regarding FOOT authorization for a "No U-Turn" sign. Staff recommended affirming the
          decision of the PZB with revised and new conditions of approval. Upon concluding, Ms. Rahman
          responded to Council Members' questions. City Attorney Diana Grub Frieser reviewed the procedure
          of the public hearing, noting that a settlement had been reached between the applicant and the
          Homeowner's Association (HOA), resulting in the withdrawal of the HOA's appeal. A blacklined
          resolution had been provided to Members that reflected the withdrawal of one appeal and proposed
           staff revision. Ms. Frieser also noted that, pursuant to the Settlement Agreement (Agreement) between
           the HOA and property owner, consideration would be paid to the HOA and the applicant would disclose
           this information.

          The public hearing was opened. Ele Zachariades, representing the property owner appellant, thanked
          everyone for their work on the project and confirmed that a settlement had been reached that involved
          payment. She provided additional information regarding the undergrounding of utility lines. At the
          request of Mayor Singer, Ms. Frieser noted that paragraph 3 of the private Agreement between the
          Hotel Properties and Harbour East Association, provided for a payment to the HOA upon approval of
          the project. Ms. Zachariades confirmed the terms noted by Ms. Frieser. Larry Schner, attorney
          representing the Harbour East Association, confirmed that an Agreement had been reached. He
          pointed out that not only had monetary conditions been agreed to, but that additional concerns of the
          HOA had been addressed regarding traffic, noise, pollution, and other issues. Ms. Frieser reiterated
          that the Agreement was private, and additional requirements or conditions would not be enforced by
          the City. Mr. Schner stated that the agreed upon conditions in the Agreement were important to the
          HOA and asked that that be taken into consideration . Chris Dinallo, Tammy Dinallo, Andrew Bessio
          and Clark Stevens, spoke in opposition to the project, specifically the drive-through restaurant
          proposed for the property, citing traffic, noise, safety, and privacy concerns. Council Member Andy
          Thomson then asked the City Attorney to clarify the settlement of the HOA appeal. Ms. Frieser provided
          information and advised that the appeal of the property owner to modify certain conditions was still
           being heard. Council Member Mayotte stated that some of the issues raised by speakers appear to
           have been addressed in the settlement agreement and inquired if it had been shared with the residents
           of Harbour East. Ms. Frieser reiterated that the City was not a party of the settlement and added that
Regular Minutes                                           8                                          October 12, 2022
          the agreement between the property owner and the Association did not bind every individual
          homeowner in the neighborhood. Scott Zaslav, counsel for the applicant, explained that the only items
          they believed were being considered were the two conditions in the appeal. Marilda Hoover, Bill Riley,
          Kimberly Trombly-Burmeister, Ray Lopez, James Burmeister, Charles Morganstern, and Gloria
