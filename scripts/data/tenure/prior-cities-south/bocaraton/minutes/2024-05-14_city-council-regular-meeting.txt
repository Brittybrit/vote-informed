 The Deputy Mayor noted that the public hearings on items 10.A. through A.4 would be conducted
 contemporaneously as the items were related. She noted that a separate vote would be taken on each
 item and should item 10.A. not be approved, no vote would be taken on the remaining items. Ordinance
 No. 5693 requires four votes for adoption.

 The City Attorney read the quasi-judicial procedures which would govern the public hearing. Members
 made their ex parte disclosures. The City Clerk administered the oath to those who wished to speak.

 Jacob German, Planner, gave the PowerPoint presentation. He began by noting that since the
 introduction at the last Regular City Council Meeting, updated drawings had been submitted by the
 applicant and his presentation had been updated to reflect the revisions. He then proceeded to review
 the application, including the conditional use and site plan approvals, variances, and technical
 deviations. Aerial photos and site plans of the property and landscape plans were shown. Mr. German
 discussed the request for two variances and two technical deviations, which were supported by staff.
 Both the Planning and Zoning Board and staff recommended approval. Upon concluding, Mr. German

        Diana Grub Frieser provided additional information on proposed SB 1084 preemption of EV parking
        requirements.

        The public hearing was opened. Attorney Ele Zachariades of Miske! Backman gave a brief overview of
        the request including future development, rezoning, site plan, and examples of proposed interactive
        historic exhibits. The request for conditional use, variance setbacks and parking analysis were also
        reviewed. She then discussed changes made since the Planning and Zoning Board meeting and
        response to residents' concerns. Upon concluding, Ms. Zachariades responded to questions and Mr.
        Schaad provided additional information. Discussion ensued on the possibility of preserving one of the
        buildings for historical reference. Jeffrey Katz expressed concerns with parking, lack of trash chutes
        and elevators, expressed support for the preservation of one building for historical purposes but asked
        that Council Members vote no on the project. Cherie Bender spoke in opposition to the project. Marie
        Hester commented on being a good steward of history, spoke favorably of the Boca Raton Historical
        Museum, and advocated preserving one of the buildings. Charles Graves commented on meetings with
        the developer and residents' requests for elevators, trash chutes in the buildings and additional parking.
        John Martin spoke about preservation of one of the buildings, proposed traffic calming, and recognition
        of the community in the City's Centennial celebration. Erica White, Cassie Moorman, Vichaun
        Mansfield, Chiquita Lofton, Kristen Silvestre, Geline Saint Martin, Shaniece Williams, Fabiola Bernier,
        Angela McDonald , and Calvin Davis also spoke in support of the project and preservation of a building.
        Joseph Martin spoke about the history of the Tuskegee Airmen, support of preserving the historic
        barracks and asked Council to reject the project. Boca Raton Housing Authority Executive Director
        Ashley Whidby spoke on the importance of the preservation of history and steps they are taking in that
        direction. She then responded to questions. Chuck Ridley, chair of Unify Palm Beach County, spoke in
        support of preserving the history of Dixie Manor. Danielle Iverson, chair of the Boca Raton Housing
        Authority, thanked Council and residents for this discussion and spoke in support of providing affordable
         housing and encouraged approval of the application. Seeing no one else come forward, the public
         comment period was closed.

                        Motion was made by Mayor Scott Singer, seconded by Council Member Fran Nachlas,
                        to approve Ordinance No. 5693.

         Mayor Singer shared his comments in support of the application and suggested a recess to discuss an
         amendment to ensure the preservation of a building for historical purposes. There was consensus for
         recess and Ms. Frieser noted that the applicant waived their rebuttal time.

The meeting was recessed at 8:42 PM and resumed at approximately 9:07 PM.

         City Attorney Frieser advised that, during the recess, additional language was drafted and agreed upon
         by both parties. She then read language proposed to be added on page 8, Condition 6 of the Site Plan
         Amendment, Resolution No. 37-2024, which provided for the preservation of one building prior to
         demolition if funding from a third party was secured. Ms. Frieser noted that this would also result in an
         amended exhibit to the Comprehensive Plan (Ordinance No. 5693). The applicant indicated that they
         agreed to the additional language as read.

                        Motion was made by Mayor Scott Singer, seconded by Council Member Marc Wigder, to
