          The Mayor noted that the public hearings on items 10.A. and 10.A.1. would be conducted
          contemporaneously as the items were related, and a separate vote would be taken on each item. The
          City Attorney reviewed the quasi-judicial procedures which would govern the public hearing. Members
          made their ex-parte disclosures. The City Clerk administered the oath to those who wished to speak.

           Planner Jacob German gave the PowerPoint presentation. He began with an overview of the
          applicants' request and provided a comparison to the previous approval. An aerial photograph of the
          location was shown, and the site plan was displayed. Mr. German reviewed the requested technical
          deviation for a 30-foot driveway reservoir and the proposed amendment to the FLUM Amendment
          ordinance to reference the correcUrevised site plan. The proposed medical related uses are permitted
          in the current zoning district. Mr. German advised that the intensity did not exceed the maximum limit
          allowed, and although the number of beds proposed was higher than previously approved, the impact
          on emergency medical services was expected to be lower. The Planning and Zoning Board and staff
          recommended approval. Upon concluding, he made himself available for Members' questions.

          The public hearing was opened. Michael Marshall spoke on behalf of the applicant. He shared
          renderings of the proposed facility and provided additional information regarding the expected
          number of emergency medical service calls. Upon concluding, he responded to Members' questions.
          Jonathan Ounjian voiced his concerns over the project and local zoning. Seeing no one else come
          forward, the public hearing was closed. Mr. Marshall responded to a number of comments made by
          Mr. Ounjian regarding zoning and expected traffic impacts.
