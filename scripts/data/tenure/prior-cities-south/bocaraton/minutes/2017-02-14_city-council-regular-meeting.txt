                  Ordinance No. 5369
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton amending Chapter 28 "Zoning," Code of Ordinances, to
                  incorporate new provisions relating to self-storage facilities (interior storage unit access);
                  amending Section 28-307(1) relating to building intensity; amending the C-1 District regulations,
                  Sections 28-872 and 28-873; amending the W-1 District regulations, Sections 28-1017 and 28-
                   1019; amending the IG/SI District regulations, Sections 28-1042 and 28-1043; amending the M-1
                  District regulations, Sections 28-1072 and 28-1073; amending the M-2 District regulations,
                  Sections 28-1097 and 28-1098; amending the M-3 District regulations, Sections 28-1123 and 28-
                   1124; amending Article XV, Supplementary District Regulations, Division 1, by creating a new
                  Section 28-1310 entitled "Self-Storage Facility (interior storage unit access)" and establishing
                  development standards for such use; providing for severability; providing for repealer; providing
                  for codification; providing an effective date (AM-15-03)
                           Ordinance No. 5369
                           Drawings

         Mayor Haynie noted that the public hearings on Ordinances 5368 and 5369 would be conducted
         contemporaneously as these related petitions, and a separate vote would be taken on each ordinance.

         Ingrid Allen, Senior Planner, gave the PowerPoint presentation, beginning with a review of the ordinances
         followed by background information on the presentations to date. The amendments proposed by the
         applicant to policy LU.1.1.5 of the Comprehensive Plan (Comp Plan) as well as the numerous
         amendments to the Code of Ordinances (Code) were detailed. Staff's analysis was presented, noting that
         the increase in FAR (floor area ration) limits the amount of construction on a parcel. The Comp Plan
         amendment was transmitted to the State land planning Agency as required. The City received no
         comments resulting from that review. Based on review of the petitions, the Development Services
         Department recommended denial of both the amendment to the Comp Plan and the Code. The Planning
         & Zoning Board       unanimously recommended approval of the Comp Plan amendment and the Code
         changes, and also approved a site plan for reconfiguration of an existing building; said approval would
         only become effective should the both ordinances be adopted. Upon concluding, Ms. Allen responded to
         a number of questions from Council members.

         The public hearing was opened. Charles Siemon, Gray Robinson on behalf of the applicant, gave a
         PowerPoint presentation.    He noted this request began as a proposed renovation to the
         applicant's

         existing building. So as to avoid spot zoning, the Comp Plan and Code amendments were proposed. Mr.
         Siemon then provided details. It was noted that there is one active opponent to this proposal. In
         conclusion, he advocated approval and then responded to questions.

         Michael Weiner, attorney for Madison Holdings, addressed numerous aspects of the initial proposal and
         the resulting proposed Comp Plan and Code amendments. He opined that the Comp Plan should not be
         changed nor should the Code for a particular building configuration. Mr. Weiner urged that Council follow
         staff's recommendation to deny the petitions. Chris Baczewski, owner of Addison Storage, addressed
         both the numerous reasons that he would be aggrieved by passage of these ordinances and the
         unintended consequences resulting from said passage. He then responded to questions.

         Bonnie Miske! shared her opinion that there is a need for self-storage in Boca Raton. She also posed a
         question related to the loss of warehouse space that might result from passage of these ordinances. Tom
         Head, petitioner, addressed a number of comments made by a previous speaker, asserting a lack of
         veracity. He then made himself available for questions. Mike Krones offered his support of the project.
         Lenore Wachtel inquired as to how to prevent people from living in self-storage facilities. Seeing no one
         else come forward to speak, the public hearing was closed.

         The City Attorney addressed a question about re-transmittal and the initial need for the Comp Plan
         amendment. Council members directed a number of questions to staff and the petitioner, as well as
         offering further comments. A number of suggestions for changes to Ordinance 5369 (the Code
         amendments) were made. Discussion of building spacing ensued.

At approximately 7:48 pm, Council Member Rodgers left the meeting.

         Mr. Weiner addressed various previous statements and advocated denial of the petitions. The City

         requirement for a manned onsite presence, and the prohibition of cantilevering. In response to a question
         from Council Member Weinroth, the petitioner agreed to go ahead with vote tonight despite only four
         Council members being present to vote. Further discussion ensued; staff responded to questions about
         the building spacing requirement. Development Services Director Brandon Schaad provided a handout of
         minor clean up changes to Ordinance 5369.

                  (interior access) as permitted uses: amending the LIRP District, Section 28-979(j), to remove

                            Ordinance No. 5377

         Beth Eassa, Planner, gave the PowerPoint presentation. As this is the first of two public hearings, it was
         noted that no vote would be taken. Ms. Eassa reviewed the proposed changes to the Code of
         Ordinances that would accommodate high-end specialty storage as a permitted use. Staff's analysis was
         detailed. The Planning & Zoning Board unanimously recommended approval of the amendment. Staff
         recommends approval as well. Upon concluding, Ms. Eassa responded to questions.

         The public hearing was opened. Bonnie Miske!, on behalf of the applicant, made brief comments, noting
         that this is a reuse of an existing building on the old IBM site. Seeing no one else come forward to speak,
         the public hearing was closed. Mayor Haynie reiterated that this is the first of two public hearings and no
         vote would be taken. The ordinance would be heard again at the next Coucil meeting.

         D.       Ordinance No. 5378
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton amending Chapter 1, Section 1-2, "Definitions and Rules
                  of Construction," Code of Ordinances, affirming that the City Code is construed in compliance
                  with all applicable Federal and State laws: amending Chapter 28, Article I, Section 28-2,
                  "Definitions," Code of Ordinances, amending certain definitions and creating new definitions
                  related to student housing; amending Chapter 28, Article Ill, Division 7, "M-2 General Industrial
                  District," Section 28-1106, "Minimum Floor Area: Student Housing," amending the minimum floor
                  area of a student sleeping room: providing for severability: providing for repealer: providing for
                  codification: providing an effective date
                            Ordinance No. 5378

         Beth Eassa, Planner, gave the PowerPoint presentation. Background information on student housing
         regulations in the Code of Ordinances and the resulting lawsuit were provided. It was noted that the
         Code does not expressly prohibit minor children from residing in a student housing facility and that this
         ordinance clarifies that children living with a qualified student are not prohibited. The Planning & Zoning
         Board unanimously recommended approval of the text amendment. Staff recommends approval as well.
         Upon concluding, Ms. Eassa made herself available for questions. Council Member Weinroth inquired
         about including students in two year programs as well. Ms. Eassa noted that the Planning & Zoning
         Board also asked about that situation. The City Attorney suggested not making any changes until the
         lawsuit is resolved; further changes can be made to the Code afterward. Discussion followed.

         The public hearing was opened and, seeing no one come forward to speak, closed.

                        Motion was made by Council Member Scott Singer, seconded by Deputy Mayor Michael
                        Mu/laugh, to approve Ordinance No. 5378. Motion carried on a 3-1 vote; Mayor Susan
                        Haynie, Deputy Mayor Michael Mu/laugh, Council Member Scott Singer voting yes; Council
                        Member Weinroth voting no.
