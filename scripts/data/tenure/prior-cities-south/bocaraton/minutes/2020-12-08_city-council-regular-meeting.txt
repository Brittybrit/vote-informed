 12.     REGULAR PUBLIC HEARINGS:

              A.    Ordinance No. 5537
                    An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton considering a Universal Conditional (UC) amendment to
                   the Future Land Use Map of the Comprehensive Plan by redesignating certain property
                   consisting of approximately 12.08 acres, generally located at 2600 Northwest 5th Avenue and
                   2500 Northwest 5th Avenue, from Residential Low (RL) to Residential High (RH), subject to
                   conditions; providing for revisions to the Future Land Use Map; providing for repealer; providing an
                   effective date (UC-17-03/SC)

         Mayor Singer advised this was a transmittal hearing.

         Tamashbeen Rahman, Chief Planner, gave the PowerPoint presentation. She began by explaining that the
         only element of the application currently under consideration was the transmittal of the large-scaled Future
         Land Use Map (FLUM). Ms. Rahman then reviewed the steps for consideration of the FLUM amendment
         request. The location and future land use designation of the subject property were discussed. Staff
         evaluation was provided regarding the project's consistency with both the Comprehensive Plan and City
         Code, compatibility with surrounding land use and development, and concurrency with available public
         services. The traffic impact study was reviewed. The Planning and Zoning Board recommended approval.
         Development Services staff recommended denial, noting that the proposed change to the property's zoning
         designation was incompatible and inconsistent with surrounding future land uses. Upon concluding, Ms.
         Rahman made herself available for members' questions. Council Member Mayotte inquired what a typical
         land use transition would encompass and asked if the transmittal were approved, what additional planning
         would be involved. Deputy Mayor O'Rourke asked if there were plans for future work on N.W. 5th Avenue.
         Ms. Rahman and Marie Tejera, City Traffic Engineer, provided additional information. Mayor Singer inquired
         about other Residential High (RH) zoning designated areas within the City. Ms. Rahman and City Manager
         Ahnell responded.

         Bonnie Miskel, on behalf of the applicant, gave the PowerPoint presentation. She began by describing the
         location of the proposed development and highlighted features of the surrounding areas. John Clifford,
         speaking on behalf of the property management of the proposed project, provided additional information
         regarding community design and safety features for future student residents. Ms. Miskel highlighted
         components of the application request, including the Future Land Use Map amendment and the rezoning
         justification, and noted the City's need for additional student housing. She stated that the application was
         consistent with the City's Comprehensive Plan and provided results from a poll of the community regarding
         student housing. Upon concluding, she made herself available for members' questions. Mayor Singer asked
         Ms. Miskel if the project had received endorsement from FAU. Ms. Miskel advised that the University had
         approved the concept but could not endorse private entities. She was asked to expound on the proposed
         pedestrian bridge over the El Rio canal. Ms. Miskel responded to additional questions from Council
         Members Mayotte and Drucker. Mr. Ahnell provided information.

         The public comment period was opened. Renata Pawlowski, a board member of the Windwood community,
         expressed concerns about traffic impacts of the proposed development. Carol Campion, Kathleen
         MacDonald, Marcus Wood, Patricia Fyke expressed their opposition to the project. Hailey Berman, Nhu
         Nguyen, Alan Feinberg, Kevin Buchanon, Malcolm Butters, and Paul Kilgallon spoke in support of the
         project. Ryan Bowersox suggested Windwood expand onto the subject property. Sally Celano cited traffic
         and security as her main concerns in opposition to the project. Steve Edwards stated he was in support of
         additional student housing but felt the proposed density was incompatible with the area. Lorena Villegas
         and Margarita Pazmino spoke in opposition to the rezoning. Joseph Graubart commented that he did not
         feel the project served the community. The City Clerk read comments submitted by Neal Temple who stated
         that the City was not responsible to supply student housing, and by Peggy Lowenberg, who inquired if the
         size of the property could be cut in half. Seeing no one else indicate their desire to speak, the public
         comment period was closed. Bonnie Miske!, on behalf of the applicant, responded to comments made
         during the public comment period. She remarked that existing traffic issues would be improved by the
         project and commented on zoning issues.

         Council Members asked additional questions regarding the proposed project's pedestrian connection to the
         FAU campus and future widening of N.W. 5th Avenue. Brandon Schaad, Development Services Director,
         and City Manager Leif Ahnell provided additional information. Discussion ensued.

           B.      Ordinance No. 5552
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton amending Chapter 28, "Zoning," Code of Ordinances, by
                  amending Article XI, Division 10, "MC Medical Center District," Section 28-927, "Yards," Code of
                  Ordinances, to modify the minimum yard (setback) requirements of buildings over 25 feet in height,
                  and by amending Article XVI, "Off-Street Parking and Loading," Section 28-1653, "Design," Code
                  of Ordinances, to reduce the minimum parking stall dimension requirements for surface parking
                  lots accessory to a hospital in the MC Medical Center zoning district; providing for severability;
                  providing for repealer; providing for codification; providing an effective date (AM-20-07)

         Brandon Schaad, Development Services Director, gave the PowerPoint presentation. He began by
         reviewing the setback provisions of the amendment. Revisions of the proposed amendment were
         highlighted, including changes to parking stall dimensions and differences regarding setbacks of property
         abutting both residential and non-residential properties. The Planning and Zoning Board and Development
         Services staff recommended approval. Upon concluding, Mr. Schaad made himself available for members'
         questions. Deputy Mayor O'Rourke remarked on the change to parking space size and asked if this should
         be addressed in City streamlining priorities. Mr. Schaad and the Mr. Ahnell provided additional information.

         The public hearing was opened. Bonnie Miske!, on behalf of the applicant, thanked Development Services
         staff for their work on the project. She described the applicant's outreach to nearby residential property
         owners and made herself available for members' questions. Seeing no one else indicate their desire to
         speak, the public hearing was closed.
