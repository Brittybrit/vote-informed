          B.      Ordinance No. 5589
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton amending Chapter 19, "Buildings and Building
                  Regulations," Code of Ordinances, to establish a building recertification inspection program based
                  on, primarily, building age, and including timeframes for the recertification of such buildings, the
                  process and requirements for recertification, timeframes for repairs or modifications found to be
                  necessary resulting from recertification inspections, providing for remedies based on failure to
                  either submit a recertification inspection report or make repairs/modifications of buildings, and
                  establishing penalties for noncompliance with the recertification inspection program; providing for
                  severability; providing for repealer; providing for codification; providing an effective date (AM-21-
                  05)

           Brandon Schaad, Development Services Director, began the presentation by defining the types of
           buildings that would be subject to the proposed ordinance. Those that are 30 years and older and those
           defined as Threshold Buildings, excluding single-family homes and duplexes, would be required to
           complete the building recertification inspection. The process would include, but is not limited to, a notice
           of inspection to be mailed 365 days prior to coming due, the submission of an engineering/inspection
           report, identification of deficiencies and the actions needed to address them, and a repair plan, if
           necessary. Mr. Schaad then outlined actions that could result in Code Enforcement action or fines. He
           noted that staff had identified 242 buildings within the city that are immediately subject to recertification,
           and proposed a four-year schedule based on geographic zones to address this backlog. A map of the
           proposed zones, prioritized from east to west, was displayed. A table was shown that compared the
           City's ordinance to a proposal of the League of Cities. Staff recommends approval of the ordinance. Mr.
           Schaad noted that this was a new program being implemented from scratch and advised that there may
           be revisions in the future. Upon concluding, he made himself available for members' questions. Mayor
           Singer began by thanking staff for their hard work on the ordinance. He suggested that the words
           "threshold building" be replaced with the actual description used in State statute. Council Member
           Mayotte asked if staff would provide a suggested format for the required report and requested additional
           information on the timeframe for the repair plan and schedule. Council Member Thomson highlighted
           the importance of transparency in the process and questioned if there had been changes made to the
           ordinance to address it. Deputy Mayor O'Rourke requested clarification regarding who the 'owners' were
           that would be receiving notification and commented on the availability of structural engineers to complete
           the assessments. Council Member Drucker shared that, ideally, she hoped staff would have more time
           to craft the ordinance before being put forward for a vote and she inquired what the impact of the program
           would be to staff levels and budgets. Mr. Schaad and Mr. Ahnell responded to members' questions and
           provided additional information.

           The public hearing was opened. Steven Wallach encouraged Council to wait before adopting the
           ordinance and stated that the cause of the Surfside Condominium collapse still was not known. Richard
           Zimmer provided a handout with information included in Broward County's program for their building
           recertification, and he addressed specific portions of the proposed ordinance that he felt needed to be
           changed. Peter Sachs spoke in support of the ordinance and encouraged staff to move ahead with the
           program. Paula McClure commented that the 'owner' of a building is sometimes a board and noted the
           issues that could present when it came to notifying unit owners. Charlie Helton suggested that the city
           add additional engineers to staff to assist with the implementation of the program. Seeing no one else
           come forward to speak, the public hearing was closed.

                           Levine O'Rourke, to approve Ordinance No. 5589.

          A        Resolution No. 107-2021
                  A resolution of the City Council of the City of Boca Raton considering, for the Beresford
                  Condominium located at 350 South Ocean Boulevard, a Coastal Construction Control Line (CCCL)
                  variance, from Section 28-1556(3), Code of Ordinances, which variance is required for the
                  alteration of an existing pool, spa, deck and walkway area seaward of the CCCL (where no
                  structure is permitted seaward of the CCCL without a variance), and other site changes including
                  the replacement of some herbaceous vegetation east of the bulkhead wall on the upper beach,
                  and the removal of all seagrapes on the dune, to be replaced with herbaceous dune mix
                  vegetation; providing for repealer; providing an effective date (SC-19-03)

        The City Attorney confirmed that quasi-judicial procedures remained in effect. Council Members made
        their ex parte disclosures. The City Clerk administered the oath to those who wished to speak.

        John Lindgren, Senior Planner, gave the PowerPoint presentation. Components of the application were
        reviewed, and aerial photos of the subject property were shown. A site plan was displayed that outlined
        proposed modifications, as well as those previously completed without a permit. It was noted that the
        applicant wished to rectify the situation through the current variance process. Mr. Lindgren pointed out that
        the condominium was originally built prior to the establishment of the CCCL, and that the proposed
        hardscape and vegetative changes would have either a minimal, or advantageous impact to the health of
        the dune. Both the Environmental Advisory Board and the Planning & Zoning Board recommended
        approval. Staff recommended approval subject to the conditions set forth in the resolution. Upon
        concluding, Mr. Lindgren and Mr. Schaad responded to members' questions.

        The public hearing was opened. Ele Zachariades, on behalf of the applicant, thanked City staff for their
        presentation and repeated that the applicant desires to rectify the issue of prior work being completed
        without a permit. She made herself available for members' questions. There was no cross examination or
        rebuttal. Seeing no one else come forward, the public hearing was closed.
