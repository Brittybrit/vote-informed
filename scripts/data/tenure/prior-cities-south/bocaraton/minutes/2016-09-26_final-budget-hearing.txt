3.   REBUDGETING OF FY 2015- 2016 BUDGET APPROPRIATIONS

     Mayor Haynie highlighted the purpose of the Special Meeting.

     Sharon McGuire, Office of Management and Budget Director, referring to Exhibits 1 and 2 distributed to
     Council, reviewed the amendments to the proposed budget, representing rebudgets of dollars not spent

4.   PUBLIC HEARING TO FINALIZE THE BUDGET AND ADOPT MILLAGE RATES

     A.     Ordinance No. 5362
            An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton adopting final millage rates and levying ad valorem taxes
            for the fiscal year beginning October 1, 2016 and ending September 30, 2017; stating the
            percentage by which the millage to be levied exceeds the "rolled-back rate"; directing the City
            Manager or the Office of Management and Budget Director to adjust the adopted millage rates in
            the event of changes in the assessment roll and taxable value; providing for severability;
            providing for repealer; providing an effective date
                    Ordinance No. 5362

     B.     Ordinance No. 5363
            An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton adopting a final budget and appropriating funds for the
            fiscal year beginning October 1, 2016 and ending September 30, 2017; providing for severability;
            providing for repealer; providing an effective date

     The City Clerk read Ordinance No. 5362 in full and the title of Ordinance No. 5363.

     C.     Open public hearing on final budget and final millage rates

     Mayor Haynie opened the public hearing. City Manager Leif Ahnell gave a PowerPoint presentation,
     reviewing the goals and priorities set by Council. There followed a number of charts and graphs providing
     information on the millage rate and property tax comparison, proposed increase and decreases in general
     fund operating costs, new personnel, a typical tax bill, assessed valuations, tax rate history and favorable
     comparisons of Boca Raton to other cities with regard to tax rate, fire assessment fee, water & sewer
     rates. Upon conclusion, Mr. Ahnell thanked staff for their work and made himself available for questions.
         Sharon McGuire, 0MB Director, again referring to Exhibit 1, noted a change to the budget to include the
         salary increase for Council members as approved by voters at the August special election. She reviewed
         the 2016/17 - 2021/22 Capital Improvement Program (GIP). Ms. McGuire noted the sources of revenue
         that will fund capital improvements and reviewed, by department, the projects, vehicles and equipment
         included in the GIP for 2016-2017 fiscal year. She then made herself available for questions from Council
         members.

         D.       Discussion and explanation of percentage increase in millage over the rolled-back rate
                  necessary to fund the budget

          E.      Public comment

         There was no public comment.

         F.       Close public comment

         Seeing no one come forward, the public comment period was closed.

         G.       City Council shall amend the adopted tentative budget as it sees fit

         Mayor Haynie requested funding to construct a putting green in the Mizner Park median at a cost not to
         exceed $10,000. Discussion ensued.

                   Motion was made by Mayor Susan Haynie, seconded by Council Member Robert S. Weinroth, to
                   amend the budget to provide the appropriate funding for construction of a putting green in the
                   Mizner Park median. Motion carried unanimously on a voice vote.

         Mayor Haynie then requested $12,000 in funding for the Spanish River High School Band parade trip.
         Discussion ensued.

                   Motion was made by Mayor Susan Haynie, seconded by Council Member Scott Singer, to amend
                   the budget to include $12,000 in funding for the Spanish River High School Band. Motion carried
                   unanimously on a voice vote.

         Deputy Mayor Weinroth recommended that the Mayor's suggestions for non-profit funding be
         incorporated into the budget. After discussion, several changes were made to the Mayor's allocations.

                   Motion was made by Deputy Mayor Michael Mu/laugh, seconded by Council Member Robert S.
                   Weinroth, to amend the budget to include $423,500 in funding of non-profit organizations. Motion
                   carried unanimously on a voice vote.

         H.       Recomputation of proposed millage rates

         No recomputation of the millage rate was necessary.

         I.       Adopt Ordinance No. 5362 incorporating recomputed final millage rates

         The City Manager noted that the budget ordinance would need to be amended to include Exhibits 1, 2
         and 3 as presented, as well as the non-profit modifications. Mr. Ahnell also clarified that, on Exhibit 1, the
         increase of council salaries is $138,200. The Mayor's amendments would also be included.

                   Motion was made by Council Member Robert S. Weinroth, seconded by Deputy Mayor Michael
                   Mu/laugh, to adopt Ordinance No. 5362. Motion carried unanimously; Mayor Susan Haynie,
                   Deputy Mayor Michael Mu/laugh, Council Member Jeremy Rodgers, Council Member Scott
                   Singer, Council Member Robert S. Weinroth voting yes.

5.      RESOLUTIONS AND OTHER BUSINESS

          A.      Resolution No. 103-2016
                  A resolution of the City of Boca Raton adopting an amended and restated Boca Raton Municipal
                  Facilities and Services User Fee Schedule; providing for severability; providing for repealer;
                  providing an effective date
                            Resolution No. 103-2016

         Shannon Crouch, Processing and Compliance Manager, gave a brief PowerPoint presentation on the
         proposed changes to the Boca Raton Municipal and Services User Fee Schedule effective October 1,
         2016. Ms. Crouch then made herself available for questions. The City Manager responded to a question
         regarding cemetery fees.

         The public hearing was opened. Rebecca Hahessy, Jennifer Bielec and Joseph Forstot advocated not
         increasing the non-resident youth athletic fees. Bob Rollins, Greater Boca Raton Beach & Park District
         Commissioner, commented on the philosophy of user fees and asked that the increase in the non­
         resident sports fee be tabled. Caroline Linden, Geoff Solomon and Scott Shields also spoke against the
         fee increase. Lenore Wachtel commented on the lack of transportation for some children to participate in
         youth sports. Seeing no one else come forward, the public hearing was closed.

         Council discussion ensued. Mickey Gomez, Recreation Services Director, responded to questions
         regarding the way in which fee increases were calculated, field capacity, and the possibility of offering a
         family discount.

                   Motion was made by Council Member Scott Singer, seconded by Mayor Susan Haynie, to adopt
                   Resolution No. 103-2016 as amended to maintain the non-resident youth athletic fee at $55 and
                   to explore a family discount.

         Members made final comments. Council Member Weinroth suggested raising the non-resident fee to $65.

                   Motion to adopt Resolution No. 103-2016 as amended to maintain the non-resident youth athletic
                   fee at $55 and to explore a family discount failed 3 to 2; Deputy Mayor Michael Mu/laugh, Council
                   Member Jeremy Rodgers, Council Member Robert S. Weinroth voting no; Mayor Susan Haynie
                   and Council Member Scott Singer voting yes.

                   Motion was made by Council Member Robert S. Weinroth, seconded by Council Member Jeremy
                   Rodgers, to adopt Resolution No. 103-2016 as amended to increase the non-resident youth
                   athletic fee to $65 and to explore a family discount.

         Members agreed to also explore a bulk discount for youths playing multiple sports, as suggested by
         Council Member Singer.
