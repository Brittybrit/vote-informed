          Osny Jean, Planner, gave the PowerPoint presentation. He began with a review of the request, which
          included phasing of the development, revision of approved architectural elements, amendment of a
          condition of approval regarding a right-of way, and an abandonment of a utility easement. A map of
          the parcel was displayed, and a history of the past zoning approvals was provided. Drawings of the
          proposed architectural changes were shown. Mr. Jean then explained the request for the right-of-way
          dedication and the abandonment of a City utility easement along the property's western driveway.
          Components of the phasing request were discussed, and Mr. Jean noted that while staff
          recommended approval of the proposed architectural revisions, easement abandonment, and right­
          of-way dedication, staff recommended denial of the phasing portion of the application. The Planning
          and Zoning Board recommended approval of all components of the application, as well as
          amendments to conditions 3.h. and 7 in the site plan resolution. Upon concluding, Mr. Jean and
          Development Services Director Brandon Schaad responded to members' questions.

          Ele Zachariades, on behalf of the applicant, thanked staff for their presentation. She provided
          clarification regarding the request for phasing and explained how the driveway permit and right-of­
          way dedication are connected. She relayed the applicant's desire to move ahead with the permit and
          expressed their desire to work with the City. Alex Rosemurgy, property owner, asked for Council to
          approve the phasing request so that there would be time to obtain the desired tenants and configure
          the footprint to their specifications. Ms. Zachariades then made herself available for members'
          questions. At the Mayor's request, Diana Grub Frieser, City Attorney, briefly reviewed a proposed
          alternative to condition 3.h. and ?(iii) of Resolution No. 140-2021 that she had discussed with Ms.
          Zachariades. She read language into the record and explained that it would allow the applicant to go
          forward with the building permit process, but that building would be limited pending the applicant's
          completion of the right-of-way dedication and driveway permit through Palm Beach County. If the
          County processes would not be completed within 6 months, the building permit would be considered
          null and void.

           The public hearing was opened and, seeing no one come forward, closed. The Mayor suggested a
           brief recess to allow staff and representatives for the applicant to review the proposed amendment to
           Resolution No. 140-2021.

           The meeting recessed at approximately 6:43 pm and reconvened at approximately 7:00 pm.

           Ele Zachariades advised that her client was amenable to the compromise reached with staff, but
           asked that the building permit be suspended rather than terminated if the process with the County
           were not completed within the allowable time frame. The City Attorney provided additional information
           and members weighed in. There was consensus to allow the requested phasing, the architectural
           changes, and to extend the time frame for the applicant to secure the necessary project components
           from the County.

          A.      Ordinance No. 5592
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton considering an amendment to the Future Land Use
                  Map of the Comprehensive Plan by redesignating certain property consisting of approximately
                  1.4 acres, generally located at 644 Park of Commerce Way, from Planned Mobility (PM) to
                  Conservation (N); providing for revisions to the Future Land Use Map; providing for repealer;
                  providing an effective date (SC-21-02)

        John Lindgren, Senior Planner, gave the PowerPoint presentation. He began with a description of the
        subject location and the project site plan. He noted that because the site plan had been approved to
        include the preserve, City code required that the Future Land Use designation be changed from
        Planned Mobility to Conservation. During staff analysis of the submitted application, it was determined
        that the subject area would be effective at providing native plant species and wildlife habitat that would
        support the preservation of the environmental area. The Environmental Advisory Board and Planning
        and Zoning Board recommended approval. Staff recommended approval. Upon concluding, Mr.
        Lindgren made himself available for members' questions.

        The public hearing was opened and, seeing no one come forward, closed.

                          Motion was made by Deputy Mayor Andrea Levine O'Rourke, seconded by Council
                          Member Andy Thomson, to approve Ordinance No. 5592. Motion carried unanimously;
                          Mayor Scott Singer, Deputy Mayor Andrea Levine O'Rourke, Council Member Yvette
                          Drucker, Council Member Monica Mayotte, Council Member Andy Thomson voting yes.

          B.      Ordinance No. 5600
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton relating to public hearing notice requirements for various
                  development-related applications; amending portions of Chapter 19, "Building Regulations,"
                  Chapter 23, "Planning and Development," Chapter 24, "Signs," Chapter 25, "Streets and
                  Sidewalks," Chapter 26, "Subdivision Regulations," and Chapter 28, "Zoning," Code of
                  Ordinances, to consolidate various public hearing notice requirements, and to: (i) amend and
                  standardize the notice requirements and procedures; (ii) update the type(s) of notice
                  (published, mailed and posted) required for different applications; and (iii) update the
                  requirements (including timing) for each type; providing for severability; providing for repealer;
                  providing for codification; providing an effective date (AM-20-13)

           Brandon Schaad, Development Services Director, presented the PowerPoint presentation. He began
           by outlining the need to streamline and make consistent the City's requirements for public hearing
           notices. The benefits of standardizing to a 10-day notification timeframe were described. Mr. Schaad
           explained that the proposed amendment would focus more on the use of mailed and posted notices,
           rather than newspaper advertising, although all three methods would still be utilized for the most
           significant applications. The Planning and Zoning Board recommended approval with a modification
           of 14-day advertising timeframe, but staff recommended against the proposed modification. Upon
           concluding, he made himself available for members' questions.

          The public hearing was opened and, seeing no one come forward, closed.

                          Motion was made by Council Member Monica Mayotte, seconded by Council Member
                          Yvette Drucker, to approve Ordinance No. 5600. Motion carried unanimously; Mayor
                          Scott Singer, Deputy Mayor Andrea Levine O'Rourke, Council Member Yvette Drucker,
                          Council Member Monica Mayotte, Council Member Andy Thomson voting yes.
