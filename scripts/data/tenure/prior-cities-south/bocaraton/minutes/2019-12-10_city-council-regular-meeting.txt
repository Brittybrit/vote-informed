          A        Ordinance No. 5509
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton considering a long-term lease agreement with Virgin Trains
                  USA LLC (VTUSA), pursuant to Chapter 13, Article Ill, "Sale of City Property," Code of Ordinances,
                  for a portion of the City-owned property located at 400 Northwest 2nd Avenue (generally in the
                  northeast of the parcel}, for the purpose of construction, maintenance, and operation of a
                  passenger train station and a parking garage, pursuant to terms and conditions of the Agreement;
                  determining, in accordance with Chapter 13, that the Agreement and the purposes served by the
                  Agreement are of significant economic benefit to the City: authorizing temporary easements for
                  parking and construction purposes, and providing for the relocation of the "Community Garden":
                  authorizing a right of first refusal with respect to the leasehold lands and additional lands, as
                  described in the Agreement; and authorizing the expenditure of funds in connection with the
                  construction of the parking garage, as provided in the Agreement: providing for severability:
                  providing for repealer: providing an effective date (SC-19-06)

         George Brown, Deputy City Manager, gave a PowerPoint presentation on the updated lease agreement
         with Virgin Trains USA, LLC (VTUSA). Mr. Brown began with a comparison of elements between the initial
         lease request and current proposed lease. Location and design elements of the proposed train station and
         parking garage were displayed. Aspects of garage operations and a general construction timeline were
         presented. Terms of the lease were discussed, including, but not limited to, relocation of the Community
         Garden, construction parking and coordination with the Library Commons residential neighborhood.
         Environmental considerations were also reviewed. City Attorney Diana Grub Frieser then reviewed changes
         in the updated redline version of the lease, noting that many changes involved cleanup to the lease

         clarification of garage revenues, provisions for a City-declared state of emergency, and bankruptcy

         Mr. Gonzalez, Mr. Kronberg and Jeff Swiatek of VTUSA provided additional information. Discussion

         inquiries about buffer areas between the parking garage and adjacent neighborhood, shade trees,
         construction time periods and future development of the property.

           B.      Resolution No. 157-2019
                  A resolution of the City of Boca Raton considering an amendment to an approved planned mobility
                  development (PMD) site plan (Resolution No. 57-2014, as amended by Resolution No. 91-2016),
                  to authorize the construction of a 124-room hotel in Phase II of the 900 Broken Sound PMD Site
                  Plan; modifying conditions of approval for Phase I and Phase II of the site plan; together with a
                  technical deviation from Section 23-190(b), Code of Ordinances, to increase the allowable length
                  of five (5) parking rows from 250 feet to lengths ranging from 280 feet to 330 feet; and abandonment
                  of a portion of a platted public utility easement; for the property generally located at 850 and 900
                  Northwest Broken Sound Parkway, and 900 Northwest Broken Sound Boulevard; providing for
                  repealer; providing an effective date (PMD-13-01R3/SPA)

         The Mayor advised the public hearings on Items 11 A. and B. would be conducted contemporaneously as
         the items are related, and a separate vote would be taken on each resolution. The City Attorney reviewed
         the quasi-judicial procedures which would govern the public hearing. Council Members made their ex parte
         disclosures. The City Clerk administered the oath to those who wished to speak.

         Jim Bell, Long Range Project Manager, gave the PowerPoint presentation. He began with a review of the
         request and description of the property. Mr. Bell outlined the amendments to the master plan and phases
         of the project development. Modifications to development parameters were summarized. The Community
         Appearance Board recommended approval 3-1, with 3 conditions. Results of the traffic study were provided.
         The Planning and Zoning Board recommended approval. The proposed amended PMD project is consistent
         with the City's Comprehensive Plan. Staff recommends approval of the resolutions with a date correction.
         Upon concluding, Mr. Bell responded to members' questions.

         The public hearing was opened. Bonnie Miskel, on behalf of the applicant, gave a PowerPoint presentation.
         She provided the history of the original approval and described the conditions being contested. Ms. Miskel
         asked that her client's contributions to the City's Beautification Program be split into two payments, that
         Condition 6 be stricken, and lastly, that there be an alternative to the transit fee conditions. Upon concluding,
         she made herself available for members' questions. Discussion ensued. Brandon Schaad, Development
         Services Director, provided further information regarding the petitioner's request and discrepancy regarding
         project phases. The City Attorney provided additional clarification. Paul Kilgallon, on behalf of the applicant,
         supplied additional background regarding the history of the site plan and development phases. Mr. Schaad
         and Ms. Frieser responded to comments made by the applicant. Further discussion ensued.

                  Motion was made by Deputy Mayor Jeremy Rodgers, seconded by Council Member Andy
                  Thomson, to amend Resolution Nos. 156-2019 and 157-2019 to revise language, to be finalized
                  by staff, regarding the transit fee; change to the exhibit for the Planned Mobility Matrices; and to
                  strike the median beautification contribution for Phase 1 in Resolution No. 157-2019. Motion carried
                  unanimously on a voice vote.

                  Motion was made by Deputy Mayor Jeremy Rodgers, seconded by Council Member Andy
                  Thomson, to amend Resolution Nos. 156-2019 and 157-2019 to split the Phase 2 median
                  beautification contribution into Phase 2 and 3 contributions. Motion carried unanimously on a voice
                  vote.
