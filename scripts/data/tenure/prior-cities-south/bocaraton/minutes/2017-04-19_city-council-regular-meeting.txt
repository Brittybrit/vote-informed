         A.       Resolution No. 48-2017
                  A resolution of the City of Boca Raton considering a Planned Mobility Development (PMD) Master
                  Plan and Site Plan to allow development of a 322-unit multifamily residential project generally
                  located at 777 West Yamato Road and 750 Park of Commerce Boulevard (based upon Planning
                  and Zoning Resolution No. 86-9 as amended and the new site plan considered together);
                  considering seven (7) modifications of development parameters, pursuant to Section 28-50, Code
                  of Ordinances, related to eliminating sidewalks on one (1) side of a right-of-way (where a
                  sidewalk is required on both sides of a right-of-way), eliminating planted buffers along sidewalks,
                  and five (5) separate modifications related to reducing building setbacks fronting Congress
                  Avenue and West Yamato Road; considering a waiver of plat requirement; considering repealing
                  a condition of Planning and Zoning Board Resolution No. 06-11 requiring replatting of
                  conservation easements; considering increasing and altering the location of the conservation
                  easements; providing for repealer; providing an effective date (PMD-15-03)

Regular Minutes                                       4                                                  April 19, 2017
                          Resolution No. 48-2017
                          Plans

         Members made their ex parte disclosures. The City Attorney reviewed the quasi-judicial procedures that
         would govern the public hearing. The City Clerk administered the oath to those who wished to speak.

         Tamashbeen Rahman, Senior Planner, gave the PowerPoint presentation. The location of the site was
         identified and details of the project were provided. Elements of the current and proposed modifications to
         the Master Plan were provided. Topics included, but were not limited to, parking, vehicular and
         pedestrian access, cross-access, sidewalks, and bus shelters. Details of the site plan and the residential
         project were also provided; renderings and elevations were displayed. Ms. Rahman then reviewed the
         modifications requested by the applicant that affect sidewalks, landscape buffers along sidewalks, and
         building setbacks. The traffic analysis was reviewed. Ms. Rahman noted that the project meets the
         mobility objectives of the Comprehensive Plan. The applicants request to waive platting requirements was
         explained. The Planning & Zoning Board recommended approval with additional conditions. Staff clarified
         language in conditions (4)r, (4)s, (4)t, and (7)(b) and recommends approval with said clarifications. Ms.
         Rahman and Brandon Schaad, Development Services Director, then responded to questions from
         Council members.

         The public hearing was opened. Bonnie Miske!, Dunay Miske! Backman on behalf of the petitioner, gave
         a PowerPoint presentation. The existing site conditions of the property were illustrated. Details of the
         compliance with the PMD parameters of this third phase and the first two phases of the project were
         provided. Project architect Juan Caycedo commented on the design of the buildings and the landscaping,
         which was modified in response to the suggestions of the Community Appearance Board. Ms. Miske!
         reported on a meeting with Lake Worth Drainage District (LWDD) regarding landscaping on the LWDD
         easement along Yamato Road. She noted the petitioner's commitment to continue to work with LWDD.
         She then addressed the undergrounding of utilities and the limitations that exist in along Yamato. Ms.
         Miske! then responded to a number of questions. Paul Borman commented on the project. Jo Ann
         Landon commented on the need for shade trees on the property. Glenn Gromann offered support of the
         project. There was no cross examination. Seeing no one else come forward to speak, the public hearing
         was closed.

         Ms. Rahman read the several modifications to conditions (4)r, (4)s, and (4)t addressing undergrounding
         utilities and correction of a scrivener's error in condition (7)(b). The City Attorney then read into the
         record a new condition (4)v relating to supplemental landscaping in the LWDD easement.

                  Motion was made by Council Member Robert S. Weinroth, seconded by Council Member Andrea
                  Levine O'Rourke, to approve Resolution No. 48-2017 as amended.

         Deputy Mayor Rodgers inquired as to the timeline for completion of the tunnel under Yamato Road for the
         El Rio Trail. The City Manager provided information.

                  Motion carried unanimously; Mayor Susan Haynie, Deputy Mayor Jeremy Rodgers, Council
                  Member Andrea Levine O'Rourke, Council Member Scott Singer, Council Member Robert S.
                  Weinroth voting yes.

         A.       Ordinance No. 5385
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton amending Section 4-2 , Code of Ordinances; affirming and
                  providing for uniform hours of operation for all alcoholic beverage vendors in the City; providing a
                  time period for compliance in annexed areas; providing for severability; providing for repealer;
                   providing for codification in the Code of Ordinances; providing an effective date
                           Ordinance No. 5385

         Mayor Haynie noted that she requested th is ordinance be brought forward and deferred to the City
         Attorney to explain the substance of the ordinance and the timeframe for compliance. Police Chief Dan
         Alexander provided information on the numerous police calls that have been made at the two locations
         affected by this ordinance. He stated that the Police Department supports the passage of this ordinance.

         The public hearing was opened . Karl Dickey, representing the Libertarian Party, stated his opposition to
         the ordinance. Bob Zeigler and Cyndi Bloom commented on the ramifications of the noise generated at
         Blue Martini over the last five years and stated their support of the ordinance. Ryan Moyer stated
         opposition to the ordinance. Jeff Brown , representing Blue Martini, asked that Council consider
         postponing a decision on the ordinance. Una Kohmann , noting that everyone deserves a good night's
         sleep, stated her support of the ordinance. Bruce Tumin offered his opposition to the ordinance. Thomas
         Mullin read into the record comments from Cindy Oliveri in opposition to the ordinance. Mr. Mullin,
         representing Nippers, then offered a suggestion to revise the ordinance to extend the transition time to 18
         months and offered to work with police on problems. He provided handouts of the suggested language
         and a petition opposing the ordinance. Danny Rosenberg, General Manager of Nippers, addressed
         comments made regarding noise and other complaints. Roberto Roman , Yasmin Silva , Jarett Loperfito,
         and Anni-May Hurmemaa spoke in support of Blue Martini and in opposition to the ordinance. Sergio
         Vera and Daniel Cardenas spoke in opposition to the ordinance. Bruce Gregory, General Manager of
         Blue Martini, stated opposition to the ordinance but also asked that the time for implementation be
         extended. Amy Liga McNamara spoke in support of Blue Martini, the charitable work of the bar and in
         opposition the ordinance. She advocated postponement. Byron Gardiner, founding partner of Blue
         Martini, commented on the efforts that they have made to mitigate the sound issues. He requested that
         the time for implementation be extended. Michael Marshall, Gray Robinson on behalf of Crocker
         Partners, expressed support of the ordinance. Glenn Gromann commented on the crime statistics
         presented and supported the extension of the time for implementation and passage of the ordinance.
         Alan Fineburg Jr. offered comments in opposition to the ordinance. Jerry Ruderman addressed a number
         of previous comments and offered his support of the ordinance. Scott Roseman, marketer for Blue
         Martini, responded to previous comments and stated his opposition to the ordinance. Jeff Brown, again
         on behalf of Blue Martini, requested a postponement of consideration of the ordinance. He noted
         willingness to work with the Council, the police and code enforcement to address problems and reach a
         solution. Adam Beighley, attorney representing Via Verde, addressed comments made by the previous
