                  Ordinance No. 5393
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton amending Chapter 28 "Zoning," Code of Ordinances,
                  relating to the Medical Center (MC) and Motel-Business (R-B-1) zoning districts to provide
                  regulations for parking structures and master plans; amending Section 28-925 to provide for the
                  height, length and location (yard/setback) of parking structures in order to allow parking structures
                  (accessory to a hospital) to be located closer to single family residential properties than other
                  buildings and structures in the MC zoning district; amending Section 28-927(6) to establish yard
                  (setback) measurements where a master plan is applicable; creating new Section 28-932 of the
                  MC zoning district to establish development parameters which relate to a master plan and
                  subsequent site plans; amending the R-B-1 Zoning District, Section 28-742, to allow master plan
                  approval for property contiguous to a hospital; providing for severability; providing for repealer;
                  providing for codification; providing an effective date (AM-13-04)
                           Ordinance No. 5393

         Jim Bell, Acting Deputy Director of Development Services, gave the PowerPoint presentation beginning
         with background information and a summary of the proposed text amendment. Topics included, but were
         not limited to, parking structure height, length, setback, and screening; parameters for MC Master Plans;
         relationship of structures to adjacent multi-family residential; and permitted uses. Mr. Bell reviewed a
         revision to page 4 of the ordinance that was provided to Council. It was noted that the proposed
         amendment is compatible and consistent with the Comprehensive Plan. Staffs analysis of the various
         elements of the ordinance was reviewed in detail. Mr. Bell noted that the Planning and Zoning Board
         recommended approval. Staff recommends approval as well. Upon concluding, Mr. Bell made himself
         available to answer questions from Council members.

         The public hearing was opened. Bonnie Miske!, on behalf of Boca Raton Regional Hospital, gave a
         PowerPoint presentation, noting that should the ordinance be approved, the Hospital would have to get
         approval, in a quasi-judicial proceeding, for any change to their property. She summarized the three
         parts of the Code amendment: parking structure distance from residential, yard measurement in master
         plans, and allowing R-B-1 zoned property contiguous to MC districts to be included in a master plan. Ms.
         Miske! then discussed the changes being requested, comparing setbacks in various zoning districts. She
         noted her client had no objection to a condition for additional screening, advocated passage of the
         ordinance, and made herself available for questions.

         Colleen White, president of Spanish Oaks Condominium Board of Directors, expressed opposition to
         passage of the ordinance, noting that a parking structure would affect the residents' quality of life. Krista
         Maki proposed a number of ways in which the Hospital could address the parking issue. She advocated
         postponement of a decision in order to study the proposal for a different location of the parking gorge.
         Barring postponement, Ms. Maki suggested that the distance of a structure from residential be increased
         to 140 feet. lgal Bloch, on behalf of Spanish Oaks Condominium Association, expressed concerns with
         the negative effects of the ordinance. Michael Weiner, on behalf of Spanish Oaks Condominium, asserted
         that the proposed zoning changes should be considered a quasi-judicial matter because the changes
         proposed benefit only the Hospital. He advocated that the process start over again. Michelle Valle
         expressed opposition to the ordinance. Peter Gary commented on the current state of the Hospital,

         addressed the existing parking issue, and advocated passage of the ordinance. Andrew McLaughlin
         expressed opposition to the ordinance and advocated having a 250 foot setback. Malka Cabal expressed
         a number of concerns including safety and security, and stated her opposition to the ordinance. Seeing
         no one else come forward to speak, the public hearing was closed.

         Council Member Singer proposed three amendments to the ordinance including increasing the distance
         of a parking structure from single family homes to 140 feet (page 3, line 25), new language specifying a
         standard for the fac;;ade to prevent light spillage for a parking structure (page 4, new section 28-925.c.),
         and signage to prevent non-official use of any access road (page 4, after line 10). In response to a
         question from Deputy Mayor Rodgers, Bonnie Miskel stated her client's agreement to the distance
         increase to 140 feet. Jim Bell then read into the record a revision to c.ii. on page 4 of the ordinance that
         would require a double of row of landscaping between a parking structure and single family homes.
         Council was provided with a handout containing the language. Members suggested that this revision
         should also apply to multi-family residential. Mr. Bell noted that staff had prepared a revision that would
         include multi-family residential buffering. This amendment would be a new subsection 28-925 d. Some
         discussion followed, after which Mr. Bell read the new language into the record. Mayor Haynie noted that
         five amendments had been proposed. The City Attorney suggested a brief recess to allow staff to
         organize and review the language suggested for the various amendments.

                  Motion was made by Council Member Scott Singer, seconded by Deputy Mayor Jeremy Rodgers,
                  to recess the meeting. Motion carried unanimously on a voice vote.

         The meeting recessed at approximately 8:15 pm and reconvened at approximately 8:24 pm.

         Acting City Manager George Brown reviewed the proposed amendments, identified where in the
         ordinance the new language would be inserted, and noted that the amendments had been reorganized
         with subsection d. now having three paragraphs requiring landscaping, fac;;ade, and access road signage
         that would be applicable to all residential, both single and multi family. Discussion ensued. Bonnie
         Miskel noted that there were no objections to most proposed amendments, but that the fac;;ade
         requirements should not be specific. The City Attorney noted that the design of any facade would be dealt
         with in a future site plan. However, Ms. Miskel stated that her client could not to agree to an amendment
         specifying fac;;ade design. Members discussed a possible change to the language addressing the fac;;ade
         requirements. The City Attorney addressed the objection to the legislative language. Mr. Brown
         suggested alternate language that eliminated the reference to 'shutters' and 'window-like' to which Ms.
         Miskel agreed.

         The City Attorney addressed at length Mr. Weiner's earlier comments with regard to legislative matters
         versus quasi-judicial matters, noting that the decision before Council is a policy decision, not one granting
         any development rights. When the Hospital submits a site plan, it will be considered at a quasi-judicial
         hearing.

                  Motion was made by Deputy Mayor Jeremy Rodgers, seconded by Council Member Robert S.
                  Weinroth, to approve Ordinance No. 5393 as amended.

         Members made final comments. Ms. O'Rourke posed several questions. Staff provided responses, in
         particular regarding height, location and employee parking.        The City Attorney noted that this
         conversation is theoretical as there is no plan being considered at this time. All are questions that are
         appropriate when a site plan is before Council. Mr. Singer suggested that the rules be suspended and
         that the Hospital be allowed to respond. Ms. Miskel provided a response. Deputy Mayor Rodgers and
         Council Member Singer weighed in.

                  Motion carried unanimously; Mayor Susan Haynie, Deputy Mayor Jeremy Rodgers, Council
                  Member Andrea Levine O'Rourke, Council Member Scott Singer, Council Member Robert S.
                  Weinroth voting yes.
