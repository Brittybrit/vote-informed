           B.      Ordinance No. 5637
                  An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton proposing an amendment to the City Charter changing
                  the terms of office of the Mayor and City Council Members from three (3) years to four (4) years;
                  extending the terms of those council members who terms are due to expire March 2024 by one
                  additional year; providing for submission of the proposed City Charter amendment to the electors
                  of the City at the regular election on March 14, 2023; providing for severability; providing for
                  repealer; providing for codification; providing an effective date

           Mayor Scott Singer gave the presentation. The Mayor outlined his reasons for bringing forth the
           proposed ordinance, citing the amount of time it takes to get acclimated to the duties of the position,
           the need for consistency and continuity in local government, and the time it takes to see some goals
           to fruition. He noted most Florida cities similar in population size have 4-year terms, and he named a
           number of cities that had recently extended their council terms. Mayor Singer advocated extending the
           terms as a way to not only save on costs and staff time, but to allow candidates to focus more on policy
           than politics, pointing out that the proposed new election cycle would no longer fall on the same year
           as presidential races. Upon concluding he made himself available for questions. Council Member
           Drucker agreed that it takes time to learn the role of Council Member and with the proposed change
           in election cycles in order to remain non-partisan. Deputy Mayor O'Rourke observed that a component
           of proposed Ordinance No. 5638 would relieve some of the election duties from staff and stated that
           all of the correspondence she received from residents had been negative.

           The public hearing was opened. Brian Stenberg expressed his opposition to the proposed ordinance,
           inquired which residents and businesses would benefit from it and asked that Members vote no. Amy
           Price spoke against the proposed term extension and urged the Council to vote no on the ordinance
           as well as enact term limits for City boards and committees. Seeing no one else come forward, the
           public hearing was closed.

                          Motion was made by Council Member Monica Mayotte, seconded by Mayor Scott
                          Singer, to approve Ordinance No. 5637.

           Mayor Singer explained that the only way to enact the legislation and not have it affect current Council
           Members would be to have it take effect in 2029, and that he did not see the merit in postponing it.
           Council Member Nachlas shared that she would not be supporting the measure as it could directly
           impact her own term on the Council. Deputy Mayor O'Rourke stated that candidates should prepare
           as much as possible for the position prior to being elected and that she did not see the benefit in
           extending terms. Council Member Drucker clarified that there was an inevitable amount of on-the-job
           learning that comes with the position. Council Member Mayotte agreed that much effort and dedication
           goes into learning and performing the job, no matter the amount of preparation.

                          Motion passed 3 votes to 2; Mayor Scott Singer, Council Member Yvette Drucker,
                          Council Member Monica Mayotte voting yes, Deputy Mayor Andrea Levine O'Rourke
                          and Council Member Fran Nachlas voting no.

           A.      Resolution No. 96-2022
                  A resolution of the City of Boca Raton considering an appeal of the decision of the Planning and
                  Zoning Board in connection with the redevelopment of an approximately 3.5 acre property
                  generally located at 2700 North Federal Highway, specifically an appeal of the site plan approval
                  (Resolution No. 2022-010) (by the applicant) requesting that the site plan approval be modified
                  to remove certain conditions of approval; providing for repealer; providing an effective date
                  (SPA-21-04)

          The City Attorney reviewed the quasi-judicial procedures that would govern the public hearing. Members
          made their ex parte disclosures. The City Clerk administered the oath to those who wished to speak.

           Tamashbeen Rahman , Chief Planner, gave the PowerPoint presentation and noted that this was a
          continuation of the October 12, 2022, hearing. An aerial map of the subject location was shown, and the
          current status of the property was described. Ms. Rahman explained that the appellant was requesting
          removal of conditions relating to undergrounding of utility lines and project phasing, and that she would
          be presenting the entire project per the City Council's request. The Planning and Zoning Board (P&Z)
          approvals were reviewed, which included a site plan for three new commercial buildings and two
          variances. A drawing of the proposed site plan and construction phasing was displayed. Vehicular
          access, landscape buffers and traffic and parking study results were reviewed. Staff analysis concluded
          that the proposed uses and site plan met code requirements, and that the requested variances were
          supported by staff. Ms. Rahman than discussed the applicant's request to change the order of
          construction phases and to remove the condition of undergrounding utility lines on NE 61h Drive. She
          then reported on Council questions posed to staff at the October hearing relating to traffic patterns and
          signal timing. The Florida Department of Transportation (FOOT) had indicated that it was up to the
          applicant to initiate a request for their inp·ut on the proposed options to modify the access points. Ms.
          Rahman confirmed that the appellant had not yet taken action or responded to staff. FDOT and City staff
          had determined that changing the location of the median cut would likely present significant traffic
          operation challenges. Staff also updated the traffic signal condition to provide greater flexibi lity for the
