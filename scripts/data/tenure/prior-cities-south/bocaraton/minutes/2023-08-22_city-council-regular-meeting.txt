          A.     Ordinance No. 5653
                 An ordinance of the City of Boca Raton establishing procedures and regulations to implement
                 Section 166.04151 (7), Florida Statutes, as created by Chapter 2023-17, Laws of Florida (Live
                 Local Act of 2023) for development of statutorily authorized mixed-use residential development
                 (including affordable housing); noting the business and commercial, industrial, and
                 miscellaneous zoning districts in which the mixed-use directive is applicable; providing land
                 development regulations (including those generally applicable in multifamily zoning districts
                 and providing an administrative approval process); noting the statutorily mandated maximums
                 for density and height; providing for severability; providing for repealer; providing for
                 codification; providing an effective date (AM-23-07)

        Acting City Manager George Brown briefly reviewed the previous meeting and introduced Development
        Services Director Brandon Schaad who gave the presentation. Council Members discussed the order
        of presentation for related items on the agenda and discussion ensued. Mr. Schaad described the
        changes worked into the proportionality requirement that would provide more flexibility to developers
        and advised that the floor area ratio (FAR) was not included in this ordinance. Deputy Mayor Mayotte
        read a portion of a memo from the City Attorney which provided an analysis of the 10% and 40%
        regulations, and asked if we needed to restate what was already in code. City Attorney Diana Grub
        Frieser provided clarification of the information from her memo and, at the request of Council Member
        Fran Nachlas, explained the statutory provision called the Bert Harris Act. Council Member Wigder
        noted that the Live Local Act would eventually sunset, and asked if the proposed ordinance would
        accordingly be sunset in tandem. Ms. Frieser agreed that it would be prudent to add it to the City's
        ordinance. Council Member Drucker explained her hesitancy to add more regulations to the City code
        if it wasn't necessary.




Regular Agenda                                       4                                             August 22, 2023
      The public hearing was opened. Michael Marshall explained his thoughts for wanting to exclude the
      floor area ratio (FAR) component from the ordinance. In response to questions from Council Member
      Nachlas, he provided clarification of his remarks. Ms. Frieser then referred to City code and provided
      her thoughts on Mr. Marshall's comments. Bonnie Miske! shared a brief PowerPoint presentation and
      offered her interpretation of the Live Local Act's rules compared to the ordinance being proposed. Lon
      Tabatchnick spoke against the FAR limit in the ordinance as it restricts the number of units that can be
      built. Glenn Gromann expressed his opposition to the restrictions in the proposal and shared his opinion
      that no builder would want to create a development that met the 40% restrictions. Seeing no one else
      come forward, public comment was closed.

      Mr. Brown advised Members that staff would need time to research and redraft the ordinance, and if it
      was the Council's decision not to adopt the 40% ordinance, then he suggested the applicable multi­
      family regulations be drafted into an administrative memorandum to provide direction to staff should an
      application be received. Mr. Singer and Ms. Nachlas indicated their desire to draft some kind of
      regulation. Mr. Wigder, Ms. Mayotte, and Ms. Drucker suggested that staff study the issue further. Ms.
      Mayotte asked for staff to research how municipalities are handling applications based on the 40%
      projects if they do not have an ordinance addressing it. Additional discussion ensued. There was
      consensus from Members to postpone the item until a later date when staff could provide further clarity.

                        Motion was made by Mayor Scott Singer, seconded by Council Member Yvette
                        Drucker, to continue Ordinance No. 5653 to the September 27, 2023, City Council
                        Meeting. Motion carried 4 to 1; Mayor Scott Singer, Council Member Yvette Drucker,
                        Council Member Fran Nachlas, Council Member Marc Wigder voting yes; Deputy
                        Mayor Monica Mayotte voting no.

15.     RESOLUTIONS AND OTHER BUSINESS:

          A. Discussion: Live Local Act: 10% Minimum Affordable Units Discretionary Approvals (Item D on
             the 8/21 /23 Workshop Agenda.)

          Development Services Director Brandon Schaad gave the PowerPoint presentation . He began with
          a background of the Live Local Act and the recommended regulations. Eligibility requirements were
          reviewed and involved transit shelters, sidewalks and street trees, and participation in transit
          programs, if applicable. Among the recommended regulations would be the requirement that projects
          be mixed use, meet the maximum density and property development regulations, and have an
          affordability commitment. Upon concluding, Mr. Schaad responded to Members' questions regarding
          inclusion of amenities into square footage calculations, transit and mobility, and the impact on City
          services. Discussion ensued.

          The public hearing was opened. Lon Tabatchnick spoke in favor of less restriction and against the
          use of FAR to address density. Bonn ie Miske! shared a brief PowerPoint presentation and spoke in
          favor of incentivizing the 10% rule in order to make the new rules profitable, and shared information
          on what other cities are doing. John Donaldson advised that most of the transit stops within the City
          do not meet the 20-minute headway requirement, and that he would prefer additional reductions
          made to the parking conditions. Michael Marshall spoke about the public transit concerns with the
