          Development Services Planner Elizabeth Eassa gave the PowerPoint presentation on the City-initiated
          ordinances that would create two new zoning districts and rezone certain annexed land in the southwest
          planning area into these new zoning districts. Background information was provided. Ms. Eassa went on
          to outline the provisions of the ordinances. Modifications regarding outdoor restaurant seating and
          proximity of establishments to residential properties were also covered. The Planning and Zoning Board
          unanimously recommended approval. Staff recommends approval of both ordinances. Upon concluding,
          Ms. Eassa responded to members' questions, as did Brandon Schaad, Development Services Director.

          The public hearing was opened. Henry Handler, attorney representing several property owners in the
          Midtown area, noted his clients' opposition to the ordinances. Referring to the lawsuits his clients had
          filed against the City, Mr. Handler offered his opinion that the lack of comprehensive planning for Midtown
          had adversely affected his clients. Seeing no one else come forward to speak, the public hearing was
          closed. Mayor Singer again noted that no vote would be taken at this meeting and that the second public
          hearing would be on September 12.

          In response to a request from Deputy Mayor Rodgers, the City Attorney responded to a number of Mr.
          Handler's comments. Ms. Frieser noted that the proposed ordinance takes existing zoning and makes it
          part of the City's zoning code. She then addressed several other comments made by Mr. Handler.
          Council members made final comments.

Council Member O'Rourke commented on the call to artists for the murals project and the fundraiser at which the
mural will be unveiled, iguanas in Wildflower Park, and the date for the upcoming EDSA public outreach meeting
on the Wildflower Park.

Council Member Mayotte commented on the timing of the closure of Spanish River Bridge during upcoming
maintenance and the devastation caused by the red tide along Florida's west coast exacerbated by fertilizer run
off. She noted that the City would soon adopt a fertilizer ordinance to address this problem.

Deputy Mayor Rodgers commented on the upcoming report on chemical weed control. He reminded citizens of
the upcoming primary election and early voting.

Mayor Singer stated the ongoing communication with the Florida Fish and Wildlife Commission regarding the
iguana population. He also noted that although there are quiet zones for trains traveling through the City, the
horns may still sound when track work is being done, asking that the City Manager to keep in touch with Brightline
about upcoming work.

Council Member O'Rourke noted that an upcoming Environmental Advisory Board hearing that will be
rescheduled. Deputy Mayor Rodgers inquired as to the application process for a CCCL variance. The City
