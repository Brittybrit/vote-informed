4.       PUBLIC HEARING ON EMERGENCY ORDINANCE:

          A.     Ordinance No. 5625
                 An emergency ordinance of the City of Boca Raton repealing Chapter 9, Article VI, "Prohibition
                 of Conversion Therapy on Minors," Code of Ordinances; providing for severability; providing for
                 repealer; providing an effective date

          City Attorney Diana Grub Frieser read the text of Ordinance No. 5626 and provided some brief
          background. She explained that in 2017, the City adopted Ordinance No. 5407, prohibiting certain
          therapeutic practices. Ms. Frieser stated that Palm Beach County had adopted a similar ordinance
          and both the City and County had then been named as co-defendants in a federal lawsuit challenging
          the ordinances. Ms. Frieser reviewed case history, concluding with the mandate issued by the 11 th
          Circuit Court of Appeals on August 1, 2022, which found that the City's and County's ordinances were
          unconstitutional. Ms. Frieser explained that because the court found the ordinance violated the First
          Amendment, the City wanted to act quickly to repeal the ordinance and that an emergency ordinance
          is authorized under Section 3.14 of the City Charter. Ms. Frieser recommended adoption of the
          emergency ordinance which, if approved, would be in effect for only 60 days, and introduction of a non­
          emergency ordinance to similarly repeal the ordinance (permanently).           Ms. Frieser noted a
          communication received by Rand Hoch of the Palm Beach Human Rights Council, an advocate of
          Ordinance No. 5407, encouraging the City Council to adopt the proposed ordinances in lieu of
          appealing the case to the U.S. Supreme Court.

          The public hearing was opened and several members of the public expressed their views. Seeing no
          one else indicate their desire to speak, the public hearing was closed. The City Attorney briefly
          highlighted portions of the proposed emergency ordinance that stated that, although the City disagrees
          with the decision of the Court, it respects the Court's authority, and she concluded by reading a section
          affirming the City had no intention of reenacting Ordinance No. 5407 or as similar ordinance.

                         Motion was made by Council Member Monica Mayotte, seconded by Council Member
                         Andy Thomson, to approve Ordinance No. 5625.

         Council Member Mayotte commented that she disagreed with the ruling of the Court but recognized the
         need for the ordinance, and she proposed a resolution stating the Council's opposition to certain
         therapeutic practices. Council Members weighed in and echoed Ms. Mayotte's desire for the resolution.

                          Motion carried unanimously; Mayor Scott Singer, Council Member Yvette Drucker,
                          Council Member Monica Mayotte, Council Member Andy Thomson voting yes.
