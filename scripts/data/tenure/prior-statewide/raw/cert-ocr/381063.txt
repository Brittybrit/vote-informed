Project Description

The Myakka Ranchlands project, comprised of multiple tracts on both the north and south side of
Myakka River State Park, protects a system of conservation areas, making a connection among
state, county, water management district lands and non-profit conservation lands in Southwest
Florida. The intention of this project is to expand and buffer diverse habitats, conserve land that
is known to have rare plants and animals and protect, restore and maintain the quality and natural
functions of land, water and wetlands systems of the state. This protection ensures that sufficient
quantities of water are available to meet the current and future needs of natural systems and the
citizens of the state. Myakka Ranchlands, also known as Myakka Island Conservation Corridor
ranches, builds on a landscape-sized protection area in the Myakka River watershed and
upstream of the Charlotte Harbor Estuary.

Property Description

The subject parcel is owned by the Pallardy Ranch Land Trust 2006 and has been in the family for
more than 30 years. It is a small cow/calf operation of about 25 head of cattle. The 559-acre
subject parcel consists of mostly uplands, with the wetland area being Ogleby Creek that runs
through the center of the subject parcel.

The subject parcel is adjacent to Flatford Swamp, owned by the SWFWMD, which is an important
aquifer recharge area and surface water feature for the headwaters of the Myakka River corridor.
There are seven tributaries that run into Flatford Swamp and then out to Ogleby Creek and Myakka
River, which then reconnects and flows into Charlotte Harbor. The subject parcel protects and
buffers Flatford Swamp from additional encroachments and benefits the Myakka River by
protecting a large storage area in the headwaters. Due to the large surface water present,
acquisition of the subject parcel will contribute to the current and future water quality and quantity
needs of the region. Intense agriculture uses, mining activities, and the threat of development
makes conservation of the subject parcel of the utmost importance.

Additionally, the subject parcel is a critical link within a regional wildlife corridor connecting
conservation lands along the Myakka River, and its tributaries, with conservation lands in the
Manatee River and Little Manatee River watersheds. The subject parcel has a high diversity of
quality natural communities, including mesic flatwoods, hardwood hammocks, riparian forested
wetlands, depression marshes, and wet prairies. The subject parcel supports many listed species,
and at least one Florida panther has been seen on the subject parcel. The remaining intact land
corridors in the Myakka River watershed are an important landscape for the recovery effort of the
Florida panther in southwest Florida, north of the Caloosahatchee River.
