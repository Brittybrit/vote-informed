Item5 Kevin and Jessica Finch (Welaka Lodge & Resort)/Nonwater Dependent Activities/
Determination

REQUEST: Consideration of a request (1) for a determination, pursuant to section 253.03(15),
F.S., and rule 18-21.004(1)(g), F.A.C., that it is in the public interest to allow nonwater dependent
activities to occur on sovereignty submerged lands leased by the Applicants; and (2) to allow
nonwater dependent activities to be conducted on a total of 1,900 square feet, more or less, of the
Applicants’ existing structures.

VOTING REQUIREMENT FOR APPROVAL: Three votes
APPLICANTS: Kevin and Jessica Finch

LOCATION: 1001 Front Street, Welaka, Florida
St Johns River, Putnam County

CONSIDERATION: $0.30 per square foot, based on the appraised upland market rental value,
will be applied annually to the proposed 1,900 square feet, more or less, associated with the
nonwater dependent activity, pursuant to rule 18-21.011(1)(b)6., F.A.C.

STAFF REMARKS: The Applicants are requesting authorization for nonwater dependent
activities within their lease area that is open to the public and includes tables, chairs, sink,
refrigerator, and a small enclosed pantry/storage area under a covered roof. Rule 18-21.004(1)(g),
F.A.C., provides that activities on sovereignty lands shall only be limited to water dependent
activities and minimal secondary nonwater dependent uses unless the Board of Trustees
determines it is in the public interest to allow an exception as determined by a case-by-case
evaluation. The Applicants are requesting an evaluation by the Board of Trustees to determine if
the current nonwater dependent activities are in the public interest.

Background

In 1978, the facility’s sovereignty submerged lands lease included 10 slips and a motor storage
shed constructed over the water. The Applicants acquired the property in 2004 and the lease was
modified to reflect the change in ownership in 2008 but was not renewed in 2013. The Applicants
believed their facility was within the boundary of a 1947 legislative act which conveyed the state
waters of the St. Johns River within the town of Welaka to the town. The Department of
Environmental Protection’s (DEP) Division of State Lands has determined that the Applicants’
facility was annexed into the town limits in 2004, well after the act was enacted and, therefore,
would not apply. The Applicants have accepted DEP’s determination and are seeking a lease for
their 12-slip commercial docking and recreational facility which includes nonwater dependent
activities. The current Temporary Use Agreement allows for the larger facility Qwhich was
reconfigured from the original 10-slip layout) and acknowledges the nonwater dependent activities
until the Board of Trustees makes a determination that the nonwater dependent activities are in the
public interest.

Lease Fees

Rule 18-21.011(1)(b)6., F.A.C., currently provides that the annual lease fees for nonwater
dependent uses shall be negotiated considering the appraised market rental value of the riparian
uplands. A market rental value appraisal was completed in order to obtain a basis for negotiating
annual lease fees. DEP recommends that the nonwater dependent structure should be assessed at
a negotiated nonwater dependent rate of $0.30 per square foot annually or at the rate in effect in
rule 18-21.011(1)(b)6., F.A.C., on the anniversary date of the lease. The remaining lease area will
be assessed the standard water dependent rate which is currently $0.17997 per square foot
annually. The Applicants’ lease will define the nonwater dependent structure in a survey so that
the area cannot be improved or expanded without Board of Trustees’ approval. Approval of this
item does not supersede or eliminate any local, state, or federal permitting requirements.

Public Interest and Compatibility

Rule 18-21.004(1)(g), F.A.C., requires the Board of Trustees to determine that it is in public
interest to authorize the requested activity. The nonwater dependent structure is part of an existing
facility within the town of Welaka. DEP is recommending the Board of Trustees find the project
meets the public interest requirement and is compatible because:

e the nonwater dependent activities will continue to provide public access and enhance
public enjoyment of sovereignty submerged lands without impairing traditional access;

* the nonwater dependent activities do not entail a significant adverse impact to sovereignty
submerged lands and resources under the existing structures; and

e it will also continue to provide an economic benefit to the Applicants and potential
secondary economic benefits to nearby businesses.
