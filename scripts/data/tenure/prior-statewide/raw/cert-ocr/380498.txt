STAFF REMARKS: The subject property is located within the Etoniah/Cross Florida Greenway
Florida Forever project, ranked number 11 in the Critical Natural Lands project category, approved
by the Board of Trustees on June 4, 2019. The project contains 92,180 acres, of which 35,748
acres have been acquired or are under agreement to be acquired. If the Board of Trustees approves
this agreement, 56,432 acres, or 61 percent of the project, will remain to be acquired.

Project Description

The Etoniah/Cross Florida Greenway consists of flatwoods, sandhills, and scrub ecosystems in
central Putnam County, extending to the Ocklawaha River. Conserving land within the project is
important for the survival of a wide variety of wildlife and plants. The Greenway is a unique strip
of land for recreation and conservation that makes a cross-section of the peninsula from the
Withlacoochee River to the St. Johns River. The Etoniah/Cross Florida Greenway project will
conserve land in Putnam County, ensuring that wildlife such as the Florida black bear and scrub
jays and plants such as the Etoniah rosemary, will have areas in which to exist. The project
provides recreation for the public ranging from long-distance hiking trails to fishing, camping, and
hunting. This project also helps to complete the Florida National Scenic Trail (FNST), a statewide
non-motorized trail that crosses several Florida Forever project sites.

Property Description

The subject property is composed of mesic flatwoods, bottomland forest, floodplain swamp, and
sandhill ecosystems, and is adjacent to the Rice Creek Conservation Area, owned by the St. Johns
River Water Management District. Numerous seepage streams originate along the western ridge
of the property and flow eastward into Rice Creek, a tributary of the St. Johns River. The subject
property is currently being converted to longleaf pine in appropriate areas during silviculture
operations and is being thinned to reduce the pine canopy. This allows more sunlight to reach the
forest floor, thus stimulating native groundcover, an essential habitat for many wildlife species,
including the gopher tortoise.

With the conservation easement, 160 acres of usable timber land will instead be used specifically
for Gopher Tortoise habitat. The Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission (FWC) has
discovered that the subject property houses the largest population of spotted turtles m Florida, a
species that has potential to be listed as endangered by FWC. The subject property has documented
45 reptile and amphibian species, most notably the indigo snake (federally threatened), Florida
pine snake (state threatened), gopher tortoise (state threatened), and rusty mud salamander (species
of greatest conservation need). In addition, FWC has documented over 100 species of birds on the
subject property. The FNST currently crosses over a portion of the subject property, connecting
to the Palatka-to-Lake Butler State Trail.
