STAFF REMARKS: This acquisition was negotiated by FDACS/FFS under its RFLPP. If
approved, this project will be the fifty-eighth perpetual conservation easement proposed for
acquisition, with a total of approximately 68,303 acres preserved under the RFLPP. The Buck
Island Ranch project is ranked in Tier One on the RFLPP Acquisition List.

Property Description

The Buck Island Ranch property consists of 6,754 +/- acres located between State Road 70 and
the C-41 Harney Pond Canal in Highlands County. The current RFLPP project site contains
approximately 1,882 acres of the Ranch. If this easement is approved, approximately 67% of the
Buck Island Ranch RFLPP project will be protected. The project site has been enrolled in the
FDACS Best Management Practices (BMP) program since the program’s beginning.

Buck Island Ranch is located in the southeast corner of Highlands County, just south of Lake
Placid. Buck Island, a name originally attributed to local Native Americans, was originally a
4,500-acre dry prairie with seasonal wetlands and connecting sloughs. The land lay within the
Indian Prairie with its waters flowing south to Lake Okeechobee. The Ranch was drained from
the 1940s to the 1970s as part of the expansion of regional canal flowing in Lake Okeechobee.
This included a new section of the C-41 Harvey Pond Canal, which now runs along the north,
west, and southern boundaries of the Ranch.

John D. MacArthur acquired the Ranch in 1968, and the John D. and Catherine T. MacArthur
Foundation oversaw Ranch management until 1988. In that year, Archbold Expeditions assumed
management of the Ranch and established the MacArthur Agro-ecology Research Center for
agriculture, scientific research, and education. With a 3,000 head cow calf operation, Buck Island
Ranch is among the top cattle producers in Florida. The Ranch produces more than 2,100 calves
annually.

Despite habitat loss from historical pasture conversion, the seasonal wetlands, bay tree swamp,
and oak and palm hammocks on the Ranch still support a high regional native species diversity.
The Ranch includes 371 native plants and 171 documented bird species. The Ranch also provides
habitat for several listed animal species, including the indigo snake, Florida black bear, Florida
panther, and snail kite.

The Ranch is located within the Fisheating Creek Ecosystem Florida Forever Project and contains
or is adjacent to several USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service wetland reserve program
easements.
