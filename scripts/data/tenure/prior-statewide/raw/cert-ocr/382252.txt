STAFF REMARKS:

Overview

Under the Aquatic Preserve Resolution of 1969 in chapter 258, Part II, Florida Statutes, aquatic
preserves are set aside to be maintained in essentially the natural condition for the benefit of future
generations. Fort Pickens Aquatic Preserve (FPAP) was designated in 1970. In 1974, Fort Pickens
State Park was deeded to the National Park Service and became part of Gulf Islands National
Seashore (GUIS). Much of FPAP is now co-managed with GUIS.

FPAP comprises more than 30,000 acres, including portions of Big Lagoon, Pensacola Pass, Santa
Rosa Sound, and the waters surrounding east Perdido Key and west Santa Rosa Island.
Approximately two-thirds of FPAP’s acreage is located in the Gulf of Mexico. FPAP includes
seagrass beds and salt marshes, which provide nursery and foraging habitat for roughly seventy

Public and advisory committee meetings were held on August 28 and 29, 2019 in Pensacola to
obtain public input about the FPAP draft management plan.

Management Plan Overview

The hallmark of Florida’s Aquatic Preserve Program is that each site’s natural resource
management efforts are designed in direct response to unique local and regional issues. In this
management plan, FPAP characterizes its issues and delineates the unique goals, objectives, and
strategies that will set the framework for meeting the challenges presented by these issues. The
goals, objectives, and strategies employed to address issues in FPAP are specific to the ecological
and socioeconomic conditions present within and around this site.

The management plan identifies the following issues and objectives:

© Water Quality - Objective

© Implement research, restoration and enhancement projects, throughout FPAP and

adjacent bays, as needed and appropriate, that focus on improving water quality.
e Addressing Habitat Loss and Decline - Objectives

o Continue the restoration of lost submerged aquatic vegetation (SAV) within FPAP.

o Address species decline for indicator species, such as scallops and horseshoe crabs.

o Place additional signage at all access points to display the importance of the habitat
in FPAP.

o Share knowledge and tools with public on habitat preservation and improving water
quality.

o Educate and inform public regarding marine debris impacts and effects to wildlife
and environment.

o Work with stakeholders to develop a Disaster Response Plan to address areas of
concern, develop proper procedures to protect species, and to minimize damages to
the natural communities.

e Improving Information about Submerged Resources - Objective
o Conduct and maintain a record of submerged and emergent resources, including
SAV, algae, marsh grasses, and archaeological and historical resources.
e Public Awareness — Objectives
o Conduct with local, state, and federal agencies, as well as the community to
promote awareness of the aquatic preserve and its significance to users.
