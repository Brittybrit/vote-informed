Project Description

The Northeast Florida Blueway is a chain of marshes and tidal lands extending from the Village
of Mayport in Duval County south through much of St. Johns County. Upland buffers to the St.
Johns and Matanzas Rivers and Estuarine Tidal Marshes cover much of the project. Marshes and
open water areas of the Blueway provide important nurseries for many species of game fish and
shrimp and support recreational and commercial fisheries. The intention of the project is to
connect existing natural areas and greenspace to form a conservation lands corridor along the
north-south waterway.

Property Description

The subject property, known as Fish Island, is one of the last remaining undeveloped waterfront
properties in the City of St. Augustine. Preservation of this important ecologically and historically
rich parcel is vital for the protection of coastal wetlands that serve as nurseries for fisheries and
maritime hammock that is essential for storm and ground water protection. Preventing
development of coastal land susceptible to hazardous events, such as hurricanes, storm surge and
flooding, provides the local community with continued coastal resilience.

Fish Island is part of a land mass known as Anastasia Island and located immediately south of the
Mickler-O’Connell Bridge on State Road 312 and southeast of St. Augustine’s Historical District.
The Matanzas River, part of Florida’s Intracoastal Waterway, borders Fish Island’s western
boundary.

The history and significance of the archaeological remains on Fish Island were formally
recognized when the property was listed in the National Register of Historic Places in 1971. The
Fish Island Site, known as “El Vergel,” was owned by Jesse Fish and his heirs from 1760 to the
1820’s. The site is documented to be one of Florida’s earliest commercial fruit plantations.
