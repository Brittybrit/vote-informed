STAFF REMARKS: The subject parcel is located within the St. Joseph Bay Buffer Florida
Forever project, removed as a substantially complete project in 2005. Pursuant to rule 18-24.002
(2)(b), F.A.C., the Board of Trustees is authorized to allocate Florida Forever funding for
acquisitions within this project.

Project Description

The St. Joseph Bay Buffer Florida Forever project encompasses one of the least disturbed coastal
bay systems in Florida. The land within the St. Joseph Bay State Buffer Preserve (SJBSBP) is of
special biological significance and is acquired to preserve a full range of threatened coastal habitats
and species. Five federally-listed plant species, and 18 other confirmed state-listed endangered or
threatened plants species, are found within the Preserve.

The St. Andrew Bay watershed, Apalachicola River Basin, and the Money Bayou watershed all
converge within the Preserve. Money Bayou is a tidal creek that flows through the SJBSBP and
empties directly into the Gulf of Mexico. In addition to rare species, a unique marine ecosystem,
and significant archaeological value, the St. Joseph Bay Buffer project also hosts a myriad of
drainage systems that affect the immediate water bodies, including Apalachicola Bay, St. Vincent
Bay Indian Lagoon, and the Guif of Mexico.

Property Description

Acquiring the subject property will protect the basin and nearshore waters, where the Money
Bayou tidal creek flows into the Gulf of Mexico and preserves in perpetuity the entire Money
Bayou marsh system; facilitating its best management. This creek expands and contracts
frequently, as seen in historical aerial photography. Acquisition of this subject property will
preserve the connectivity of the system from uplands to the Gulf and protect the critical dynamic
outflow of this system, while preventing further loss and degradation of these coastal wetlands
through development. The proposed acquisition contains the tidal creek channel, adjacent
estuarine intertidal emergent wetlands, marine intertidal wetlands, and pine and beach dune
uplands.

The Money Bayou subject property provides important habitat for nine federally-listed threatened,
or endangered animals, and an additional 10 state-listed species. Protection of the subject property
will preserve the wetlands, beach dunes, and open water that provides important, high-quality
habitat for threatened or endangered marine turtles, migratory shore birds, and other species.

There are four species of sea turtles that inhabit the Gulf of Mexico near the subject property with
loggerheads, occasionally green turtles, nesting along the beaches and near these lands. Many
coastal-dependent and migratory birds utilize the coastal habitats of the outlet of Money Bayou for
breeding, nesting, and feeding. The federally threatened piping plover and the red knot, as well as
the state-protected snowy plover are just a few shorebirds documented along this beachfront.
