STAFF REMARKS: This acquisition was negotiated by FDACS/FFS under its RFLPP. If
approved, this will be the fiftieth perpetual conservation easement proposed for acquisition, with
a total of approximately 55,053 acres preserved under the RFLPP. The Micco Bluff Ranch project
is ranked in Tier One on the 2018 RFLPP Acquisition List.

Property Description

Micco Bluff Ranch is a 2,138-acre cow/calf operation in Okeechobee County, approximately 19
miles northwest of the City of Okeechobee. The Chandler family has been ranching in Okeechobee
County since the turn of the 20" century and purchased the Micco Bluff Ranch property in 1981.
Should this easement be approved, approximately 1,580 acres will remain in the Micco Bluff
Ranch Tier One project.

Micco Bluff Ranch manages a herd of approximately 600 cattle on the entire ranch. The Chandler
family has progressively implemented Best Management Practices (BMPs) to improve production
and land management. The family has worked closely with FDACS and the local livestock agent.

Micco Bluff Ranch is located within the Avon Park Air Force Range Sentinel Landscape. The
Ranch is adjacent to a South Florida Water Management District (SFWMD) flowage easement as
part of the restored Kissimmee River and shares a boundary with the Corona Ranch RFLPP project.
Several other conservation lands and projects lie within 10 miles in several directions. These lands
include Kissimmee Prairie Preserve State Park, and the Triple Diamond and Corrigan Ranch
Florida Forever BOT projects. This Micco Bluff Ranch acquisition would contribute to increased
wildlife corridors along the Kissimmee River.

During the site visit to Micco Bluff Ranch, Florida sandhill cranes and gopher tortoises were
detected. According to the landowner, Audubon’s crested caracara, Sherman’s fox squirrel, indigo
snakes, and Florida burrowing owls are frequently observed on portions of the property where
suitable habitat exist for these species. Florida sandhill cranes have been seen with colts indicating
probable nesting in the area’s marshes. Also, the remaining depression marshes and streams
distributed throughout the property likely attract several imperiled wading bird species (tricolored
heron, little blue heron, roseate spoonbill, and wood stork) as water levels recede during the dry
season. To maintain productive wildlife habitat, the landowner routinely implements habitat
management practices such as exotic weed control, prescribed burning, rotational grazing, and
annual mowing of pastures. As a result of these efforts, the plant communities found on the
property possess a vegetative composition and structure that creates favorable foraging conditions
for imperiled wildlife such as the gopher tortoise, the Florida burrowing owl, Audubon’s crested
caracara and the Florida sandhill crane.
