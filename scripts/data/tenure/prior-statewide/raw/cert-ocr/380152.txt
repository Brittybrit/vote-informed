Project Description

Wakulla Springs, located south of Tallahassee, is one of the largest and deepest artesian springs in
the world and is largely protected by a state park and state forest lands. The enormous caverns
that feed the springs spread far to the north and west outside of public ownership. The Wakulla
Springs Protection Zone Florida Forever project is designed to protect the spring shed of Wakulla
Springs and the land above the conduits that feed it.

Property Description

The 717-acre subject property is an integral part of the protection zone that connects the
Apalachicola National Forest with the Edward Ball Wakulla Springs State Park and its national
natural landmark spring. Additionally, the subject property preserves a wildlife and recreation
corridor from the national forest to the state park. The subject property includes a 2,353-square-
foot single family structure that will be utilized by the park service, 300 acres of longleaf pine
trees, and 9 karst windows that connect to a unique network of underground cave systems forming
the aquifer conduit system of Wakulla Springs.

Mr. Ferrell’s family has owned the property since the 1870’s. His great-grandfather, John Stallon
Ferrell, settled in Wakulla County after the Civil War buying 2,000 acres to farm cotton, corn,
peanuts, timber and turpentine. Today, the property is situated in an area heavily favored for
development by new residents working in the capital city. The area is also highly prone to having
land converted to more intense agricultural uses. DEP has pursued acquisition of the subject
property several times over the past 23 years to preserve and protect the important watershed of
Wakulla Springs.

The subject property buffers and protects an additional 13 karst features with hydrological
connections to the Floridan Aquifer. Seventy-three percent of the property has sandy soils
overlaying highly permeable karst areas, thus allowing for significant rates of aquifer recharge to
the spring shed for Wakulla Springs, the source of water for the nine-mile Wakulla River, an
Outstanding Florida Water. The aquifer recharge provided by subject property is essential to the
water quality and quantity at Wakulla Springs and the Big Bend Seagrasses Aquatic Preserve along
St. Mark’s shores in the Apalachee Bay.
