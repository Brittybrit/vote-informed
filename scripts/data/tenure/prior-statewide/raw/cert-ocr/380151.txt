STAFF REMARKS: The subject property is located within the Strategic Managed Area Lands
List (SMALL) Florida Forever Project, ranked number 8 in the Critical Natural Lands Category,
approved by the Board of Trustees on June 4, 2019. The project contains 11,573 acres and the
subject parcel is proposed as the initial purchase within this new project. If the Board of Trustees
approves this agreement, 11,443 acres, or 99 percent of the project, will remain to be acquired.
The anticipated contribution from the Federal Wildlife and Sport Fish Restoration program toward
the acquisition is $296,737 (75 percent) and the anticipated contribution from the State is $98,913
(25 percent).

Project Description

The SMALL Florida Forever project, sponsored by the Department of Environmental Protection’s
(DEP) Division of Recreation and Parks, the DEP’s Office of Greenways and Trails, the
Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services’ Florida Forest Service, and Florida Fish and
Wildlife Conservation Commission, is a collection of one-owner parcels statewide that would
augment or improve management of existing state-managed conservation lands, if acquired. The
parcels included in this project have been identified as part of the optimum management boundary
within the approved management plans of ten state parks, seven trail corridors, ten state forests,
and seven wildlife management/environmental areas.

Property Description

The 129.3-acre subject property is a strategic inholding consisting of a 100-foot wide, elevated dirt
and shell rock-graded former railroad right-of-way that bisects the 65,758-acre Babcock-Webb
Wildlife Management Area (BWWMA). It extends 8.2 miles in a general north-south direction in
southwest Charlotte County and now resembles an all-weather roadway. The subject property
provides the only road on the BWWMaA with full north-south access to many parts of the wildlife
management area, which are otherwise inaccessible except by off-road travel.

Located near the densely populated cities of Punta Gorda, Cape Coral, and Fort Myers, the
BWWMA’s forests, seasonal ponds, marshes, and wetland depressions provide essential habitat
for an extensive array of imperiled, rare, and common wildlife species. These species include the
red-cockaded woodpecker, Florida sandhill crane, roseate spoonbill, Florida bonneted bat, as well
as white-tailed deer and the northern bobwhite quail. The BWWMA is managed by FWC and
provides ecological diversity, managed habitat for imperiled, rare and common wildlife, and the
public with outdoor recreational opportunities.

Hunting, fishing, target shooting, wildlife viewing, camping, horseback-riding, scenic driving,
bicycling, and hiking are included among the outstanding recreational opportunities offered on
BWWMA. Additionally, the wildlife management area has one of the only field trial grounds
facilities on public land in southwest Florida. The field trial grounds cover over 6,000 acres and
are managed for northern bobwhite habitat, to provide hunting opportunities, and as a site for bird
dog training and competition.
