STAFF REMARKS: The City has applied to purchase the 6.64-acre parcel of filled, formerly
submerged, sovereignty lands. The 6.64-acre parcel of filled, formerly submerged, sovereignty
lands was historically part of Lake Clarke, prior to the Board of Trustees authorizing the dredging
of sovereignty submerged lands and the filling of the 6.64-acre parcel as part of the West Palm
Beach Canal project and the I-95 bridge project. Conveyance of the 6.64-acre parcel of filled,
formerly submerged, sovereignty lands will not interfere with the conservation of fish, marine and
other wildlife, or other natural resources since the dredge and fill project was completed by 1986.

The City owns a 196-acre upland parcel adjacent to the land-locked 6.64-acre parcel of filled,
formerly submerged, sovereignty lands. The City’s public golf course is located on the adjacent
uplands. In January 2017, the City sought proposals (RFP No. 16-17-209) for the redevelopment
and operation of the West Palm Beach Municipal Golf Course and associated development area.
In December 2018, the City additionally advertised an invitation to potential participants to submit
Public Private Partnership (“P3”) proposals for the redevelopment. The City’s requirements for
the project include a recreational destination through a redesigned public championship golf course
to remain germane to the 1947 historic feel and part of the Florida Historic Golf Trail; public
boating access; public pedestrian waterfront boardwalk; hotel and a residential/mixed use
development to attract new residents to invigorate the City’s economy.

Constitutional and Rule Requirements/Public Interest

Pursuant to Article X, Section 11 of the Florida Constitution and rule 18-21.004(1)(a), F.A.C., the
Board of Trustees may convey sovereignty lands if determined by the Board of Trustees to be in
the public interest. The Department of Environmental Protection (DEP) offers the following to
assist the Board of Trustees in making an affirmative determination that conveyance of the 6.64-
acre parcel of filled, formerly submerged, sovereignty lands is in the public interest:

e it will enable the City to expand its efforts on currently inaccessible, unmanaged lands;

e it will provide multiple public recreation opportunities, attract businesses, jobs and housing
to the area for the benefit of the local economy; and

e it will resolve an encroachment of a portion the golf course maintenance facility located on
the 6.64-acre parcel of filled, formerly submerged, sovereignty lands.

Although the Board of Trustees, under section 253.03(1), F.S., has the authority to dispose of
filled, formerly submerged, sovereignty lands, there are no guidelines in either the statutes or the
Board of Trustees’ rules to determine the amount to charge for such conveyance. DEP
recommends, and the applicant has agreed to pay $87,000, which represents the appraised value
of the 6.64-acre parcel of filled, formerly submerged, sovereignty lands.

Special Approval Condition
Prior to this conveyance, requirements of section 253.03(1), F.S., must be met by the South Florida

Water Management District’s Spoil Site Easement No. 26190 (3151-50) being released to the
Board of Trustees.

Noticing

The City is the only property owner within 500 feet of the subject project area, and the City is the
riparian landowner; therefore, noticing was not required, pursuant to sections 253.111 and
253.115, FS.
