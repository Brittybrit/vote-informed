COUNTY: Leon

APPLICANT: Florida Department of Environmental Protection’s Coastal and Aquatic Managed

LOCATION: Lake Jackson Aquatic Preserve (LJAP) is located in the Florida Panhandle just
north of Tallahassee.

STAFF REMARKS:

Overview

Under the Aquatic Preserve Resolution of 1969 and then in Chapter 258, Part II, Florida Statutes,
aquatic preserves are set aside to be maintained in essentially the natural condition for the benefit
of future generations. The Florida Legislature designated and defined the boundaries of LJAP in
1973.

LJAP comprises 5,133 acres, including Lake Jackson itself, Carr Lake and Mallard Pond. Lake
Jackson is one of the only large sinkhole lakes in which hydrology has not been altered. LJAP is
predominantly comprised of a clastic upland lake, transitioning to wet prairie depending on the
hydrologic conditions. Natural drawdowns in the lake occur periodically, which helps to maintain
water quality and support the natural communities.

The Florida Department of State’s Division of Historical Resources has identified seven earthen
temple mounds within Lake Jackson Archaeological Mounds State Park, which is adjacent to
LJAP. In addition, there are 37 archaeological and historical sites near LJAP, including
archaeological sites, farmsteads, plantations, and other historic structures.

Management Plan Public Involvement
Public and advisory committee meetings were held on December 5 and 6, 2018 at the Lake Jackson
Community Center in Tallahassee to obtain public input about the LJAP draft management plan.

Management Plan Overview

The hallmark of Florida’s Aquatic Preserve Program is that each site’s natural resource
management efforts are designed in direct response to unique local and regional issues. In this
management plan, LJAP characterizes its issues and delineates the unique goals, objectives, and
strategies that will set the framework for meeting the challenges presented by these issues. The
goals, objectives, and strategies employed to address issues of LJAP are specific to the ecological
and socioeconomic conditions present within and around this site.

The management plan identifies the following issues and objectives:
e Water and Sediment Quality - Objectives
o Determine status and trends of water and sediment quality at LIAP to provide
recommendations to address potential impacts to natural resources.
o Identify nutrients, sediments and other pollutants entering the lake and their
sources.
o Support efforts of regulatory programs, local government, and landowners to
reduce impacts from development in the watershed.
o Coordinate with regulatory programs and local governments to develop a
prioritized work plan for potential lake drawdown actions.
o Ensure that management activities and public use do not impede the natural cycle.
e Habitat Restoration and Resource Protection - Objectives
o Reduce invasive aquatic, wetland, and upland species in LJAP.
Improve habitat in LJAP for species diversity.
Reduce the amount of man-made debris in the aquatic preserve.
Encourage increased law enforcement presence on Lake Jackson and at access
points.
e Public Involvement - Objectives
o Promote awareness of the aquatic preserve and its significance to users.
o Educate visitors, residents, and users about actions they can take to conserve and
restore resources in LJAP.
© Increase public capacity to assist in controlling invasive species as well as avoiding
the introduction of new invasive species.
e Sustainable Public Use - Objectives
o Increase public access and low impact recreational opportunities on LJAP.
