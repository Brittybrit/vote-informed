Item 6 BOT/ United States of America/ National Park Service/ Donation/ Conveyance/ Big
Cypress National Preserve/ Big Cypress National Preserve Addition

REQUEST: Approval to donate and convey 14,114.88 acres of state-owned Section 16 School
Lands and Swamp and Overflow Lands in Big Cypress National Preserve and Big Cypress
National Preserve Addition to the United States of America, on behalf of the National Park Service,
to complete the legislatively required conveyance.

VOTING REQUIREMENT: Three votes
COUNTIES: Collier and Miami-Dade
APPLICANT: United States of America on behalf of the National Park Service

LOCATION: Big Cypress National Preserve (Preserve) and Big Cypress National Preserve
Addition (Addition), (collectively, Big Cypress)

CONSIDERATION: $1,044,480 to be deposited into the State School Trust Fund

STAFF REMARKS: Big Cypress was established by the U.S. Congress in 1974 and expanded
in 1988 to assure the preservation, conservation, and protection of the natural, scenic, hydrologic,
floral and faunal, and recreational values of the Big Cypress Watershed in the State of Florida and
to provide for the enhancement and public enjoyment thereof. Both state and federal acquisitions
were pursued under legislative direction designating monetary participation and land ownership.

Land Requested to be Conveyed — 14,114.88 total acres of state-owned land to NPS

The Board of Trustees acquired or owned a total of 43,527.76 acres in Big Cypress that were to be
donated to the National Park Service (NPS) pursuant to section 380.055, F.S. (The Big Cypress
Conservation Act of 1973), Public Law 100-301, Public Law 93-440, and the December 1974
agreement between the Board of Trustees and NPS. On December 7, 2010, the Board of Trustees
approved the conveyance of 29,412.88 acres of these state-owned lands and received partial
reimbursement to meet the 80/20 cost share for the acreage in the Addition. The NPS is now

requesting the conveyance of the remaining 14,114.88 acres to complete the required transfer of
all state-owned lands in Big Cypress to the federal government.

The subject 14,114.88 acres are comprised of 12,194.88 acres in the Preserve (7,255.88 acres of
Section 16 State School Lands and 4,939 acres of land formerly owned by the Board of Education
classified as Swamp and Overflow Lands) which are being donated at no cost, plus 1,920 acres of
Section 16 State School Lands in the Addition.

A cost share reimbursement to the Board of Trustees from NPS of $1,044,480 for the 1,920 acres
in the Addition was previously determined based on $680 per acre (1,920 x $680 =
$1,305,600/80% = $1,044,480) and will be made to ensure that the 80/20 cost share provisions
contained in Public Law 100-301 are met. The land is to be conveyed “as is” and NPS is
responsible for funding and completing all due diligence products.

Section 16 school land — Board of Education reimbursement

Section 1010.71, F.S., states “¢1) The State School Trust Fund shall be derived from the following
sources: (a) The proceeds of all lands that have been or may hereafter be granted to the state by
the United States for public school purposes.” Because Public Law 93-440, the federal law
establishing the Preserve, requires that all state-owned lands in the Preserve be donated to the
federal government, there will be no proceeds associated with the conveyance of the 12,194.88
acres in the Preserve.

To comply with the 80/20 cost share for the 1,920 acres in the Addition, the federal government
will reimburse the Board of Trustees $1,044,480 which will be deposited into the State School
Trust Fund.

Project Approvals

Acquisition and Restoration Council - Because the conveyance of Big Cypress lands to NPS for
continued conservation purposes was legislatively agreed to pursuant to section 380.055, F.S., and
16 U.S.C. §698f(d), the Acquisition and Restoration Council’s recommendations were not
solicited.

Comprehensive Plan

A consideration of the status of the local government comprehensive plan was not made for this
item. The Department of Environmental Protection has determined that land conveyances are not
subject to the local government planning process.
