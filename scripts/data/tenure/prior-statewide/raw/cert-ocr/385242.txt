Project Description

The Horse Creek Ranch project (Project) is located in both Hardee and DeSoto counties southwest
of the town of Wauchula, Florida. The county line divides the ranch about midway north-south.
The site’s natural communities include mesic and wet flatwoods, bottomland forest, depression
and basin marshes, mesic hammock, scrubby flatwoods, baygall, and blackwater streams. The
natural uplands are principally longleaf-pine-dominated mesic flatwoods with pockets of scrubby
flatwoods on the highest elevations and more hydric wet flatwoods in the transitional area between
the uplands and the Horse Creek floodplain. The project lies within a wildlife corridor in the
Florida Ecological Greenways Network.

Property Description

The 11,958-acre subject property, known as Horse Creek Ranch, has been maintained by the
Carlton family for cattle ranching and other agricultural and recreational activities since the mid-
1800s. Nine generations of the Carlton family have worked and recreated on the property. Mr.
Carlton and his family are continuing the family legacy of stewardship in their care of the land and
have a strong commitment to protect it for future generations. The conservation of Horse Creek
Ranch will protect in perpetuity the wildlife and water that flows through the ranch, across
southwest Florida into Charlotte Harbor.

About half of the property is in improved pastures and the remaining portion is in natural condition.
Approximately 11 miles of Horse Creek, a major contributor of the Peace River, runs south through
the subject property. Limestone Creek, as well as a small tributary to Horse Creek, are located on
the western side of the property. Horse Creek drains south into the western part of the Peace River
basin and flows south to the Charlotte Harbor Estuary.

The proposed conservation easement acquisition is a coordinated effort between the Department
of Environmental Protection (Department) and the Southwest Florida Water Management District
(District) to purchase the entire 16,316-acre Project. As part of the coordinated effort, the
Department is proposing to acquire the land east of Horse Creek (11,958-acre subject property)
and the District is acquiring the land west of Horse Creek (4,358 acres).

The subject property is located in the Peace River basin, one of eight basins that form the Charlotte
Harbor Estuary. Preservation of this hydrologically rich parcel is critical for the overall protection
of the area’s drinking water supply, as it enhances the sustainability of the water resource by
restricting development, including potential phosphate mining currently proposed less than a mile
from the site. Conserving the property through a conservation easement protects the Peace River
and ultimately the Charlotte Harbor Estuary which supports a vibrant and economically vital
tourism industry and local commercial and sport fishing industry.
