Item 2 Lignumvitae Key Aquatic Preserve Management Plan

REQUEST: Consideration of a request to approve the Lignumvitae Key Aquatic Preserve
Management Plan.

VOTING REQUIREMENT FOR APPROVAL: Two members, one of whom is the Governor,
when four members are voting; or any two members, when three members are voting.

COUNTY: Monroe
APPLICANT: Florida Department of Environmental Protection

LOCATION: Lignumvitae Key Aquatic Preserve (LKAP), located in the Village of Islamorada
at the boundary between the Upper and Lower Keys.

STAFF REMARKS:

Overview

Under the Aquatic Preserve Resolution of 1969, as well as in Chapter 258, Part I, Florida Statutes,
aquatic preserves are set aside to be maintained in essentially the natural condition for the benefit
of future generations. Lignumvitae Key was designated as an aquatic preserve in 1972 and is co-
managed with the Lignumvitae Key Botanical State Park and Florida Keys National Marine
Sanctuary. The LKAP Management Plan was last approved by the Board of Trustees on December
17, 1991.

LKAP is comprised of the 6,700 acres that surround Lignumvitae Key, which is located in the
Village of Islamorada, between the upper and lower Matecumbe Keys. LKAP’s acreage is 80
percent seagrass beds that serve to protect the waters north of the Everglades National Park.

The Florida Department of State’s Division of Historical Resources has documented 22 historical
structures and archeological sites near the aquatic preserve, including prehistoric Native American

Management Plan Public Involvement

Meetings were held to obtain public input about the construction and subsequent draft of the LKAP
Management Plan. Participants’ primary concerns pertained to water quality and monitoring as
well as concerns about maintaining, but not expanding, public access in the aquatic preserve.
These concerns were incorporated into the draft management plan.

Abovementioned meetings included: a scoping meeting on January 16, 2020 (prior to the
development of the management plan), an advisory committee meeting on November 18, 2021,
and a public meeting on January 25, 2022.

Management Plan Overview

The hallmark of Florida’s Aquatic Preserve Program is that each aquatic preserve’s natural
resource management plan is unique and designed to directly address local and regional
challenges. Thus, the LKAP management plan, characterizes the specific challenges facing the
aquatic preserve, and delineates the corresponding goals, objectives, and strategies, which serve
as the framework for overcoming each challenge. The goals, objectives, and strategies enumerated
in the management plan are specific to the ecological and socioeconomic conditions present within
and around LKAP.

The LKAP Management Plan identifies the following challenges and the associated goals,
objectives, and strategies:

e Water Quality
o Improve water quality within LKAP by identifying and addressing problem areas.
o Improve LKAP’s long-term water quality monitoring. This will be achieved by
gaining a better understanding of the current water quality status, improving water
collection techniques, and then utilizing this knowledge to project future changes
in LKAP’s natural resources.

e Wildlife and Habitat Protection

o Obtain better data on LKAP’s natural resources in order to more effectively manage
and protect them.

o Reduce damage from marine debris to habitats and wildlife, including seagrass
beds, hardbottom, and mangrove islands.

co Increase enforcement of existing regulations.

o Expand community volunteer opportunities in resource management activities,
including marine debris removal and resource monitoring.

o Identify and locate unknown archaeological and historical resources within LKAP.

e Public Awareness
o Enhance knowledge of natural resources and wildlife habitats within LKAP and
educate visitors on how they can be responsible stewards.
o Increase the awareness of management activities inside the aquatic preserve by
providing timely and accurate water quality data to the public and other
stakeholders.
