 5        Item 1 is request for approval of the 2020
 6   Statewide Emergency Shelter Plan submitted by the
 7   Division of Emergency Management.   Danny Kilcollins

10        Danny.
11        MR. KILCOLLINS:    Good morning, Governor and
12   Cabinet members.    I appreciate the opportunity to
13   present the Division's 2020 Statewide Emergency
14   Shelter Plan.   The Division of Emergency Management
15   is responsible for preparing the Plan pursuant to
16   Section 1013.372(2) and Section 252.3852(b),
17   Florida Statutes.
18        The Plan was prepared in late 2019 and
19   delivered on January 31st, 2020 as required by the
20   statute.   The Plan serves as a guide to determine
21   need for new school facilities to be designed and
22   built as hurricane evacuation shelters.
23        This Plan is updated every two years and
24   projects needed space required over a five-year
25   period and breaks up this information for general



           C & N REPORTERS        850-697-8314
                                                          6

 1   population and special-need shelters.    Also
 2   included is information pertaining to the
 3   availability of pet friendly shelters.
 4        Florida has made significant strides in
 5   reducing its deficit of safe public hurricane
 6   evacuation shelter space over the past 20 years.
 7   When we started this process in 2000, there was a
 8   deficit of more than 1.5 million shelter spaces.
 9   Statewide we have created more than one million
10   spaces now; and combined with reducing demand for
11   shelters, we have eliminated the deficit on a
12   statewide aggregate basis.
13        Some regions still have deficits.    This change
14   statewide though allows us to be flexible and use
15   host sheltering when we need to do so.   This has
16   been achieved through a combination of retrofitting
17   of existing public schools and other facilities and
18   use of enhanced hurricane resistant construction
19   for new schools.
20        About 45 percent of the spaces have been
21   created through schools being designed and
22   constructed to meet the Department of Education's
23   public shelter design criteria.   About 32 percent
24   of the spaces were created through retrofitting of
25   suitable existing facilities.



           C & N REPORTERS        850-697-8314
                                                              7

 1          The 2020 Plan determined that seven RPC
 2   regions -- Regional Planning Council regions --
 3   have sufficient capacity of general population
 4   hurricane evacuation shelter space in 2020.       They
 5   are:   Region 1, West Florida; Region 2, Apalachee;
 6   Region 3, North Central Florida; Four, Northeast
 7   Florida; Region 5, East Central Florida; 9,
 8   Treasure Coast; and 10, South Florida.
 9          Three RPC regions have deficits of general
10   population space, and they are:   Region 6, Central
11   Florida; Region 7, which is Tampa Bay; and
12   Region 8, which is Southwest Florida.
13          Five of the ten RPC regions have a deficit of
14   special-need shelters, and the deficit will
15   continue through 2025.    They are:   Region 2,
16   Apalachee; Region 4, Northeast Florida; Region 5,
17   East Central Florida; Region 6, Central Florida;
18   and Region 8, which is Southwest Florida.
19          For special-need shelters, five regions can
20   demonstrate they have sufficient spaces though.
21   They are:   Region 1, West Florida; Region 3, North
22   Central Florida; Region 7, which is Tampa Bay;
23   Region 9, which is Treasure Coast; and Region 10,
24   which is South Florida.
25          This is a significant improvement over 2018 in
