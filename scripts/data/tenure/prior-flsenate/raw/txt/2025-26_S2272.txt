4. Project/Program Description
    At Glory House, we provide long-term restoration to survivors of human trafficking -- one heart at a time. Through our
   Independent Living Program, our team provides wraparound support and specialized care coordination for survivors who
   are currently living on their own and seeking a life of freedom. In 2025, we are opening our residential home to provide the
   safety, stability, and support survivors need to get to the root of their healing and re-enter society as whole individuals who
   have broken the cycle of exploitation and trauma. This will be the first long-term residential home for adult female survivors
   in South Florida.

13. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The funding will allow Glory House to roll out operations and staffing for our safe home and independent living program
    by providing high-caliber staff to mentor, advocate, and serve survivors so that they can achieve true healing and
    autonomy. Our program greatly reduces the outcome of survivors returning to the cycle of exploitation, depending on
    government assistance, and breaks inter-generational sexual exploitation in their children.
   b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Our Residential Program provides survivors a minimum of twelve (12) months to live in the safe home to heal from their
    trauma and re-enter society as a thriving individual. Our Independent Living Program provides survivors with over thirty-
    five (35) wraparound services, and our goal in both programs is to help survivors reach true autonomy, health, and
    stability.
   c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Glory House will be providing direct care and wraparound support to adult female survivors in our Independent and
    Residential Living Programs. We provide over thirty-five (35) wraparound support services and supply targeted in-house
    care coordination, partnership referrals, financial literacy, trauma therapy, job readiness, and strategic goal setting along
    with measured accountability and milestones that are met, so each survivor can heal and reach the autonomy and self-
    sufficiency to flourish as they re-enter society as a restored person.
   d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The target population is adult female survivors of sex trafficking. A minimum of (25) survivors in our Independent Living
    Program and a minimum (4-6) survivors in our Residential (for 12 months) will be served through our holistic, life-
    changing model of support.
   e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
   be measured?

     At Glory House, we track a multitude of metrics for each survivor demonstrating how we provide direct financial
    assistance (rent, groceries, personal aid, etc.) through our program along with wraparound support. This includes
    strategic short and long-term goal setting, job readiness, health, financial literacy, and other metrics showing the
    transformation that happens for a human trafficking survivor when long-term support and restoration is the goal.
   f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
   for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?

    A corrective action plan will be formed to meet deliverables and performance measures.
