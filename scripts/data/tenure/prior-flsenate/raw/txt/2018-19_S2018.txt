4. Project/Program Description:
    Holocaust education has recently been identified by the Governor's Task Force on Holocaust Education as the most
    impactful way of teaching citizenship in Florida schools, with long lasting results into adulthood. Funding is required
    to strengthen education and outreach, to continue current and introduce new programming for students that will
    further fulfill the Holocaust Memorial's mission. 1. Student Holocaust Theatre/Literary/History Project/Contest; 2.
    Holocaust Education Symposiums for High School/Community College Students; 3. Exhibit: Deadly Medicine:
    Creating the Master Race; 4. Online Holocaust Documentary Streaming Webpage; 5. Vocational/College Student
    Initiative; 6. Community Commemorations for Kristallnacht and Holocaust Memorial Day; 7. Holocaust Education
    Week; 8. Teacher Education Institute; 9. Teen-Survivor Program

11. Program Performance:

    a. What is the specific purpose or goal that will be achieved by the funds requested?

           The Holocaust Memorial Miami Beach will create and implement innovative programming for students grades
          5-12 and college, centered around key issues including intolerance, hatred, racism, human rights and
          citizenship responsibilities. It accomplishes this mission via its permanent onsite exhibit, free and open to the
          public 365 days a year, community programs, year round events and educational opportunities for students,
          the local community and tourists from all over the world.

    b. What are the activities and services that will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

           1. Student Holocaust Theatre/Literary/History Project/Contest - An educational contest will be create din
          which secondary students using the Holocaust as a catalyst prepare a project centered around the key issues of
          citizenship and fighting intolerance. The categories would be in original short theatre production, video
          production, and historical and/or literary project. 2. Holocaust Education Symposiums- A series of Holocaust
          Education seminars will be created specifically for high school and community college students with a focus on
          undeserved communities. 3. Exhibit: Deadly Medicine: Creating the Master Race- This exhibition that focuses
          on Nazi Germany's medical experiments will be organized and displayed in a location open to the public. 4.
          Online Holocaust Documentary Streaming Webpage 5.Vocational/College Student Initiative 6. Community
          Commemorations 7. Holocaust Education Week 8. Teacher Education Institute 9. Teen- Survivor Program

d. What are the direct services to be provided to citizens by the appropriations project?

      Educating hundreds of teachers per year with fulfilling the Florida State mandate to teach the Holocaust.
     Public school teachers have on average 150 students per year. Providing quality and innovative programming to
     students, teachers and the public regarding the Holocaust, combatting intolerance, racism and bigotry. Year
     round programming on a daily basis, tours for the public led by trained docents, to Holocaust Education Week,
     Teen-Survivor Program, Student Education Initiative and the Memorial's additional programming.

e. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

      More than 500 teachers per year and their students; the 8-10,000 5th-12th graders, post-secondary and adult
     students who visit the Memorial annually on field trips; the more than 2,500 people who attend our programs
     such as our community commemorations and the 125,000 visitors to the Memorial.

f.   What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome
     will be measured?
      The funding will be used to implement impactful and meaningful educational experiences that will benefit the
     attendees of the programs to be conducted under the auspices of this grant. The Holocaust Memorial attracts
     students from public schools, charter schools, Catholic and private schools, home school groups and alternative
     education groups such as Miami Job corp. Daily visitors to the Memorial come from the local community as well
     as tourists who come from all over the world. Teachers will benefit from the Teacher-Education Institute
     Seminars learning the latest in Holocaust pedagogy which they will in turn use to educate their students. The
     new Holocaust Theatre/Literary Project/History Project/Contest will provide participating schools an avenue to
     expose and educate their students the lessons of the Holocaust giving them a venue to express their
     understanding of this unprecedented event and how it relates to them and the modern world.
