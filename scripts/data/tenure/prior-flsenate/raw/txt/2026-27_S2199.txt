13. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     By providing consistent, caring mentors and evidence-based guidance, the program will strengthen resilience, reduce
    risky behaviors, and empower students to thrive in school, at home, and in their communities. The requested funds will
    allow Youth for Christ Miami and Broward to expand mentorship and life-skills programming that supports at-risk youth
    in achieving academic success, emotional stability, and personal growth.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     We provide innovative programs utilizing consistent mentoring in Juvenile Detention Centers, underserved
    neighborhoods, school campuses and strategic youth centers. We teach life-skills classes, goal setting, conflict
    resolution, and decision making. We use trauma-informed, evidence-based practices, family meals, and round table
    discussions to deepen our impact by addressing the emotional and behavioral roots of student struggles. This
    integration helps youth regulate emotions, build resilience, and form healthy relationships.
   c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Our intervention programs throughout Miami and Broward funnel youth through large group programs that lead to
    small group mentoring and one-on-one follow up. This personal involvement allows mentors to directly address unique
    needs and refer to additional community-based support services.
   d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The target population is at-risk youth ages 11–19 from under-resourced schools and communities across Miami-Dade
    and Broward counties, many of whom face academic struggles, trauma, and unstable home environments. Through
    mentorship, life-skills development, and trauma-informed Rebalance programming, the Youth Matter Mentorship
    Program expects to serve approximately 4,000 students this fiscal year, equipping them to succeed academically,
    emotionally, and socially.
   e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
   be measured?

     Expected outcomes include stronger relationships, better attendance, reduced behavioral incidents, and healthier
    decision-making. Success will be measured through pre- and post-program surveys, attendance tracking, mentor
    evaluations, and school feedback on academic and behavioral progress.
   f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
   for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?

    We would suggest a mandatory performance improvement plan for failing to meet deliverables or performance
    measures provided for in the contract.
