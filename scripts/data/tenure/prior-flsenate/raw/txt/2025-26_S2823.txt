1. Project Title          Uplifting School Mental Health Support in Miami-Dade Schools

2. Senate Sponsor        Bryan Avila

3. Date of Request       2/24/2025

4. Project/Program Description
    The specific purpose of the funds requested is to develop and implement a comprehensive training program for school
   personnel, focusing on early mental health intervention. This initiative is accomplished through a formal Certificate in
   School Mental Health Support. The focus is on equipping them with skills to effectively recognize, assess, and respond to
   the early mental health needs of middle school students, fostering a healthier and more supportive learning environment.
   Activities and services provided include interactive workshops, access to mental health toolkits, and ongoing support from
   mental health professionals. These services aim to ensure that educators are well-prepared to meet the mental health
   needs of their students effectively.

13. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     This grant will increase access to mental health support within school systems by equipping school personnel with the
    skills and knowledge to act as first-level interventionists. Through specialized training, school personnel will learn to
    recognize the early manifestations of mental health concerns in their students and respond effectively. This training will
    empower educators to de-escalate challenging behaviors, provide initial support, and identify when and how to connect
    students with appropriate mental health resources. By enhancing school personnels' capacity to intervene early, the
    program addresses a critical gap in mental health services within schools, ensuring that students receive timely and
    appropriate care.
   b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     This initiative involves the completion of a Certificate in School Mental Health Support by 100 school professionals, which
    activities include curriculum development, training in evidence-based practices for mental health support, early
    intervention, and resilience-building strategies tailored to middle school students. It will also include crisis intervention
    skills, resource development, mentorship and networking opportunities, as well as follow-up support, assessment and
    program outcomes on both the direct participants as well as the students they serve.
   c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     The initiative will provide the following direct services to participants: a) Mental Health Training for School Professionals:
    Prepare100 school personnel with the skills to recognize and address mental health issues in middle school students, b)
    Crisis Response Preparedness: Enable school staff to handle mental health crises effectively, fostering safer school
    environments for all students, d) Support for Middle School Students: Directly impact the well-being of middle school
    students in Miami-Dade County by providing them with better access to mental health services within their schools. e)
    Resource Access for Schools: Supply trained personnel with toolkits and ongoing resources, enhancing the mental health
    infrastructure across the district. f) Pathway to Advanced Mental Health Careers: 15 graduate level credits earned can be
    applied to earning an advanced mental health degree.
   d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The primary target population for this initiative includes 100 school professionals from 58 middle schools, which serve
    18,500 students. These students represent a diverse demographic with varied cultural and socioeconomic backgrounds,
    many of whom face barriers to accessing mental health services. By training 100 school professionals, this initiative will
    indirectly serve these students by significantly increasing the mental health support available within their schools.
   e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
   be measured?

     The Uplifting School Mental Health Initiative will have a transformative impact on Miami-Dade’s middle school students.
    By training 100 school professionals, the program will enhance the availability and quality of mental health support within
    schools. Specifically, it will increase access, promote early intervention, improve academic performance, and foster
    resilience. The following methods will be employed to measure the success of the initiative: Pre- and Post-Training
    Assessments, Student Impact Surveys, School Performance Metrics, and Follow-Up Evaluations
   f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
   for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?

     Options if deliverables or performance measures are not met: 1) Withhold payment of the portion of contracted payment
    impacted by not being able to complete deliverables, 2) Reduction in Contract Scope or Budget. Grant may need to be
    realigned with the resources and ability to complete the deliverables not yet met, and 3) A structured Performance
    Improvement Plan (PIP) can be implemented, containing specifics of goals, objectives and action steps within specified
    timeline. Failure to continue to not meet deliverables may mean contract termination.
