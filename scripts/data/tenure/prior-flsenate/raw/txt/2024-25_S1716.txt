4. Project/Program Description
    The Permanency Roundtable (PRT) Program serves children and youth in Miami’s foster care system, 2-5 years. Many
   are free for adoption without a prospective adoptive parent. The program's primary goal is to ensure children exit foster
   care to a legally permanent family through adoption, permanent guardianship or reunification. If they do not achieve legal
   permanency, the program's secondary goal is to ensure they have a stable, supportive, parent-like adult in their life before
   exiting care.

     The Permanency Roundtable (PRT) Program serves children and youth in Miami’s foster care system, 2-5 years. Many
    are free for adoption without a prospective adoptive parent. The program's primary goal is to ensure children exit foster
    care to a legally permanent family through adoption, permanent guardianship or reunification. If they do not achieve legal
    permanency, the program's secondary goal is to ensure they have a stable, supportive, parent-like adult in their life
    before exiting care.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     The PRT Program builds and convenes multi-disciplinary teams for each child served. PRT Specialists facilitate a
    structured 2-hour Initial PRT to develop a Permanency Action Plan and facilitate PRTs every 30-45 days to review
    progress, address barriers and update the plan. Between PRTs, staff complete assigned tasks; work with youth and
    caregivers; search for family; dig through case files; etc.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Although the PRT Program does not provide direct services to citizens in a traditional sense, adults who aged out of
    foster care at 18 are more likely than their peers to drop out of high school, be unemployed, become incarcerated or
    homeless, and abuse drugs or alcohol. Studies have shown that every child who ages out of foster care costs society
    approximately $300,000 across their lifetime. With about 1000 youth aging out of Florida’s foster care system annually,
    this roughly translates to $300,000,000 in “social costs” every year in Florida alone. The single most effective way to
    mitigate these devastating outcomes is to ensure that every child has a forever family or, at the very least, a lifelong
    connection to a parent-like, supportive adult. Permanency roundtables connect children and youth to a family and lifelong,
    unconditional adult support.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Annually, the PRT Program typically serves 120-160 children ages 10 through 17 who have been languishing in Miami’s
    foster care system for 2 to 5 years without a prospective permanent family.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Improve life outcomes for children and youth who experienced foster care by connecting them to a permanent family and
    lifelong, adult support. FFCR tracks the number of youth served by the PRT Program who exit the program with the care
    and support of a legally permanent family or committed parent-like adult (legal or relational permanency) compared to
    those who exit the program without permanency.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Unless force majeure or Florida Foster Care Review meets its annualized minimum requirement for a specific
    deliverable, 1% reduction of the monthly payment shall be applied for each monthly deliverable not met.
