4.   Project/Program Description
     Incarceration of parents/caregivers impacts 300,000 Florida children. Research states that these children are acutely at-risk for anti-social
     behaviors, mental illness and cognitive delays stemming from exposure to Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACE). Since 2012, five
     infants of Children of Inmates (COI) clients have passed away due to accidental deaths. COI provides support services for children ages
     0-5 with an incarcerated parent, including home safety visits, Ages and Stages Questionnaire Assessment and caregiver community
     supports program. COI, Inc. is an innovative approach to interrupt negative patterns by collaborating with state and local agencies to
     rebuild families with wellness and age appropriate learning experiences that support children, caregivers and their incarcerated parent's
     improvements as a family unit. The program started in South Florida and has expanded to serve over 1,000 children 0-5 years of age.

11. Program Performance
     a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?
     When a parent is incarcerated, infants and young children suffer from insecure attachment and bonds to their parent and this insecurity undermines
     children's ability to cope and to grow in a healthy manner. These broken bonds have been linked to diminished cognitive abilities and inabilities to
     establish positive relationships. Over 25% of children with incarcerated parents are under 5 years of age. COI works with the caregivers to provide
     safety and developmental supports. The program provide critical training on brain development and parenting within the correctional facilities to guide
     the incarcerated parent on how to support their child in overcoming the challenges and achieve a positive developmental track.




 b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?
      Babies 'N Brains provides inmate education on infant brain development and child wellness. In the community, 300 home visits are conducted as well
      as child safety assessments and Ages and Stages Questionnaire (ASQ) for children 0-5 to measure appropriate maturation. Efforts will be made to
      help families establish a relationship with a primary care provider and enroll in healthcare, where possible. Over the past five years, five infants/young
      children of COI clients have passed away due to accidental deaths. As a result, COI staff will work to conduct home visits with 85 percent of
      households with children 5 years of age and under to conduct home safety checks, to provide safety equipment and assist with questions of caregivers
      about the child's development.



  c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?
     Inmate education on infant brain development and child wellness. 300 home visits, child safety assessments and Ages and Stages Questionnaire
     (ASQ) for children 0-5 to measure appropriate maturation. Direct services to assist with developmental stages and facilitated referrals to specialist to
     assist with developmental delays. Home safety assessments and home safety materials to 85 percent of households with children age 5 years old and
     under. Advocacy services will be provided to enhance public understanding of the plight of children of incarcerated parents.




d.   Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?
     The target population served by this project are children, ages 0-5 year of age with incarcerated parents and their caregivers. We serve currently and
     formerly incarcerated parents, assisting them to establish and maintain positive relationships with their children and caregivers. We anticipate serving
     500 children with these funds. This equates to approximately 200 families or incarcerated individuals. Research from Cambridge University documents
     that children with incarcerated parents are at twice the risk of mental illness and three times the risk of antisocial behaviors. Many are economically
     disadvantaged and often face homelessness. Further, many within the COI population struggle with developmental disabilities and/or physical
     disabilities. Children with incarcerated parents are exposed to higher levels of community and direct violence.



 e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?
     The expected benefits are to interrupt intergeneration incarceration by identifying and working with children with incarcerated parent early in their
     development. Activities will reduce Adverse Childhood Experiences by teaching brain development and child safety precautions. Family visits will
     create strong bond between incarcerated parent, children and caregivers to promote healthy families. Improving parenting skills is the focus during
     individual sessions, support groups and learning experiences for incarcerated parents, caregivers and children. Measures include pre and post
     surveys, observational data using proven research tools and techniques, such as the Ages and Stages Assessment and other evidence-based tools.
