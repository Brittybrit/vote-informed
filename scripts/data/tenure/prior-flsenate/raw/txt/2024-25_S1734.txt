1. Project Title          Inclusive Transition and Employment Management (ITEM) Program

2. Senate Sponsor         Bryan Avila

3. Date of Request        11/13/2023

4. Project/Program Description
    The ITEM Program offers a multifaceted approach for individuals with autism, pairing in-person mentorships, experiential
   learning, and innovative technology to address challenges such as lack of employment, social isolation, and struggles with
   independent living. The program connects people with autism by establishing a platform and community that fosters
   support, resources, and relationships. Key elements include a user-friendly app that serves as a central hub linking training,
   resources, and activities. The Hub offers ongoing engagement, inclusivity, and a responsive approach to the evolving
   needs of individuals with autism. The Hub includes a job portal that lists employment opportunities, skill training modules,
   mentorship, and an event calendar. The program tailors workshops and experiences, that support specific needs while
   fostering an inclusive environment, significantly contributing to the participants’ overall well-being and success.

    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Training young adults with autism to enhance their skills and confidence in the areas of independent living, career
    exploration, and social competency while preparing them for various situations to ensure safety including interactions with
    police officers. Progress toward these skills may vary based on the participant’s unique abilities and interests which will
    be addressed through technology and expanded collaborations with health and wellness, education, police, and business
    partners.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     An immersive community-based program to foster personal and professional growth; Training participants and police
    officers to promote positive and safe interactions; Service delivery at multiple sites for greater access to workshops, life
    and career coaching, certificate programs, and social events relevant to career exploration, social skills and independent
    living.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Certification program with workforce skills to pursue a diverse career path including opportunities to start new
    businesses; 1:1 sessions on social competency, career exploration, and independent living; General sessions on
    interviewing, self-advocacy skills, social competency, conflict resolution, and positive police interactions; Skill
    reinforcement through the utilization of technology tools.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     101-200 individuals; Target population includes: Jobless persons, economically disadvantaged persons, developmentally
    disabled, physically disabled, University/College students.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     BENEFIT: Improve physical health; METHODOLOGY: YMCA attendance records; annual satisfaction and health
    improvement surveys.
    BENEFIT: Enrich cultural experience - exposure to the arts community, theaters, museums, music venues, and libraries
    to enhance knowledge and appreciation. METHODOLOGY: Attendance records; annual satisfaction surveys.
    BENEFIT: Improve quality of education - increased interview proficiency and executive functioning skills through
    technology tools, mentors, and certificate programs. METHODOLOGY: Attendance; course completion; assessments.
    BENEFIT: Enhance economic self-sufficiency - online and in-person career development through skill training courses,
    worksite tours, entrepreneurial experiences. METHODOLOGY: Attendance; course completion; assessments.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?
