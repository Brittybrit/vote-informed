4. Project/Program Description
    At Glory House, we provide long-term restoration to survivors of human trafficking—one heart at a time. Our residential
   program provides survivors with the safety, structure, and therapeutic support they need to address the root of their
   trauma, rebuild their lives, and re-enter society with the necessary skills <and tools> to thrive. This home is the first long-
   term residential safe home for adult female survivors in South Florida, and it offers women a stable environment,
   individualized wraparound care, and a pathway for survivors to confidently transition into independent living. Through our
   Independent Living Program, our team offers continued wraparound support and specialized care coordination to
   survivors who are stable enough to their own and are pursuing lives of stability and freedom.

    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The requested funding will allow Glory House to sustain and expand the operations of our residential and independent
    living programs by supporting high-caliber staff who mentor, advocate for, and provide individualized care to survivors.
    Our program enables survivors to address the root of their trauma, rebuild their lives, and transition successfully into
    independent, self-sufficient living. By providing consistent, long-term support, we reduce recidivism into cycles of
    exploitation and dependence on government assistance, while breaking generational trauma for their children.
   b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Our Residential Program provides survivors with a minimum of 12 months in our safe home, giving them the time,
    structure, and support needed to heal from complex trauma and transition successfully into independent, thriving lives.
    Our Independent Living Program empowers survivors who are living on their own to achieve their goals and maintain
    self-sufficiency. Across both programs, we provide wraparound support, including specialized care management,
    partnership referrals, and in-house resources such as financial literacy training, trauma therapy, job readiness support,
    and strategic goal setting to help them build sustainable, empowered futures.
   c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Glory House provides direct care and wraparound support to adult female survivors through both our Independent and
    Residential Living Programs. We offer targeted services including in-house care coordination, partnership referrals,
    financial literacy training, trauma therapy, job readiness support, and strategic goal setting. Each survivor's progress is
    guided by measurable milestones and ongoing accountability, ensuring each woman has the tools and support needed
    to heal, achieve autonomy, and transition into becoming independent, self-sufficient adults.
   d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The target population is adult female survivors of sex trafficking. A minimum of 30 survivors will be served through our
    Independent Living Program, and 12 survivors will participate in our Residential Program for a 12-month period.
   e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
   be measured?

     At Glory House, we track a variety of metrics for each survivor to demonstrate the impact of our programs. This
    includes direct financial assistance (rent, groceries, personal care items, etc.), participation in wraparound support
    services, and achievement of strategic short- and long-term goals. We also monitor outcomes such as job readiness,
    educational progress, and personal development milestones. These measures demonstrate the complete turnaround
    that is possible for a human trafficking survivor when long-term support and restoration are the focus.
   f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
   for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?

    A corrective action plan will be formed to meet deliverables and performance measures.
