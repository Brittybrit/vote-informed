4. Project/Program Description
    The Breast Cancer and Heart Disease Health Literacy Advocate Program (BCHDHLA) is a proven, evidence-based
   initiative designed to reduce two of Florida’s leading causes of largely preventable death—breast cancer and heart
   disease. The program mobilizes cohorts of college students and low-income residents as Health Literacy Advocates,
   empowering them with vital knowledge in prevention, early detection, and healthy lifestyle practices. Participants extend
   this impact by sharing knowledge within their families and communities, multiplying outreach and measurable outcomes.
   By improving health literacy statewide, the program promotes earlier detection, adherence to preventive care, and
   reduced hospitalization rates—yielding long-term savings in healthcare expenditures and lives saved.

    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Requested funds will expand non-traditional, evidence-based health education to improve health literacy with a focus
    on breast cancer and heart disease, two of the most costly and leading causes of largely preventable deaths in Florida.
    These funds will empower college students and low-income individuals with measurable increased knowledge needed
    to live disease deterrent lifestyles which has been proven to save lives, reduce healthcare costs, and improve public
    health outcomes.
   b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     To meet the intended purpose of the requested funding, digital curriculum delivery will be provided to college students
    about breast cancer, heart disease, disease prevention, early detection, clinical trials, and the SDOH. Participants will
    share gained knowledge with their peers to broaden the impact. Additionally, low-income individuals will receive health
    education through community outreach engagement and health educational materials at their retail worksites and
    where they shop.
   c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     This appropriation project will directly provide: 1) virtual interactive education about breast cancer, heart disease,
    disease prevention, early detection, clinical trials, and SDOH to citizens attending post-secondary academic
    institutions. 2) low-income citizens with critical health education about breast cancer, heart disease, disease
    prevention, and early detection through their participation in health educational rallies and the receipt of health
    educational materials which include sites providing low-cost preventive screenings and care.
   d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The Breast Cancer and Heart Disease Health Literacy Advocate Program will educate 900 multi-disciplined college
    students including future healthcare professionals about breast cancer, heart disease, disease prevention, early
    detection, clinical trials, and SDOH; and 300 low-income individuals will receive health education about breast cancer,
    heart disease, disease prevention, early detection, and clinical trials. In total 1,200 individuals will be served through
    virtual health education and direct community engagement.
   e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
   be measured?

     The expected benefit is increased health literacy about breast cancer and heart disease among college students and
    low-income individuals. Success will be measured by 1) 900 college students completing virtual education with
    assessments documenting increased knowledge; and advocacy documented by 3,000 social media impressions and at
    least 9,000 engagements 2) 300 individuals experience increased knowledge as documented at health educational
    rallies and by surveys conducted with individuals after receiving health education packages.
   f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
   for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?

     In the event the Women’s Breast & Heart Initiative, Florida Affiliate, Inc. does not meet contracted deliverables or
    performance measures, the organization will submit a corrective action plan with defined benchmarks and timelines.
    Any unexpended funds related to unmet deliverables will be returned to the contracting agency in compliance with
    state accountability standards.
