4.   Project/Program Description
     Pace is recognized nationally as an effective program for keeping girls out of the juvenile justice system. Through a network of 21 Pace
     Centers across Florida, Pace Center for Girls works with more than 3,000 middle- and high-school aged girls annually who are at risk of
     involvement with the justice system. Using a gender-responsive, trauma informed and strengths based framework Pace reduces the
     likelihood of negative outcomes tied to this set of risk factors and instead fosters academic engagement, positive youth development, and
     healthy relationships that increase rates of graduation and prevent future involvement with the justice system, leading to long- term well-
     being and success. The Pace Reach model provides comprehensive wrap around social services, social-emotional, behavioral and
     mental health counseling and a year of after-care to at-risk adolescent girls and their families. The request is for funding for the Pace
     Reach program in Citrus County.

11. Program Performance
a.    What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?
      To prevent girls in Citrus County from school dropout, juvenile justice system involvement, and long term economic dependency. The goal of the Pace
      model is for all girls to lead productive, engaged and fulfilling lives. Towards that goal, Pace works to 1) Improve girls' academic outcomes; 2) Reduce
      risk behaviors that can lead to criminal involvement; 3) Improve interpersonal relationships; 4) increase self-efficacy and self-advocacy; 5) Improve
      skills in goal setting.




 b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?
      Services provided by Pace Reach include: Individual assessments, case management, individual trauma informed cognitive behavioral counseling and
      therapy, group counseling and therapy, Spirited Girls! Life skills curriculum, family case management, counseling and therapy, and aftercare services
      for a year following completion of the program. Girls in Pace Reach are enrolled in public school or another alternative education institution, and while
      Pace does not provide the academic school day for girls in Reach, Pace monitors attendance and grades to ensure school engagement.




  c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?
      Comprehensive wrap around social services are provided to 40 girls at risk of juvenile justice justice system involvement. The Reach program provides
      the comprehensive wrap around social services, but girls in Reach do not receive their academic school day from Pace. Case management for the
      girl's family is also provided to ensure a stable home environment.




d.    Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?
      40 females ages 11-17 in Citrus County who are at risk of juvenile justice system involvement and dropping out of school.




 e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?
      Adopting a prevention and early intervention approach. The overall goal of this program is to reach girls at risk of delinquency and provide counseling
      and therapeutic services in community settings that can leverage their support systems (For example, parents, teachers or guidance counselors). Short
      term outcomes for girls include demonstrated increase in self-efficacy and pro-social coping skills, and reduction in risk behaviors. Long term outcomes
      for girls include no involvement in the criminal justice system and decreased economic dependency. The girls' families benefit from Pace both indirectly
      and through family engagement, home visits and counseling. The public benefits through increased public safety. Pace Centers utilize a combination of
      output data (e.g., number of girls served, dosage of services), and outcome data (e.g. effectiveness or quality of services provided), to measure
      outcomes.


 f.   What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard
      penalties for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?
      Our current contract with DJJ outlines penalties for failing to meet deliverables. DJJ may reduce the amount of monthly payment if sufficient evidence
      shows that deliverables were not met.
