4. Project/Program Description:
    PACE is recognized nationally as an effective program for keeping girls out of the juvenile justice system. PACE
    serves girls ages 11-17 and has developed an effective system of care across 20 PACE Centers serving 20 counties in
    Florida to meet the needs of girls with histories of trauma, abuse, and violence in their past. The PACE model
    supports social, emotional and academic success in a holistic, strength based environment and serves as a gender-
    responsive alternative to the delinquency system for adolescent girls. The PACE model provides comprehensive
    wrap around social services, academic services with daily academic instruction, school-to-work readiness skills
    development and three years of after-care. PACE Centers operate year-round and are structured like a school in
    that girls attend daily, take core subjects and work towards a high school diploma, GED or to return to their home
    school on grade level. The request for FY 2018-19 is to open a PACE Center in Citrus County.

11. Program Performance:

    a. What is the specific purpose or goal that will be achieved by the funds requested?

           Funds will be used to open a PACE Center in Citrus County that operates 250 days per year and serves 160 (60
          in the PACE day program, 100 in Reach) girls at risk of juvenile justice system involvement in FY 2019. The goal
          of the PACE model is for all girls to lead productive, engaged and fulfilling lives. Towards that goal, PACE works
          to 1) Improve girls' academic outcomes; 2) Reduce risk behaviors that can lead to criminal involvement; 3)
          Improve interpersonal relationships; 4) increase self-efficacy and self-advocacy; 5) Improve skills in goal setting.

    b. What are the activities and services that will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

           All girls attending the PACE Center receive comprehensive wrap-around social services, and the full academic
          school day. Once a girl transitions from PACE she receives 3 years of follow-up services and after care. All girls
          in PACE Reach receive comprehensive wrap-around social services and the full academic school day is provided
          by others. Services provided include: individual assessments, case management, individual trauma informed
          cognitive behavioral counseling and therapy, group counseling and therapy, Spirited Girls! Life skills curriculum,
          and family case management.

d. What are the direct services to be provided to citizens by the appropriations project?

     Comprehensive wrap around social services and the full academic school day are provided for 60 girls at risk of
    delinquency through our PACE Day program. PACE Citrus will also house a Reach program expected to serve an
    additional 100 girls. The Reach program provides the comprehensive wrap around social services, but girls in
    Reach do not receive their academic school day from PACE. Case management for the girl's family is also
    provided to ensure a stable home environment.

    f.   What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome
         will be measured?
          Short term outcomes for girls include 1) Maintaining attendance and being enrolled in school; 2) Advancing
         grade levels; 3) Demonstrated increase in self-efficacy and pro-social coping skills; 3) Reduction in risk
         behaviors. Long term outcoes for girls include 1) High school graduation and enrollment in post-seconary
         education or the workforce; 2) Obtaining employment; 3) No involvement in the criminal justice system. PACE
         Centers utilize a combination of output data (e.g., number of girls served, dosage of services) and outcome data
         (data on the effectiveness or quality of services provided), to measure outcomes. Outcome data includes % of
         girls that increase their academic performance as measured through credits earned, report card, grade
         advancement, graduation or obtaining a GED.

    g. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard
       penalties for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?
        Our current contract with DJJ outlines penalities for failing to meet deliverables. DJJ may reduce the amount
       of monthly payment if sufficient evidence shows that deliverables were not met. Outcomes for FY 2016-17
       include the following: 90% of girls statewide improved academically while at PACE and 95% had no further
       involvement with the juvenile justice system one year after completing PACE.
