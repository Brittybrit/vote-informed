3.   Project/Program Description:
     PACE is recognized as one of the most effective program in the nation for keeping girls out of the
     juvenile justice system and for helping girls find success in school, at home and in the community.
     PACE serves girls ages 11-18 and has developed an effective system of care across 19 PACE Centers
     serving 19 counties in Florida to meet the needs of girls with histories of trauma, abuse and violence in
     their past. The PACE model supports social, emotional and academic success in a holistic, strength-
     based environment and serves as a gender-responsive alternative to the delinquency system for
     adolescent girls. The PACE model provides comprehensive wrap around social services, academic
     services with daily academic instruction, school-to-work readiness skills development and three years
     of after-care. PACE centers operate year-round and are structured like a school in that girls attend
     daily, take core subjects and work towards a high school diploma, GED or to return to their home
     school on grade level. Beyond these specific components, PACE creates an environment that
     celebrates girls and recognizes their strengths and abilities. In FY 16, 26% of girls came to PACE with
     a prior arrest and 74% were failing in school. After completing PACE, 91% had no further
     involvement with the juvenile justice system and 90% improved academically. The request for FY 17-
     18 is for $1.2M in new funding to open the 20th PACE Center in Hernando County..

           PACE model is for all girls to lead productive, engaged and fulfilling lives. Towards that goal,
           PACE works to: 1) Improve girls’ academic outcomes; 2) Reduce risk behaviors, such as
           substance abuse, that can lead to criminal involvement; 3) Improve interpersonal relationships; 4)
           increase self-efficacy and self-advocacy; 5) Improve skills in goal setting. Short term outcomes
           include 1) Maintaining attendance and being enrolled in school; 2) Advancing grade levels; 3)
           Demonstrated increase in self-efficacy and pro-social coping skills; 3) Reduction in risk
           behaviors. Long term outcomes include 1) High school graduation and enrollment in post-
           secondary education or the workforce; 2) Obtaining employment; 3) No involvement in the
           criminal justice system. The request for FY18 is to open the 20th PACE Center in Hernando
           County
      c.   Who will benefit from receipt of State funds?
           50 Middle and High School aged girls at risk for juvenile justice system involvement and dropping out of
           school, their families, and the general public through increased public safety.
      d.   What specific measures will be used to document performance data for the project, if it receives funds?
           PACE Centers utilizes a combination of output data (e.g., number of girls served, dosage of
           services) and outcome data (data on the effectiveness or quality of services provided). Outcome
           data includes % of girls that increase their academic performance as measured through credits
           earned, report card, grade advancement or graduation or obtaining a GED .
