2. Senate Sponsor        Bryan Avila

3. Date of Request       11/09/2023

4. Project/Program Description
    The Miami Lakes Septic-to-Sewer Phase 1 Project consists of the design, permitting, and construction services for
   property owners to abandon septic tank systems and connect to available sanitary sewer infrastructure. The proposed
   properties are located in south central Miami Lakes just east of the Palmetto Expressway. The Septic-to-Sewer Phase 1
   Project connections will take place east of NW 74th Avenue from NW 143rd Street to NW 145th Street in Miami Lakes. The
   Project will design, permit and construct plumbing connections from public sewer infrastructure to individual properties.
   Once a property is connected to the sanitary sewer system, the septic tank will be properly abandoned. The Project will
   enhance economical and environmental benefits, including reduced costs for property owners establishing sewer
   connections, reduced public health impacts, reduced impacts from septic systems, and increased property values. The
   Town is requesting $2.65 million to complete this Project.

    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The funding goal is to construct the Project for the purpose of property owners to abandon septic tank systems and
    connect to available sanitary sewer infrastructure for approximately 10 properties located in south central Miami Lakes.
    The existing septic tank system remains one of the the biggest barriers for property owners. Providing financial
    assistance for sanitary sewer connections will enhance sewer infrastructure and protect private property and health in
    Miami Lakes' neighborhoods.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Activities and services consist of the construction of the new plumbing connections from public sewer infrastructure for
    approximately 10 properties located in south central Miami Lakes just east of the Palmetto Expressway. Once a property
    is connected to the sanitary sewer system, the septic tank will be properly abandoned.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     A Plan of Action Report was prepared as a joint effort between the Miami-Dade Water and Sewer Department (WASD)
    and the Department of Regulatory and Economic Resources (RER) in 2020 informing policymakers of implementations to
    reduce the environmental impact and public health risks of septic tank systems and the benefits of septic to sewer
    conversion. Property owners will be provided the service of plumbing connections and septic tank abandonment,
    including the required permitting and notifications.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     This funding would support the private property connections of properties in an area with unconnected and 10 septic tank
    vulnerable properties just east of NW 74th Avenue from NW 143rd Street to NW 145th Street, located east of the
    Palmetto Expressway in south central Miami Lakes.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Expected benefits include social, economic, and environmental benefits, including reduced costs to property owners for
    establishing sewer connection, reduced impacts from septic systems, reduced nutrient effluent impacts on ground and
    surface waters, reduced public health impacts from failing septic systems that have the potential to cause sewer backups,
    reduced risk of exposure to bacteria and pathogens in floodwaters, and enhanced health and property values. The
    Project will be measured by the number of septic tank systems removed and the removal of the associated nutrients.
    Outcome will be measured by field inspections and documented events. The Project will create specific contractor,
    engineer, environmental, and consultant immediate job opportunities in construction management measured by
    contractual agreements.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     No payment will be made for a request for reimbursement which does not include an updated summary of the project,
    dated invoices, checks paid by the contracting agency, and proof of payments via copies of deposited checks.
