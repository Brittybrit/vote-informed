    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The funds requested for FCC's Fit4Life Program will provide free soccer-based programming for 1,000 at-promise youth
    (ages 6-18) in Miami-Dade, integrating movement, mental health, mentorship, and mastery (financial literacy). The project
    aims to enhance physical/ mental health, resilience, trauma healing, and civic engagement, as well as foster economic
    self-sufficiency by offering youth workforce development opportunities to work as coaches creating a positive ripple effect
    within the community. The initiative focuses to serve youth locally in Hialeah, Little Havana and the Homestead area.
   b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     The program will serve 1000 participants (ages 6-18) on. a full year academic program- with 10 Trauma and resilient -
    Informed Coaches providing 90-minute sessions twice weekly for 42 weeks. Activities include recreational soccer,
    mentorship, mental health, and financial literacy workshops, as well as referrals to free counseling, nutrition and dental
    services. Youth will gain career readiness through education and paid workforce development opportunities.
   c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     FCC USA will provide free after-school programming to 1000 local participants (ages 6-18) from Little Havana, Hialeah
    and Homestead- through FIT4LIFE Program using recreational soccer integrated with wrap-around services, as well
    financial and career readiness education. Engaging participants locally twice weekly for 10 months at our partner schools,
    parks and non- profits. FIT 4 LIFE will also lead a leadership cohort as part of our Education and Youth Workforce
    Development for our high school participants to develop career readiness.
   d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     This project serves 1000 children and youth (ages 6-18) in Homestead, Little Havana, and Hialeah, where over 80%
    qualify for free or reduced lunch, reflecting significant financial hardship. Many families lack the income for structured
    youth sports, creating barriers to participation. This program removes cost barriers and provides transferable skills in
    leadership, teamwork, and financial literacy—critical for college readiness and workforce success. By integrating
    mentorship, career exploration, and goal setting, we offer tools to break cycles of intergenerational scarcity and promote
    economic mobility.
   e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
   be measured?

     This project enhances physical and mental health, decision-making, school performance, and workforce readiness. 80%
    of participants will develop knowledge of health and financial literacy, confidence in making healthy choices, and
    improved emotional and cognitive functioning. School performance will strengthen, with 80% showing better attendance
    and teacher-reported progress. Among youth ages 14-18, 80% will complete the Youth Leadership Academy, earning
    certification in coaching and mentorship through Miami-Dade County Public Schools’ Honors Academic Internship
    Program, equipping them with career-ready skills. FCC USA tracks impact through SIMEON, using pre/post surveys,
    standardized assessments, certification completion rates, and quarterly follow-ups to ensure progress in health,
    education, and career pathways.
   f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
   for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?

     FCC ensures compliance with state appropriations by promptly addressing deficiencies through root cause analysis and
    targeted corrective measures, such as process adjustments, staff training, and resource reallocation. Progress is
    monitored via performance metrics and audits, with oversight from the Executive Director and Board of Directors. Regular
    updates are provided to stakeholders, reflecting FCC USA's commitment to accountability, continuous improvement, and
    impactful use of state funds.
