11. Program Performance
a.    What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?
      The program will provide preservation of historic buildings in urban Miami and preserve the naturally occurring affordable housing
      therein, thereby maintaining the aesthetic and density of the neighborhood (new codes do not allow for historic density) and providing
      improved living condition for residents who are most in need of affordable housing. In addition, the rehabilitation of a building is less
      expensive than new construction and is environmentally correct as there is little building construction waste headed to the landfill.




 b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?
      A multi-family building that is currently affordable will be purchased and rehabilitated to provide naturally occurring affordable housing
      and an improved living environment.




  c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?
      Residents will receive affordable housing rates based on AMI and a covenant of affordability. Miami-Dade County is experiencing an
      affordable housing crisis; public and large scale private developers cannot meet this need.




d.    Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?
      Urban residents in diverse Miami neighborhoods. With initial $1M seed funding provided by Miami-Dade County and state funding,
      Dade Heritage Trust will revolve the monies and continue purchasing and rehabilitating suitable multi-family unit buildings, servicing
      12-15 families annually.




 e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?
      This project will enable Miami's diverse urban residents to avoid displacement due to neighborhood gentrification. Urban
      neighborhoods such as Little Havana, Little Haiti, Little Santo Domingo (in Allapattah), Little San Juan (in North Wynwood), and West
      Coconut Grove are under attack as significant buildings and symbols of the neighborhoods' identity are disappearing due to
      insensitive redevelopment, resulting in resident displacement. This project will assist in mitigating those negative impacts. Outcome
      will be measured by the number of residents who are serviced.



 f.   What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard
      penalties for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?
      Return of partial funding.
