4. Project/Program Description:
   The funds will retrofit and bring into ADA compliance a building donated to the Foundation to expand its current
   use by researchers, local, state and worldwide, to study the ecological, geological, archeological and historical
   features of the Deering Estate property, a 445 acre state-owned property in South Dade.
   The Deering property is the only part of the Everglades Restoration Project located in an urban setting which is
   easily accessed by the public. The Miami Rock Ridge located on the property forms a barrier between Biscayne Bay
   and the interior of the of the south Florida peninsula and significant because it helps retain water and directs flow
   from Lake Okeechobee to the Everglades. The property is also home to more than 80 endangered or threatened
   species and has 26 known archeological sites where fossilized remains from nearly 10,000 years ago have been
   discovered.

11. Program Performance:

    a. What is the specific purpose or goal that will be achieved by the funds requested?
        The funds will assist in retrofitting a building currently used by worldwide researchers to study the ecologically,
        geologically significant features (including archeological and historical discoveries) of the Deering Estate
        property, a 445 acre state-owned property in South Dade. When completed, the expanded use of the building
        will include allowing for additional (20) researchers at one time to conduct important reserach and more space
        for additional educational programs. The property is an ecological scale model of the Everglades restoration
        efforts. Further, a recent archeological survey of this Florida Heritiage sight which includes a coastal public
        park, found 11 new Paleo Indian, Tequesta and early Pioneer sites in need of investigation.
    b. What are the activities and services that will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?
        The facility will primarily be used to house researchers while they conduct important ecological, geological,
        archeological and historical research of worldwide significance. The research conducted at the building can
        significantly enhance ongoing Everglades protection activities. Further, the upgraded building can also expand
        the programs hosted to educate the community and policymakers about the significant role the environment
        of the Deering property plays in the future of our state;. Deering is designated as an Outstanding Florida
        Waters Class III by the Florida Department of Environmental Protection. The expansion will allow for visiting
        academics, scientists, researchers, students, and educators to engage in interdisciplinary, ground-breaking
        research.

         Current visitors to the property include the pubic, students (K-12 and post graduate) as well as reseachers who
         are staying in the current building in order to be close to their study sites. The upgraded building will increase
         the number of academicians and researchers drawn to the property. Further, the general public as well as
         student will have access to the expanded educational programs provided on the property.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome
       will be measured?
         As research continues on the discoveries already made at the property, the awareness of this state-owned
         heritage site will grow worldwide through research-related publications. Additionally, educators, K-12 students
         and the general public will learn about the unique environmental aspects of the Deering Estate including 119
         acres of globally endangered pine rockland (amongst 8 other South Florida natural habitats), the submerged
         resources of Biscayne Bay Aquatic Preserve and the 10+ acre Biscayne Bay Coastal Wetlands/Cutler Slough
         Rehydration Project which is part of the statewide CERP project and the only Everglades Restoration site
         accessible to the general public. Outcomes will be measured by the increased body of research conducted that
         will benefit the property, the Everglades and the state as a whole.
