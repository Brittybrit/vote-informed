2. Senate Sponsor         Bryan Avila

4. Project/Program Description
    The funds will support a scientific study to document regional reference conditions and identify phosphorus-driven
   ecological thresholds needed to guide restoration in the Everglades. By analyzing degraded periphyton
   communities—now detected across large areas of wetland—this project will generate the data necessary to understand
   ecosystem impacts, prevent further decline, and inform targeted restoration strategies that protect overall habitat health,
   including fisheries.

    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Degraded periphyton has been detected across dozens of square kilometers of wetland through monitoring by the
    National Park Service's SFCN (NCRA 2021). Impaired periphyton communities associated with persistent above-
    ambient P exposure in the Everglades are known to lead to cascading consequences harming ecosystem health,
    including fisheries (Gaiser et al. 2006). This study documents regional reference conditions and P-driven ecological
    thresholds to effectively plan restoration in the area.
   b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Utilize a flow-through flume design, modeled after the flume study designed by Florida International University (FIU)
    for establishing protective P criterion for Everglades National Park (Childers et al. 2001). A flow-through flume design
    offers several key advantages over a mesocosm design. First, flow-through flume designs enable enrichment to occur
    in a mixing area (in our case, 2-m long).
   c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     To provide a phosphorous target that protects the WERP wetlands of the BICY from an imbalance in the natural
    populations of aquatic flora or fauna. We anticipate results from experimental P additions in the BICY to be similar to
    those in other regions of the Everglades (Gaiser et al. 2005, 2006). This data will aid policymakers and scientists to
    protect an Outstanding Florida Water.
   d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

    Users of Florida's public lands, South Florida's fisheries, and ecotourism. More than 1m visit/year.
   e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
   be measured?

     By determining the resilience of the ecosystem to the introduction of phosphorous from new sources previously
    outside of the Big Cypress Basin, appropriate targets for policymakers and system operators can be set, preserving the
    ecosystem health of, and therefore the health-supporting outcomes of interacting with an ecologically intact public land.
    We will measure this by establishing a scientifically supported numerical P-loading threshold at which ecosystem
    health significantly declines.
   f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
   for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?

    Complete repayment.
