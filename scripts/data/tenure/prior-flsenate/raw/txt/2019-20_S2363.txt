4. Project/Program Description:
   Health Information Project, known as HIP, is a health education non-profit that trains high school students to teach
   an innovative health curriculum to their high school peers. HIP, which has a proven, 10-year track record, partners
   annually with all Miami-Dade County public high schools--a total of 55 high schools. The 1,700 high school peer
   educators teach the HIP comprehensive health curriculum to every ninth grader at each of these high schools—
   34,000 ninth graders—annually. HIP knows it is not enough to just teach kids about world history, geometry,
   literature and biology. We need to teach them how to cope with stress, help a friend with depression, support a
   bullied classmate. The HIP approach is simple. We know teens are often resistant to health education when the
   educators are teachers, parents or physicians. That is why we empower student health ambassadors to teach health
   education to their peers-at their own schools.

11. Program Performance:

    a. What is the specific purpose or goal that will be achieved by the funds requested?
        HIP’s purpose is to empower young people to tackle the health crises of their generation—by allowing them to
        deliver health information directly to their peers. HIP’s goal is to: ensure that students are physically and
        emotionally well, that they are knowledgeable in a wide range of health topics, that they know they are not
        alone in the issues they are dealing with, that they feel comfortable discussing these critical health issues, that
        they know where to go for help and that they address these issues preventatively.
    b. What are the activities and services that will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?
        HIP directly serves over 34,000 students per year, EVERY 9th grader, in Miami-Dade County—with 1,700 high
        school peer health educators—in 55 public high schools. In order to implement HIP, we must continue to
        execute a robust curriculum development and training program for the HIP Peer Health Educators. Expenses for
        this program include: curriculum binders, training materials, a school health campaign of posters/HIP
        Tips/banners, and the online health resource portal. Additionally, HIP accesses community experts to review
        and update the HIP curriculum and resources on an annual basis to ensure that the program is current and
        relevant. For 2019, HIP will also be investing in impact evaluation, board development and strategic growth to
        not only ensure sustainability in Miami-Dade County but also to expand to new cities and school districts within
        Florida.

    c. What are the direct services to be provided to citizens by the appropriations project?
          HIP is taking the lead in the health education arena by providing an innovative, student-led model in Miami, the
          nation's fourth largest school district. The traditional health education model, which the State of Florida
          removed as a graduation requirement ten years ago (leaving kids with no health education), was based on
          scare tactics, cheesy videos, outdated textbooks and adults lecturing in these classes.HIP has a fresh approach
          to the traditional health education model. HIP trains 1,700 high school students (11th and 12th graders) to
          teach a health curriculum consisting of interactive discussions, videos and activities—in all 9th grade
          classrooms. HIP addresses relevant, critical health topics such as obesity, depression, bullying, abuse, eating
          disorders, stress, anxiety and suicide.In addition to peer-led discussion sessions, HIP has a school-specific health
          resource site (www.behip.org) and school health campaign.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?
          The HIP program is implemented in 55 high schools all across Miami-Dade County. Students, ages 14-18, are
          served by HIP. There are two groups that directly benefit from this program--every 9th grader (34,000 students)
          and the peer educators (1,700 students). HIP benefits all high school students in Miami-Dade County,
          regardless of geography, socio-economic class or school success. HIP is in urban and suburban schools, high-
          performing and low-performing schools, traditional large public schools as well as smaller magnet schools.
          After ten years in Miami’s high schools, HIP is sustainable and embedded into the structure and culture of each
          school.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome
       will be measured?
          HIP partners with the University of Pennsylvania’s ImpactED Institute to measure and evaluate HIP’s impact
          through rigorous research and data collection.HIP surveys all 9th graders, all 11th & 12th grade peer educators,
          all faculty sponsors, all 9th grade teachers, all school counselors and all principals in order to ensure continuous
          quality improvement and program fidelity. HIP directors and faculty sponsors at school sites supervise the
          evaluation process.By implementing the HIP program, the 1,700+ peer health educators gain important
          curriculum knowledge and leadership, public speaking and classroom management skills since they teach their
          own classes.HIP continues to demonstrate through research that those delivering and receiving the program
          are more aware of these health topics, more focused on prevention, making healthier decisions and know
          where to go to get help when they need it.
    f.    What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard
          penalties for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?
          The Department may withhold all or a portion of the funds if the contract/deliverables is deemed
          unsatisfactory.
