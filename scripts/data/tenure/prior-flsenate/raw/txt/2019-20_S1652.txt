4. Project/Program Description:
   Pace is recognized nationally as an effective program for keeping girls out of the juvenile justice system. Through a
   network of 21 Pace Centers across Florida, Pace Center for Girls works with more than 3,000 middle- and high-
   school aged girls annually who are at risk of involvement with the justice system. Using a gender-responsive
   framework for academic and social services, Pace reduces the likelihood of negative outcomes tied to this set of risk
   factors and instead fosters academic engagement, positive youth development, and healthy relationships that
   increase rates of graduation and prevent future involvement with the justice system. The Pace model provides
   comprehensive wrap around social services, academic services with daily academic instruction, school-to-work
   readiness skills development and a minimum of one year of after-care. The request for FY 2019-20 is to continue
   operations at the Pace Center in Hernando County.

11. Program Performance:

    a. What is the specific purpose or goal that will be achieved by the funds requested?
        Funds will be used for a Pace Center in Hernando County that operates 250 days per year and serves
        approximately 160 (60 in the Pace Center, 100 through Pace Reach Counseling Services) girls at risk of juvenile
        justice system involvement in FY 2019-20. The goal of the Pace model is for all girls to lead productive, engaged
        and fulfilling lives. Towards that goal, Pace works to 1) improve girls' academic outcomes; 2) reduce risk
        behaviors that can lead to criminal involvement; 3) improve interpersonal relationships; 4) increase self-efficacy
        and self-advocacy; 5) improve skills in goal setting.
    b. What are the activities and services that will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?
        All girls attending the Pace Center receive comprehensive wrap-around social services, and the full academic
        school day. Once a girl transitions from Pace she receives a year of follow-up services and after care. All girls in
        Pace Reach receive comprehensive wrap-around social services, and the full academic school day is provided by
        others. Services provided in both the Center and Reach programs include: individual assessments, case
        management, individual trauma informed cognitive behavioral counseling and therapy, group counseling and
        therapy, Spirited Girls! Life skills curriculum, and family case management.

    c. What are the direct services to be provided to citizens by the appropriations project?
         Comprehensive wrap around social services and the full academic school day are provided for 60 girls at risk of
         delinquency through our Pace Center. Pace Hernando also has a Reach program serving an additional 100 girls
         annually. The Reach program provides the comprehensive wrap around social services, but girls in Reach do
         not receive their academic school day from Pace. Case management for the girl's family is also provided to
         ensure a stable home environment.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?
         160 11-17 year old girls in Hernando County at risk for juvenile justice system involvement and dropping out of
         school. The girls' families benefit from Pace both indirectly and through family engagement, home visits and
         counseling. The general public also benefits through increased public safety.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome
       will be measured?
         Short term outcomes for girls include 1) no involvement in the juvenile justice system; 2) maintaining
         attendance and being enrolled in school; 3) advancing grade levels; 4) demonstrated increase in self-efficacy
         and pro-social coping skills; 5) reduction in risk behaviors. Long term outcomes for girls include 1) no
         involvement in the juvenile justice system; 2) high school graduation and enrollment in post-secondary
         education or the workforce; 3) obtaining employment; 4) decreased economic dependency. Pace Centers
         utilize a combination of output data (e.g., number of girls served, dosage of services) and outcome data (data
         on the effectiveness or quality of services provided), to measure outcomes. Outcome data includes the percent
         of girls that increase their academic performance as measured through credits earned, report card, grade
         advancement, graduation or obtaining a GED.
    f.   What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard
         penalties for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?
         Our current contract with DJJ outlines penalities for failing to meet deliverables. DJJ may reduce the amount of
         monthly payment if sufficient evidence shows that deliverables were not met. Outcomes for FY 2017-18
         include the following: 90% of girls statewide improved academically while at Pace and 95% had no further
         involvement with the juvenile justice system one year after completing Pace.
