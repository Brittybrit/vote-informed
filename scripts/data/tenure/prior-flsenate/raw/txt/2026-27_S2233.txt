2. Senate Sponsor        Bryan Avila

3. Date of Request       12/16/2025

4. Project/Program Description
    The City of Hialeah looks to replace its current water meters to an automated water meter reading system. Automated
   Meter Reading (AMR) would provide increased billing accuracy, reduced costs, and improved efficiency for both
   customers and the City of Hialeah Water & Sewer Department. It eliminates manual errors, estimated bills, and the need
   for meter readers to visit properties, which also enhances customer privacy and employee safety. For customers, AMR
   can provide detailed consumption data to help manage consumption use, while the Water & Sewer Department can
   better detect usage and leaks remotely. Moreover, AMR eliminates billing errors from manual readings and removes the
   need for estimated bills based on past usage.

13. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     With an AMR system you would be looking at more accurate bills, reduced costs, convenience and privacy, faster leak
    detection, reduced labor costs, improved efficiency, increased accuracy and overall better customer service to the
    consumer.

     The City of Hialeah Water & Sewer Department would oversee the complete installation and then operation of the
    automated water meters system with project management and administration. This will include communication
    hardware and network. Software and data management systems. Labor and contractor services. Testing, calibration,
    and quality assurance. Moreover, training and education, maintenance and ongoing support for the system.
   c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     To enhance the reliability of the city's water services and modernize the infrastructure, the City of Hialeah Water &
    Sewer Department would upgrade or replace 10,000 of the existing water meters that connect our customers' homes
    and businesses to the water system. Once complete, the meters will enable the city to implement an AMR system,
    which will provide more reliable and accurate service to measure customers' water usage and help our Water
    Department to better assess, meet the community's needs and modernize the infrastructure.
   d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The City of Hialeah is the sixth largest city in the State of Florida, second largest in Miami-Dade County. We expect to
    service approximately 30,000 residents.
   e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
   be measured?

     An AMR would provide increased billing accuracy, reduced costs, and improved efficiency for both customers and the
    City of Hialeah Water & Sewer Department. It eliminates manual errors, estimated bills, and the need for meter readers
    to visit properties, which also enhances customer privacy and employee safety. For customers, AMR can provide
    detailed consumption data to help manage consumption use, while the Water & Sewer Department can better detect
    the usage by its customers. Moreover, this will help in reducing the municipal bureaucracy and overhead.
   f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
   for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?

     Performance-based payment withholding, reduction of future work allocation, intensified performance monitoring fees,
    cost recovery or agency impacts, liquidated damages enhancements, performance bonds claims, corrective action
    requirements, removal or replacement of key personnel and termination-related penalties.
