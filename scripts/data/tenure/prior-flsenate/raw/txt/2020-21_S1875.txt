4.   Project/Program Description
     Pace is recognized nationally as an effective program for keeping girls out of the juvenile justice system. Through a network of 21 Pace
     Centers across Florida, Pace Centers for Girls works with more than 3,200 middle- and high-school aged girls annually who are at risk of
     involvement with the justice system. Using a gender-responsive framework for academic and social services, Pace reduces the likelihood
     of negative outcomes tied to this set of risk factors and instead fosters academic engagement, positive youth development, and healthy
     relationships that increase rates of graduation and prevent future involvement with the justice system. The Pace model provides
     comprehensive wrap around social services, daily academic instruction, school-to-work readiness skill development and a year of after-
     care. The request for FY 2020-21 is for funding to purchase and renovate or buy land and build a 15,000 sq. ft. facility for the Pace Reach
     Program.

11. Program Performance
a.    What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?
      The new building will be used for the Pace Reach Program that operate 250 days per year and serve approximately 250 girls at risk of juvenile justice
      system involvement in FY19-20. The new facility will include a safe location for classrooms, counseling, a Reach office and other school safety
      features such as a single point of entry. The goal of the Pace model is for all girls to lead productive, engaged and fulfilling lives. Towards that goal,
      Pace works to 1) Improve girls' academic outcomes; 2) Reduce risk behaviors that can lead to criminal involvement; 3) Improve interpersonal
      relationships; 4) increase self-efficacy and self-advocacy; and 5) Improve skills in goal setting.




 b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?
      The building will be utilized to serve approximately 250 girls annually who are at risk of juvenile justice system involvement. Girls receive wrap around
      social services such as therapy, counseling and life skills. Girls in the day program also receive their full academic school day. Services are provided in
      a trauma informed, gender responsive and strength based environment.




  c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?
      The building will house a Pace Center and central operations for Pace Reach, providing comprehensive wrap around social services and the full
      academic school day to 100 girls at risk of delinquency through our Pace Center and comprehensive wrap around social services to an additional 150
      girls annually through Pace Reach. Case management for the girl's family is also provided to ensure a stable home environment.




d.    Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?
      Middle and high school age girls at risk for juvenile justice system involvement and dropping out of school. The girls' families benefit from Pace both
      indirectly and through family engagement, home visits and counseling. The general public also benefits through increased public safety.




 e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?
      Funding will provide a safe space for Pace girls to receive comprehensive wrap around social services and their full academic school day. Short term
      outcomes for girls include 1) maintaining attendance and being enrolled in school; 2) advancing grade levels; 3) demonstrated increase in self-efficacy
      and pro-social coping skills; and 3) reduction in risk behaviors.. Long term outcomes for girls include 1) high school graduation and enrollment in post-
      secondary education or the workforce; 2) obtaining employment; 3) no involvement in the criminal justice system; and 4) decreased economic
      dependency. Pace Centers utilize a combination of output data (e.g., number of girls served, dosage of services) and outcome data (data on the
      effectiveness or quality of services provided), to measure outcomes. Outcome data includes the percent of girls that increase their academic
      performance as measured through credits earned, report card, grade advancement, graduation or obtaining a GED.


 f.   What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard
      penalties for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?
      All Pace contracts with DJJ outline penalties for failing to meet deliverables. DJJ may reduce payments to Pace if sufficient evidence shows that
      deliverables were not met.
