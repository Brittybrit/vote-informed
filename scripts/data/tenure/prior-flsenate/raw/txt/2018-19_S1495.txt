1. Title of Project: Deering Estate - Field Station Research Center
2. Senate Sponsor: Jose Rodriguez
3. Date of Submission: 11/21/2017
4. Project/Program Description:
    Funds are requested to bring a 10,700 sf facility, originally built in 1982 and located within an 8.75 acre gift of
    property ($4.9M value) donated in 2015, up to current accessibility and fire codes with limited reconfiguration to
    the interior, so it can be brought on-line in service to our community as a field study research center; housing an
    archival library, field staff office, and temporary living quarters for up to 14 researchers at any one given time to
    conduct their research at the Deering Estate. Through a series of public meetings, the property has been
    successfully rezoned for this purpose. The project has full support of the Miami-Dade County Commission, R-1093-
    17, a partnership agreement in the form of an MOU with FIU, and letters of support from: U.S. Senator Bill Nelson,
    Office of the Mayors of Palmetto Bay, Cutler Bay and Pinecrest, Beacon Council of MDC, GMCVB, and more.
    Architectural drawings are complete, and the project is shovel-ready.

11. Program Performance:

    a. What is the specific purpose or goal that will be achieved by the funds requested?

           The specific goal is to bring this donated facility up to public accessibility an fire codes with limited
          reconfiguration to the interior so it can be brought online as a functioning research center promoting and
          advancing the conservation of Florida's natural resources through expanded programming and new audiences
          at the Deering Estate (DE) - benefitting the general public, fulfilling critical field study needs of schools and
          universities, and driving economic development through public access, programming participation, advanced
          studies and eco-tourism. The research center will provide for the expansion of the DE’s environmental
          education field study programs in STEM related curriculums and targeted professional research, while also
          providing enrichment opportunities for DE’s existing K-12 experiential learning programs. Additionally, the
          project will support the advancement and achievement of Goal 2, Objective A.5 and C.7 of the adopted DE’s
          General Management Plan (GMP) 2013-2023.

    b. What are the activities and services that will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

           Completion of this project will enable the Deering Estate to: 1) pursue collaborations with universities,
          researchers, and conservation organizations to improve research and data collection that engenders public
          support for preserving and protecting the Estate's natural and cultural resources; 2) support regional and
          statewide partnerships with universities and investments in industry readiness for STEM related careers; 3)
          continue to develop, enhance and promote use of the site as a center for research to better understand site
          resources and improve site management practices through adaptive management techniques; 4) drive new

    d. What are the direct services to be provided to citizens by the appropriations project?

           Expanded engagement opportunities and access to the Deering Estate, a state-owned heritage site and 444-
         acre coastal public park containing one of the most diverse collections of native habitats (8) and archaeological
         history combined in a single site, including the Deering Flow Way/Cutler Slough Rehydration Project, funded by
         Florida RESTORE - a Comprehensive Everglades Restoration Plan to ensure quantity, quality, distribution and
         timing of water for natural systems and a growing population.

    e. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

          The Deering Estate presently welcomes and serves over 80,000 visitors and program participants each year.
         This project will greatly expand the Estate's local, regional and international audiences through outreach to and
         engagement of environmental, cultural, and ecological enthusiasts, universities and academia, professional
         researchers, eco and archaeological tourists and the general public. Additionally, publications of research being
         performed on site will penetrate broad media markets and audiences significantly expanding the public's
         general awareness of this unique site being preserved and stewarded for the public's benefit by the Florida
         Department of State, Miami-Dade County and the Deering Estate Foundation.

    f.   What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome
         will be measured?
          Outcome fully leverages this $4.9M gift of property by expanding the DE natural areas and activating the
         facility in support of new programming, public engagement and revenue generation advancing economic
         development, eco-tourism, environmental and archaeological field study and research. Local businesses benefit
         from the economic impact of related residencies, visiting academia, faculty, eco-tourists and conservationists.
         Universities benefit by leveraging DE for advanced studies and STEM related career development. The broader
         public and over 18,000 K-12 students and educators in existing DE learning programs benefit from accessing
         these high-level research students and professionals. Related research publications will greatly expand
         awareness of this state-owned heritage site, driving new audiences to DE. Outcomes will be measured by the
         number of statewide, regional, national, int’l partners/groups/individuals served; and the number of research
         publications and data sets generated.
