4.   Project/Program Description
     The purpose of the Learning for Life funding is to enhance and improve student performance of low-performing, at-risk students, by
     providing additional learning opportunities through our local Learning for Life programs. The program is an age-appropriate and grade
     level specific program for students in grades K-12. Character education lessons focus on the following traits: Positive Attitude,
     Citizenship, Cooperation, Responsibility, Respect, Caring, Self-Discipline, Perseverance, Trustworthiness and Integrity. The state funding
     Will be delivered Miami-Dade, Lee, and Escambia School Districts. Each council works in partnership with each school district to deliver
     character education to the student population. The program’s objectives are to increase the knowledge of character and leadership traits
     of the target population and provide character education to over 63,000 students statewide.

11. Program Performance
     a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?
     The purpose of the Learning for Life funding is to enhance and improve student performance of low performing, at-risk students by providing additional
     learning opportunities through our local Learning
     for Life programs. The program objectives are to increase the knowledge of character and
     leadership traits of the target population and provide character education to over 63,000 students.




 b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?
      The Learning for Life character education curriculum is delivered to students through enhanced instruction, mentoring activities, and leadership
      training, and added value activities that offer additional character building opportunities.




  c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?
     In each school district, the Learning for Life (LFL) program collects data on students' understanding of eight core character traits, using a pre/post
     survey method. If a school scores exceptionally low on the pre-test, LFL develops an action plan to increase students' knowledge. This action plan
     includes additional lessons, as well as one on one interaction between LFL staff and each school's students. At the end of the school year,
     students are given a post-test. The tests are scored and recorded as a whole. Each of the 63,000 participating students receive at least 8
     character education lessons based on each school district’s determined character traits. The LFL program works closely with school districts to
     provide a program that meets the needs of their schools and students. Overall, based on the results of prior pre/post surveys, the majority of
     students improve their knowledge of character traits from 5-10% per district.

d.   Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?
     Low income, at-risk youth in K-12 grades.

 f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard
    penalties for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?
     Under the current LFL program, DOE may assess performance penalties of $2 per student if the program does not deliver the specific number of
     lessons to the number of students who are funded to serve.
