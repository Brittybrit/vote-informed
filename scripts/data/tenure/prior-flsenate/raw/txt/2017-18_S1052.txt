3. — Project/Program Description:
Annually educating more than 125,000 visitors and thousands of students, the Holocaust Memorial
Miami Beach concentrates on lessons centered on civil rights, basic freedoms and citizenship.
Opportunities are created for meaningful engagement around many key issues including intolerance,
hatred, racism, promotion of human dignity and citizenship responsibilities. The Memorial interprets
the stories of survivors, the lessons learned and in inspiring citizens and leaders to prevent genocide
in a constantly changing world. Visitors are of every age, faith and background — local residents and
out-of-town visitors, students and educators, individuals and groups.
Strengthened and effective educational strategies, developed in alignment with the State of Florida
mandate and goals for Holocaust education enables the Holocaust Memorial to reach out to
populations not currently engaged in teaching about the Holocaust, a watershed event in the history of
humanity. Holocaust education has recently been identified by a study commissioned by the
Govemors Task Force on Holocaust Education as a leading component of teaching citizenship in our
schools with long lasting results into adulthood for Florida residents.
Funding is required to strengthen education and outreach and by introducing programming for
students that will further fulfill the Holocaust Memorial's mission.
The following key, innovative educational projects will be implemented:
1. “Night of Broken Glass”-Kristallnacht Community-Wide Commemoration
2. Yom Hashoah- Holocaust Memorial Day Community-Wide Commemoration
3. Visitor and Student Education Initiative of Experiences at the Holocaust Memorial including
field trips.
4. Holocaust Education Week
5. Holocaust Video Lending Library
6. Online Holocaust Documentary Streaming Webpage
7. Docent Orientation and Professional Development
8. Teacher Education Institute
9. Teen-Survivor Program

8. Teacher Education Institute- State funding of $30,000 would be used to ensure that
additional teachers are able to participate the Teacher Education Institute professional
development seminars that brings Holocaust Scholars and Holocaust education experts

9. Teen-Survivor Program- State funding of $14,500 would be used to continue a highly
effective program which pairs teens and their parents with holocaust survivors in an
ongoing living history project. Additional teenagers above the 25 participation goal in
Miami-Dade County would be recruited, choosing those who show leadership potential
and who desire to develop a meaningful relationship with elderly Holocaust survivors. lt
would be used to develop and execute three months of programming on lessons of the
Holocaust, to teach and develop skills in relationship-building with the elderly, and to
provide transportation for participating survivors who are no longer able to drive.

In addition, a series of visits by Holocaust Survivors to area schools would be organized
in order to enhance and capitalize on teacher visits to the Holocaust Memorial and in
order to reach as many students as possible.
