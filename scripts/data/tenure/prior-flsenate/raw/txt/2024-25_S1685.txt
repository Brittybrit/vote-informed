4. Project/Program Description
    Habitat for Humanity of Greater Miami is seeking assistance for financing the infrastructure costs for the creation of a new,
   150 townhome community which will focus on creating home-ownership opportunities to low income families (earning 80%
   or below of AMI). Miami Dade County is facing a catastrophic housing affordability issue, resulting in scores of people
   leaving the community due to housing unaffordability.

   The Habitat Acres community will provide 150 families witht he chance to purchase home at below market value with a
   Habitat provided 0% interest mortgage. To date,. Habitat Miami has helped more than 1300 families achieve this dream.

   Funds would go towards the creation of paving and drainage for the project, as well as towards roads and electrical
   infratsructure.

14. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     Addressing the lack of affordable homeownership within Miami Dade County. Funding will be dedicated to support
    horizontal development activities for the construction of 150 affordable homeownership units in south Miami-Dade. These
    activities include infrastructure development, expenses that are necessary for the successful completion of the
    development.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Habitat for Humanity of Greater Miami will act as the sole developer for all affordable housing developments that receive
    funding, managing all aspects of construction which includes, selecting contractors, subcontractors and overseeing their
    work. Lastly, our organization will ensure each unit is occupied by income-qualified homeowners.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Habitat builds and sells homes at no profit and no interest to households who do not qualify for a traditional mortgage.
    The program prepares homeowner partners to be successful homeowners through an education curriculum of 12 classes,
    from budgeting to home maintenance. The program is predicated on the principle of a hand-up and not a handout.
    Homeowners purchase their home from Habitat and are provided a ‘conventional’ 30-year 0% interest mortgage, never
    paying over 30% of their total household income for their housing expense.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The expected population will be low-income African American and Hispanic families. It is expected that more than 1000
    people will be directly impacted by the project.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

    The project will be measured by families served. 150 sold homes to qualified low income families will be the yardstick by
    which results will be measured.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     Penalties for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures in a contract must comply with the rules and
    regulations of the agency and the State of Florida. In the event of a nonperformance the agency may implement a variety
    of penalties to address the issue. Ultimately, the agency should have the ability to recover funds that were paid to the
    contractor for work that was not completed or not completed to the agency's satisfaction.
