4. Project/Program Description
    Pace is recognized nationally as an effective program for keeping girls out of the juvenile justice system. Through a
   network of 21 Pace Centers across Florida, Pace Centers for Girls works with more than 3,000 middle- and high- school
   aged girls annually who are at risk of involvement with the justice system. Using a gender-responsive framework for
   academic and social services, Pace reduces the likelihood of negative outcomes tied to this set of risk factors and instead
   fosters academic engagement, positive youth development, and healthy relationships that increase rates of graduation and
   prevent future involvement with the justice system. The Pace model provides comprehensive wrap around social services,
   daily academic instruction, school-to-work readiness skill development and a year of after-care. The request for FY 2022-23
   is for funding to build a 15,000 sq. ft. facility for the Pace Center in Citrus County and Pace Reach Counseling Services
   Program.

12. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The new building will be used for the Pace Center and Reach Program in Citrus County that operates 250 days per year
    and serves girls at risk of juvenile justice system involvement in FY 22-23. Currently Pace Citrus and the Reach Program
    are operated out of a building rented from the Citrus County Public Schools, and intended as a temporary location. The
    new facility will include a safe location for classrooms, counseling, a Reach office and other school safety features. The
    goal of the
    Pace program is for all girls to lead productive, engaged and fulfilling lives.
    b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     The building will be used to serve approximately 178 girls annually who are at risk of juvenile justice system involvement.
    Girls receive wrap around social services such as therapy, counseling and life skills. Girls in the Pace Center also receive
    their full academic school day. Services are provided in a trauma-informed, gender-responsive and strength-based
    environment.
    c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     The building will house a Pace Center and central operations for Pace Reach, providing comprehensive wrap around
    social services and the full academic school day to approximately 94 girls at risk of delinquency through our Pace Center
    and
    comprehensive wrap around social services to an additional 84 girls annually through Pace Reach. Case management
    for the girl's family is also provided to ensure a stable home environment.
    d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     Pace will serve approximately 178 middle and high school age girls at risk for juvenile justice system involvement and
    dropping out of school. The girls' families benefit from Pace both indirectly and through family engagement, home visits
    and counseling. The general public also benefits through increased public safety.
    e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?

     Funding will provide a safe space for Pace girls to receive comprehensive wrap around social services and their full
    academic school day. Short term outcomes for girls include 1) maintaining attendance and advancing grade levels
    2) demonstrated increase in self-efficacy and pro-social coping skills; and 3)reduction in risky behaviors. Long term
    outcomes include 1) high school graduation and enrollment in post-secondary education or the workforce; 2) obtaining
    employment; 3) no involvement in the criminal justice system; and 4) decreased economic dependency. Pace Center
    utilizes a combination of output data and outcome data to measure outcomes.
    f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
    for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for the contract?

     All Pace contracts with DJJ outline penalties for failing to meet deliverables. DJJ may reduce payments to Pace if
    sufficient evidence shows that deliverables were not met.
