4. Project/Program Description
    The Opioid Neuromodulation program supports continued collaboration between the Florida Department of Children and
   Families (DCF) and Insightec to advance low-intensity focused ultrasound (LIFU) research and development and
   potential non-invasive trial treatments for addiction, PTSD, and related behavioral health disorders. Building on prior
   state investments, the program funds R&D, proof-of-concept, and multicenter clinical trials evaluating LIFU’s ability to
   safely modulate brain circuits involved in dependency and relapse. Activities include technology and hardware
   refinement, pilot studies, and data generation required to advance regulatory and translational milestones. Funding
   directly supports clinical and technical work to develop a noninvasive therapeutic alternative to opioids, expanding
   Florida’s leadership in neuromodulation and strengthening its statewide capacity to address addiction and mental health
   through advanced brain technology.

   a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The purpose of this appropriation is to advance the DCF-Insightec collaboration for next-generation low-intensity
    focused ultrasound (LIFU) R&D and clinical trial implementation targeting addiction, PTSD, and related behavioral
    health and movement disorders. Building on prior state investment, funds will support continued technology and
    hardware refinement, proof-of-concept studies, pilot data generation, and multicenter clinical trial readiness. The
    program seeks to accelerate development of noninvasive neuromodulation therapies that may reduce dependence on
    opioids and improve long-term recovery outcomes. This initiative strengthens Florida’s scientific and clinical
    infrastructure, expands public-private collaboration, and reinforces the state’s national leadership in advancing
    innovative brain-based approaches to address addiction and mental health.
   b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Funds will support applied R&D and clinical trial activities conducted under the DCF-Insightec partnership to advance
    low-intensity focused ultrasound (LIFU) neuromodulation for addiction, PTSD, and related behavioral health disorders.
    Activities include continued system and software optimization, pilot and proof-of-concept studies, and preparation for
    multicenter clinical trials at partner hospitals and research centers. These efforts will advance both pre-clinical and
    clinical applications of Exablate Neuro technology, enabling implementation across Florida health systems. The
    program promotes collaboration among state, national, and international institutions, expands access to noninvasive
    therapeutic trials, and strengthens Florida’s infrastructure for delivering next-generation brain health and addiction
    treatment solutions.
   c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Direct services supported by this appropriation will expand access for Florida patients to advanced, noninvasive low-
    intensity focused ultrasound (LIFU) neuromodulation therapies targeting addiction, PTSD, and related behavioral
    health conditions. Funds will facilitate applied R&D, pre-clinical and clinical trial work and technology enhancements
    designed to improve treatment precision, safety, and patient outcomes. As these innovations are validated and
    deployed across Florida’s hospitals and research institutions, citizens will directly benefit through participation in
    advanced clinical programs and access to next-generation noninvasive therapeutic options. The program’s continued
    progress supports statewide and international collaboration, accelerates translational milestones, and advances
    Florida’s leadership in innovative approaches to addiction and mental health treatment.
   d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The target population includes individuals affected by addiction, PTSD, and other behavioral or neurological health
    disorders, as well as economically disadvantaged and underserved populations who may otherwise lack access to
    advanced noninvasive trial treatment options. The program will also serve adult and elderly persons and individuals
    experiencing co-occurring mental health and neurodegenerative conditions. Through Florida’s hospital and research
    networks, the initiative is expected to directly serve hundreds of participants in applied and clinical trial settings, while
    indirectly benefiting thousands more through the expansion of statewide, domestic and international diagnostic,
    therapeutic, and clinical research capabilities and partnerships. These efforts strengthen Florida’s ability to deliver
    innovative, brain-based approaches for treating addiction and improving behavioral health outcomes.
   e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
   be measured?

     The expected outcome is the continued advancement of low-intensity focused ultrasound (LIFU) neuromodulation
    technologies under the DCF-Insightec collaboration, broadening noninvasive treatment possibilities for addiction,
    PTSD, and related behavioral health disorders. Applied R&D and clinical trial progress are anticipated to yield
    improved safety, precision, and therapeutic effectiveness, while strengthening Florida’s leadership in innovative brain-
    based care. Measurable outcomes will derive from defined R&D milestones, clinical progress reports, and documented
    technology enhancements. This evidence-based framework allows for flexible, high-level evaluation of scientific,
    clinical, and societal benefit statewide.
   f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
   for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?

    Return of state funds
