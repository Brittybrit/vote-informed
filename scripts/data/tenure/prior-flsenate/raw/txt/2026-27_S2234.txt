1. Project Title          Hialeah - Volumetric Concrete Mixer Truck

2. Senate Sponsor         Bryan Avila

3. Date of Request        12/18/2025

4. Project/Program Description
    Funding for this project will equip the City of Hialeah with a volumetric concrete truck capable of producing high-quality
   concrete on-site and on-demand, eliminating delays caused by plant availability or inconvenient ready-mix delivery
   schedules. This advanced equipment allows crews to adjust mix design, slump, strength, and admixtures in real time to
   meet exact engineering specifications, an essential capability for the small, scattered, or unpredictable repair volumes
   common in municipal work. By generating only, the material needed at each location, the volumetric truck significantly
   reduces labor hours, boosts productivity, and removes costly scheduling conflicts and short-load fees associated with
   traditional ready-mix suppliers.

13. Program Performance
    a. What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?

     The goal of this request is to give the City of Hialeah the ability to produce concrete on site and on demand so
    sidewalk, curb, and small roadway repairs can be completed faster, at lower cost, and with better quality. The
    volumetric concrete truck will let crews adjust mix design in real time for each location, reduce wasted material, and cut
    delays tied to ready mix plant schedules. This will allow the City to reduce its backlog of sidewalk and concrete safety
    repairs, respond more quickly to trip hazards and ADA issues, and extend the useful life of existing streets and
    drainage infrastructure.
   b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?

     Funds will be used to purchase and equip one volumetric concrete truck that will be assigned to the Streets
    Department sidewalk and concrete repair program. Crews will use the truck to:
    Produce concrete on site for sidewalk panel replacement, curb and gutter repair, and any other public right-of-way
    concrete repairs.
    Support ADA upgrades such as curb ramps near schools, parks, transit stops, and senior facilities.
    Rebuild or adjust concrete around inlets and drainage structures as part of stormwater and flood mitigation work.
    Work orders will be generated through the City’s existing complaint and inspection process, scheduled by priority, and
    completed by a dedicated concrete crew using this equipment.
   c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?

     Residents will receive safer and more accessible sidewalks, curbs, and street edges through faster repair of broken
    sidewalks, uneven surfaces, and missing or damaged concrete. The project directly supports ADA access for people
    with disabilities, children walking to and from school, seniors, and transit users by improving curb ramps and pedestrian
    routes. Citizens will also see shorter response times to concrete related service requests and fewer long term
    "temporary” patches, since the City will have the ability to pour permanent concrete repairs without waiting on outside
    suppliers.
   d. Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?

     The project serves all residents, workers, and visitors who use sidewalks and streets in the City of Hialeah. Priority will
    be given to areas with high pedestrian activity such as schools, parks, transit stops, commercial corridors, and
    neighborhoods with large senior or disabled populations. Hialeah has an estimated population of roughly 222,000
    residents, and the improved sidewalk and concrete repair program will be available citywide, so the project has the
    potential to benefit essentially the entire population plus thousands of daily visitors.
   e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
   be measured?

     The project is expected to reduce the backlog of sidewalk and concrete safety repairs, shorten response times for high
    priority hazards, lower the City's unit cost for small concrete work, and reduce trip and fall liability claims against the
    City. With a volumetric concrete truck in service, the City expects to complete more permanent repairs per year, rely
    less on temporary asphalt patches, improve ADA accessibility at sidewalks and curb ramps, and remove unsafe
    conditions sooner. Outcomes will be measured using existing work order, financial, and risk management systems.
    Before the truck is deployed, the City will document a one-year baseline of: (1) number of sidewalk and concrete work
    orders completed, (2) average response time for high priority hazards, (3) number of ADA ramp and accessibility
    improvements, (4) average cost per repair, and (5) number and total cost of sidewalk related liability claims. After the
    truck is placed into service, these same metrics will be evaluated.
   f. What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard penalties
   for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?

     If the City fails to purchase and place the volumetric concrete truck into service as described or does not provide the
    required performance and financial reports, the contracting agency may require the return of unspent funds and
    withhold further reimbursements until deficiencies are corrected. If funds are used for purposes other than those
    approved, the agency may seek full repayment of the misused amount and may consider this in evaluating any future
    state funding requests from the City.
