4.   Project/Program Description
     Last updated in 2010, the statewide regional evacuation study provides essential data to counties and municipalities on the impact of
     hurricane storm surge for determining evacuation zones, mapping evacuation routes, and coordinating emergency shelter needs. Section
     163.3178, F.S., states that the Florida Division of Emergency Management is responsible for the update of these studies. Florida's 10
     Regional Planning Councils serve as key partners with FDEM to accomplish this mandate. This funding request is the first step in a full
     update of the study which begins with coordinating federal, state, regional, and local entities. It includes partnering with the private sector
     to complete a behavioral analysis based on the 2016 through 2019 hurricane seasons. Regional planning councils have the expertise to
     leverage technological advances and maximize coordination and participation from multiple disciplines involved in emergency
     preparedness, response, and recovery.

11. Program Performance
a.    What specific purpose or goal will be achieved by the funds requested?
      These funds will be used to begin to update the regional evacuation studies across the state as required in s. 163.3178(2)(d), F.S.




 b. What activities and services will be provided to meet the intended purpose of these funds?
      Tasks include the design and implementation of a behavioral survey to determine the evacuation behavior of citizens from the most
      recent storm seasons of 2016-2019. Begin work on a methodology to produce an updated regional evacuation study including data
      needs, technological advances, and coordination with local, state, and federal emergency management partners.




  c. What direct services will be provided to citizens by the appropriation project?
      Valuable data to local and statewide decision makers regarding the evacuation behavior of Floridians. This directly impacts hurricane
      preparedness efforts and helps to protect the citizens of Florida.




d.    Who is the target population served by this project? How many individuals are expected to be served?
      The entire population of Florida is vulnerable to the effects of a hurricane, whether directly (storm surge, winds, and flooding) or
      indirectly (increased congestion on roadways, lack of services, and etc.).




 e. What is the expected benefit or outcome of this project? What is the methodology by which this outcome will
    be measured?
      1. Behavioral survey results from a representative sample across the state of Florida.
      2. Updated methodology to complete the update to the regional evacuation studies, last completed in 2010.
      3. Coordination among local, state, and federal emergency management partners and all key stakeholders.




 f.   What are the suggested penalties that the contracting agency may consider in addition to its standard
      penalties for failing to meet deliverables or performance measures provided for in the contract?
      Reimbursement of funds to the state.
