17. CITY MANAGER’S REPORT:

Acting City Manager Daughtrey acknowledged that it was forty nine years ago today that he left
the Marine Corps. He said the county will have taken over ninety six percent (96%) of the water
accounts by the end of this month. The City is continuing to stop the leaks around the City so it
can save on the money it is paying to the county. The City thought it would receive almost two
million dollars ($2,000,000.00) from the tax roll, but in actuality, it was over four hundred
thousand dollars ($400,000.00) that was reported from the county. He said the auditor general
spent almost two years in the City and they will come back in eighteen months to see if the 99
Findings that they compiled have been corrected and the City is in compliance. The City has to
remain in a no deficit spending to get out of the financial emergency it is currently in. They also
need to deal with the fifty year old pipes that are underground that could cause an issue if the
infrastructure is not updated.

Vice Mayor Davis asked how the lighting concerns are being addressed because it has come up
in the past.

Acting City Manager Daughtrey said the City submits a ticket whenever there are lighting issues
and it is addressed accordingly.

Vice Mayor Davis said he hopes that a safe environment is maintained for the community and all
of the lights are in working order.

Commissioner Kelley commended the manager and his staff for the work they have been doing
and the update that was provided to the commission and the public at the workshop last week. He
said he hopes that the 99 Findings are addressed and the issues that need handling stays on the
managers radar.

Acting City Manager Daughtrey said it will cost an additional five hundred thousand dollars to
complete Cairo Lane. The City will have to pay upfront and be reimbursed through the state
revolving loan fund.

Mayor Pigatt commended the manager for his years of service with the Marines. He asked for a
report on the water bill transition, the operational audits, and the drainage pipes. He impressed
upon the manager to provide these things along with the solutions and the timeline for how they
will be addressed. The City needs to be able to tell the residents exactly what is going on.
