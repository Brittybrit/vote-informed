Ballot317

How it works

Upload the sample ballot for your precinct and Ballot317 turns it into a list of every race and question on it. Tap a candidate to see who funds them and who endorses them, with a link for every claim.

Your ballot

Marion County publishes one sample ballot per precinct. Find yours on the Indiana Voter Portal, save it as a PDF and upload it. The races are read from the PDF by AI, so compare them with your official sample ballot. Your picks stay in your own browser.

Who funds each candidate

Congress
Itemized contributions from the Federal Election Commission, refreshed daily, plus outside groups’ spending reported to the FEC.
Secretary of State, Comptroller, Treasurer, Indiana Senate and House
Contributions reported to the Indiana Election Division in 2025 and 2026, from its public data downloads. The page says which reports are included and through what date. Indiana sets no limit on gifts from individuals to state candidates, so large single gifts are normal here and are shown as given.
Prosecutor, sheriff, other county offices, township offices, judges and school boards
These candidates file with the Marion County Election Board, which posts the reports as PDF files, some handwritten. Until the county publishes them as data, the donors shown for these races come from news coverage and web research, and each one links to its source. The county’s reports are at indy.gov.

Only money for this election cycle (2025 and 2026) is shown.

How local is the money

Each giver is placed by the address on the filing: in the area that votes for the office, elsewhere in Marion County, elsewhere in Indiana, or outside Indiana. Marion County is matched by ZIP code, and a few ZIP codes on the county line reach into neighboring counties, so the split is approximate. Legislative districts are matched to Marion County as a whole, not the district. Groups are placed by the address they filed under, which shows where the money sits, not who votes.

Endorsements and ratings

Endorsements are listed only when a group or person explicitly backs the candidate in this election, with a link. Groups urging a vote against a candidate are listed separately. Ratings (bar polls, qualification ratings, scorecards) are shown only when the rating appears on the rating group’s own website. Labor unions are labeled by the kind of workers they represent, not left or right. Judicial candidates are also checked for ties to the Federalist Society, with the evidence shown.

What it can get wrong

Background, endorsements and donors for county races come from AI-assisted web research. Every item links to its source; links the research did not actually find are removed. Research can still miss things or attach something to the wrong person with the same name, so check the sources, and use Report a problem with this page if something is wrong. Spanish text is machine-translated.
